# Day 321 🚪🚪

**L-321** · **STAGE 8 EXIT GATE** — two live 45-minute LLD interviews

**Time:** 3+ hrs · **Mode:** REVIEW · **Stage exit gate**

> **⭐ The gate is not "can you design a parking lot?" You have done that. It is: can a human hand you
> a problem you have not prepared, and watch you produce a defensible design in forty-five minutes
> while narrating?**
>
> **⭐ Two live interviews with a real person. Not self-assessed.** Everything else today prepares
> for those two conversations.

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ The method card

**⭐ Write this from memory. If you cannot, re-read Day 308 before doing anything else.**

```
⭐  0–05  CLARIFY     3 questions → ⭐ SAY THE SCOPE → get agreement
⭐  5–10  FLOW        actors; ⭐ pick ONE core flow
⭐ 10–18  ENTITIES    nouns → prune → ⭐ where does each RULE live?
⭐ 18–25  RELATIONS   multiplicity on every line; ⭐ NAME THE AGGREGATE
⭐ 25–40  CODE        ⭐ one flow, end to end, real Java
⭐ 40–45  CLOSE       ⭐ concurrency · extensions · what you cut  ← UNPROMPTED
```

## ⭐ The five recurring decisions

| Decision | ⭐ Default | ⭐ Switch when |
|---|---|---|
| Enum or class hierarchy? | ⭐ **enum + field** | ⭐ genuinely different *behaviour* (chess pieces, not vehicles) |
| Strategy or `if`? | `if` for two fixed cases | ⭐ **they said it varies** |
| Where does the rule live? | ⭐ **on the entity it constrains** | it spans two aggregates |
| State pattern or switch? | ⭐ enum with abstract methods | ⭐ 6+ states and 5+ events |
| Lock, CAS, or confine? | ⭐ **CAS on the resource** | multiple fields must move together |

## ⭐⭐ The two rules that generalise across every problem

> **⭐ RULE 1 — The limited resource.** *Spot, seat, balance, driver, library copy, stock.* **Claimed
> by an atomic conditional update whose `rowsAffected` decides · held with an owner and an expiry
> because a human is in the loop · and expired holds treated as free by a read-time predicate, never
> by a sweeper.**

> **⭐ RULE 2 — Frozen inputs.** *Risk scores, split amounts, exchange rates, surge multipliers,
> prices, fares.* **Anything a user is charged for or judged by must be reproducible from the values
> that were true at the time — stored on the record, never re-derived later.**

**⭐ Rule 1 covers six of the eleven problems. Rule 2 covers four. Being able to state both, and then
apply one to a problem you have never seen, is what Stage 8 was for.**

## ⭐ The other recurring shapes

| Shape | ⭐ Where it appeared |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **The thing vs its availability-in-context** | ⭐ Seat/ShowSeat · Book/BookItem · Product/Stock · Spot/occupancy |
| ⭐ **A cost function beats a rule chain** | ⭐ elevator dispatch · driver matching · seat adjacency |
| ⭐ **Lazy evaluation beats a background sweeper** | ⭐ cache TTL · seat holds · stock reservations |
| ⭐ **Immutable event + derived state** | ⭐ Splitwise balances · order status from shipments · the ATM journal |
| ⭐ **An offer/hold is not an assignment** | ⭐ driver matching · seat holds · library holds |
| ⭐ **Sorted acquisition order** | ⭐ multi-seat holds · account transfers |

# Part 2 — 🚪🚪 ⭐ The gate: two live interviews

## ⭐ The setup

| Requirement | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **A real human interviewer** | ⭐ **peer, mentor, or a paid mock. Not yourself.** |
| ⭐ **A problem you have not prepared** | ⭐ ask them to pick, and not to tell you in advance |
| ⭐ **45 minutes, timed, recorded** | ⭐ the recording is the deliverable |
| ⭐ **They must interrupt and push back** | ⭐ **a silent interviewer is not a rehearsal** |
| Two separate sessions | ⭐ **on different days** — one good round can be luck |

**⭐ Give your interviewer this instruction sheet:**

```
⭐ Pick any of these, do NOT tell me in advance:
⭐   hotel booking · a coffee-shop ordering system · a car-rental system · an auction site ·
⭐   a warehouse inventory system · a music streaming library · a task scheduler ·
⭐   a bike-share dock system · an online quiz platform · a restaurant reservation system

⭐ During the round, please:
⭐  1. Answer my clarifying questions BRIEFLY, and be vague where a real one would be.
⭐  2. At ~minute 20, ask me to add a requirement I did not plan for.
⭐  3. At ~minute 35, ask "what happens if two users do this simultaneously?"
⭐  4. Push back ONCE on a decision, even if you agree with it.
⭐  5. Score me on the rubric below and give me the two lowest rows.
```

⚠️ **Every problem on that list is deliberately not one of the eleven** — and every one is solvable
by Rule 1, Rule 2, or the thing/availability split. **That is the test.**

## ⭐ The rubric — they score, not you

| # | Criterion | ⭐ Fail | ⭐ Pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ 1 | **Clarified before designing** | dived in | ⭐ 3 questions + a stated scope |
| ⭐ 2 | **Scope agreed out loud** | implicit | ⭐ **explicit, and they confirmed** |
| 3 | Nouns pruned | 15 classes | ⭐ entities, values and enums separated |
| ⭐ 4 | **Rules live in the right place** | all in a service | ⭐ **on the entity, unbypassable** |
| ⭐ 5 | **Aggregate named** | never mentioned | ⭐ **named, with the reason** |
| 6 | Multiplicity stated | none | on every relationship |
| ⭐ 7 | **Working code for one flow** | fragments | ⭐ **one flow, end to end** |
| ⭐ 8 | **Trade-offs narrated** | silent choices | ⭐ **"X over Y because ___, I'd switch if ___"** |
| ⭐ 9 | **Concurrency raised unprompted** | ⭐ only when asked | ⭐ **volunteered, with a named race** |
| ⭐ 10 | **Distributed answer given** | ⭐ none | ⭐ in-process guard mapped to its equivalent |
| ⭐ 11 | **Extension absorbed** | required a redesign | ⭐ **a new strategy/class** |
| ⭐ 12 | **Cuts stated** | looked forgotten | ⭐ **deliberate, and named** |
| 13 | Handled push-back | folded or dug in | ⭐ named the cost, then decided |
| ⭐ 14 | **Finished on time** | still drawing at 40 | ⭐ **closed at 45 with the summary** |

**⭐ Pass = 12 of 14 in BOTH rounds, with rows 7, 9 and 14 mandatory.** Failing row 7 or 14 is an
automatic re-run — those are the two that end real interviews.

## ⭐ Play the recording back

| Check | ⭐ Fail signal |
|---|---|
| ⭐ Longest silence | ⭐ **over 20 seconds without narration** |
| Time in each phase | ⭐ coding started after minute 30 |
| ⭐ Trade-offs per decision | ⭐ choices made with no reason given |
| ⭐ **Who talked more** | ⭐ **you should be at roughly 70%** |
| Recovery from push-back | you changed your mind with no reason |

# Part 4 — 🚪🚪 ⭐ The gate

## ⭐ You may not proceed until

- [ ] ⭐ **Two live 45-minute interviews with a human, on different days, both recorded**
- [ ] ⭐ **12 of 14 rubric rows in both**, with rows 7, 9 and 14 passed in each
- [ ] ⭐ **Both problems were ones you had not prepared**
- [ ] ⭐ **Both recordings played back**, with the silence and phase-timing checks run
- [ ] ⭐ **All 40 questions answered cold**, gaps re-read rather than noted
- [ ] ⭐ **The method card written from memory**, both rules included
- [ ] ⭐ **One problem re-run in a plain text editor** with no diagram at all
- [ ] ⭐ **Eleven solutions committed**, each with a README naming the hard part in one sentence

## ⭐ What Stage 8 proved

```
⭐ You can take a vague problem, agree its scope, and produce a coherent model.
⭐ You put rules where they cannot be bypassed, and you can say why that is correctness
⭐    rather than taste.
⭐ You find the race before you are asked, and you know its distributed equivalent.
⭐ ⭐ And you generalise: a problem you have never seen resolves to a limited resource
⭐    held across a human, or a decision that must be reproducible from frozen inputs.
```

> **⭐⭐ The last one is the real outcome.** Eleven problems were never the point — **the point was
> that the twelfth, which nobody prepared you for, is recognisably one of them.**

---

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
|---|---|
| ⭐ Self-assessing instead of using a human | ⭐ **you cannot hear your own silences** |
| ⭐ Choosing a problem you have practised | ⭐ the gate tests transfer, not recall |
| One round instead of two | one good round is luck |
| ⭐ Not recording | ⭐ the playback is where the findings are |
| ⭐ Noting a rubric gap instead of fixing it | ⭐ it arrives in the real interview instead |
| Memorising eleven solutions | ⭐ **the twelfth problem exposes it in four minutes** |
| Skipping the text-editor run | remote rounds have no whiteboard |
| ⭐ Treating the two rules as trivia | ⭐ they are the transfer mechanism |

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes, recorded, no notes:** *"How do you approach a design problem you have never seen?"*

**⭐ The answer that closes Stage 8: the clock, then the two rules.** *"I clarify for five minutes and
state the scope. Then I look for two things: is there a limited resource being held across a user
interaction — because that is a reservation with an expiry and an atomic claim — and is there
anything a user gets charged for or judged by, because that has to be reproducible from inputs frozen
at the time. Most problems are one, the other, or both."*

**⭐ That is not a script. It is what eleven problems taught you**, and it is why the twelfth is
tractable.
