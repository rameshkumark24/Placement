# Day 462

**L-462** · The resume I — every line is a question you invited

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW · **⭐ Stage 15 opens**

> **⭐⭐ Your resume is not a summary of your life. It is a list of questions you have volunteered to be
> asked. If you cannot defend a line for five minutes under pressure, it is not an achievement — it is a
> liability, and deleting it makes the document stronger.**
>
> Today: what actually gets read and in what order, **the bullet formula and the "so what" test**, the
> skills section that gets people rejected, and ATS mechanics that silently destroy good resumes.

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ What is actually read, and in what order

```
⭐⭐ PASS 1 — the recruiter, ~7 SECONDS. They look for exactly five things:
⭐   current/most recent TITLE · company · ⭐ years of experience · ⭐⭐ the 3–4 keywords in the JD ·
⭐   whether the formatting is normal
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ if those are not findable in seven seconds, nothing else on the page matters.

⭐⭐ PASS 2 — the engineer, ~60 SECONDS. They look for:
⭐   ⭐⭐ something specific and non-generic to ask about · ⭐ evidence of depth in ONE thing ·
⭐   ⭐⭐ anything that looks inflated ⇒ ⭐ THAT is where the interview starts.
```

| ⭐ Rule | |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **One page** | ⭐⭐ under ~8 years of experience. **Two pages is not a reward for effort** |
| ⭐ Reverse chronological | ⭐ always — "functional" resumes read as hiding something |
| ⭐⭐ **Top third is the whole game** | ⭐⭐ **the most impressive true thing goes above the fold** |
| ⭐ No objective statement | ⭐ "seeking a challenging role" costs a line and says nothing |
| ⭐ No photo, no age, no marital status | ⭐ (market-dependent; default to omitting) |
| ⭐ No "references available on request" | ⭐ assumed |

## ⭐ The section order that works for you

| ⭐ Fresher / <1 yr | ⭐ 1–3 yrs |
|---|---|
| Contact + links | Contact + links |
| ⭐⭐ **Projects** (flagship first) | ⭐⭐ **Experience** |
| Skills | Skills |
| Experience / internships | ⭐ Projects (flagship only — 3 bullets) |
| Education | Education (one line) |

> **⭐⭐ For a fresher, the Projects section IS the experience section**, and it should be written exactly
> like one — same bullet formula, same numbers, same specificity. **A flagship you built and operated
> (Stages 7, 10, 11) outranks an internship where you fixed CSS**, and formatting it as an afterthought
> throws that advantage away.

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The bullet formula

```
⭐⭐ [ACTION VERB] + [WHAT you built] + [HOW / with what] + [⭐⭐ MEASURED RESULT]

⭐ ❌ "Worked on the backend using Spring Boot."
⭐    ⇒ ⭐⭐ no action, no scope, no result. ⭐ It invites nothing and proves nothing.

⭐ ⚠️ "Built REST APIs with Spring Boot and PostgreSQL."
⭐    ⇒ ⭐ true, generic, indistinguishable from 400 other resumes.

⭐ ✅ "Cut p99 latency from 820 ms to 240 ms on the orders endpoint by removing an N+1 query
⭐     and adding a Redis read-through cache; verified with a 500 rps k6 load test."
⭐    ⇒ ⭐⭐ specific · ⭐⭐ measured · ⭐⭐ CHECKABLE · ⭐ and it invites three questions you can answer.
```

| ⭐⭐ The "so what" test — apply to every bullet | |
|---|---|
| ⭐ "Implemented JWT authentication" | ⭐⭐ **so what?** anyone can follow a tutorial |
| ⭐⭐ "Implemented JWT with **refresh-token rotation and reuse detection**, so a stolen refresh token invalidates the family" | ⭐⭐ **that is a decision, and it has a reason** (Day 258) |

| ⭐ Verbs that carry weight | ⭐ Verbs that do not |
|---|---|
| ⭐ Designed · Built · Cut · Reduced · Migrated · Automated · Diagnosed · Instrumented | ⭐⭐ Worked on · Helped with · Was responsible for · Participated in · Assisted |

⚠️ **"Responsible for" describes a job description, not a contribution.** Nobody is hired for what they
were *assigned*. **Rewrite every such bullet as something that changed because you were there.**

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The skills section that gets people rejected

```
⭐⭐ THE TRAP: listing a technology to look broad, then being asked about it.
⭐   You list "Kubernetes" because you did a tutorial.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ the interviewer asks a normal question — "how do you do a zero-downtime deploy?"
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ you cannot answer ⇒ ⭐⭐ THEY NOW DISCOUNT EVERY OTHER LINE ON THE PAGE.
⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ One unsupportable claim is worth MORE damage than three extra keywords are worth gain.
```

| ⭐⭐ Tier your skills honestly | ⭐ Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Strong** | ⭐⭐ **I can be interviewed on this for 45 minutes** — Java, Spring Boot, PostgreSQL |
| ⭐ **Working** | ⭐ I have built and shipped with it — Docker, Kafka, Redis, AWS, React |
| ⭐ **Familiar** | ⭐⭐ **I have used it; do not go deep** — Kubernetes, Terraform |

**⭐ Label the tiers explicitly.** It reads as self-awareness rather than modesty, **and it directs the
interviewer toward the ground you are strongest on** — which is a genuinely useful thing to control.

| ⭐ Also | |
|---|---|
| ⭐ Do not list an IDE, Windows, or MS Office | ⭐ it dates the document |
| ⭐ Do not list "OOP, DSA, problem-solving" | ⭐⭐ everyone claims these; they are noise |
| ⭐⭐ **Do list the specific version/flavour where it matters** | ⭐ "Java 21 (records, virtual threads)" is a signal |

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ ATS mechanics, and links

| ⭐ ATS reality | |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Single column, no tables, no text boxes** | ⭐⭐ multi-column layouts frequently parse into nonsense |
| ⭐ Standard section headings | ⭐ "Experience", "Education", "Skills" — not "My Journey" |
| ⭐⭐ **Nothing important in headers/footers** | ⭐⭐ **many parsers drop them — including your phone number** |
| ⭐ PDF, unless the portal says otherwise | ⭐ named `Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf` |
| ⭐ No images, icons, or graphical skill bars | ⭐⭐ a "Java ████░ 80%" bar conveys nothing and parses as nothing |
| ⭐ Mirror the JD's exact terms | ⭐ "Spring Boot" if they say Spring Boot; both if unsure |
| ⭐⭐ **Never white-text keyword stuffing** | ⭐⭐ it is detected, and it is an instant permanent rejection |

```
⭐⭐ THE TEST THAT TAKES TWO MINUTES:
⭐   copy your PDF's text and paste it into a plain text editor.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ If the reading ORDER is scrambled or anything is MISSING,
⭐     ⭐⭐ that is exactly what the ATS saw. Fix the layout, not the wording.
```

| ⭐ Links — and the rule that matters | |
|---|---|
| ⭐ GitHub | ⭐⭐ **only if the pinned repos are good.** A GitHub of half-finished tutorials is a negative |
| ⭐⭐ **Live demo** | ⭐⭐ **a dead demo link is far worse than no link** — it proves you did not check |
| ⭐ LinkedIn | ⭐ must not contradict the resume |
| ⭐ A README that explains the architecture | ⭐⭐ this is often what actually gets read (Day 470) |

⚠️ **Check every link the morning of every application.** A 404 on your own project, discovered by the
interviewer, **is the single cheapest possible way to look careless** — and it happens constantly because
free hosting tiers expire.

## Interview questions

**Q: How do you decide what goes on your resume?**
By whether I can defend it for five minutes under pressure. Every line is a question I have volunteered
to be asked, so anything I cannot go three follow-ups deep on is a liability rather than an achievement.
That rule removes a surprising amount — half-remembered technologies, things I watched someone else do —
and what is left is short, specific and safe to attack. It also means the strongest thing I have goes in
the top third, because a recruiter reads for about seven seconds and an engineer for about sixty.

**Q: Your bullet says you improved performance. Tell me more.**
That is exactly why I would not write it that way. My version names the endpoint, the before and after
numbers, the mechanism and how it was verified — p99 from eight hundred and twenty milliseconds to two
hundred and forty on the orders endpoint, by removing an N+1 query and adding a read-through cache,
measured under a five hundred request per second load test. Specific and checkable beats impressive and
vague, because vague invites the interviewer to assume the smallest possible version of what you did.

**Q: You list Kubernetes. How do you do a zero-downtime deploy?**
This is the question the skills section has to survive, which is why I tier mine explicitly — strong,
working, familiar. Anything in the strong tier I can be interviewed on for forty-five minutes. Listing a
technology to look broad and then failing a normal question about it is the worst outcome available,
because one unsupportable claim makes the interviewer discount every other line on the page. Three extra
keywords are never worth that.

**Q: Why does formatting matter so much?**
Because a large fraction of resumes are parsed before a human sees them, and multi-column layouts, tables
and text boxes routinely come out scrambled. Contact details in the header get dropped by many parsers,
which means an otherwise good resume arrives with no phone number. The check takes two minutes — copy the
PDF text into a plain editor and look at the reading order. Whatever you see there is what the system
saw. If it is broken, the fix is the layout, not the wording.

**Q: Should a fresher lead with projects or education?**
Projects, formatted exactly like an experience section — same verbs, same numbers, same specificity. A
system I designed, built, containerised, deployed to AWS and load-tested is stronger evidence than a
degree classification, and burying it under education signals that I think the same. Education becomes
one line, and it moves to the bottom permanently after the first real job.

## Mini task

1. ⭐⭐ Print your resume. For **every bullet**, write down the **three questions** it invites. Answer them
   aloud.
2. ⭐⭐ **Delete every line you could not defend for five minutes.** Count what you removed.
3. ⭐ Rewrite every "responsible for" / "worked on" bullet using the **four-part formula.**
4. ⭐⭐ Apply the **"so what" test** to all of them. Anything a tutorial-follower could also claim gets
   rewritten to name the *decision*.
5. ⭐ Add a **number** to at least half your bullets. Tomorrow covers where numbers come from.
6. ⭐⭐ **Tier your skills** into strong / working / familiar. Be brutal about the strong tier.
7. ⭐ Cut every noise skill: OOP, problem-solving, MS Office, IDEs.
8. ⭐⭐ **Paste your PDF's text into a plain editor.** Fix anything scrambled or missing.
9. ⭐ Move all contact details **out of the header** into the body.
10. ⭐⭐ Open **every link** on the resume. Fix or remove anything dead. Put a reminder to re-check weekly.
11. ⭐ Give it to someone for **ten seconds**, then ask what they remember. If it is not your best thing,
    reorder.
12. ⭐ Read the whole thing **aloud**. Anything you stumble on is badly written.

# 🚪 Exit questions

1. State the rule that decides what belongs on a resume.
2. What does a recruiter look for, and in how long?
3. What does an engineer look for, and where does the interview then start?
4. Give the four-part bullet formula with a good and bad example.
5. What is the "so what" test?
6. Why is "responsible for" always wrong?
7. Explain the skills-section trap and the cost asymmetry.
8. Give the three tiers and what each means operationally.
9. Give five ATS formatting rules.
10. What is the two-minute parse test?
11. Why is a dead demo link worse than no link?
12. How should a fresher order the sections, and why?

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes:** *"Walk me through your resume."*

**⭐ This is not a reading of the document — they have it. It is a 90-second narrative with a shape:
where you are now, the one thing you have gone deepest on, and why this role is the next step.**

**⭐ The middle is the part that matters: pick one project and give it three sentences — what it does, the
one hard decision in it, and a number. "It is a multi-tenant SaaS API; the hard part was making event
publishing reliable, which I solved with a transactional outbox rather than publishing after commit; it
runs on ECS behind an ALB and holds p99 under 250 ms at 500 rps."**

**⭐ Then stop. A walkthrough that lists every line invites nothing; one that plants a specific,
defensible hook decides which forty minutes you are about to spend.**
