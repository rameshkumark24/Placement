# Day 356

**L-356** · Design 12 — ticket booking and the flash sale

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** BUILD

> **⭐ [Day 316](Day-316.md) solved seat holds. At scale the problem is completely different: ten
> million people arrive in the same sixty seconds for fifty thousand seats.**
>
> No amount of database tuning survives that. **The answer is not to make the system faster — it is
> to stop most of the traffic from reaching it at all**, and the mechanism is one most candidates
> have never considered.

---

# Part 1 — ⭐ Requirements and the number that changes everything

| ⭐ Functional | ⭐ Non-functional |
|---|---|
| Browse events, view a seat map | ⭐⭐ **never oversell — this is a legal obligation, not a preference** |
| ⭐ **Hold seats while paying** | ⭐ **fairness** — arrival order should mostly matter |
| Pay, confirm, deliver tickets | ⭐ browse may be stale; **claims must be exact** |
| Cancel, refund | ⭐⭐ **survive a 1,000× traffic spike** |
| ⭐ **Bot resistance** | availability during the on-sale, above all else |

```
⭐ ESTIMATE
⭐   Normal: 1M tickets/day ⇒ ~12/s ⇒ ⭐ trivially one database
⭐   ⭐⭐ ON-SALE: 10M users in 60 s for 50,000 seats
⭐     ⇒ ⭐⭐ 170,000 arrivals/s
⭐     ⇒ ⭐⭐ 200:1 people-to-seats — ⭐⭐ 99.5% of that traffic CANNOT succeed
```

> **⭐ The last line is the whole design.** Almost everyone who arrives is going to fail. **Serving
> them a seat map, a session, and a chance to contend for a row is spending your entire capacity on
> requests that were always going to be rejected.**

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The deep dive: the virtual waiting room

```
⭐ WITHOUT:  10M users ──► seat map, holds, payment  ⇒ ⭐⭐ everything melts
⭐
⭐ WITH:     10M users ──► ⭐ WAITING ROOM (a queue + a counter)
⭐                              │ ⭐ admit ~500/s
⭐                              ▼
⭐                        ⭐ purchase flow (seat map, hold, pay)  ⇒ ⭐ sized for 500/s
```

| ⭐ Why it works | |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **The queue is trivially cheap** | ⭐⭐ a position and a token — no seat map, no session, no database |
| ⭐ **The purchase system is sized for reality** | ⭐ 500 concurrent buyers, not 10 million |
| ⭐ Admission rate is a control knob | ⭐ **turn it down if the backend struggles** |
| ⭐ **It is honest** | ⭐ "you are 1,240,000th" beats a spinner and a timeout |
| ⭐ Fairness becomes explicit | ⭐ **FIFO by arrival, or a lottery — a product decision you can state** |

```
⭐ THE QUEUE ITSELF:
⭐   1. Arrive → ⭐ get a signed token with a position (an atomic INCR — Day 208)
⭐   2. Poll (or receive a push) for your position — ⭐ served from a CDN-cacheable endpoint
⭐   3. ⭐ Admitted ⇒ receive a short-lived ACCESS TOKEN for the purchase flow
⭐   4. ⭐⭐ The purchase flow REJECTS anyone without a valid token
```

> **⭐ Step 4 is what makes it real.** A waiting room you can bypass by opening a direct URL is
> theatre. **The purchase endpoints must require the token**, or the queue protects nothing.

⚠️ **Fairness has a product answer, and it should be asked about.** Pure FIFO rewards whoever had the
fastest connection at 09:00:00.000 — **which in practice means bots.** A **lottery among everyone who
arrived within the first N seconds** is fairer and is increasingly what real ticketing systems do.
**Naming that trade-off is a strong move.**

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ Contention: splitting the inventory

**⭐ Even at 500 buyers/s, 50,000 seats in one table is a contention problem** — everyone wants row A.

```
⭐ NAIVE: one inventory row per seat, 500 buyers all attacking the popular rows
⭐ ⇒ ⭐ conditional updates mostly return rowsAffected = 0 ⇒ retry storms

⭐⭐ BUCKETED INVENTORY:
⭐   Split 50,000 seats into ⭐ 100 buckets of 500.
⭐   ⭐ Assign each admitted user to ONE bucket (hash their token).
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ contention drops 100×; a user only ever contends with ~5 others
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ leftovers are rebalanced between buckets as they drain
```

| ⭐ Property | |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Contention falls linearly with bucket count** | ⭐ the same idea as key splitting for a hot cache key (Day 326) |
| ⭐ Rebalancing is needed | ⭐ **or the last user finds an empty bucket while seats remain elsewhere** |
| ⭐ **Two-phase works too** | ⭐ decrement a *count* first ("is there any seat?"), then choose a specific seat |
| Best-available flows suit buckets | seat-picking flows need the map, and therefore care |

⚠️ **The "any seat" versus "that seat" distinction matters.** A general-admission or best-available
sale is a **counter decrement**, which shards beautifully. **A reserved-seating sale where users pick
from a map is inherently more contended** — and the honest answer is that the seat map is a *cached,
stale* view and only the claim is authoritative.

**⭐ The claim itself is unchanged from [Day 316](Day-316.md):**

```sql
UPDATE seat SET status='HELD', held_by=:user, held_until=now()+interval '8 minutes'
 WHERE id=:seat AND (status='AVAILABLE' OR (status='HELD' AND held_until < now()));
-- ⭐ rowsAffected decides. ⭐ Expiry is a read-time predicate, not a sweeper.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐ Bots, payment, and what breaks

## ⭐ Bot resistance is a functional requirement here

**⭐ Scalping is the defining abuse of this product**, and a design that ignores it is incomplete.

| Control | ⭐ Note |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Identity before the queue** | ⭐ account age, verified phone — **raises the cost of a thousand identities** |
| ⭐ Per-account and per-payment-instrument limits | ⭐ **the payment card is harder to fake than an account** |
| ⭐ **Rate limiting at the edge** (Day 336) | before anything expensive |
| Device fingerprinting, proof of work, CAPTCHA at admission | ⭐ **not at arrival — that is where the load is** |
| ⭐ Post-hoc cancellation | ⭐ detect and void bulk purchases afterwards; **you cannot win in real time** |

> **⭐ The realistic framing: you cannot fully prevent bots, you can only make them expensive and
> reversible.** Saying that, rather than claiming a CAPTCHA solves it, is the credible answer.

## ⭐ Payment, and the case that has no clean answer

**⭐ Day 316's paid-after-expiry problem, at scale.** With a 200:1 ratio it happens constantly.

| ⭐ Rule | |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Hold TTL must exceed the payment gateway timeout** | ⭐ or you manufacture the case yourself |
| ⭐ **Never oversell** | ⭐⭐ **refund automatically and notify** |
| ⭐ Idempotency on callbacks | ⭐ gateways retry (Day 335) |
| ⭐ **Payment is a saga** (Day 342) | ⭐ hold → charge → confirm, with compensation at each step |
| ⭐ Queue the confirmations | ⭐ **the payment provider is itself rate-limited** (Day 350) |

## ⭐ The read path

```
⭐ Event pages and seat maps: ⭐ CDN-cached, seconds stale, and THAT IS FINE.
⭐ ⭐ Availability shown is a hint. ⭐⭐ The conditional update is the truth.
⭐ ⇒ ⭐ 10M people refreshing a page costs you nothing.
```

| ⭐ Breaks first | ⭐ Mitigation |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Arrival rate** | ⭐⭐ **the waiting room — a counter and a CDN** |
| ⭐ Seat-row contention | bucketed inventory |
| ⭐ **Payment provider throughput** | queue confirmations; multiple providers |
| Seat map reads | CDN; stale is acceptable |
| ⭐ Hold expiry backlog | ⭐ lazy predicate — **there is no backlog** |

```
⭐ MONITOR: ⭐ admission rate vs backend saturation · queue depth and estimated wait
⭐          ⭐⭐ oversell count (must be ZERO) · hold-to-purchase conversion
⭐          ⭐ tickets sold/min (business metric) · bot-flagged accounts
⭐ CUT:     recommendations, dynamic pricing, resale marketplace, social features
⭐ AT 10×:  ⭐ the waiting room absorbs it — ⭐⭐ that is precisely why it exists
```

## ⭐ Traps

| Trap | Consequence |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ Letting 10M users into the purchase flow | ⭐⭐ **capacity spent on requests that cannot succeed** |
| ⭐ A waiting room you can bypass | ⭐ theatre — **the purchase endpoints must require the token** |
| ⭐ Pure FIFO with no bot answer | the fastest connections are bots |
| ⭐ **One inventory row per popular section** | ⭐ retry storms on `rowsAffected = 0` |
| ⭐ Bucketing with no rebalancing | ⭐ **empty buckets while seats remain** |
| ⭐ Treating the seat map as authoritative | ⭐ it is a stale hint; **only the claim is truth** |
| ⭐ **Hold TTL shorter than the gateway timeout** | ⭐⭐ you manufacture paid-after-expiry |
| Overselling "just this once" | a legal problem, not a technical one |
| ⭐ CAPTCHA at arrival rather than admission | ⭐ you put work where the load is |
| Claiming bots are solved | they are made expensive and reversible |
| A sweeper for hold expiry | Day 316 — read-time predicate |

## Interview questions

**Q: Ten million people arrive for fifty thousand seats. What do you do?**
Keep almost all of them out of the purchase system. Two hundred to one means ninety-nine and a half
percent of that traffic cannot succeed, so serving them seat maps and sessions spends the entire
capacity on requests that were always going to be rejected. A virtual waiting room — a counter and a
signed token, cacheable at the edge — admits perhaps five hundred a second into a purchase flow sized
for exactly that.

**Q: How do you make the waiting room real rather than cosmetic?**
The purchase endpoints require a valid short-lived access token issued at admission. If someone can
open a direct URL and buy, the queue protects nothing. Admission rate then becomes a control knob you
turn down when the backend struggles, which is much better than discovering saturation from timeouts.

**Q: Even at five hundred a second, everyone wants the front row. How do you handle contention?**
Split the inventory into buckets and hash each admitted user to one, so contention falls linearly
with bucket count and a user only competes with a handful of others rather than everyone. It is the
same key-splitting fix as a hot cache key. Buckets need rebalancing as they drain, or the last buyer
finds an empty bucket while seats remain elsewhere. And for best-available sales I decrement a count
first, then pick a specific seat, which shards far better than seat-picking does.

**Q: What is the truth about availability?**
The conditional update. The seat map is a CDN-cached, seconds-stale hint, which is what lets ten
million people refresh a page for free. Only the update with `rowsAffected` deciding is
authoritative, and the hold it creates expires by a read-time predicate rather than a sweeper. If the
seat map were authoritative I would have to make ten million reads consistent, which is the design
that fails.

**Q: How do you keep bots out?**
Not entirely — you make them expensive and reversible. Identity requirements before the queue so a
thousand accounts cost something, limits per account and per payment instrument since cards are
harder to fake than emails, edge rate limiting, and challenges at admission rather than at arrival,
because arrival is where the load is. Then post-hoc detection and cancellation of bulk purchases,
because you cannot win in real time and pretending otherwise is worse than admitting it.

## Mini task

1. **Run the 45-minute framework**, timed, with **the waiting room as the deep dive.**
2. ⭐ Load-test the purchase flow **without a queue** at 50× capacity. Record error rates and latency.
   **Add a waiting room and repeat.**
3. ⭐ Implement the queue with an atomic counter and **signed tokens.** Then **bypass it** with a
   direct call and confirm the endpoint rejects you.
4. ⭐ Serve position lookups from a **CDN-cacheable endpoint** and measure the load removed.
5. ⭐⭐ **Reproduce row contention**: 500 threads claiming from one section. Measure the failed-update
   rate. **Bucket into 100 and re-measure.**
6. ⭐ Drain buckets unevenly and **demonstrate the empty-bucket problem.** Implement rebalancing.
7. Implement the **conditional claim** and run [Day 310](Day-310.md)'s harness — **assert exactly one
   winner per seat over 1,000 runs.**
8. ⭐ Set the hold TTL **below** the gateway timeout and **measure how often paid-after-expiry
   occurs** under load. Fix and re-measure.
9. ⭐ Implement **FIFO admission and lottery admission.** Simulate 100 bots with fast connections and
   **compare their success rate under each.**
10. ⭐ Assert **zero oversells** across the whole load test. That number is the deliverable.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Three minutes:** *"Tickets go on sale at nine. Ten million people are waiting. Walk me through
09:00:00."*

to one means almost all of this traffic cannot succeed, so it never reaches the purchase flow — it
gets a queue position from a counter and polls a CDN-cached endpoint. I admit five hundred a second
into a system sized for five hundred a second."*

endpoints require an admission token, and the seat map is a stale hint while only the conditional
update is truth.**
