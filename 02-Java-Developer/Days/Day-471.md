# Day 471

**L-471** · Project defence V — the full 45-minute deep dive

**Time:** 3+ hrs · **Mode:** DRILL · **⭐ Project defence drills, 5 of 5**

> **⭐⭐ The deep dive is not a quiz about your project. It is a simulation of what you will be like in a
> design review — and the interviewer is deciding whether they would want you in the room when something
> is on fire.**
>
> Today: the full 45-minute round run end to end, **the interviewer's real arc**, a fifteen-row rubric,
> and the four questions that most often end a deep dive badly.

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ The interviewer's arc

```
⭐ 0–5    ⭐⭐ "Tell me about the project."          ⇒ ⭐ your 3-minute version (Day 467)
⭐ 5–12   ⭐⭐ "Draw the architecture."              ⇒ ⭐ the 90-second diagram, narrated
⭐ 12–25  ⭐⭐ "Why did you...?" × 6–10              ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE DECISION LOG (Day 468)
⭐ 25–35  ⭐⭐ "What breaks? What if X dies? 10×?"   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE FAILURE INTERROGATION (Day 469)
⭐ 35–42  ⭐⭐ "Let's change something."             ⇒ ⭐ a new requirement, live — Part 3
⭐ 42–45  ⭐ your questions                          ⇒ ⭐ Day 465
```

| ⭐ What is being scored in each block | |
|---|---|
| ⭐ 0–12 | ⭐⭐ **communication** — can they follow you without asking |
| ⭐ 12–25 | ⭐⭐ **judgement** — did you choose, or default |
| ⭐ 25–35 | ⭐⭐ **operational maturity** — do you know where it breaks |
| ⭐⭐ **35–42** | ⭐⭐ **THE HIGHEST-VALUE BLOCK — can you think, live, about a system you did not prepare for** |

> **⭐ Everything before minute 35 is preparable, and interviewers know that.** The change request is
> where they find out whether you understand the system **or memorised a description of it** — which is
> why Part 3 gets its own section.

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The four questions that end deep dives badly

| ⭐ Question | ⭐⭐ Why people fail it | ⭐ The shape that works |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **"Walk me through what happens when a user clicks Save."** | ⭐⭐ they know the components but not the **path** | ⭐⭐ request → auth filter → controller → service → transaction → outbox row → commit → relay → consumer, **naming what happens if each step fails** |
| ⭐⭐ **"Where does the transaction begin and end?"** | ⭐⭐ vague answers reveal a vague model | ⭐ exactly which method is `@Transactional`, what is inside it, **and what is deliberately outside** |
| ⭐⭐ **"Show me the data model."** | ⭐ they describe tables, not **invariants** | ⭐⭐ the aggregate boundaries, the FKs, the unique constraints, **and which invariant each protects** |
| ⭐⭐ **"What's the thing you're least confident about?"** | ⭐⭐ "nothing" or a fake weakness | ⭐⭐ **a real one, with what you would do to close it** |

```
⭐⭐ THE "CLICK SAVE" QUESTION IS THE BEST ONE IN THE ROUND, and it is worth over-preparing:
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ it traverses ⭐ auth, ⭐ validation, ⭐ transactions, ⭐ persistence, ⭐ events, ⭐ caching,
⭐     ⭐ errors and ⭐ observability — ⭐⭐ IN ONE ANSWER
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ and at every step you can add "and if this fails, the user sees X and Y alerts".
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ Done well, it answers six of their remaining questions before they ask them.
```

⚠️ **The transaction-boundary question is the most reliable depth probe in Java interviews.** Someone who
can say *"the service method is transactional, the outbox insert is inside it, the HTTP call to the
payment provider is deliberately outside it because I will not hold a transaction across a network call,
and the email is triggered by the consumer"* **has demonstrably built the thing.** Vagueness here is very
hard to recover from.

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The live change request

```
⭐⭐ THEY WILL ADD A REQUIREMENT. Typical ones:
⭐   ⭐ "Now the same order can be edited by two people at once."      ⇒ Day 409, optimistic versioning
⭐   ⭐ "Now you need an audit trail of every change."                 ⇒ Day 405, event sourcing lite
⭐   ⭐⭐ "Now a tenant wants their data in the EU only."               ⇒ ⭐⭐ data residency — genuinely hard
⭐   ⭐ "Now there are 10,000 tenants instead of 12."                   ⇒ Day 329, partitioning
⭐   ⭐ "Now uploads can be 5 GB."                                      ⇒ Day 386, multipart + presigned
⭐   ⭐⭐ "Now this must be exactly-once with a payment provider."       ⇒ ⭐⭐ Day 407, idempotency keys
```

| ⭐⭐ The method — four moves, always the same | |
|---|---|
| ⭐ 1. **Restate and constrain** | ⭐⭐ "Just to be precise: EU-only for storage, or for processing too?" |
| ⭐ 2. **Say what breaks in the current design** | ⭐⭐ **name the specific component, not "it would be hard"** |
| ⭐ 3. **Give two options with the trade** | ⭐ cheap/limited vs expensive/complete |
| ⭐⭐ 4. **Recommend one, and say what you would measure** | ⭐⭐ **a recommendation, not a survey** |

> **⭐⭐ Move 4 is where most candidates stop one step short.** Listing two options and pausing invites
> the interviewer to choose, **which forfeits the judgement signal you were being offered.** Say which
> one you would do and why — **and be willing to be argued out of it, which is Day 465's disagree and
> commit in real time.**

# 🚪 The deep-dive rubric

**⭐ Run the full 45 minutes with a partner. Score honestly. Rows 3, 7, 11 and 15 are mandatory.**

| # | ⭐ Criterion | ⭐ 0 | ⭐ 1 | ⭐ 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3-minute version lands the problem, not the features | features | mixed | ⭐ problem-first |
| 2 | Architecture drawn from memory, arrows labelled | fumbled | drawn | ⭐ fluent + narrated |
| ⭐⭐ 3 | **Every "why" has force / rejected / cost** | benefits only | some | ⭐⭐ **all three, unprompted** |
| 4 | Defaults admitted as defaults | invented | some | ⭐ clean |
| 5 | "Click Save" traversal is complete | partial | complete | ⭐ complete + failure at each step |
| 6 | Transaction boundaries stated exactly | vague | roughly | ⭐ exact, with what is outside |
| ⭐⭐ 7 | **Bottleneck named with a MEASURED number** | opinion | reasoned | ⭐⭐ **measured, with the test** |
| 8 | "If X dies" answered for every component | some | most | ⭐ all, incl. the Redis follow-on |
| 9 | 10× answered as an ordered sequence | list | ordered | ⭐ ordered + expensive one named |
| 10 | Measured vs projected distinguished | blurred | mostly | ⭐ explicit |
| ⭐⭐ 11 | **Live change request handled in four moves** | froze | described | ⭐⭐ **recommended and justified** |
| 12 | Data model described as invariants | tables | mixed | ⭐ invariants + constraints |
| 13 | Known debt named accurately with a priority reason | denied | admitted | ⭐ named + fix + why not yet |
| 14 | Security questions survive two follow-ups each | one | most | ⭐ all |
| ⭐⭐ 15 | **A real "least confident about", with a plan** | "nothing" | vague | ⭐⭐ **specific + how to close it** |

```
⭐ 24–30 ⇒ ⭐⭐ ready. ⭐ 17–23 ⇒ one more pass over the weak rows.
⭐ under 17 ⇒ ⭐⭐ the gap is usually NOT knowledge — it is that you have not said these things ALOUD.
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
|---|---|
| ⭐ Opening with features | ⭐ the problem is what makes the rest legible |
| ⭐⭐ **Fumbling the architecture diagram** | ⭐⭐ the most visible failure in the round |
| ⭐ Benefits-only answers to "why" | ⭐⭐ Day 468 — no cost, no evaluation |
| ⭐⭐ **Vague transaction boundaries** | ⭐⭐ the most reliable depth probe, failed |
| ⭐ Describing tables instead of invariants | ⭐ a schema is not a data model |
| ⭐ Bottleneck as an opinion | ⭐⭐ measured beats correct-by-reasoning |
| ⭐ "It still works, just slower" | ⭐⭐ the incomplete Redis answer (Day 469) |
| ⭐⭐ **Freezing on the change request** | ⭐⭐ the block that cannot be prepared, and the one that matters |
| ⭐⭐ **Listing options without recommending** | ⭐⭐ forfeits the judgement signal |
| ⭐ Refusing to be argued out of the recommendation | ⭐ the opposite failure |
| ⭐ "Nothing, I'm confident in all of it" | ⭐⭐ reads as no self-assessment |
| Blurring measured and projected | the credibility move, skipped |

## Interview questions

**Q: Walk me through what happens when a user clicks Save.**
Request hits the ALB and terminates TLS at the edge; the auth filter validates the access token and puts
the tenant on the security context; the controller validates the DTO and rejects malformed input before
anything else runs. The service method opens the transaction — inside it are the entity write and the
outbox row, deliberately nothing else. Commit. A relay picks up the outbox row and publishes keyed by
aggregate id, so per-order ordering survives, and the consumer inserts a dedup key and does its work in
one transaction. If the commit fails the user gets a 4xx or 5xx and no event exists; if the process dies
after commit the outbox row is still there and the relay publishes it later. That last sentence is the
whole reason the outbox is there.

**Q: Where does the transaction begin and end?**
The service method is transactional. Inside it: the entity write, the outbox insert, and any other
database work that must be atomic with them. Deliberately outside it: the call to the payment provider,
because I will not hold a database transaction open across a network call — that ties up a connection for
the duration of somebody else's latency and turns their slowness into my pool exhaustion. The email is
not in there either; it happens in the consumer, triggered by the event.

**Q: A tenant now wants their data stored in the EU only. How do you handle that?**
First I would constrain it: EU-only for storage at rest, or for processing too, and does that include
backups and logs — because those three have very different costs. In the current design the break is
specific: one database and one bucket, with tenancy as a column rather than a boundary, so residency is
not expressible at all. Two options. Cheap and limited: a separate database and bucket in an EU region
with tenant routing at the application layer, which handles storage but leaves logs and metrics
flowing to one region. Complete and expensive: a full regional cell per residency zone, with its own
stack. I would do the first, because it satisfies the actual contractual requirement at a fraction of the
cost — and I would measure how many tenants ask for it, because if it becomes common the cell
architecture is the right end state.

**Q: What are you least confident about?**
The multi-tenant isolation at the database level. Tenancy is enforced in one place at the persistence
boundary and there is a test asserting that a query without a tenant context fails, but it is still one
schema with a tenant column — so a bug in that layer is a cross-tenant data leak rather than an error.
Row-level security in Postgres would make it a database guarantee rather than an application one, and
that is the change I would make first if the tenant list included anyone regulated. So: specific, and
with the fix named.

**Q: Why is the live change request the most important part of the round?**
Because everything before it is preparable and interviewers know that. The three-minute version, the
diagram and the decision log can all be rehearsed. Adding a requirement mid-conversation is how they find
out whether I understand the system or memorised a description of it. My method is always four moves:
restate and constrain, name the specific component that breaks, give two options with the trade, and then
recommend one and say what I would measure. The fourth move is where most people stop short — listing two
options and pausing hands the decision back, which forfeits exactly the signal they were offering.

## Mini task

1. ⭐⭐ Run the **full 45 minutes** with a partner using the arc in Part 1. Record it.
2. ⭐⭐ Score yourself against the **fifteen-row rubric.** Be harsh on rows 3, 7, 11 and 15.
3. ⭐⭐ Over-prepare the **"click Save"** traversal, including the failure behaviour at each step.
4. ⭐⭐ State your **transaction boundaries exactly**, including what is deliberately outside.
5. ⭐ Describe your data model as **invariants and constraints**, not tables.
6. ⭐⭐ Have your partner spring **three change requests** you have not seen. Use the four moves each time.
7. ⭐⭐ Practise **recommending** rather than surveying — then practise being argued out of it gracefully.
8. ⭐ Prepare a real **"least confident about"** with a concrete plan to close it.
9. ⭐⭐ Repeat the whole round for **NexOps** — retrieval, evaluation, injection, cost.
10. ⭐⭐ Run it a **second time with a different partner** and compare scores. The gap between them is
    usually your communication, not your knowledge.

3. Give the four questions that end deep dives badly.
4. Why is "click Save" the best question in the round?

6. Why is vagueness there so hard to recover from?

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Forty-five minutes, recorded, with a partner who has read Days 467–470.**

**⭐ Run the whole arc. Then watch it back with the rubric open and score it row by row. The two rows to
watch yourself on hardest are 7 and 11: did the bottleneck answer contain a number you measured, and did
you actually recommend something when the requirement changed.**

**⭐ The final check is the one that predicts the real round: at minute 35, when they added something you
had not thought about, did you go quiet — or did you say "let me restate that to be precise" and start
working? That reflex is the entire deliverable of these five days, and it is worth rehearsing until it is
automatic.**
