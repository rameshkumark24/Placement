# Day 295 🏗️

**P-295** · The ADR you never wrote — reconstructing the decisions

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** BUILD

> **⭐ The flagship recorded every decision before it was made. NexOps recorded none — because you
> were building, not documenting, and that is what everyone does.**
>
> **⭐ Today you reconstruct them.** Not to pretend they were written at the time, but because **an
> undocumented decision is indistinguishable from an accident** — and the whole value of this project
> is being able to say which is which.

---

# Part 1 — ⭐ Find the decisions

**⭐ A decision is anywhere the system could reasonably have been different.** Go looking:

```
⭐ 1. The technology choices     — ⭐ what did you NOT use?
⭐ 2. The architectural shape    — one service? a job? a queue?
⭐ 3. The data model             — ⭐ Day 289's diff is a list of decisions
⭐ 4. ⭐ The risk engine's design — determinism, explicability, rules-as-data
⭐ 5. ⭐ The RAG boundary         — ⭐ what the model may and may not do
⭐ 6. ⭐ The things you did NOT build — ⭐ often the most interesting
```

**⭐ Category 6 deserves the effort.** "We did not build multi-tenancy because there was one customer"
is a decision; **its absence in the documentation makes it look like an oversight**, and that is the
difference between "I scoped it" and "I forgot".

## ⭐ The three kinds of decision you will find

| Kind | ⭐ How to write it |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Deliberate** | you weighed options — ⭐ record them, including the rejected ones |
| ⭐ **Default** | ⭐ you took the tutorial's path — ⭐ **say so**, then evaluate it now |
| ⭐ **Accidental** | ⭐ it emerged — ⭐ **say so**, and decide whether to keep it |

> **⭐⭐ Being honest about the second and third categories is what makes the document credible.** An
> ADR set where every decision was carefully weighed reads as fiction, because **nobody weighs every
> decision.**

**⭐ And an accidental decision that turned out well is worth recording as such** — "this emerged
rather than being chosen, and on review it is correct because…" is a genuinely good line.

# Part 2 — ⭐ Write them, with the section that matters

**⭐ Day 101's format, with one addition for a retrospective ADR:**

```markdown
# ADR-NEXOPS-004: The risk engine stores frozen inputs with every assessment

## Status
⭐ Accepted — ⭐ **documented retrospectively on 2026-08-15; decision originally implicit**

## Context
⭐ What was true at the time: one customer, a three-week deadline, no audit requirement yet.
⭐ What is true now: assessments are cited in customer disputes.

## Decision
Every assessment stores its inputs, the ruleset version, and per-rule contributions.

## Consequences
⭐ Gained:  reproducibility, explicability, backtesting (Day 291).
⭐ Cost:    ⭐ storage grows with assessments; a retention policy is now required.
⭐ Gave up: ⭐ the ability to retroactively "fix" a score by changing a weight — deliberately.

## ⭐ Alternatives rejected
⭐ Recompute on read — ⭐ rejected: history would change when rules change.
⭐ Store only the score — ⭐ rejected: no assessment could be explained.

## ⭐ Revisit when
⭐ Assessment volume exceeds N/day, or storage cost exceeds £X — ⭐ then partition (Day 200).
```

**⭐ Three sections do the work, and two of them are usually missing from ADRs people write:**

| Section | ⭐ Why it matters |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **"Gave up"** | ⭐ a decision with no cost is not a decision |
| ⭐ **"Alternatives rejected"** | ⭐ this is where the reasoning lives |
| ⭐ **"Revisit when"** | ⭐⭐ **a trigger, so the decision is re-examined by event rather than by memory** |

**⭐ "Revisit when" is the one almost nobody writes and it is the most useful.** It converts a decision
from permanent to provisional, **and it is a very strong thing to be able to point at**: "I knew this
was right for the scale we had, and I wrote down the condition under which it stops being right."

## ⭐ The set you should end today with

```
⭐ 001  Why NexOps exists — the problem and the scope
⭐ 002  Technology choices — ⭐ and what was rejected
⭐ 003  The data model — ⭐ Day 289's diff, as decisions
⭐ 004  ⭐ Risk engine: determinism, frozen inputs, explicability
⭐ 005  ⭐ Risk engine: rules as data, versioned rulesets
⭐ 006  ⭐ RAG: chunking and retrieval strategy (Days 292–293)
⭐ 007  ⭐⭐ RAG: THE MODEL DOES NOT DECIDE — the trust boundary (Day 294)
⭐ 008  ⭐ What was deliberately not built
⭐ 009  ⭐ Known limits and accepted risks
```

**⭐ ADR-007 is the thesis of the whole system.** It is the one to write most carefully, because it is
the one that explains why a non-deterministic component sits next to a decision engine without
compromising it.

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The honest critique

**⭐ Write `docs/CRITIQUE.md`. This is harder than the ADRs and worth more.**

```markdown
# What I got wrong, and what I would do differently

## ⭐ Genuinely wrong — always was
| # | What | ⭐ Why it was wrong | ⭐ Impact | Fixed? |
|---|------|------------------|--------|--------|
| 1 | Scores overwritten in place | ⭐ no reproducibility, ever | ⭐ Critical | ✅ Day 289 |
| 2 | Re-embedding unchanged content | ⭐ pure waste | ⭐ £X/month | ✅ Day 292 |

## ⭐ Right at the time, wrong now
| # | What | ⭐ What changed |
|---|------|--------------|
| 1 | Hardcoded rule weights | ⭐ one customer → several; tuning is now routine |
| 2 | No multi-tenancy | ⭐ the customer count changed |

## ⭐ Still unsure
| # | What | ⭐ The tension |
|---|------|-------------|
| 1 | Rules as data vs code | ⭐ data is flexible; ⭐ code is type-checked and reviewable |

## ⭐ What I would do differently from the start
⭐ 1. Freeze assessment inputs from day one — ⭐ the retrofit was the hardest part of this rebuild.
⭐ 2. Build the evaluation harness before the RAG pipeline — ⭐ I tuned blind for months.
⭐ 3. Measure cost per query from the first week.
```

**⭐ The "still unsure" section is the most credible one in the document.** An engineer with no open
questions about their own system has not examined it. **Naming a genuine tension you have not
resolved — and explaining both sides — is a stronger signal than a confident answer.**

**⭐ And the "from the start" list is what interviewers actually ask for.** Item 2 in the example is
the kind of answer that lands: *"I tuned retrieval by intuition for months because I had no
measurement; building the harness first would have saved most of that."*

# Part 4 — ⭐ The comparison, and what the two projects prove together

**⭐ Write `docs/COMPARISON.md` — how the flagship differed, and what that taught you.**

| Aspect | ⭐ NexOps (as originally built) | ⭐ Flagship (Days 248–287) |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ Design phase | ⭐ none — straight to code | ⭐ five days before any code |
| ⭐ Decisions | ⭐ implicit | ⭐ recorded before implementation |
| ⭐ Testing | after the fact | ⭐ query counts and concurrency from day one |
| ⭐ Observability | added when something broke | ⭐ deployed with metrics on day one |
| ⭐ Failure modes | ⭐ discovered in production | ⭐ triggered deliberately (Day 286) |
| ⭐ The hard problem | ⭐ found late | ⭐ identified on Day 249, proven by test |

> **⭐⭐ The honest conclusion, and it is worth stating plainly: the flagship is better engineered
> because of what NexOps taught you. Say that.** It reframes the older project from a weakness into
> the reason the newer one is good.

## ⭐ What the pair proves

```
⭐ The flagship:  ⭐ you can build correctly, with recorded reasoning, and operate it
⭐ NexOps:        ⭐ you can inherit a system, understand it, critique it honestly,
                  ⭐ and improve it incrementally WITHOUT rewriting it
```

**⭐ The second is rarer and more valuable**, because almost all professional work is the second kind.
**A candidate who has only ever greenfielded has not demonstrated the harder skill.**

## ⭐ Update the README

```markdown
⭐ ## The rebuild
⭐ This system was built in 2025 under a deadline. In 2026 I audited it against
⭐ everything I had since learned, rebuilt the data model, the risk engine and the
⭐ RAG pipeline, and wrote the decisions down.

```

**⭐ That paragraph is one of the strongest things you can have in a portfolio README**, because it
demonstrates a quality no feature can: **that you re-examine your own work.**

## ⭐ Traps

| Trap | Consequence |
|---|---|
| ⭐ Writing ADRs as if decided at the time | ⭐ reads as fiction; loses credibility |
| ⭐ Every decision presented as deliberate | ⭐ nobody weighs everything — it is not believable |
| ⭐ No "gave up" section | ⭐ a decision with no cost is not a decision |
| ⭐ No "alternatives rejected" | the reasoning is invisible |
| ⭐ **No "revisit when"** | ⭐ decisions become permanent by default |
| ⭐ Omitting what you did not build | ⭐ scope decisions look like oversights |
| ⭐ A critique with no "still unsure" | ⭐ you have not examined it honestly |
| Critique that is only self-flagellation | ⭐ signals poor judgement, not humility |
| ⭐ Not connecting the two projects | you lose the strongest framing available |
