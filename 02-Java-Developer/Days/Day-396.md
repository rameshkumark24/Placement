# Day 396

**L-396** · ⭐⭐ Cost as an engineering constraint

**Time:** 3+ hrs · **Mode:** NEW · **⭐⭐ Flagship day of Stage 11**

> **⭐⭐ Your AWS bill is an architecture diagram drawn by an accountant. Every line is a design decision
> somebody made, and most of them were made by accident.**
>
> Today: the four things that actually cost money, **the data transfer table nobody learns until it
> costs them**, the ten highest-return fixes in order, **cost per request as a first-class SLI** — and
> the counterweight that stops this becoming dogma: **engineer time is more expensive than most
> infrastructure.**

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ Where the money actually goes

| ⭐ Category | ⭐ Typical share | ⭐ Where it hides |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Compute** | ⭐ 40–60% | ⭐ over-provisioning, idle dev, old generations, no commitment |
| ⭐⭐ **Data transfer** | ⭐⭐ **5–25%, and almost always a surprise** | ⭐⭐ NAT, cross-AZ, internet egress |
| ⭐ **Managed services** | ⭐ 20–40% | ⭐ RDS Multi-AZ, Aurora I/O, ElastiCache, MSK |
| ⭐⭐ **Observability** | ⭐⭐ **5–20% — routinely more than the app it watches** | ⭐⭐ log retention, custom metrics, Container Insights |
| Storage | 5–15% | ⭐ orphaned volumes, old snapshots, versioned buckets |

```
⭐⭐ THE UNCOMFORTABLE FACT: for many small teams, the observability bill exceeds
⭐ the bill for the infrastructure being observed. ⭐⭐ Day 394 was not a cost aside — it was the
⭐ second-largest line item, hiding behind a default that says "never expire".
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ Data transfer: the table to memorise

| ⭐ Path | ⭐⭐ Price |
|---|---|
| ⭐ Into AWS from the internet | ⭐⭐ **free** |
| ⭐⭐ **Out to the internet** | ⭐⭐ **~$0.09/GB** (first 100 GB/mo free) |
| ⭐⭐ **Between AZs, same region** | ⭐⭐ **~$0.01/GB — CHARGED IN BOTH DIRECTIONS** |
| ⭐ Within an AZ, private IPs | ⭐⭐ **free** |
| ⭐ Between regions | ~$0.02/GB |
| ⭐⭐ **NAT Gateway** | ⭐⭐ **$0.045/hr + $0.045/GB processed** |
| ⭐⭐ **S3/DynamoDB gateway endpoint** | ⭐⭐ **FREE** |
| ⭐ Interface endpoint | ~$0.01/hr/AZ + ~$0.01/GB |
| ⭐ CloudFront out | ⭐ ~$0.085/GB, **and origin fetch from S3 is free** (Day 392) |

```
⭐⭐ WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — the chatty service
⭐   Service A calls Service B 2,000×/s, ⭐ 5 KB each way, ⭐⭐ randomly across 3 AZs
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ ~2/3 of calls cross an AZ ⇒ ~13 GB/hour crossing ⇒ ~9.4 TB/month
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ ×$0.01 ×2 directions ≈ ⭐⭐ $190/month ⭐ to send data 400 metres.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Mitigations: fewer, larger calls · compression · topology-aware routing.
⭐   ⚠️ ⭐⭐ BUT: zonal affinity trades cost against availability. Do NOT pin to one AZ.

⭐⭐ WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — the image pull
⭐   400 MB image × 60 tasks × 20 deploys/day, ⭐ pulled THROUGH NAT
⭐   ⇒ 480 GB/day ⇒ ~14 TB/month ⇒ ⭐⭐ ~$650/month in NAT PROCESSING ALONE
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ FIX: S3 gateway endpoint (free) + ECR interface endpoints ⇒ ⭐⭐ ~$45/month.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ AND: a smaller image (Day 363) reduces it linearly. ⭐⭐ Layer caching is a cost lever.
```

> **⭐⭐ Cross-AZ charges apply in both directions and people count them once.** And note what is free:
> **traffic between resources in the same AZ over private IPs.** That asymmetry is why "spread across
> AZs" is an availability decision with a real price attached — **which is a trade to state, not a
> default to assume.**

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The ten highest-return fixes, in order

| # | ⭐ Fix | ⭐ Effort | ⭐ Typical saving |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ 1 | ⭐⭐ **Set retention on every CloudWatch log group** | ⭐⭐ **minutes** | ⭐⭐ often 10–30% of the whole bill |
| ⭐ 2 | ⭐⭐ **S3 gateway endpoint (+ ECR interface endpoints)** | ⭐⭐ **minutes** | ⭐⭐ most NAT processing |
| ⭐ 3 | ⭐⭐ **Abort-incomplete-multipart + noncurrent-version expiry** (Day 386) | minutes | ⭐ sometimes enormous |
| ⭐ 4 | ⭐ **Delete unattached EBS volumes and stale snapshots** | ⭐ an hour | ⭐ steady |
| ⭐ 5 | ⭐⭐ **Turn dev and staging OFF nights and weekends** | ⭐ a day | ⭐⭐ **~73% of those environments** |
| ⭐ 6 | ⭐ **gp2 → gp3** (Day 385) | ⭐ an hour | ⭐ ~20% of EBS |
| ⭐ 7 | ⭐⭐ **Right-size from real utilisation** (Compute Optimizer) | ⭐ days | ⭐⭐ 20–40% of compute |
| ⭐ 8 | ⭐⭐ **Graviton** (Day 385) | ⭐ days | ⭐ 20–40% of compute |
| ⭐ 9 | ⭐⭐ **Savings Plans on the measured floor** | ⭐ an afternoon | ⭐ 30–40% of committed spend |
| ⭐ 10 | ⭐ **Spot for CI, batch and queue workers** | ⭐ days | ⭐⭐ 70–90% of that slice |

```
⭐⭐ THE DEV ENVIRONMENT ARITHMETIC, because it is the one people never do:
⭐   24×7 = 168 hours/week.  ⭐ 9am–7pm weekdays = 50 hours/week.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ 50/168 = 30%. ⭐⭐ YOU ARE PAYING 3.4× WHAT YOU NEED for every non-prod environment.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ An EventBridge schedule stopping ASGs/RDS at 19:00 and starting at 08:30 is a day's work.
```

## ⭐ Commitment: buy the floor, never the peak

| ⭐ Model | ⭐ Flexibility | ⭐ Use for |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Compute Savings Plans** | ⭐⭐ **any region, any family, EC2 + Fargate + Lambda** | ⭐⭐ **the default commitment** |
| EC2 Instance Savings Plans | one family, one region | a very stable fleet |
| Reserved Instances | least flexible; can be sold | legacy, or RDS/ElastiCache |
| ⭐ On-Demand | full | ⭐ **everything above the committed floor** |

> **⭐⭐ Commit to your minimum, not your average.** Look at twelve months of hourly spend, find the level
> you never drop below, and commit to ~80% of that. **An over-commitment is a cost you pay whether you
> use it or not — the discount is real but so is the obligation**, and a 3-year commitment made during a
> growth spurt has ended more than one architecture discussion badly.

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Making cost an engineering property

```
⭐⭐ COST PER REQUEST as an SLI, alongside latency and error rate.
⭐   monthly bill ÷ monthly requests = ⭐ $/request
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ Now a design change can be evaluated: "3× cheaper at the same p99" is a MEASURABLE claim.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ And a REGRESSION is visible: cost per request rising while traffic is flat is a BUG.
```

| ⭐ Practice | |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Mandatory tags** (`env`, `service`, `owner`, `cost-center`) | ⭐⭐ **enforced by SCP/tag policy — without tags, optimisation is guesswork** |
| ⭐⭐ **Separate accounts per environment** | ⭐⭐ the cleanest possible cost attribution (Day 380) |
| ⭐ **Budgets + anomaly detection** | ⭐ alert on a *change*, not just a total |
| ⭐ **Cost Explorer + CUR in Athena** | ⭐ the per-resource truth |
| ⭐⭐ **Cost in the design review** | ⭐⭐ "what does this cost at 10× traffic?" as a standing question |
| ⭐ Infracost in CI | ⭐ the price diff appears **in the pull request**, next to the plan (Day 379) |

## ⭐⭐ The multiplication that gets people

```
⭐ A design that writes 3 extra CloudWatch metrics and 2 KB of logs per request:
⭐   at ⭐ 10 rps  ⇒ ⭐ trivial. Nobody notices. It ships.
⭐   at ⭐⭐ 10,000 rps ⇒ ⭐⭐ 52 TB of logs/month and a metrics bill with five figures.
⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE DECISION WAS FREE WHEN IT WAS MADE AND RUINOUS WHEN IT MATTERED.
⭐ ⇒ ⭐ Which is why the design-review question is "at 10× traffic", not "today".
```

## ⭐⭐ Serverless and managed-service inversions

| ⭐ Inversion | ⭐ Crossover |
|---|---|
| ⭐ Lambda vs container | ⭐⭐ sustained throughput (Day 391) |
| ⭐ Aurora standard vs I/O-Optimized | ⭐ when I/O > ~25% of Aurora spend (Day 387) |
| ⭐ Fargate vs EC2 | ⭐⭐ **packing efficiency** — 40%-packed EC2 loses (Day 390) |

| ⭐ API Gateway vs ALB | ⭐⭐ ~an order of magnitude at a billion requests (Day 392) |

# Part 5 — ⭐⭐ The counterweight, so this is judgement rather than dogma

```
⭐⭐ A senior engineer's fully loaded cost is roughly $60–120/HOUR.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ Two weeks optimising a $200/month bill costs ~$8,000 to save ~$2,400/YEAR.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ That is a LOSS, and presenting it as a win is a judgement failure.
```

| ⭐ Optimise when | ⭐ Do not optimise when |
|---|---|
| ⭐ The line is large, or growing faster than traffic | ⭐ the total is small and stable |
| ⭐⭐ **The fix is minutes** (fixes 1–4 above) | ⭐ the fix is a rewrite |
| ⭐ Cost per request is regressing | ⭐ absolute cost rises **because you have more customers** |
| ⭐ It also improves something else | ⭐ it trades away availability or safety |
| ⭐⭐ **Growth makes it a future cliff** | ⭐⭐ **it would delay revenue-generating work** |

> **⭐⭐ The mature position, and the one to say out loud: cost is a non-functional requirement with the
> same status as latency — measured, reviewed and owned — but not the one that outranks all the
> others.** **Availability, correctness and delivery speed routinely beat it, and the good engineer is
> the one who knows which situation they are in.**

⚠️ **And the specific trade to never get wrong: do not remove availability to save money without saying
so explicitly.** One NAT gateway instead of three, one AZ instead of three, no Multi-AZ on the database
— **each is a legitimate choice in dev and a decision that needs a name on it in production.**

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Log groups with no retention** | ⭐⭐ frequently the largest quiet line on the bill |
| ⭐⭐ **No S3 gateway endpoint** | ⭐⭐ every image pull and object read billed via NAT |
| ⭐ Counting cross-AZ transfer once | ⭐ it is charged **both ways** |
| ⭐⭐ **Non-prod running 24/7** | ⭐⭐ 3.4× what you need |
| ⭐ Unattached volumes, old snapshots, idle load balancers | ⭐ pure waste, invisible |
| ⭐⭐ **Committing to peak spend** | ⭐⭐ you pay for it whether used or not |
| ⭐ 3-year commitments during a growth spurt | inflexible for a long time |
| ⭐⭐ **No tags** | ⭐⭐ optimisation becomes guesswork |
| ⭐ Cost never discussed in design review | ⭐⭐ the free-at-10rps, ruinous-at-10k decision |
| ⭐ Tracking total cost only | ⭐⭐ cost **per request** is the engineering metric |
| ⭐ Assuming serverless is always cheaper | it inverts on sustained throughput |
| ⭐⭐ **Optimising a small bill for weeks** | ⭐⭐ engineer time costs more |
| ⭐⭐ **Quietly removing availability to save money** | ⭐⭐ a trade that must be named, not smuggled |

## Interview questions

**Q: A team's AWS bill doubled. How do you find out why?**
Cost Explorer grouped by service, then by usage type, comparing month over month — that usually names the
line in about two minutes. Then by tag or account to attribute it to a team, which only works if tagging
is enforced. My prior list is short: log ingestion and retention, NAT gateway data processing, cross-AZ
transfer from a newly chatty service, a versioned bucket with no expiry, and custom metrics with a
high-cardinality dimension. And I would separate two very different explanations — cost rising because
traffic rose is not a problem, cost per request rising while traffic is flat is a regression.

**Q: What are the highest-return quick wins?**
Retention on every CloudWatch log group, because the default is never expire and it is often ten to
thirty percent of the whole bill. An S3 gateway endpoint, which is free and removes image pulls and
object traffic from NAT data processing. Lifecycle rules for incomplete multipart uploads and noncurrent
object versions, which are billed and invisible. Deleting unattached volumes and stale snapshots. And
switching non-production environments off outside working hours, which is a seventy percent saving on
those environments because fifty hours out of a hundred and sixty-eight is thirty percent. All of those
are hours of work, not weeks.

**Q: How does data transfer pricing change your design?**
It makes chattiness across availability zones a real cost, because cross-AZ traffic is charged in both
directions while traffic within a zone over private IPs is free. Two thousand small calls a second
crossing zones is a couple of hundred dollars a month to move data a few hundred metres. So the levers
are fewer and larger calls, compression, and endpoints so service traffic never touches a NAT gateway.
The honest part is that zonal affinity trades cost against availability, so I would not pin traffic to a
single zone — I would reduce the volume rather than the spread.

**Q: How do you make cost a real engineering constraint rather than a finance conversation?**
Track cost per request as an SLI beside latency and error rate. That turns cost into something a design
change can be evaluated against — "three times cheaper at the same p99" becomes a measurable claim — and
it makes regressions visible, because cost per request rising while traffic is flat is a bug. Then
enforce tagging so attribution is possible, use separate accounts per environment so attribution is
trivial, put a price diff in the pull request alongside the Terraform plan, and make "what does this cost
at ten times the traffic" a standing design review question. That last one catches the decisions that are
free at ten requests a second and ruinous at ten thousand.

**Q: When should you not optimise cost?**
When the arithmetic does not work. A senior engineer costs sixty to a hundred and twenty dollars an hour
fully loaded, so spending two weeks to save two hundred dollars a month is an eight thousand dollar
investment for a twenty-four hundred dollar annual return — that is a loss, and calling it a win is a
judgement failure. I optimise when the line is large or growing faster than traffic, when the fix takes
minutes, or when growth makes it a future cliff. Cost is a non-functional requirement with the same
status as latency — owned and measured — but availability, correctness and delivery speed routinely
outrank it, and the specific thing I refuse to do is quietly trade away availability to save money
without naming the trade.

## Mini task

1. ⭐⭐ Open **Cost Explorer**, group by service then usage type, and **write down your top five lines.**
2. ⭐⭐ **Audit every log group** for retention. Set it. **Calculate the annual saving.**
3. ⭐⭐ Add the **S3 gateway endpoint** and measure NAT data processing before and after.
4. ⭐ Add lifecycle rules for **incomplete multipart uploads** and **noncurrent versions.** Measure.
5. ⭐ Find and delete every **unattached volume, stale snapshot and idle load balancer.**
6. ⭐⭐ Build an **EventBridge schedule** that stops non-prod at 19:00 and starts it at 08:30. Verify the
   saving next month.
7. ⭐ Run **Compute Optimizer** and act on its recommendations for one service.
8. ⭐⭐ Enforce **mandatory tags** with a tag policy, then produce a **cost-per-service report** from them.
9. ⭐⭐ Instrument **cost per request** for your flagship. Put it on the dashboard next to p99.
10. ⭐⭐ Add **Infracost to your pipeline** so every Terraform PR shows the price delta next to the plan.
11. ⭐⭐ **Model your bill at 10× traffic.** Find the line that grows fastest and say what you would change
    first.
12. ⭐ Write a one-page cost review of your own architecture, **including the optimisations you decided
    NOT to do and why.**

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes:** *"How do you think about cost?"*

**⭐ Open by refusing the false frame: cost is not a finance conversation, it is a non-functional
requirement with the same standing as latency — so I track cost per request beside p99, which makes a
design change evaluable and makes a regression visible.**

**⭐ Then be specific, because specificity is what proves you have done it:** log retention defaults to
never expire and is often the largest quiet line; an S3 gateway endpoint is free and removes image pulls
from NAT data processing; cross-AZ transfer is charged in both directions; non-prod running around the
clock costs 3.4× what it needs to. **All of those are hours of work.**

**⭐ Then close with the judgement, which is the part most people miss: an engineer costs a hundred
dollars an hour, so two weeks spent on a two-hundred-dollar-a-month bill is a loss. I optimise where the
line is large, growing faster than traffic, or about to become a cliff — and I never trade away
availability to save money without saying that is what I am doing.**
