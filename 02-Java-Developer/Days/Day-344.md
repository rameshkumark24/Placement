# Day 344 ⭐⭐

**L-344** · ⭐⭐ The system design interview framework

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> **⭐ A system design round is not a test of whether you know Kafka. It is a test of whether you can
> take three vague sentences, impose structure on them, make defensible choices out loud, and go
> deep on the one part that is genuinely hard.**
>
> Day 308 did this for LLD. **This is the same discipline at a different altitude — and the failure
> modes are almost identical.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ The clock

```
⭐  0–05  ⭐ REQUIREMENTS   functional + non-functional. ⭐ SAY THE SCOPE. Get agreement.
⭐  5–10  ⭐ ESTIMATION     QPS, storage, bandwidth (Day 323). ⭐ SAY WHAT EACH NUMBER RULES OUT.
⭐ 10–15  ⭐ API            3–5 endpoints. ⭐ This forces the data model.
⭐ 15–20  ⭐ DATA MODEL     entities, ⭐ THE ACCESS PATTERNS, the store choice (Day 332)
⭐ 20–30  ⭐ HIGH LEVEL     boxes and arrows — ⭐ and a reason for every box
⭐ 30–40  ⭐⭐ DEEP DIVE     ⭐⭐ ONE hard thing, properly. THE MOST IMPORTANT SECTION.
⭐ 40–45  ⭐ BOTTLENECKS    what breaks first, what you'd monitor, what you cut
```

| ⭐ Scored on | ⭐ Looks like |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Structure** | ⭐ you drive; they never have to ask "what next?" |
| ⭐⭐ **Trade-offs, out loud** | ⭐ **"X over Y because ___. I'd switch if ___."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Depth somewhere** | ⭐ one component understood to mechanism level |
| ⭐ **Numbers** | estimates that eliminate options |
| ⭐ Communication | you check in, you adapt to their signals |

**⭐ The five failure modes**, in order of frequency:

1. ⭐⭐ **Drawing boxes with no requirements.** A design with no scale, no SLO and no consistency
   requirement cannot be right or wrong — **and it cannot be defended.**
2. ⭐⭐ **Breadth with no depth.** Twelve components, none understood. **The interviewer cannot give a
   senior rating from a diagram.**
3. ⭐ **Buzzword architecture.** Kafka, Cassandra, a mesh and a Lambda for 500 requests a second.
4. ⭐ **Silence while thinking.** An unnarrated good design loses to a narrated adequate one.
5. ⭐ **Never mentioning failure.** Every box you draw can be down. Say what happens.

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The seven steps

## ⭐ 1. Requirements (5 min) — functional, then the ones that matter

```
⭐ FUNCTIONAL: "So: post a message, read a feed, follow a user. Not: DMs, search, media."
⭐ NON-FUNCTIONAL — ⭐ THESE DECIDE THE ARCHITECTURE:
⭐   scale        ⭐ DAU? read:write ratio? (Day 323)
⭐   latency      ⭐ what must be fast — and what may be slow?
⭐   availability ⭐ what may be down? ⭐ what may NEVER be down?
⭐   consistency  ⭐⭐ how stale, in seconds, before a user is harmed? (Day 331)
⭐   durability   ⭐ what may we lose? (usually: nothing)
```

> **⭐ The consistency question is the one that separates candidates**, because it is asked as a
> number rather than a preference. **"Feed can be 30 seconds stale; follower count can be minutes;
> a payment can be zero"** immediately determines caching, replication and store choice.

**⭐ Then say the scope sentence and get agreement** — Day 308's move, unchanged. It fixes the target
and makes the round collaborative.

## ⭐ 2. Estimation (5 min) — Day 323, applied

**⭐ Peak QPS, storage per year with multipliers, bandwidth, cache size** — and after each number,
**one sentence on what it eliminates.** *"Six thousand writes a second: that is one primary, so no
sharding on the write path yet."*

## ⭐ 3. API (5 min) — the step people skip, and it is free structure

```
⭐ POST   /posts                  { text }            → { postId }
⭐ GET    /feed?cursor=...&limit=20                   → { posts[], nextCursor }
⭐ POST   /users/{id}/follow
```

| ⭐ Why it earns its five minutes | |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **It forces the data model** | you cannot define `/feed` without deciding what a feed is |
| ⭐ **It surfaces pagination** | ⭐ cursor, not offset (Day 107) — a free correctness point |
| ⭐ It exposes idempotency needs | `POST /posts` retried — Day 335 |
| ⭐ **It makes the design concrete** | boxes become endpoints with owners |

## ⭐ 4. Data model (5 min) — access patterns first

**⭐ Entities, then the access patterns, then the store** (Day 332). **Name the primary key or
partition key out loud** — that is where the interviewer is listening. **And say which store and
why**, in one sentence each, including what you gave up.

## ⭐ 5. High-level design (10 min)

```
⭐ clients → CDN → gateway → services → cache → datastore
⭐                              ↓
⭐                            queue → workers
```

**⭐ Rule: every box needs a reason, said as you draw it.** *"A cache here because reads are a
hundred to one and this data tolerates thirty seconds of staleness."* **A box with no reason is a box
you will not be able to defend.**

## ⭐⭐ 6. The deep dive (10 min) — where the round is decided

> **⭐ Pick the part that is genuinely hard for THIS problem, and go to mechanism level.**

| Problem | ⭐ The hard part |
|---|---|
| News feed | ⭐ **fan-out on write vs read, and the celebrity** |
| Chat | ⭐ delivery guarantees, ordering, presence at scale |
| Ticket booking | ⭐ **seat holds under contention** (Day 316) |
| Rate limiter | ⭐ distributed counting (Day 336) |
| URL shortener | ⭐ **key generation and the hot-link cache** |
| Payments | ⭐⭐ **idempotency, the ledger, reconciliation** |

⚠️ **If the interviewer steers, follow them — that is the signal.** *"Tell me more about how you'd
handle the celebrity case"* is not a question, **it is the deep dive being assigned.**

## ⭐ 7. Bottlenecks and wrap (5 min)

```
⭐ "What breaks first: ___ at roughly ___ QPS."
⭐ "I'd monitor: ___ (Day 338's RED, plus one business metric)."
⭐ "What I cut: ___, and I'd add ___ first."
⭐ "At 10× this design changes here: ___."
```

# Part 3 — ⭐ The moves that consistently score

| ⭐ Move | ⭐ Why |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **A number before a component** | ⭐ "300k reads/s, so a cache" beats "we'll add Redis" |
| ⭐⭐ **Name the cost of your own choice** | ⭐ **the single strongest signal, unchanged since Stage 7** |
| ⭐ **"What happens when this is down?"** for one box, unprompted | Day 339 |
| ⭐ **Start simple, then scale it** | ⭐⭐ "one service, one database — now let me break it" is a better narrative than starting distributed |
| ⭐ Say the consistency requirement as seconds | Day 331 |
| ⭐ **Recognise a shape you know** | ⭐ "this is the limited-resource-held-across-a-human pattern" (Day 320A) |
| ⭐ Admit a gap cleanly | ⭐ **"I haven't run X in production; here's how I'd reason about it"** |

⚠️ **Starting distributed is a common self-inflicted wound.** Beginning with the simple version and
**evolving it under the pressure of your own estimates** demonstrates judgement; **beginning with
nine components demonstrates that you memorised a diagram.**

## ⭐ When you do not know

> **⭐ "I don't know that specifically. Here's how I'd reason about it: ___. And here's what I'd
> measure to decide."** That answer scores. **Bluffing does not, and interviewers detect it in one
> follow-up.**

# Part 4 — ⭐ The reusable checklist

**⭐ Almost every design in Days 345–359 needs an answer to these. Have them ready:**

```
⭐ READS      cache layer? (Day 326)  CDN? (Day 327)  read replicas? (Day 328)
⭐ WRITES     one leader enough? (Day 323's estimate)  shard key? (Day 329)
⭐ HOT KEYS   ⭐ the celebrity / popular item — local cache, splitting (Day 326)
⭐ CONSISTENCY ⭐ per operation, in seconds (Day 331)
⭐ ASYNC      what can move off the request path? (Day 333)
⭐ IDEMPOTENCY ⭐ every retry-able write (Day 335)
⭐ FAILURE    timeouts, breakers, ⭐ the degradation matrix (Day 339)
⭐ BACKPRESSURE ⭐ bounded queues, shedding (Day 336)
⭐ OBSERVABILITY ⭐ RED + one business metric (Day 338)
⭐ SECURITY   authN at the edge, authZ per service (Day 341)
⭐ COST       ⭐ egress and storage dominate for media (Day 323)
```

> **⭐ Running this list mentally in the last five minutes catches the thing you forgot** — and
> mentioning two or three of them unprompted is what makes a design feel complete rather than
> abandoned when the clock ran out.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ Designing before requirements | ⭐⭐ **the design cannot be right or wrong** |
| ⭐ No numbers | every choice is a preference |
| ⭐⭐ Breadth without depth | ⭐⭐ **no senior signal available from a diagram** |
| ⭐ Starting distributed | you memorised a picture |
| ⭐ Buzzword components | Kafka for 500 rps |
| ⭐ Skipping the API step | you lose the free structure it gives |
| ⭐ Not asking the consistency requirement | ⭐ the most architecture-changing question |
| ⭐ Ignoring the interviewer's steer | ⭐ **that steer was the deep dive being assigned** |
| ⭐ Never mentioning failure | every box can be down |
| Silence | an unnarrated design cannot be assessed |
| ⭐ Bluffing | one follow-up exposes it |
| Not closing with bottlenecks | it sounds like you ran out of time |

## Interview questions

**Q: How do you approach a system design question?**
Five minutes on requirements, functional and non-functional, ending with a scope sentence I get
agreement on. Five on estimation, saying what each number eliminates. Five defining three to five API
endpoints, because that forces the data model. Five on the model and store choice. Ten on the
high-level design with a reason for every box. Then ten on one deep dive — the part that is genuinely
hard for this problem — and five on bottlenecks, monitoring and what I cut.

**Q: Which part matters most?**
The deep dive. Breadth is easy to fake and a diagram gives an interviewer nothing to rate; ten
minutes at mechanism level on the hard part is where a senior signal comes from. The corollary is
that I pick it deliberately — and if the interviewer steers me somewhere, that is not a question,
that is the deep dive being assigned.

**Q: What is the most important question you ask?**
How stale can this be, in seconds, before a user is harmed — asked per operation. It decides caching,
replication, store choice and whether an operation can be asynchronous, and it converts consistency
from a preference into a requirement. Read-to-write ratio is a close second, for the same reason.

**Q: How do you avoid over-engineering?**
By starting simple and letting my own estimates force each addition. One service and one database,
then: three hundred thousand reads a second, so a cache; ninety terabytes a year, so sharding; media
egress, so a CDN. Every component arrives with the number that demanded it. Starting with nine boxes
means I cannot justify any of them individually.

**Q: What do you do when you do not know something?**
Say so, then reason from principles and name what I would measure to decide. Bluffing fails on the
first follow-up and costs more than the gap did. "I have not run that in production, but here is how
I would reason about it" is an acceptable answer; a confident wrong mechanism is not.

## Mini task

1. **Write the seven-step clock from memory.** Then run it end to end on *"design a pastebin"*,
   timed, out loud, recorded.
2. ⭐ **Play the recording back**: how long in each phase, how many seconds of silence, and how many
   times you said "because".
3. ⭐ For three different problems, **write the non-functional requirements list** — with the
   consistency requirement as a number in seconds.
4. Take one design and **write the API first.** Note how much of the data model it decided for you.
5. ⭐ For five classic problems, **name the deep dive in one line each.** If you cannot, you do not
   yet know what is hard about them.
6. ⭐ **Practise the simple-then-scale narrative**: draw the one-server version, then add each
   component with the number that forced it.
7. Take a design you have done and **run the Part 4 checklist.** Count what you missed.
8. ⭐ **Have someone give you a problem you have not prepared** and run the full 45 minutes. Ask them
   to steer you into a deep dive at minute 30.

# 🚪 Exit questions

1. Give the seven phases with their minutes.
2. Give the five things being scored.
3. Give the five failure modes in order of frequency.
4. Give the five non-functional requirements and the one that decides most.
5. Why is the API step worth five minutes?
6. What must every box have?
7. Why is the deep dive the most important section, and how do you choose it?
8. What does an interviewer's steer mean?
9. Give four things in the closing five minutes.
10. Why start simple rather than distributed?
11. What do you say when you do not know something?
12. Give the eleven items on the reusable checklist.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Ten minutes, recorded:** a full run on a problem you have not prepared — *"design a food delivery
tracking system"* — following the clock strictly.

**⭐ Three markers.** You spent the first five minutes on requirements and said a scope sentence.
**Every component arrived attached to a number.** And you spent a full ten minutes on one hard thing
rather than a shallow pass over nine.

**⭐ If you finished early, you went too broad.** A 45-minute design round should feel like you ran
out of time on the deep dive, not like you covered everything.
