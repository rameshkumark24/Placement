# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The defence, rehearsed until it is boring

## ⭐ The two walkthroughs

**⭐ Record both. Play both back.**

```
⭐ FLAGSHIP (10 min)
⭐  problem → architecture and WHY IT IS SMALL → ⭐ the hard problem WITH THE TEST
⭐  → auth → the outbox → ⭐ load-test numbers → ⭐ chaos findings → what you left out

⭐ NEXOPS (10 min)
⭐  what it does → ⭐ what the audit found → ⭐ what you rebuilt and why
⭐  → ⭐ the risk engine: determinism and explicability
⭐  → ⭐ RAG: ⭐ THE MODEL DOES NOT DECIDE → ⭐ what you deliberately left alone
```

**⭐ Five checks on each recording:**

| Check | ⭐ Fail signal |
|---|---|
| ⭐ Opened with the **problem** | ⭐ "Spring Boot, PostgreSQL, Redis…" |
| ⭐ **Showed a test**, did not describe one | you talked about the race |
| ⭐ **Cited numbers** | "it's pretty fast" |
| ⭐ Named a trade-off **unprompted** | only benefits |
| ⭐ Said what you **did not build** | it sounded finished and unconsidered |

## ⭐ The forty questions, cold

**⭐ Everything from Days 262, 270, 287 — plus these, which are the ones that separate candidates:**

```
⭐ 1.  What is your throughput, and what saturated first?
⭐ 2.  Show me a performance improvement with numbers.
⭐ 3.  How long does a rollback take? A restore?
⭐ 4.  Which alerts have you seen fire?
⭐ 5.  What did you break on purpose, and how fast did you know?
⭐ 6.  What is your availability target, and what did it rule out?
⭐ 7.  What does it cost to run?
⭐ 8.  What breaks first at 100× traffic?
⭐ 9.  ⭐ Why is a risk score an event rather than a column?
⭐ 10. ⭐ Can you reproduce a six-month-old assessment exactly?
⭐ 11. ⭐ Why is this score 0.82?
⭐ 12. ⭐ How do you change a rule weight and know you have not made things worse?
⭐ 13. ⭐ Why isn't vector search enough on its own?
⭐ 14. ⭐ If an answer is wrong, was it retrieval or generation?
⭐ 15. ⭐⭐ Someone hides instructions in an uploaded PDF. What happens?
⭐ 16. ⭐ What can your RAG system get wrong that you have not fixed?
⭐ 17. ⭐ What did you get wrong originally, and what was right at the time?
⭐ 18. ⭐ Why didn't you rewrite NexOps?
⭐ 19. ⭐ What are you still unsure about?
⭐ 20. ⭐ Which project are you prouder of, and why?
```

**⭐ Question 20 is the one to think about carefully.** The strongest answer is usually NexOps — **not
because it is better engineered, but because rebuilding something you already wrote requires
judgement that greenfielding does not.** Saying that demonstrates exactly the self-awareness the
question is probing.

**⭐ Questions 15 and 16 are the AI questions that matter**, and almost nobody answers them well:
**"the model cannot change a score because the architecture does not let it, and here is the test"**
followed by **an honest list of remaining limits** is a far stronger answer than a claim of safety.

# Part 3 — ⭐ Package them

## ⭐ The README both projects need

```markdown
⭐ # Name — one paragraph: what, who for, why not trivial
⭐ ## The hard problem      ← ⭐ SECOND. The race, the fix, ⭐ A LINK TO THE TEST
⭐ ## Architecture          ← one diagram, ⭐ and why it is this size
⭐ ## Performance           ← ⭐ the before/after table
⭐ ## Running it            ← ⭐ under five minutes, tested from a fresh clone
⭐ ## Decisions             ← ⭐ links to the ADRs
⭐ ## What I left out       ← KNOWN-GAPS, FUTURE
```

**⭐ The hard-problem section goes second, immediately after the pitch**, because it is the reason the
project is interesting — **and a link to the two-thread test is the single strongest element on the
page.**

## ⭐ The artefacts that differentiate

| Artefact | ⭐ Why almost nobody has it |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **A postmortem** | ⭐ requires having operated the system |
| ⭐ **An honest critique of your own work** | requires re-examining it |
| ⭐ **`KNOWN-GAPS.md`** | ⭐ requires owning the gaps rather than hoping |
| ⭐ **Before/after performance numbers** | requires having measured |
| ⭐ **ADRs with "revisit when"** | ⭐ requires treating decisions as provisional |
| ⭐ **An injection test on an AI feature** | ⭐ requires understanding the threat model |

**⭐ Any three of these puts you ahead of most portfolios.** They are also the things that generate
follow-up questions you can answer well, which is the actual purpose.

## ⭐ Two-minute versions

**⭐ Write and rehearse a two-minute version of each project** — for the moment someone says "tell me
about a project you've worked on" and you have no whiteboard.

```
⭐ Problem (20s) → ⭐ the hard part and how you solved it (60s)
⭐ → ⭐ one number (20s) → ⭐ one thing you'd do differently (20s)
```

# Part 4 — 🚪🚪 ⭐ The gate

## ⭐ You may not proceed until

- [ ] ⭐ **All 15 audit rows marked for BOTH projects**, with evidence or a gaps entry
- [ ] ⭐ **Both walkthroughs recorded**, ⭐ passing all five checks
- [ ] ⭐ **All 40 questions answered cold**, ⭐ gaps fixed rather than noted
- [ ] ⭐ Both READMEs complete; ⭐ **both fresh-clone tested under five minutes**
- [ ] ⭐ **At least four differentiating artefacts exist across the two**
- [ ] ⭐ **Both two-minute versions rehearsed**
- [ ] ⭐ **A human has reviewed at least one project and you acted on their feedback**
- [ ] ⭐ Both deployed, monitored, and ⭐ **currently running**

## ⭐ What Stage 7 proved

```
⭐ The flagship:  ⭐ you can design before building, record why, build correctly,
                  ⭐ and OPERATE it — with numbers rather than claims.

⭐ NexOps:        ⭐ you can inherit a system, understand it from the code,
                  ⭐ critique your own past work honestly, and improve it
                  ⭐ INCREMENTALLY — without rewriting it.
```

> **⭐⭐ The second is the rarer skill and the one most professional work requires.** Almost every
> candidate can show something they built; **far fewer can show something they inherited, judged, and
> made better while it kept running.**

## ⭐ And the thing that ties both together

> **⭐⭐ Every claim in both projects has evidence behind it.** The concurrency guard has a test. The
> performance improvement has two numbers. The alert has fired. The restore has been timed. The
> injection defence has a test that demonstrates it.
>
> **⭐⭐ That is what "defensible" means, and it is the only thing that survives a follow-up
> question.**

---

## ⭐ Traps

| Trap | Consequence |
|---|---|
| ⭐ Treating the gate as a formality | ⭐ the questions arrive in the interview instead |
| ⭐ Noting a gap instead of fixing it | ⭐ the gap is what they will ask about |
| ⭐ Not recording the walkthroughs | you never hear how you actually sound |
| ⭐ Opening with the tech stack | you sound like a CV |
| ⭐ Describing rather than showing | a claim, not evidence |
| ⭐ No numbers | every operational answer is a guess |
| ⭐ Only having a greenfield project | ⭐ the harder skill is undemonstrated |
| ⭐ Claiming the AI feature is safe | ⭐ state the limits — it reads as competence |

## ✅ Done when

- ⭐ Every gate box is ticked
- ⭐ **Both recordings are ones you would send to an interviewer**
- ⭐ **You can answer all forty questions with evidence**
