> **⭐ Eight sentences. If those are automatic, an unfamiliar problem has nowhere to hide** — because

# Part 2 — 🚪🚪 ⭐ The gate: three live interviews

| ⭐ Requirement | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Three separate sessions, different days** | ⭐ one good round is luck |
| ⭐⭐ **A real human, and problems you have NOT prepared** | ⭐ **the whole point is transfer** |
| ⭐ 45 minutes, timed, **recorded** | the recording is the deliverable |
| ⭐ **At least one interviewer more senior than you** | ⭐ they must be able to push |
| Different problem *shapes* across the three | one read-heavy, one write-heavy, one correctness-critical |

**⭐ Give them this sheet:**

```
⭐ Pick one I have NOT prepared. Do not tell me in advance. Suggestions:
⭐   a hotel booking system · a food-delivery marketplace · an online exam platform ·
⭐   a fleet-tracking system · a coupon/promotions service · a document e-signature service ·
⭐   an IoT sensor platform · a multiplayer game leaderboard · an ad-serving system ·
⭐   a warehouse/inventory system · a subscription billing platform

⭐ During the round, please:
⭐  1. Be vague where a real stakeholder would be. Make me ask.
⭐  2. ⭐ At ~minute 20, add a requirement I did not plan for.
⭐  3. ⭐ At ~minute 30, pick a component and say "go deeper here."
⭐  4. ⭐ Push back ONCE on a decision, even if you agree with it.
⭐  5. ⭐ Ask "what breaks first?" and "what would you do at 10×?"
⭐  6. Score the rubric below and give me my two lowest rows.
```

## ⭐ The rubric — they score, not you

| # | Criterion | ⭐ Pass |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ 1 | **Requirements before design** | ⭐ functional + NFRs, **consistency asked as a number** |
| ⭐ 2 | **Scope stated and agreed** | explicit, confirmed |
| ⭐ 3 | **Estimation** | ⭐⭐ **and every number ELIMINATED something** |
| ⭐ 4 | API defined | 3–5 endpoints; forced the data model |
| ⭐ 5 | **Data model with access patterns** | ⭐ partition/shard key named and justified |
| ⭐ 6 | **Every box has a reason** | said as it was drawn |
| ⭐ 7 | ⭐⭐ **Deep dive at mechanism level** | ⭐⭐ **ten minutes, one component, genuinely hard** |
| ⭐ 8 | **Trade-offs narrated** | "X over Y because ___; I'd switch if ___" |
| ⭐ 9 | **Failure discussed unprompted** | what is down, what degrades |
| ⭐ 10 | **Concurrency / consistency handled** | the race named, the guard chosen |
| ⭐ 11 | **Extension absorbed** | the minute-20 curveball did not force a redesign |
| ⭐ 12 | **Bottlenecks and 10×** | named, with numbers |
| ⭐ 13 | **What you cut** | deliberate, stated |
| ⭐ 14 | Handled push-back | named the cost, then decided |
| ⭐ 15 | **Finished on time** | ⭐ closed at 45 with a summary |

**⭐ Pass = 13 of 15 in ALL THREE rounds, with rows 3, 7, 9 and 15 mandatory in each.**

> **⭐ Rows 3, 7, 9 and 15 are mandatory because each maps to a failure that ends real interviews:**
> designing without numbers, breadth without depth, ignoring failure, and running out of time.

## ⭐ Play each recording back

| Check | ⭐ Fail signal |
|---|---|
| ⭐ Minutes before the first box was drawn | ⭐ **under 5 — you designed before you understood** |
| ⭐ Longest silence | over 20 seconds |
| ⭐ **Time in the deep dive** | ⭐⭐ **under 8 minutes** |
| ⭐ Count of "because" | ⭐ under 10 in 45 minutes means you asserted rather than reasoned |
| ⭐ **Who spoke more** | ⭐ you should be ~70% |
| Numbers cited | ⭐ under 5 is not an engineering conversation |

# Part 4 — 🚪🚪 ⭐ The gate, and what "COMPLETE SDE" means

## ⭐ You may not proceed until

- [ ] ⭐ **Three live 45-minute rounds, different days, unprepared problems, all recorded**
- [ ] ⭐ **13/15 in all three**, with rows 3, 7, 9 and 15 passed in each
- [ ] ⭐ **All three recordings played back** with the six checks run
- [ ] ⭐ **All 50 questions answered cold** — gaps re-read, not noted
- [ ] ⭐ **The pattern card written from memory**, both halves
- [ ] ⭐ **One design done in a plain text editor**, no diagram (remote rounds)
- [ ] ⭐ **A written one-pager per design** — the hard part in one sentence each

## ⭐ What Stages 8 and 9 proved together

```
⭐ Stage 8: given a problem, you produce a coherent MODEL in one process,
⭐          with rules where they cannot be bypassed and the race found before you are asked.
⭐
⭐ Stage 9: given a problem, you produce a coherent SYSTEM across machines,
⭐          with every component justified by a number, every failure mode named,
⭐          ⭐ and every claim carrying its own cost.
```

> **⭐⭐ And the thing that ties them: you generalise.** The twelfth LLD problem was a limited resource
> held across a human. **The sixteenth system design will be some combination of a read-heavy path
> that wants a cache, a write path that wants a partition key, a limited resource that wants a
> conditional claim, and a number that must be reproducible later.**

## ⭐ What it does not mean

⚠️ **"Complete SDE" means interview-ready across the full stack of a backend engineer's craft.** It
does **not** mean you have operated any of this at scale — **and claiming otherwise is the fastest way
to lose a round.** The honest framing you have practised since Stage 7 still applies:

> **⭐ "I have not run this at a million requests a second. Here is how I would reason about it, and
> here is what I would measure to find out whether I was right."**

**⭐ That sentence has been the marker in every stage, and it is the one to carry into the interviews
themselves.**

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
|---|---|
| ⭐ Self-assessing instead of a human | ⭐ **you cannot hear your own silences** |
| ⭐ Choosing prepared problems | ⭐⭐ **the gate tests transfer, not recall** |
| One round instead of three | luck is indistinguishable from skill at n=1 |
| ⭐ Not recording | the playback is where the findings are |
| ⭐ Memorising fifteen designs | ⭐⭐ **the sixteenth exposes it in five minutes** |
| ⭐ Noting a rubric gap instead of fixing it | it arrives in the real interview |
| Skipping the text-editor run | remote rounds have no whiteboard |
| ⭐ **Overclaiming operational experience** | ⭐ one follow-up ends it |

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes, no notes:** *"How do you approach a system you've never designed before?"*

**⭐ The answer that closes Stage 9: the clock, then the patterns.** *"Five minutes on requirements
with consistency asked as a number. Five on estimation, saying what each number rules out. Then I
look for the shapes: is there a read path that wants a cache, a write path that needs a partition
key, a limited resource held across a human that wants a conditional claim and an expiring hold, and
anything a user is charged or judged by that must be reproducible from frozen inputs. Most systems
are a combination of those."*

**⭐ That is not a script — it is what fifteen designs taught you**, and it is why the sixteenth is
tractable.
