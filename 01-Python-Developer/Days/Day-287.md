# Day 287 🚪

**P-287** · 🚪 **The production readiness gate** — ⭐⭐ the flagship is finished

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** BUILD · **Gate**

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: "finished" does not mean complete, and it does not mean perfect. It
> means defensible — every gap is a decision you can name, and every number is one you measured
> yourself.**
>
> ⭐ Forty days ago (Day 248) you wrote twelve requirements and an out-of-scope list. ⭐⭐ Today you
> check the thing you built against the thing you promised, attack it for forty-five minutes, and
> then stop — because the hardest engineering judgement in a portfolio project is knowing when to.

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ What "finished" means here

```
⭐⭐ THREE DEFINITIONS. ONLY THE THIRD IS ACHIEVABLE:
⭐   ⭐ ❌ COMPLETE — every feature you imagined. ⭐ Nothing is
⭐     ever complete; a scope you keep extending is a project
⭐     you never finish, and an unfinished project defends
⭐     nothing.
⭐   ⭐ ❌ PERFECT — no known gaps. ⭐⭐ A SYSTEM WITH NO KNOWN
⭐     GAPS HAS NOT BEEN LOOKED AT PROPERLY. It is a claim about
⭐     your attention, not about the code.
⭐   ⭐ ✅ ⭐⭐ DEFENSIBLE — someone hostile can look at any part
⭐     of it and you can say WHY, WHAT IT COSTS, AND WHAT WOULD
⭐     CHANGE YOUR MIND.

⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ WHICH MEANS `known-gaps.md` IS NOT AN APOLOGY —
⭐   ***IT IS THE PRIMARY EVIDENCE THAT THE PROJECT IS
⭐      FINISHED.*** ⭐ A candidate who lists twelve things they
⭐   deliberately did not do, with reasons, is demonstrating
⭐   exactly the judgement the job requires.

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ Four things you must be able to show live

```
⭐⭐ IN UNDER FIVE MINUTES, ON A SHARED SCREEN, WITHOUT
⭐   PREPARATION. ⭐ These four are chosen because each one is
⭐   almost impossible to fake:

⭐ ① ⭐ A DEPLOY — push, watch the gates, watch it go out, curl
⭐      `/version` and see the new sha.
⭐ ② ⭐⭐ A ROLLBACK — promote the previous digest and show
⭐      `/version` change back. ⭐ Time it out loud.
⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ AN ALERT FIRING — stop the relay (or the scheduler) and
⭐      let the dead-man check page you WHILE THEY WATCH.
⭐      ⇒ ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE SINGLE MOST CONVINCING THING IN THE
⭐        ENTIRE PROJECT, because it proves the observability is
⭐        real rather than configured.
⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ THE INVARIANT HOLDING — fire 50 concurrent bookings at
⭐      one slot and show exactly one succeeding, then show the
⭐      `EXCLUDE` constraint that makes it true (Day 250).

⭐ ⇒ ⭐ If any of the four needs "let me just fix something
⭐   first", ⭐⭐ THAT IS TODAY'S WORK, AND IT MATTERS MORE THAN
⭐   ANY REMAINING FEATURE.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Forty-five minutes of attack

> ⭐ Answer aloud, timed, out of order. ⭐⭐ An answer that takes more than 90 seconds is one you
> have not consolidated — mark it and rewrite it, because in an interview it will come out as
> rambling rather than as depth.

```
⭐⭐ THE DESIGN (5)
⭐   ① Why this problem? What made it worth building?
⭐   ② Which requirement did you cut, and why that one?
⭐   ③ Show me an aggregate boundary and justify it.
⭐   ④ Which invariant could not live in an aggregate, and what
⭐     enforces it instead?
⭐   ⑤ Which ADR would you now reverse?

⭐⭐ THE BACKEND (6)
⭐   ⑥ Where is the transaction boundary and why there?
⭐   ⑦ Show me a request schema and its response schema. Why two?
⭐   ⑧ What happens when a refresh token is reused?
⭐   ⑨ Where does authorisation live for a list endpoint?
⭐   ⑩ How do you paginate, and what breaks without a tiebreaker?
⭐   ⑪ What does your test suite NOT cover?

⭐⭐ THE ASYNC HALF (6)
⭐   ⑫ Draw the path from "payment succeeded" to "customer
⭐     notified". Where can it break?
⭐   ⑬ What happens on a provider timeout?
⭐   ⑭ How does a job survive being run twice?
⭐   ⑮ What is in your cache key, and why each part?
⭐   ⑯ A worker is killed mid-task. What happens?
⭐   ⑰ How would you know within fifteen minutes that the relay
⭐     stopped?

⭐⭐ PRODUCTION (7)
⭐   ⑱ Walk me through a deploy, then a rollback.
⭐   ⑲ How do you rename a column with zero downtime?
⭐   ⑳ Your database fails over. What do users see?
⭐   ㉑ What is your p99 and at what throughput?
⭐   ㉒ What is the first thing that breaks under load?
⭐   ㉓ What does it cost, and where does the money go?
⭐   ㉔ ⭐⭐ WHAT WOULD YOU DO DIFFERENTLY, AND WHAT WOULD YOU
⭐     DO THE SAME? ⭐ Prepare this one properly — it is the
⭐     question that most often decides the interview, and
⭐     "nothing" is the worst possible answer.
```

```

⭐   SHUT IT DOWN?

⭐     STAY green — ⭐⭐ A LINK TO A DEAD DEMO IS WORSE THAN NO
⭐     LINK, and a project with a broken CI badge on the day

⭐     ⭐⭐ AND SAY SO IN THE README. "Torn down after the load

## Interview questions

**Q: Is the project finished?**

> Yes, and I'd define what I mean, because "complete" and "perfect" aren't achievable. It's
> defensible: someone can look at any part of it and I can say why it's that way, what it costs, and
> what would change my mind. The evidence for that is `known-gaps.md` — twelve things I deliberately
> didn't do, each with a blast radius and a trigger that would change the decision. A system with no
> known gaps hasn't been examined properly; that's a claim about attention, not about code.

**Q: Show me something that proves the observability is real.**

> I'd stop the outbox relay and let the dead-man alert page me while you watch. That one takes about
> two minutes and it's very hard to fake, because it requires the metric to exist, the alert to be
> wired, the thresholds to be sane, and the runbook to say something useful. The other three I keep
> ready are a deploy, a rollback by promoting the previous digest with `/version` changing back, and
> firing fifty concurrent bookings at one slot to show exactly one succeeding — then showing the
> `EXCLUDE` constraint that makes it true rather than the application code that hopes it is.

**Q: What would you do differently?**

> Three things. I'd write the reconciliation job at the same time as the outbox rather than after,
> because I discovered late that reconciliation is only possible if you attached your own reference
> when you made the charge. I'd measure before caching rather than caching and then measuring — one of
> my caches turned out to be saving two milliseconds. And I'd have built the load test earlier, because
> the first bottleneck was the connection pool and that changed decisions I'd already made. What I'd
> keep: deploying a walking skeleton on day one, writing the spec before the code, and putting the
> invariants in the database rather than in the service layer.

**Q: What does this project prove that a tutorial project doesn't?**

> That I've operated it. I can tell you the throughput at which it degrades and the mechanism, the
> measured restore time for a backup, the monthly cost broken down by line item, how long a rollback
> takes because I've rehearsed it, and the count of real bugs my own gates caught before I did. Those
> aren't things you can get from building something and then screenshotting it — each one requires
> having run it, broken it, and looked at the bill.

**Q: Why did you stop where you did?**

> Because the marginal feature was worth less than the second project. The flagship demonstrates a
> transactional system: concurrency, money, tenancy, async work, operations. Adding a tenth endpoint
> demonstrates none of that a second time. The next project is deliberately a different shape — rules
> and retrieval, where the hard parts are evaluation and failure modes rather than transactions —
> because two projects that prove the same thing prove one thing.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Four minutes: the full project pitch.**

Thirty seconds on the problem, in domain language, with why it was worth building. Sixty seconds on
architecture, and choose the parts that carry risk rather than the ones that are easy to draw: the
transaction boundary, the outbox, the worker tier, the single scheduler. Ninety seconds on the three
hard problems — **the double booking, prevented by an `EXCLUDE` constraint rather than by application
code that hopes; the money path, where a provider timeout is neither success nor failure and gets its
own state plus a reconciliation job; and tenancy, where the same predicate serves the list and the
detail query so they cannot diverge.** Thirty seconds of numbers, because almost nobody has them:
**capacity with the mechanism, the measured restore time, the rehearsed rollback duration, and the
monthly cost itemised.** Then thirty seconds of gaps, stated confidently rather than apologetically —
name three, with what each would cost and what would make you fix it. Close on the question that
decides interviews: **what you'd do differently**, with three specific answers and, just as
importantly, three things you'd do exactly the same. Then stop talking. **The project is defensible,
which is what finished means — and a project you keep extending is a project you never defend.**
