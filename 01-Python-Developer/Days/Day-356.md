# Day 356 🎫

**P-356** · 🎫 **Design 12 — ticket booking and the flash sale** — ⭐⭐ the virtual waiting room

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** BUILD

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: a flash sale is the one workload where the peak is a thousand times the
> average and arrives with no ramp at all, so you cannot scale into it — the design is to convert an
> unbounded arrival spike into a bounded, admitted stream at the very edge, and to make the inventory
> decrement provably atomic for the few requests you let through.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐ 0–8: scope and the number that makes it hard

```
⭐⭐ FUNCTIONAL:
⭐   ① Browse events and seat availability.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ HOLD SEATS, THEN PAY, THEN CONFIRM.
⭐   ③ ⭐ Release expired holds.
⭐   ④ ⭐⭐ SURVIVE A FLASH SALE.
⭐   ⭐⭐ OUT: payment processing (Day 357), seat maps rendering,
⭐     dynamic pricing, resale.

⭐⭐ NON-FUNCTIONAL:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ NEVER OVERSELL. ⭐ Zero tolerance — it is a legal and
⭐     reputational failure, not a metric.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ SLIGHT UNDERSELL IS ACCEPTABLE (an abandoned hold
⭐     that expires late costs one unsold seat). ⭐ Stating that
⭐     asymmetry early is what licenses every later trade.
⭐   ⭐ Normal traffic: 1,000 rps.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ FLASH SALE: 1,000,000 PEOPLE HIT "BUY" WITHIN 10
⭐     SECONDS FOR 50,000 TICKETS.

⭐⭐ ⇒ THE THREE FACTS THAT FOLLOW:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ 100,000 RPS AGAINST A 1,000 RPS SYSTEM — ***A 100×
⭐      SPIKE WITH ZERO RAMP.*** ⭐ Autoscaling reacts in
⭐      minutes; the event lasts seconds.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ 95% OF THOSE USERS ***CANNOT*** GET A TICKET — there
⭐      are only 50,000. ⭐ So the system's main job is to
⭐      disappoint 950,000 people ***quickly, fairly and
⭐      cheaply***.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ AND THE CONTENTION: 50,000 TICKETS MEANS THE
⭐      INVENTORY ROW(S) ARE THE HOTTEST DATA IN THE COMPANY
⭐      FOR TEN SECONDS.
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The virtual waiting room

```
⭐⭐ THE KEY REALISATION: ***YOU CANNOT SCALE TO 100,000 RPS
⭐   FOR TEN SECONDS. SO DO NOT TRY — ADMIT PEOPLE AT A RATE
⭐   YOUR SYSTEM CAN ACTUALLY SERVE.***

⭐⭐ THE MECHANISM:
⭐   ① ⭐ A user arriving at sale time is redirected to a
⭐      ***static*** waiting page served entirely from the CDN
⭐      — ⭐⭐ ZERO ORIGIN LOAD FOR THE 950,000 WHO ARE WAITING.
⭐   ② ⭐ They are issued a signed queue token with a position.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ THE ADMISSION SERVICE RELEASES N USERS PER SECOND
⭐      INTO THE REAL APPLICATION — where N is your ***measured***
⭐      capacity (Day 322), not a hope.
⭐   ④ ⭐ Admitted users get a short-lived token; ⭐ the app
⭐      rejects anyone without one.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐ The waiting page polls for position ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND THE POLL
⭐      MUST BE CDN-CACHEABLE OR SERVED BY A TINY DEDICATED
⭐      SERVICE, OR ***THE QUEUE BECOMES THE LOAD YOU WERE
⭐      AVOIDING***. ⭐ This is the trap inside the fix.

⭐⭐ WHY IT WORKS — ⭐ three properties:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE SPIKE IS ABSORBED AT THE EDGE, IN A COMPONENT
⭐     THAT DOES ALMOST NOTHING.
⭐   ⭐ Your real system runs at its designed capacity the whole
⭐     time, ⭐ so latency never degrades and nothing cascades.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AND IT IS ***FAIRER***: a queue position is honest;
⭐     a free-for-all rewards whoever has the fastest connection
⭐     and the most aggressive retry script.

⭐⭐ THE FAIRNESS DETAILS THAT MATTER:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ RANDOMISE POSITION AMONG EVERYONE PRESENT ***AT
⭐     OPENING***, rather than pure first-come. ⭐ Otherwise the
⭐     queue rewards clock precision and bots that connect
⭐     milliseconds early, ⭐⭐ AND THE ENTIRE QUEUE FILLS IN THE
⭐     FIRST 200 MILLISECONDS.
⭐   ⭐ Bind the token to a session/account; ⭐⭐ RATE-LIMIT
⭐     ***QUEUE ENTRY*** PER ACCOUNT, OR ONE PERSON HOLDS
⭐     FIFTY POSITIONS.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ SHOW AN HONEST ESTIMATED WAIT. ⭐ "Position 340,000"
⭐     with no context is worse than "about 25 minutes" — and
⭐     an honest "tickets will likely be gone before your turn"
⭐     is better than both, because it lets people leave.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ Inventory: never oversell

```
⭐⭐ TWO SHAPES OF INVENTORY, AND THEY NEED DIFFERENT ANSWERS:

⭐⭐ ① ***RESERVED SEATING*** (seat 14F) — ⭐ Day 316's design,
⭐   exactly:
⭐   ⭐ Three states: `free` / `held` / `sold`, ⭐⭐ AND A HOLD IS
⭐     ***DATA WITH AN EXPIRY, NOT A LOCK*** — so it survives a
⭐     restart and works across replicas.
⭐   ⭐ One conditional `UPDATE … WHERE (status='free' OR
⭐     (status='held' AND hold_expires_at < now())) RETURNING`
⭐     ⇒ ⭐⭐ LAZY EXPIRY, SO CORRECTNESS NEVER DEPENDS ON THE
⭐     SWEEPER RUNNING.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Contention is naturally spread: 50,000 seats is 50,000
⭐     rows. ⭐⭐ THE HOT-ROW PROBLEM IS MILD HERE.

⭐⭐ ② ***GENERAL ADMISSION*** (50,000 identical tickets) —
⭐   ⭐⭐ THE HARD CASE, BECAUSE IT IS ***ONE ROW*** AND ONE ROW
⭐   IS ONE LOCK.
⭐   ⭐ ❌ `UPDATE events SET remaining = remaining - 1 WHERE
⭐     id = X AND remaining > 0` ⇒ ⭐ correct, ⭐⭐ AND EVERY
⭐     TRANSACTION SERIALISES ON THAT ROW. ⭐ At ~1 ms per
⭐     update that is ***1,000 tickets/second maximum***, and
⭐     everything else waits.
⭐   ⇒ FIXES:
⭐      ⭐ (a) ⭐⭐ ***BUCKET THE INVENTORY***: 100 rows of 500
⭐        tickets each. ⭐ A buyer hashes to a bucket and
⭐        decrements ***that*** row ⇒ ⭐⭐ 100× THE THROUGHPUT.
⭐        ⭐ On empty, try another bucket; ⭐ sum for display.
⭐        ⭐⭐ THE COST: THE LAST FEW TICKETS ARE AWKWARD
⭐        (bucket A has 3, bucket B has 0), ⭐ so the final
⭐        stretch falls back to a single consolidated row.
⭐      ⭐ (b) ⭐⭐ ***DECREMENT IN REDIS*** with an atomic
⭐        `DECR`, and persist asynchronously.
⭐        ⭐ ✔ 100k+ ops/s. ⭐ ✘ ⭐⭐ REDIS IS NOW THE SYSTEM OF
⭐        RECORD FOR INVENTORY DURING THE SALE — ⭐ so it needs
⭐        AOF persistence, and reconciliation against the
⭐        database afterwards. ⭐⭐ SAY THAT OUT LOUD; IT IS THE
⭐        REAL COST OF THE FAST ANSWER.
⭐      ⭐ (c) ⭐ Pre-generate 50,000 ticket rows and hand them
⭐        out with `SKIP LOCKED` (Day 261) ⇒ ⭐⭐ NO CONTENTION
⭐        AT ALL, BECAUSE EVERY BUYER TAKES A ***DIFFERENT***
⭐        ROW. ⭐ Elegant, and it makes the seat/GA cases the
⭐        same code.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ The three-transaction shape

```
⭐⭐ THE MISTAKE: one transaction spanning hold → payment →
⭐   confirm.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE PAYMENT CALL TAKES 3–10 SECONDS. ⭐ With a pool
⭐     of 100 connections that is ***10–30 CHECKOUTS PER
⭐     SECOND***, and the site is down before the show sells
⭐     out (Day 316's arithmetic).

⭐⭐ THE CORRECT SHAPE:
⭐   ⭐ ***TX 1*** — hold the seats. Milliseconds. Commit.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ***NO TRANSACTION*** — call the payment provider,
⭐     with an idempotency key derived from the booking id
⭐     (Day 335).
⭐   ⭐ ***TX 2*** — mark sold, record payment, emit the ticket.
⭐     Milliseconds. Commit.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE HOLD IS WHAT MAKES THE GAP SAFE. ⭐ That is the
⭐     entire reason holds exist, and it is worth saying in
⭐     exactly those words.

⭐⭐ AND THE THIRD OUTCOME (Day 317): ⭐⭐ THE PAYMENT CALL TIMES
⭐   OUT. ⭐ You do not know whether the customer was charged.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Move to `payment_unknown`, ⭐⭐ EXTEND THE HOLD (do not
⭐     release the seat — releasing a seat you may have charged
⭐     for is the worst of both outcomes), ⭐ reconcile against
⭐     the provider by ***your own*** reference, ⭐ and let the
⭐     webhook resolve it.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND THE POLICY QUESTION TO NAME: IF RECONCILIATION
⭐     TAKES FIVE MINUTES AND THE SHOW SELLS OUT MEANWHILE, WHO
⭐     GETS THE SEAT? ⭐ The answer is the paying customer, which
⭐     means the extended hold must outlast reconciliation.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐⭐ The read path, and why the seat map is a lie

```
⭐⭐ DURING A SALE, ***READS*** DWARF WRITES: everyone is
⭐   refreshing the seat map.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ A LIVE SEAT MAP AT 100,000 RPS IS 100,000 QUERIES/S
⭐     AGAINST THE ROWS EVERY WRITE IS ALSO CONTENDING FOR. ⭐ It
⭐     is the worst possible read pattern.

⭐⭐ THE ANSWER — ⭐ and it is a product decision stated honestly:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ CACHE THE SEAT MAP WITH A 1–5 SECOND TTL AND ACCEPT
⭐     THAT IT IS STALE.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE UI MUST SAY SO: "AVAILABILITY UPDATES EVERY FEW
⭐     SECONDS." ⭐ And the failure path must be gentle —
⭐     ⭐⭐ "THAT SEAT JUST WENT; HERE ARE THREE NEARBY" IS A
⭐     DESIGNED STATE, NOT AN ERROR.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Because ⭐⭐ THE ***HOLD*** IS AUTHORITATIVE AND THE
⭐     ***MAP*** IS ADVISORY. ⭐ You cannot make the map correct;
⭐     you can make the hold correct, and that is sufficient.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THIS IS DAY 331'S RULE IN ITS PUREST FORM: SHRINK THE
⭐     LINEARIZABLE PART UNTIL IT IS ONE ROW AND ONE STATEMENT,
⭐     AND LET EVERYTHING ELSE BE STALE.

⭐⭐ AND THE STATIC-FIRST PRINCIPLE:
⭐   ⭐ Event details, artwork, venue layout, terms ⇒ ⭐⭐ FULLY
⭐     STATIC, CDN-CACHED, IMMUTABLE URLS. ⭐ Only ***seat
⭐     status*** is dynamic.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE PAGE THAT A MILLION PEOPLE LOAD MUST BE 99%
⭐     EDGE-SERVED, WITH ONE SMALL DYNAMIC FETCH.
```

# Part 6 — ⭐ Bots, fairness and abuse

```
⭐⭐ THE UNCOMFORTABLE TRUTH: ⭐⭐ A LARGE FRACTION OF FLASH-SALE
⭐   TRAFFIC IS AUTOMATED, AND SCALPERS ARE BETTER RESOURCED
⭐   THAN MOST OF YOUR USERS.

⭐⭐ THE CONTROLS, CHEAPEST FIRST:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ REQUIRE A LOGGED-IN ACCOUNT CREATED BEFORE THE SALE
⭐     WAS ANNOUNCED. ⭐ The single most effective control, and
⭐     it is a policy, not code.
⭐   ⭐ Per-account purchase limits enforced ***at hold time***,
⭐     ⭐⭐ NOT AT CHECKOUT — by checkout the seats are already
⭐     removed from everyone else.
⭐   ⭐ Payment-instrument and address limits (⭐ catches one
⭐     person with fifty accounts).
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ QUEUE-ENTRY RATE LIMITS PER ACCOUNT AND PER IP,
⭐     ⭐ with the Day 324 caveat that an office or a mobile
⭐     carrier is one IP.
⭐   ⭐ Device fingerprinting and CAPTCHA ⭐⭐ AT QUEUE ENTRY,
⭐     NOT AT CHECKOUT — ⭐ challenge the cheap step, never the
⭐     step that already holds inventory.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE STRUCTURAL ONE: RANDOMISED QUEUE POSITION
⭐     (Part 2) REMOVES MOST OF THE ADVANTAGE OF BEING FAST,
⭐     WHICH IS THE ADVANTAGE BOTS ACTUALLY HAVE.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE HONEST STATEMENT: ⭐⭐ YOU WILL NOT ELIMINATE
⭐   BOTS. ⭐ You raise the cost until it exceeds the resale
⭐   margin, and you make the ***queue*** the thing they must
⭐   defeat rather than your inventory rows.
```

# Part 7 — ⭐ Bottlenecks and the summary

```
⭐⭐ WHAT BREAKS, IN ORDER:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ WITHOUT A WAITING ROOM: EVERYTHING, IN THE FIRST
⭐      SECOND. ⭐ Connection pools, then the database, then
⭐      health checks eject healthy pods (Day 339's cascade).
⭐   ② ⭐ With one: the admission service and the queue-position
⭐      poll ⇒ ⭐⭐ MAKE THE POLL CACHEABLE, OR YOU MOVED THE
⭐      PROBLEM RATHER THAN SOLVING IT.
⭐   ③ ⭐ The inventory row for GA ⇒ bucketing or `SKIP LOCKED`.
⭐   ④ ⭐ Payment provider throughput ⇒ ⭐⭐ THEY HAVE RATE
⭐      LIMITS TOO, AND YOUR ADMISSION RATE SHOULD RESPECT
⭐      ***THEIRS***, NOT JUST YOURS.

⭐⭐ AND THE OPERATIONAL PRACTICE THAT MAKES IT REAL:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ PRE-SCALE BEFORE A KNOWN SALE. ⭐ The date is known
⭐     in advance — ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE RARE SPIKE YOU CAN PREPARE
⭐     FOR, AND NOT PREPARING IS A CHOICE.
⭐   ⭐ Warm caches, pre-generate ticket rows, ⭐ freeze deploys.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ LOAD TEST AT THE REAL SHAPE: INSTANT 100× ARRIVAL,
⭐     NOT A RAMP. ⭐ A ramped load test proves nothing about
⭐     this workload.

⭐⭐ THE SUMMARY:
⭐   ***"The peak is a hundred times the average with no ramp,
⭐      so I don't scale into it — a static CDN-served waiting
⭐      room absorbs the spike and admits users at my measured
⭐      capacity, which also happens to be fairer than a
⭐      free-for-all that rewards the fastest script. Position
⭐      is randomised among everyone present at opening, or the
⭐      queue fills in two hundred milliseconds. Inventory is a
⭐      hold that's data with an expiry rather than a lock, with
⭐      lazy expiry so correctness never depends on a sweeper,
⭐      and general admission is bucketed or pre-generated
⭐      because one row is one lock. Payment sits outside the
⭐      transaction with the hold making the gap safe, and a
⭐      timeout means payment-unknown with an extended hold —
⭐      never a released seat. And the seat map is advisory: I
⭐      can't make it correct, I can make the hold correct, and
⭐      that's enough."***
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ Trying to autoscale into the spike | It reacts in minutes; the event lasts seconds. Absorb at the edge instead |
| ⭐⭐ A waiting-room poll that hits the origin | You moved the load rather than removing it |
| ⭐⭐ Pure first-come queue ordering | Rewards clock precision and bots; the queue fills in 200 ms |
| ⭐ No per-account queue-entry limit | One person holds fifty positions |
| ⭐⭐ Single-row GA inventory decrement | Every transaction serialises; ~1,000 tickets/s ceiling |
| ⭐⭐ Payment inside the transaction | 3–10 s holding a connection means 10–30 checkouts/s |
| ⭐⭐ Releasing the seat on a payment timeout | You may have charged them. Extend the hold instead |
| ⭐ A live, uncached seat map | 100,000 queries/s against exactly the rows writes contend for |
| ⭐ Treating "seat taken" as an error | It's a designed state: "that seat just went, here are three nearby" |
| ⭐⭐ Purchase limits enforced at checkout | By then the seats are already held and removed from everyone else |
| ⭐ CAPTCHA at checkout rather than queue entry | Challenge the cheap step, never the one already holding inventory |
| ⭐⭐ Redis as inventory truth without saying so | It needs AOF and post-sale reconciliation. Say the cost of the fast answer |
| ⭐ Ramped load tests | Proves nothing about a workload defined by having no ramp |

## Interview questions

**1. "A million people hit buy in ten seconds. What do you do?"**
> ⭐⭐ Don't try to scale into it — **a hundred-times spike with no ramp can't be autoscaled**, since
> scaling takes minutes and the event lasts seconds. ⭐ A static CDN-served waiting page absorbs the
> arrival with zero origin load, and an admission service releases users at **my measured capacity**.
> ⭐⭐ Two details: **the position poll must be cacheable or I've moved the load rather than removed
> it**, and **position is randomised among everyone present at opening**, because pure first-come fills
> the queue in two hundred milliseconds and rewards bots.

**2. "How do you guarantee no overselling?"**
> ⭐ For reserved seating, Day-316 style: three states, and **a hold is data with an expiry, not a lock**
> — so it survives a restart and works across replicas — with expiry evaluated lazily in the `WHERE`
> clause, ⭐⭐ **so correctness never depends on the sweeper running.** ⭐ For general admission the row
> is the problem: a single decrement serialises everything at roughly a thousand a second, so I'd
> bucket the inventory into a hundred rows, or ⭐⭐ **pre-generate the ticket rows and hand them out
> with `SKIP LOCKED`, which removes contention entirely because every buyer takes a different row.**

**3. "Where does payment go?"**
> ⭐⭐ Outside the transaction, in three parts: a short transaction to hold, the payment call with no
> transaction open, and a short transaction to confirm. ⭐ Inside one transaction, a three-to-ten-second
> provider call with a hundred connections means ten to thirty checkouts a second and the site is down
> before the show sells out. ⭐⭐ **The hold is what makes the gap safe — that's the entire reason holds
> exist.** ⭐ And on timeout: `payment_unknown` with an **extended** hold, never a release, because
> releasing a seat you may have charged for is the worst outcome available.

**4. "Why is the seat map allowed to be wrong?"**
> ⭐ Because during a sale reads dwarf writes, and a live map at a hundred thousand requests a second
> means a hundred thousand queries against exactly the rows every write is contending for. ⭐⭐ So it's
> cached with a one-to-five second TTL and the UI says so — **the hold is authoritative and the map is
> advisory.** ⭐ I can't make the map correct; I can make the hold correct, and that's sufficient.
> ⭐⭐ It's the general rule in its purest form: **shrink the linearizable part to one row and one
> statement and let everything else be stale.**

**5. "How do you handle bots?"**
> ⭐ Layered, and cheapest first: an account created before the sale was announced, which is the most
> effective control and is a policy rather than code; per-account purchase limits enforced ⭐⭐ **at hold
> time, not checkout, because by checkout the seats are already taken from everyone else**; payment
> instrument and address limits; queue-entry rate limits; and CAPTCHA at **queue entry, not checkout**
> — challenge the cheap step. ⭐⭐ And the structural one: **randomised queue position removes the
> advantage of being fast, which is the advantage bots actually have.** ⭐ You don't eliminate them; you
> raise the cost above the resale margin.

**6. "How do you test this?"**
> ⭐⭐ At the real shape: **an instant hundred-times arrival, not a ramp** — a ramped load test proves
> nothing about a workload whose defining property is having no ramp. ⭐ Plus a game day: pre-scale,
> warm caches, pre-generate ticket rows, freeze deploys, and rehearse the sale end to end. ⭐⭐ This is
> the rare spike whose date you know in advance, **so not preparing for it is a choice rather than an
> accident.**

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Lead with the shape of the problem: **a hundred-times spike with no ramp, so you cannot
scale into it** — autoscaling reacts in minutes and the event lasts seconds. Then state what the system
is actually for: **ninety-five percent of arrivals cannot get a ticket, so the job is to disappoint
nine hundred and fifty thousand people quickly, fairly and cheaply.** The waiting room does that: a
static CDN-served page absorbs the spike at zero origin cost, admission runs at your measured capacity,
**and the position poll must itself be cacheable or you've moved the load rather than removed it.** Add
the fairness detail: **randomise position among everyone present at opening, because pure first-come
fills the queue in two hundred milliseconds and rewards whoever has the best script.** On inventory:
**a hold is data with an expiry, not a lock**, evaluated lazily so correctness never depends on the
sweeper, and general admission is bucketed or pre-generated because **one row is one lock and that caps
you at a thousand a second.** Payment sits outside the transaction — **the hold is what makes the gap
safe** — and a timeout means payment-unknown with an extended hold, never a released seat. Close on the
read path, which is the elegant part: **the hold is authoritative and the seat map is advisory. I can't
make the map correct; I can make the hold correct, and that is enough.**
