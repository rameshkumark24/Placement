# Day 468 📓

**P-468** · 📓 **The decision log** — ⭐⭐ force, alternative, price

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: "why did you choose X?" fills most of a deep dive, and the answer that
> scores is never "X is good" — it is the force that made the choice necessary, the alternative you
> rejected, and the price you are still paying, because a decision with no price attached is a
> preference wearing a decision's clothes.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ The three-part answer

```
⭐ ***THE QUESTION:*** ⭐ "Why Postgres and not a vector
⭐   database?"

⭐ ❌ ***THE PREFERENCE ANSWER:*** ⭐ "Postgres is reliable and I
⭐   know it well." ⭐⭐ SCORES NOTHING. ⭐ It is a statement about
⭐   you, not about the problem.

⭐ ❌ ***THE FEATURE ANSWER:*** ⭐ "pgvector supports HNSW and
⭐   metadata filtering." ⭐⭐ TRUE AND IRRELEVANT — ⭐ so do all
⭐   the alternatives. ⭐⭐ A FEATURE LIST DOES NOT EXPLAIN A
⭐     CHOICE BETWEEN THINGS THAT ALL HAVE THE FEATURE.

⭐ ✅ ***THE THREE-PART ANSWER:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***THE FORCE*** — what about ***this*** problem made
⭐     the choice necessary?
⭐     ⭐ "The chunk row and the document row had to commit
⭐       together. ⭐⭐ IF THEY DON'T, I CAN HAVE AN INDEXED CHUNK
⭐       FOR A DOCUMENT THAT FAILED TO SAVE, AND THE INDEX IS
⭐       DERIVED STATE SO NOTHING WOULD EVER RECONCILE IT."
⭐   ⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***THE ALTERNATIVE, STEELMANNED*** — ⭐ and named as
⭐     genuinely better on some axis.
⭐     ⭐ "Qdrant has genuinely better filtered ANN, and filtered
⭐       search was on our critical path."
⭐   ⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***THE PRICE YOU ARE STILL PAYING*** — ⭐ the part
⭐     almost nobody volunteers, and the part that makes the
⭐     whole answer credible.
⭐     ⭐ "We pay for it in two ways: the HNSW index shares RAM
⭐       with our OLTP working set, so a big reindex can evict
⭐       the application's buffer cache; ⭐⭐ AND OUR FILTERED
⭐       SEARCH IS MEASURABLY WORSE THAN A PURPOSE-BUILT ENGINE'S.
⭐       ⭐ I know the four conditions that would make me switch."

⭐ ⭐⭐ ⇒ ***A DECISION WITH NO PRICE IS A PREFERENCE.*** ⭐ Every
⭐   real engineering choice costs something, and a candidate who
⭐   cannot name what theirs cost either did not make the
⭐   decision or has not run the system long enough to feel it.
```

# Part 2 — Building the log

```
⭐ ***ONE PAGE. TEN TO FIFTEEN ROWS. THE HIGHEST-RETURN DOCUMENT
⭐   IN YOUR INTERVIEW PREPARATION.***

⭐ ***FOR EACH DECISION, FIVE FIELDS:***
⭐   ⭐ ***DECISION*** — one line.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ***FORCE*** — the property of ***this*** problem that
⭐     made it necessary. ⭐ Not a general virtue.
⭐   ⭐ ***REJECTED*** — the strongest alternative, stated
⭐     ***fairly***.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ***PRICE*** — what it costs you, still, today.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ***REVISIT WHEN*** — ⭐ the condition that would change
⭐     your mind. ⭐ This field is what separates a considered
⭐     decision from a defended one.

⭐ ***A ROW, FILLED IN:***
⭐  ┌────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────┐
⭐  │ DECISION   │ Chunk on document structure, ~600 tokens,  │
⭐  │            │ heading path prepended before embedding    │
⭐  │ ⭐⭐ FORCE   │ A chunk is both the retrieval unit and the │
⭐  │            │ generation unit, and those want opposite   │
⭐  │            │ sizes. Our docs have real headings, so the │
⭐  │            │ author already did the semantic split.     │
⭐  │ REJECTED   │ Semantic chunking — one embedding call per │
⭐  │            │ sentence, a threshold to tune per corpus,  │
⭐  │            │ ⭐ and it competes with segmentation a      │
⭐  │            │ human did deliberately.                    │
⭐  │ ⭐⭐ PRICE   │ Documents without headings (transcripts,   │
⭐  │            │ OCR) chunk badly and we know it. ~4% of    │
⭐  │            │ the corpus is served worse.                │
⭐  │ REVISIT    │ If unstructured content exceeds ~20% of    │
⭐  │            │ the corpus, or recall on that slice drops  │
⭐  │            │ below 0.7.                                 │
⭐  └────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────┘

⭐ ⭐⭐ NOTICE WHAT THE "REVISIT" FIELD DOES IN A ROOM: ⭐ it
⭐   converts "I chose this" into ***"I chose this, under
⭐   conditions I can state, and I would notice if they
⭐   changed."*** ⭐⭐ THAT IS THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN A DECISION
⭐   AND AN OPINION, AND INTERVIEWERS HEAR IT IMMEDIATELY.
```

# Part 4 — The five follow-ups every decision attracts

```
⭐ ⭐⭐ REHEARSE THESE FIVE AGAINST YOUR TOP FIVE DECISIONS. ⭐ That
⭐   is twenty-five answers and it covers most of a deep dive.

⭐ ① ⭐⭐ "WHAT WOULD YOU DO DIFFERENTLY?"
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ***process, not outcome.*** ⭐ "Build the eval set
⭐     first" beats "pick a different chunk size."

⭐ ② ⭐⭐ "WHAT IF THE SCALE WERE 100×?"
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ NAME WHICH DECISION BREAKS ***FIRST***, ⭐ not a new
⭐     architecture. ⭐ "At 30 million vectors the HNSW index
⭐     stops sharing RAM comfortably with the OLTP working set —
⭐     that's the first thing to go, and it shows up as *the app
⭐     got slow*, not as a search problem."

⭐ ③ ***"WHY NOT [POPULAR THING]?"***
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ NEVER DISMISS IT. ⭐ Name what it is genuinely good
⭐     at, then the mismatch with your force. ⭐ Dismissing a
⭐     popular tool reads as not having understood it.

⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ "WHO ELSE WAS INVOLVED IN THAT DECISION?"
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ a levelling probe (Day 487). ⭐ Answer honestly and
⭐     precisely: ⭐⭐ "I PROPOSED IT, TWO OF US REVIEWED IT, MY
⭐     LEAD SIGNED IT OFF" ⭐ is a real answer and a real level.

⭐ ⑤ ⭐⭐ "WAS IT WORTH IT?"
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE BEST QUESTION IN THE SET AND THE LEAST
⭐     PREPARED. ⭐ Sometimes the honest answer is no:
⭐     ⭐ "The reranker was worth it. ⭐⭐ THE SEMANTIC CACHE WAS
⭐       NOT — IT SAVED 12% AND COST US A 4% FALSE-HIT RATE, SO
⭐       WE DEMOTED IT TO SUGGESTIONS."
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ SAYING "NO" ABOUT ONE OF YOUR OWN DECISIONS IS ONE OF
⭐     THE STRONGEST CREDIBILITY MOVES AVAILABLE IN AN INTERVIEW.
```

# Part 5 — The decisions you did *not* make

```
⭐ ***THE TRAP:*** ⭐ presenting inherited or default decisions as
⭐   yours. ⭐⭐ IT COLLAPSES IN ONE FOLLOW-UP AND IT COSTS MORE
⭐   THAN THE DECISION WAS WORTH.

⭐ ***THE THREE HONEST FRAMES:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ***INHERITED:*** ⭐ "That predated me. ⭐⭐ WHAT I DID WAS
⭐     UNDERSTAND WHY IT WAS THERE BEFORE CHANGING ANYTHING
⭐     AROUND IT — ⭐ and here's what I found."
⭐   ⭐ ② ***DEFAULTED:*** ⭐ "Honestly, that was the default and I
⭐     didn't evaluate it at the time. ⭐⭐ HAVING RUN IT, HERE IS
⭐     WHAT I NOW KNOW ABOUT WHETHER IT WAS RIGHT."
⭐     ⭐ ⭐⭐ THIS IS A COMPLETELY ACCEPTABLE ANSWER AND
⭐       CANDIDATES ARE TERRIFIED OF IT. ⭐ Nobody evaluates every
⭐       choice; pretending you did is the actual red flag.
⭐   ⭐ ③ ***TEAM DECISION:*** ⭐ "The team chose it; I argued for
⭐     the alternative. ⭐⭐ HERE'S THE CASE I MADE AND WHY I THINK
⭐     THE DECISION WENT THE OTHER WAY" ⭐ — ⭐⭐ WHICH IS ALSO
⭐     YOUR CONFLICT STORY (DAY 465), SO PREPARE IT ONCE.

⭐ ⭐⭐ THE UNDERLYING RULE, AND IT IS THE SAME AS DAY 463'S:
⭐   ***BE PRECISE ABOUT THE BOUNDARY OF YOUR OWN WORK.***
⭐   ⭐ An interviewer who trusts your boundaries will believe
⭐     everything inside them.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. "Why Postgres rather than a dedicated vector database?"**

⭐ The force was transactional: the chunk row and the document row had to commit together. ⭐⭐ Without
that I can end up with an indexed chunk for a document that failed to save, and since the index is
derived state, nothing would ever reconcile it — that's the class of bug where the system serves content
that doesn't exist any more. ⭐ The alternative I took seriously was Qdrant, and its filtered ANN is
genuinely better than pgvector's, which matters because tenant filtering is on my critical path.
⭐⭐ The price I'm still paying is that the HNSW index shares RAM with the OLTP working set, so a large
reindex can evict the application's buffer cache and it shows up as "the app got slow" rather than as a
search problem. ⭐ And I know the four conditions that would flip me: tens of millions of vectors,
high-QPS filtered search, needing to scale the index independently, or wanting operational isolation so
a reindex can't touch checkout.

**2. "What if you had a hundred times the data?"**

⭐ I'd name which decision breaks first rather than redesign it on the spot. ⭐⭐ At around thirty million
vectors the HNSW index stops fitting comfortably alongside the OLTP working set, and that's the first
thing to go. ⭐ Before that, the thing that degrades is filtered search on selective predicates — which
is the tenant filter, so it degrades worst for my smallest customers. ⭐⭐ The migration is cheap because
there's a `VectorStore` protocol in front, so it's a new class rather than a rewrite; what isn't cheap
is that I'd lose the transaction, so deletion would have to move to the outbox pattern.

**3. "Why not use LangChain?"**

⭐ I use parts of it — the loaders and splitters are genuinely useful and parsing .docx correctly isn't
anyone's competitive advantage. ⭐⭐ What I don't use is the agent and chain abstractions, and the reason
is a specific test rather than a preference: can I print the exact bytes going to the provider? ⭐ If I
can't, I can't debug a bad answer, can't see the boilerplate tokens injected on every call, can't keep a
byte-stable prefix for prompt caching, and can't hash a prompt I don't possess. ⭐⭐ I'd never dismiss it
outright, though — for a team of six each inventing their own loop, one shared mediocre abstraction
genuinely beats six good ones.

**4. "Was it worth it?"**

⭐ Some of it. ⭐⭐ The reranker was clearly worth it — nine points of recall for a hundred and fifty
milliseconds. ⭐ The semantic cache was not: it saved about twelve percent of cost and cost me a four
percent false-hit rate, where a false hit means a confidently wrong answer served faster than a correct
one. ⭐⭐ So I demoted it to serving suggestions rather than answers — all of the saving, none of the
false-confidence risk. ⭐ And HyDE I deleted outright after measuring it flat. ⭐⭐ Being able to say "no"
about one of my own decisions is more useful than defending all of them.

**5. "Who else was involved in that decision?"**

⭐ I'd answer precisely, because vagueness here reads as either over-claiming or hiding. ⭐⭐ On the
retrieval architecture I proposed it, wrote the comparison, and my lead signed it off after we ran the
eval both ways. ⭐ On the permission model I didn't propose it — a security review flagged the
post-filtering and I implemented the fix, which is where I learned why post-filtering is a security bug
rather than a performance choice. ⭐⭐ Being precise about where my work ended tends to make people
believe what's inside it.

**6. "Why did you pick that chunk size?"**

⭐ Honestly? ⭐⭐ Six hundred tokens started as a default I copied, and I didn't evaluate it at the time.
⭐ What I did later was sweep about forty configurations against thirty labelled questions, and two
things came out: the size curve is flat between four hundred and nine hundred, so the argument matters
less than people think, and the splitting *strategy* mattered far more than the size. ⭐⭐ So the honest
version is that the initial choice was a default and the informed choice came after measurement. ⭐ I'd
rather say that than pretend I'd reasoned it out from first principles — nobody evaluates every choice,
and claiming to is the actual red flag.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

⭐⭐ **Two minutes, out loud: defend one decision in three parts, then take the two hardest follow-ups.**

Force first, and make it about the problem rather than about the technology. **"We put the vector index
in Postgres. The force was transactional: the chunk row and the document row had to commit together,
because otherwise I can have an indexed chunk for a document that failed to save — and the index is
derived state, so nothing would ever reconcile that. It's the class of bug where the system keeps
serving content that no longer exists."**

Then steelman what you rejected. **"The alternative I took seriously was Qdrant, and I'd say plainly
that its filtered ANN is better than pgvector's — which matters, because tenant filtering is on my
critical path and it's exactly the case pgvector handles least well."**

Then the price, unprompted. **"What I'm still paying for it: the HNSW index shares RAM with the OLTP
working set, so a big reindex can evict the application's buffer cache — and that surfaces as 'the app
got slow', not as a search problem, which makes it a nasty thing to diagnose. And I'd switch on four
conditions: tens of millions of vectors, high-QPS filtered search, needing to scale the index
separately, or wanting a reindex to be unable to affect checkout."**

Then take the two that matter. **"At a hundred times the data, the first thing to break is the RAM
sharing at around thirty million vectors — and the migration is cheap because there's a store protocol
in front, but I'd lose the transaction, so deletion would move to the outbox."** And: **"Was it worth
it? The reranker, yes. The semantic cache, no — twelve percent saving against a four percent false-hit
rate, so I demoted it to suggestions. HyDE I deleted after measuring it flat."**
