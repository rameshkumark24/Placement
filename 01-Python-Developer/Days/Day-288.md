# Day 288 🏗️

**P-288** · 🏗 **The second project** — ⭐⭐ why a different shape, and what it must prove

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** BUILD

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: two projects that prove the same thing prove one thing — so the second
> one is deliberately built where there is no correct answer, only a measurable one, because that is
> the class of system where every instinct from the first project quietly stops working.**
>
> ⭐ The flagship's hard parts were concurrency, money and tenancy — all of which have a *right*
> answer that a test can assert. ⭐⭐ This project's hard parts are that a wrong answer looks exactly
> like a right one.

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ Why a second project at all

```
⭐⭐ THE FAILURE MODE OF A PORTFOLIO: TWO CRUD APPS.
⭐   ⭐ The second one adds nothing. It is the same architecture,
⭐     the same decisions, and the same interview answers —
⭐     ⭐⭐ SO IT COSTS FIVE WEEKS AND BUYS ONE EXTRA SENTENCE.

⭐⭐ | The flagship proved | ⭐ THIS MUST PROVE |
⭐   | you can design before building | ⭐ you can build where
⭐        the requirements CANNOT be fully specified up front |
⭐   | correctness under concurrency | ⭐⭐ QUALITY UNDER
⭐        UNCERTAINTY — measured, not asserted |
⭐   | a test suite that proves invariants | ⭐⭐ AN EVALUATION
⭐        SET, because there is nothing to assert |
⭐   | you can operate a system | ⭐ you can operate a system
⭐        that DEGRADES SILENTLY rather than failing |
```

```
⭐⭐ THE DIFFERENCE THAT DRIVES EVERYTHING ELSE:
⭐   ⭐ A CRUD BUG PRODUCES A 500. Loud, counted, alerted.
⭐   ⭐⭐ A RETRIEVAL BUG PRODUCES A FLUENT, PLAUSIBLE, CONFIDENT,
⭐     WRONG ANSWER. ⭐ Nobody notices. There is no exception,
⭐     no error rate, no graph that moves.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ WHICH IS WHY THE EVALUATION SET IS NOT A TESTING
⭐     TECHNIQUE HERE — ***IT IS THE ONLY INSTRUMENT YOU HAVE.***
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The project: `clearline`

```
⭐⭐ AN INTERNAL "CAN WE DO THIS?" SYSTEM. A user asks a question
⭐   about policy; the system answers with a DECISION, a REASON,
⭐   and CITATIONS.

⭐   ⭐ INPUT:  "Can a contractor in Karnataka claim 14 days of
⭐             paid leave in their first quarter?"
⭐   ⭐ OUTPUT: ⭐⭐ a DECISION (yes / no / needs review),
⭐             ⭐⭐ WHICH RULES FIRED and why,
⭐             ⭐⭐ CITATIONS to the policy sections used,
⭐             ⭐ and an explicit "I don't know" when it doesn't.

⭐⭐ AND IT IS TWO SYSTEMS WEARING ONE COAT:
⭐   ⭐ A ***RULE ENGINE*** for what must be exact — thresholds,
⭐     eligibility, limits, dates. ⭐ Deterministic. Testable.
⭐     Explicable. Auditable.
⭐   ⭐ ***RETRIEVAL*** for what needs context — the wording, the
⭐     exceptions, the "see also", the human explanation.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND THE SPLIT BETWEEN THEM IS THE ENTIRE ARCHITECTURE.
⭐     Get it wrong in either direction and the project fails:
⭐     ⭐ rules for everything ⇒ unmaintainable, and it cannot
⭐       answer a question nobody anticipated
⭐     ⭐⭐ RETRIEVAL FOR EVERYTHING ⇒ A SYSTEM THAT SOUNDS
⭐       AUTHORITATIVE AND IS OCCASIONALLY WRONG ABOUT A NUMBER,
⭐       WHICH IS THE WORST OF ALL POSSIBLE PRODUCTS.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The rule that governs the whole project

```
⭐⭐ ***AN LLM MAY NOT BE IN THE PATH OF A DECISION THAT MUST
⭐      BE CORRECT.***

⭐   ⭐ IT MAY: rewrite a query, rank candidates, summarise a
⭐     passage, draft an explanation, extract a structured field
⭐     for a human to confirm, route a question.
⭐   ⭐⭐ IT MAY NOT: decide eligibility, compute an amount,
⭐     choose which rule applies, or be the only thing standing
⭐     between a user and a wrong number.

⭐⭐ THE REASON IS NOT IDEOLOGICAL — IT IS TESTABILITY:
⭐   ⭐ You cannot regression-test a decision you cannot
⭐     reproduce. ⭐ You cannot audit one you cannot explain.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND IN SIX MONTHS SOMEONE WILL ASK "WHY WAS THIS
⭐     DECLINED IN MARCH?" — ⭐⭐ "THE MODEL SAID SO" IS NOT AN
⭐     ANSWER ANY ORGANISATION ACCEPTS, AND IN SOME DOMAINS IT
⭐     IS NOT ONE THE LAW ACCEPTS EITHER.

⭐ ⇒ ⭐ Write this rule at the top of the README on day one.
⭐   ⭐⭐ IT IS ALSO, BY SOME DISTANCE, THE MOST USEFUL THING
⭐     YOU WILL SAY ABOUT THIS PROJECT IN AN INTERVIEW.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐⭐ What "good" means when nothing is deterministic

```
⭐⭐ ON THE FLAGSHIP, "IT WORKS" MEANT: THE TESTS PASS.
⭐   ⭐ Here, every answer is fluent, so ⭐⭐ "IT LOOKS GOOD" IS
⭐     AVAILABLE FOR FREE AND MEANS NOTHING.

⭐⭐ SO THE DEFINITION OF DONE IS A NUMBER THAT MOVED:
⭐   ⭐ recall@10 on the eval set: ⭐ 0.71 → 0.89 after switching
⭐     to hybrid retrieval (Day 293)
⭐   ⭐ citation coverage: ⭐ 82% → 97% after enforcing spans
⭐   ⭐ cost per query: ⭐ $0.021 → $0.004 after reranking let
⭐     you drop to a cheaper generator
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE DELIVERABLE OF THIS PROJECT IS NOT A DEMO. IT IS
⭐     ***A TABLE OF CHANGES AND THE NUMBERS THEY MOVED*** —
⭐     ⭐ which is exactly what nobody who built a weekend RAG
⭐       demo has, and it takes ten minutes to recognise.

⭐⭐ AND THE COROLLARY, WHICH IS A DISCIPLINE:
⭐   ***BUILD THE EVAL SET BEFORE THE THING IT EVALUATES.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ An eval set written afterwards is written to pass, and
⭐     you will not notice yourself doing it.
```

# Part 6 — ⭐ Honest constraints before you start

```
⭐   ⭐ YOU NEED A REAL CORPUS. ⭐⭐ 20 documents proves nothing;
⭐     retrieval is trivially perfect at that size and every
⭐     lesson in Days 292–293 is invisible. ⭐ Target 200+ real
⭐     documents: public policy, standards, regulations, your own
⭐     organisation's docs with permission.
⭐   ⭐ THE CORPUS MUST HAVE STRUCTURE AND CONFLICT — sections,
⭐     effective dates, superseded versions, and at least a few
⭐     places where two documents disagree. ⭐⭐ THE CONFLICTS
⭐     ARE WHERE THE INTERESTING ENGINEERING IS.
⭐   ⭐ BUDGET: cap it, out loud, in the config. ⭐⭐ A LOOP THAT
⭐     RE-EMBEDS A CORPUS ON EVERY RUN IS A REAL BILL, and
⭐     "I capped my own spend and here is the per-query cost"
⭐     is worth saying.
⭐   ⭐ AND A LOCAL PATH: an offline embedding model and a
⭐     cached-fixture mode, so tests never call a provider.
⭐     ⭐⭐ A TEST SUITE THAT HITS AN API IS SLOW, FLAKY, AND
⭐       NON-DETERMINISTIC — THREE FAILURES FROM DAY 281 AT ONCE.
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| A second CRUD project | Same architecture, same answers. Five weeks for one extra sentence. |
| Treating a fluent answer as a working system | Fluency is free. It is not evidence of anything. |
| No evaluation set | You have no instrument at all, because failures are silent. |
| Writing the eval set after the retriever | It gets written to pass, and you won't notice. |
| Putting an LLM in the decision path | You can't regression-test or audit what you can't reproduce. |
| Rules for everything | Unmaintainable, and useless for anything unanticipated. |
| Retrieval for everything | Authoritative-sounding and occasionally wrong about a number. |
| A 20-document corpus | Retrieval is trivially perfect; every lesson stays invisible. |
| A corpus with no conflicts or versions | You removed the part that's actually engineering. |
| No spend cap | A re-embedding loop is a real bill. |
| Tests that call a provider | Slow, flaky and non-deterministic at once. |
| No out-of-scope list | Each excluded item is a better answer than a half-built feature. |
| Judging success by a demo | The deliverable is a table of changes and the numbers they moved. |

## Interview questions

**Q: Why build a second project instead of extending the first?**

> Because two projects that prove the same thing prove one thing. The flagship demonstrates
> correctness under concurrency, money and tenancy — all of which have a right answer a test can
> assert. This one is deliberately in the class where there is no correct answer, only a measurable
> one, and where a bug produces a fluent, confident, wrong response rather than a 500. That changes
> everything downstream: the test suite becomes an evaluation set, "it works" becomes a number, and
> operating it means watching for silent degradation rather than errors.

**Q: What is the architecture, in one sentence?**

> A deterministic rule engine for the things that must be exact — thresholds, eligibility, dates —
> and retrieval over a document corpus for the things that need context, with the split between them
> as the central design decision. Rules for everything is unmaintainable and can't answer anything
> unanticipated; retrieval for everything gives you a system that sounds authoritative and is
> occasionally wrong about a number, which is the worst possible product.

**Q: Where do you allow the model, and where don't you?**

> It can rewrite a query, rank candidates, summarise a passage, draft an explanation, or extract a
> field for a human to confirm. It cannot decide eligibility, compute an amount, choose which rule
> applies, or stand between a user and a number. The reason is testability rather than ideology: you
> can't regression-test a decision you can't reproduce or audit one you can't explain — and in six
> months someone will ask why something was declined in March, where "the model said so" isn't an
> answer any organisation accepts, and in some domains isn't one the law accepts either.

**Q: How do you know it's any good?**

> By numbers I moved and recorded, not by a demo. Recall@k on a hand-built retrieval evaluation set,
> citation coverage, refusal rate, and cost per query — each with a before and after, and the change
> that caused it. The discipline that makes it honest is building the eval set before the thing it
> evaluates, because one written afterwards gets written to pass and you won't catch yourself doing
> it. That table is the deliverable, and it's the thing a weekend RAG demo doesn't have.
