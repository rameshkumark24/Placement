# Day 419 🧭

**P-419** · 🧭 **Embeddings** — ⭐⭐ what a vector means, and why similarity is not relevance

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: an embedding turns text into a point in space where distance means
> "similar in the way this particular model was trained to measure similarity" — and the mistake every
> first RAG system makes is assuming that is the same thing as "answers my question", which it
> demonstrably is not.**

---

# Part 1 — What a vector actually is

```
⭐ ⭐⭐ AN EMBEDDING MODEL MAPS TEXT → A FIXED-LENGTH VECTOR
⭐   (384, 768, 1024, 1536, 3072 floats).
⭐   ⭐ The same text always gives the same vector. ⭐⭐ IT IS
⭐     DETERMINISTIC — ⭐ unlike generation (Day 418), which is
⭐     why embeddings are cacheable forever and why you compute
⭐     them once.

⭐ ⭐⭐ THE ***MEANING*** IS IN THE GEOMETRY, NOT IN THE NUMBERS.
⭐   ⭐ No individual dimension means anything you can name.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ONLY ***RELATIVE POSITIONS*** ARE MEANINGFUL, ⭐ which is
⭐     why you can never inspect a vector to debug it — ⭐⭐ YOU CAN
⭐     ONLY COMPARE IT TO OTHERS.

⭐ ⭐ SIMILARITY: ***cosine*** — the angle between two vectors.
⭐   ⭐ Range −1 to 1; ⭐ in practice text embeddings cluster in a
⭐     narrow band.
⭐   ⭐⭐ AND THAT NARROW BAND IS THE FIRST PRACTICAL SHOCK:
⭐     ***TWO COMPLETELY UNRELATED SENTENCES OFTEN SCORE 0.7.***
⭐     ⭐ So an absolute threshold like "keep anything above 0.8"
⭐     is meaningless without calibration on ***your*** data.
⭐     ⭐⭐ RANK, DO NOT THRESHOLD — ⭐ and if you must threshold,
⭐     derive the number from labelled examples, never from
⭐     intuition.
⭐ ⭐ Most modern embeddings are ***normalised*** to unit length,
⭐   ⭐⭐ IN WHICH CASE COSINE SIMILARITY AND DOT PRODUCT ARE THE
⭐   SAME OPERATION — ⭐ and dot product is faster, which is why
⭐   vector databases prefer it. ⭐⭐ IF YOU MIX NORMALISED AND
⭐   UNNORMALISED VECTORS IN ONE INDEX, YOUR RANKING IS SILENTLY
⭐   GARBAGE.
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ Similarity is not relevance

> The single most important idea in this day, and the reason most first RAG systems disappoint.

```
⭐⭐ AN EMBEDDING MODEL WAS TRAINED TO PUT ***SIMILAR TEXT***
⭐   CLOSE TOGETHER. ⭐ It was ***not*** trained to put a question
⭐   close to its answer.

⭐ ⇒ FOUR FAILURE MODES THAT FOLLOW DIRECTLY:

⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***THE QUESTION MATCHES OTHER QUESTIONS.***
⭐    ⭐ Query: "How do I reset my password?"
⭐    ⭐⭐ NEAREST NEIGHBOUR: A FAQ HEADING THAT READS "HOW DO I
⭐      RESET MY PASSWORD?" ⭐ — text that is nearly identical and
⭐      ⭐⭐ CONTAINS NO ANSWER AT ALL.
⭐    ⭐ This is why asymmetric retrieval exists (Part 4).

⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***NEGATION IS NEARLY INVISIBLE.***
⭐    ⭐ "The refund was approved" and "The refund was ***not***
⭐      approved" ⭐⭐ EMBED ALMOST IDENTICALLY. ⭐ One token of
⭐      difference, opposite meaning, ⭐⭐ AND COSINE SIMILARITY
⭐      CANNOT SEE IT. ⭐ Any domain where "not", "except",
⭐      "unless" and "excluding" carry the meaning — legal,
⭐      medical, policy, insurance — ⭐⭐ IS A DOMAIN WHERE PURE
⭐      VECTOR SEARCH IS ACTIVELY DANGEROUS.

⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***EXACT IDENTIFIERS ARE LOST.*** ⭐ Order `INV-4471`,
⭐    error code `E-2201`, a part number, a person's surname.
⭐    ⭐⭐ THESE ARE RARE TOKENS THAT CARRY ALL THE MEANING, AND
⭐    EMBEDDINGS AVERAGE THEM AWAY. ⭐ `INV-4471` and `INV-4472`
⭐    are near-identical vectors and completely different
⭐    invoices. ⭐⭐ ⇒ THIS IS THE SINGLE STRONGEST ARGUMENT FOR
⭐    HYBRID SEARCH (DAY 433) AND IT IS NOT OPTIONAL.

⭐ ④ ⭐ ***TOPICAL DRIFT.*** ⭐ A chunk about the general topic
⭐    ranks above the chunk with the specific answer, ⭐⭐ BECAUSE
⭐    IT USES MORE OF THE QUERY'S VOCABULARY.

⭐⭐ ⇒ ***THE RULE: VECTOR SEARCH IS A GOOD ***RECALL*** DEVICE
⭐   AND A POOR ***PRECISION*** DEVICE.*** ⭐ Use it to get 50
⭐   plausible candidates cheaply, ⭐⭐ THEN USE SOMETHING THAT
⭐   ACTUALLY READS THEM — A RERANKER — TO PICK 5 (DAY 433).
⭐   ⭐ That two-stage shape is Day 359's ***cheap filter,
⭐   expensive rank***, arriving in a new domain.
```

# Part 3 — Choosing an embedding model

```
⭐ THE FOUR AXES, IN ORDER OF HOW MUCH THEY MATTER:
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***DOES IT WORK ON ***YOUR*** DATA?*** ⭐ MTEB leaderboard
⭐    position is a shortlist, ⭐⭐ NOT AN ANSWER — ⭐ benchmarks are
⭐    general web text and your corpus is insurance policies or
⭐    Tamil support tickets. ⭐⭐ MEASURE recall@k ON 50 LABELLED
⭐    QUERIES OF YOUR OWN (DAY 435). ⭐ A day of labelling beats
⭐    every leaderboard.
⭐ ② ⭐ ***DIMENSIONS.*** ⭐ More is not better — it is more
⭐    storage, more memory, slower search. ⭐⭐ 1536 vs 3072 IS
⭐    DOUBLE THE INDEX FOR OFTEN-MARGINAL GAIN. ⭐ Some models
⭐    support ***Matryoshka*** truncation — ⭐⭐ TAKE THE FIRST 512
⭐    OF 1536 DIMENSIONS AND KEEP MOST OF THE QUALITY, ⭐ which is
⭐    a genuinely good deal and under-used.
⭐ ③ ⭐ ***MAX INPUT LENGTH.*** ⭐ Typically 512–8,192 tokens.
⭐    ⭐⭐ AND THE SILENT FAILURE: MANY MODELS ***TRUNCATE***
⭐    OVER-LONG INPUT WITHOUT AN ERROR, ⭐ so half your document
⭐    is simply not in the vector and nothing tells you.
⭐ ④ ⭐ ***COST AND HOSTING.*** ⭐ API embeddings are cheap
⭐    (~$0.02–$0.13/M tokens) ⭐⭐ BUT YOU EMBED EVERY DOCUMENT AND
⭐    EVERY QUERY FOREVER. ⭐ Local models (`bge`, `e5`,
⭐    `all-MiniLM`) via `sentence-transformers` are free after
⭐    the GPU, ⭐⭐ AND REMOVE A NETWORK DEPENDENCY FROM YOUR READ
⭐    PATH — ⭐ which matters more than the money.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE CONSTRAINT THAT DOMINATES ALL FOUR: ***CHANGING
⭐   EMBEDDING MODELS MEANS RE-EMBEDDING YOUR ENTIRE CORPUS.***
⭐   ⭐ Vectors from different models are ***not comparable*** —
⭐     not "less accurate", ⭐⭐ MEANINGLESS. ⭐ Mixing them silently
⭐     destroys ranking.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ SO STORE THE MODEL NAME AND VERSION ***WITH EVERY
⭐     VECTOR***, ⭐ and treat a model change as a full reindex
⭐     with a build-alongside-and-alias-swap migration
⭐     (Day 406). ⭐⭐ IT IS A DERIVED STORE AND IT MUST BE
⭐     REBUILDABLE (DAY 413's SHAPE ⑤).
```

# Part 4 — Asymmetric retrieval and instruction prefixes

```
⭐ ⭐⭐ SYMMETRIC: "find text similar to this text" — ⭐ dedup,
⭐   clustering, near-duplicate detection. ⭐ Both sides are the
⭐   same kind of thing.
⭐ ⭐⭐ ASYMMETRIC: "find a ***passage*** that answers this
⭐   ***question***" — ⭐ RAG. ⭐ The two sides are different kinds
⭐   of thing and different lengths.

⭐ ⭐⭐ MANY MODERN EMBEDDING MODELS ARE TRAINED FOR THE
⭐   ASYMMETRIC CASE AND ***REQUIRE A PREFIX***:
⭐   ⭐ `e5`: `"query: ..."` vs `"passage: ..."`
⭐   ⭐ `bge`: an instruction prefix on the query only
⭐   ⭐ Some API models take an explicit `input_type`.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ AND OMITTING THE PREFIX DEGRADES RETRIEVAL
⭐     SUBSTANTIALLY ***WITH NO ERROR AND NO WARNING***. ⭐ People
⭐     conclude "vector search doesn't work for us" when the real
⭐     answer is that they used the model wrong. ⭐⭐ READ THE MODEL
⭐     CARD. IT IS FIFTEEN MINUTES AND IT IS OFTEN THE SINGLE
⭐     BIGGEST QUALITY JUMP AVAILABLE.
⭐ ⭐ Corollary: ⭐⭐ THE QUERY AND THE DOCUMENT ARE EMBEDDED
⭐   ***DIFFERENTLY***, ⭐ so you cannot reuse a document
⭐   embedding as a query embedding.
```

# Part 5 — The Python that matters

```python
from openai import OpenAI
client = OpenAI()

def embed_batch(texts: list[str], input_type: str) -> list[list[float]]:
    # ⭐⭐ BATCH. One call per document is a latency and cost
    # ⭐   disaster — the API takes hundreds at a time.
    out = client.embeddings.create(
        model="text-embedding-3-small",
        input=texts,                 # ⭐ up to ~2048 items
        dimensions=512,              # ⭐⭐ MATRYOSHKA: SMALLER
    )                                # ⭐   INDEX, MOST OF THE
    return [d.embedding for d in out.data]   # ⭐ QUALITY

# ⭐⭐ CACHE BY CONTENT HASH — embeddings are deterministic, so
# ⭐   re-embedding unchanged text is pure waste. On a corpus
# ⭐   that is re-ingested nightly this is most of the bill.
key = hashlib.sha256(f"{MODEL}:{DIM}:{text}".encode()).hexdigest()
#      ⭐⭐ THE MODEL AND DIMENSION ARE ***IN*** THE KEY, or a
#      ⭐   model change silently serves stale vectors.
```

```
⭐⭐ AND FOUR THINGS THAT GO WRONG IN PYTHON SPECIFICALLY:
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ EMBEDDING ONE ITEM PER CALL IN A `for` LOOP. ⭐ 10,000
⭐    documents at 80 ms each is 13 minutes and 10,000 requests;
⭐    ⭐⭐ BATCHED IT IS UNDER A MINUTE AND ~20 REQUESTS.
⭐ ② ⭐ Not caching by content hash on a re-ingested corpus.
⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ STORING VECTORS AS PYTHON LISTS OF FLOATS AND DOING
⭐    SIMILARITY IN A LOOP. ⭐ Use `numpy` — ⭐⭐ A MATRIX
⭐    MULTIPLICATION IS HUNDREDS OF TIMES FASTER THAN A LOOP,
⭐    AND `numpy` RELEASES THE GIL (DAY 065).
⭐ ④ ⭐ Silently truncating over-long input (Part 3).
```

# Part 6 — What else embeddings are for

```
⭐ ⭐ RAG retrieval is the famous one, ⭐⭐ AND IT IS NOT THE ONLY
⭐   VALUABLE ONE:
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***DEDUPLICATION / NEAR-DUPLICATE DETECTION*** — ⭐ same
⭐    support ticket filed twice in different words. ⭐⭐ SYMMETRIC,
⭐    SO IT WORKS WELL.
⭐ ② ⭐ ***CLUSTERING AND TOPIC DISCOVERY*** — ⭐⭐ "WHAT ARE PEOPLE
⭐    COMPLAINING ABOUT THIS MONTH?" ⭐ without predefined
⭐    categories.
⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***CLASSIFICATION WITHOUT AN LLM CALL*** — ⭐ embed
⭐    labelled examples once, then classify a new item by nearest
⭐    centroid. ⭐⭐ MICROSECONDS AND FREE, VERSUS HUNDREDS OF
⭐    MILLISECONDS AND A BILL. ⭐ For a high-volume routing
⭐    decision this is the right engineering answer and people
⭐    reach for an LLM instead.
⭐ ④ ⭐ ***SEMANTIC CACHING*** — ⭐ "has anyone asked essentially
⭐    this before?" (Day 454, ⭐⭐ INCLUDING WHY IT IS DANGEROUS).
⭐ ⑤ ⭐ ***RECOMMENDATION AND "MORE LIKE THIS"***.
⭐ ⑥ ⭐⭐ ***ANOMALY DETECTION*** — ⭐ a request far from every
⭐    cluster centroid is unusual, ⭐⭐ WHICH IS A CHEAP AND
⭐    SURPRISINGLY EFFECTIVE ABUSE SIGNAL.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. ⭐⭐ "What is an embedding?"**
> ⭐ A deterministic map from text to a fixed-length vector where distance encodes similarity — ⭐⭐ **and
> the meaning is entirely in the geometry, not in the numbers: no individual dimension means anything
> nameable, so you can never inspect a vector to debug it, only compare it to others.** ⭐ Similarity is
> cosine, ⭐⭐ **and the practical shock is that text embeddings cluster in a narrow band, so two
> completely unrelated sentences often score 0.7 — which means an absolute threshold like "keep
> anything above 0.8" is meaningless without calibration. Rank, don't threshold.**

**2. ⭐⭐ "Why did our vector search disappoint?"**
> ⭐ Because similarity isn't relevance. ⭐⭐ **The model was trained to put similar *text* close
> together, not to put a question close to its answer** — so four things go wrong. ⭐⭐ **The question
> matches other questions: "how do I reset my password" retrieves a FAQ heading with the identical
> words and no answer.** ⭐⭐ **Negation is nearly invisible — "the refund was approved" and "the refund
> was not approved" embed almost identically, which makes pure vector search actively dangerous in
> legal, medical or policy domains.** ⭐⭐ **Exact identifiers are lost, because `INV-4471` and
> `INV-4472` are near-identical vectors and completely different invoices — that's the strongest
> argument for hybrid search.** ⭐ And topical drift, where a general chunk outranks the specific
> answer. ⭐⭐ **So vector search is a good recall device and a poor precision device: use it to get 50
> candidates cheaply, then use a reranker that actually reads them to pick 5.**

**3. "How do you choose an embedding model?"**
> ⭐⭐ **By measuring recall@k on fifty labelled queries from my own corpus — MTEB position is a
> shortlist, not an answer, because benchmarks are general web text and my data is insurance policies
> or Tamil support tickets.** ⭐ Then dimensions, where more isn't better — ⭐⭐ **1536 versus 3072 is
> double the index for often-marginal gain, and Matryoshka truncation lets you take the first 512 and
> keep most of the quality.** ⭐ Then max input length, ⭐⭐ **because many models truncate over-long input
> with no error, so half your document silently isn't in the vector.** ⭐ Then cost and whether you want
> a network dependency on your read path.

**4. ⭐⭐ "What happens if you change embedding models?"**
> ⭐⭐ **You re-embed the entire corpus. Vectors from different models aren't less comparable — they're
> meaningless together, and mixing them silently destroys ranking.** ⭐ So I store the model name and
> version with every vector and treat a change as a full reindex: build alongside, catch up, compare,
> alias-swap. ⭐⭐ **It's a derived store, so it has to be rebuildable — and that's the same discipline
> as any projection.**

**5. ⭐⭐ "Anything people get wrong at the API level?"**
> ⭐ Instruction prefixes. ⭐⭐ **Many modern embedding models are trained asymmetrically and require a
> prefix — `query:` versus `passage:` for e5, an instruction prefix for bge — and omitting it degrades
> retrieval substantially with no error and no warning.** ⭐ People conclude vector search doesn't work
> for them when they simply used the model wrong. ⭐⭐ **Reading the model card is fifteen minutes and is
> often the single biggest quality jump available.** ⭐ And the corollary: query and document are
> embedded differently, so you can't reuse a document embedding as a query.

**6. "What else are embeddings good for?"**
> ⭐ Deduplication and clustering, which are symmetric and work well. ⭐⭐ **And classification without an
> LLM call — embed labelled examples once, classify by nearest centroid: microseconds and free versus
> hundreds of milliseconds and a bill.** ⭐ For a high-volume routing decision that's the right
> engineering answer, and people reach for an LLM instead. ⭐ Also anomaly detection: ⭐⭐ **a request far
> from every cluster centroid is unusual, which is a cheap and surprisingly effective abuse signal.**

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Define it precisely: **an embedding is a deterministic map from text to a vector where
distance encodes similarity, and the meaning is in the geometry — no dimension means anything you can
name, so you can only compare vectors, never inspect one.** Give the practical shock immediately:
**text embeddings cluster in a narrow band, so two unrelated sentences often score 0.7 — which means
absolute thresholds are meaningless and you rank rather than threshold.** Then the day's central idea:
**similarity is not relevance. The model was trained to put similar text together, not to put a
question near its answer.** Give the four consequences with examples: **a question retrieves other
questions — the FAQ heading with identical words and no answer; negation is nearly invisible, so "the
refund was approved" and "was not approved" embed almost identically, which makes pure vector search
dangerous in policy and medical domains; exact identifiers are lost, because INV-4471 and INV-4472 are

**vector search is a good recall device and a poor precision device — get fifty candidates cheaply,
then rerank to five with something that actually reads them.** That's cheap filter, expensive rank,
arriving in a new domain. Close on the two operational facts: **changing embedding models means
re-embedding everything, because vectors from different models aren't less comparable, they're
meaningless together — so store the model version with every vector and treat a change as a reindex
with an alias swap.** And **check the model card for a required query/passage prefix, because omitting
