# Day 418 🎲

**P-418** · 🎲 **Inference and sampling** — temperature, top-p, ⭐⭐ and the determinism you cannot have

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: the model outputs a probability distribution and *sampling* turns it into a
> token — so temperature and top-p are not creativity dials, they are decisions about how much of the
> tail you are willing to draw from, and the right setting is a property of the task rather than a
> matter of taste.**

---

# Part 1 — What actually happens at the last layer

```
⭐ THE MODEL PRODUCES A ***LOGIT*** — a raw score — for every
⭐   token in the vocabulary.
⭐ ⭐ `softmax` turns those scores into probabilities summing
⭐   to 1.
⭐ ⭐⭐ THEN A ***SAMPLER*** PICKS ONE. ⭐ The model does not pick.
⭐   ⭐⭐ SAMPLING IS A SEPARATE, PURELY MECHANICAL STEP THAT YOU
⭐   CONTROL — ⭐ and that separation is the thing to hold onto,
⭐   because it means "the model was creative" is really "your
⭐   sampler drew from the tail".

⭐   logits    →  softmax  →  probabilities  →  sample
⭐   [2.1, 0.3, ...]        [0.71, 0.11, ...]     " Paris"
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ Temperature, top-p, and what each actually does

```
⭐⭐ ***TEMPERATURE*** — ⭐ divides the logits before softmax.
⭐   ⭐ `T = 0`   → ⭐⭐ ALWAYS TAKE THE HIGHEST (greedy).
⭐   ⭐ `T = 1`   → the distribution as the model produced it.
⭐   ⭐ `T = 2`   → ⭐⭐ FLATTENED. Unlikely tokens become likely.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ IT RESHAPES THE ***WHOLE*** DISTRIBUTION, INCLUDING THE
⭐     LONG TAIL OF NONSENSE. ⭐ At high temperature you are
⭐     explicitly buying a chance of drawing a token the model
⭐     thought was almost certainly wrong.

⭐⭐ ***TOP-P (NUCLEUS)*** — ⭐ keep the smallest set of tokens
⭐   whose probabilities sum to p, then renormalise and sample.
⭐   ⭐ `p = 1.0` → everything is eligible.
⭐   ⭐ `p = 0.9` → ⭐⭐ THE TAIL IS ***CUT OFF ENTIRELY***.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE PROPERTY THAT MAKES IT BETTER THAN TEMPERATURE
⭐     ALONE: ***IT IS ADAPTIVE.*** ⭐ Where the model is confident,
⭐     the nucleus is 1–2 tokens and you get near-greedy
⭐     behaviour; ⭐ where it is genuinely uncertain, the nucleus
⭐     is 50 tokens and you get variety. ⭐⭐ THE SAMPLER TIGHTENS
⭐     EXACTLY WHERE CORRECTNESS MATTERS AND LOOSENS EXACTLY
⭐     WHERE IT DOES NOT.

⭐ ***TOP-K*** — keep the k most likely. ⭐⭐ CRUDER, BECAUSE IT IS
⭐   NOT ADAPTIVE: k=50 IS TOO WIDE WHEN THE ANSWER IS OBVIOUS
⭐   AND TOO NARROW WHEN IT IS NOT. ⭐ Largely superseded.
⭐ ***FREQUENCY / PRESENCE PENALTY*** — ⭐ reduce the score of
⭐   tokens already used. ⭐ For repetition loops. ⭐⭐ USE SPARINGLY;
⭐   AT HIGH VALUES THEY ACTIVELY DEGRADE FACTUAL TEXT BY
⭐   PENALISING A TERM YOU LEGITIMATELY NEED TWICE.
⭐ ***STOP SEQUENCES*** — ⭐⭐ HARD-STOP GENERATION. ⭐ Cheaper and
⭐   more reliable than asking the model to stop, ⭐ and they save
⭐   real output tokens.
⭐ ***SEED*** — ⭐ some providers accept one. ⭐⭐ IT MAKES SAMPLING
⭐   REPRODUCIBLE. IT DOES NOT MAKE THE ***MODEL*** DETERMINISTIC
⭐   (PART 3).
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The determinism you cannot have

```
⭐⭐ ***`temperature=0` IS NOT DETERMINISTIC. IT IS ONLY GREEDY.***
⭐   ⭐ Greedy means "always take the argmax". ⭐⭐ IT DOES NOT MEAN
⭐   THE LOGITS ARE THE SAME EVERY TIME.

⭐ WHY THEY ARE NOT — ⭐ and this is a genuinely surprising piece
⭐   of systems knowledge:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ① ***FLOATING-POINT ADDITION IS NOT ASSOCIATIVE.***
⭐      ⭐ `(a+b)+c ≠ a+(b+c)` in floating point.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ② GPU KERNELS REDUCE IN WHATEVER ORDER THE HARDWARE
⭐      SCHEDULES, ⭐ and that order depends on the ***batch*** —
⭐      ⭐⭐ WHICH DEPENDS ON WHAT ***OTHER PEOPLE*** SENT AT THE
⭐      SAME MOMENT.
⭐   ⭐ ③ So two identical requests can produce logits differing
⭐      in the last bits.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ④ USUALLY THAT CHANGES NOTHING. ⭐ But when the top two
⭐      tokens are nearly tied — 0.5001 vs 0.4999 — ⭐⭐ THE ARGMAX
⭐      FLIPS, AND FROM THAT TOKEN ONWARD THE TWO GENERATIONS
⭐      DIVERGE COMPLETELY.
⭐   ⭐ ⑤ Add MoE routing (Day 416) and provider-side model
⭐      updates, ⭐⭐ AND EVEN BIT-EXACT REPRODUCIBILITY ACROSS DAYS
⭐      IS NOT ON OFFER.

⭐⭐ ⇒ THE ENGINEERING CONSEQUENCES, AND ALL THREE MATTER:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ① ***NEVER ASSERT AN EXACT OUTPUT STRING IN A TEST.***
⭐      ⭐ It will pass a hundred times and fail in CI on a
⭐      Friday. ⭐⭐ ASSERT ***PROPERTIES***: valid JSON, the
⭐      required keys, a value in a permitted set, a number in a
⭐      range (Day 425).
⭐   ⭐⭐ ② ***CACHING BY PROMPT HASH IS THEREFORE SAFE AND
⭐      VALUABLE*** — ⭐ you are not "hiding" non-determinism, you
⭐      are choosing one sample and reusing it (Day 454).
⭐   ⭐⭐ ③ ***AN INCIDENT MAY NOT REPRODUCE.*** ⭐ "It gave a bad
⭐      answer to this input" may not happen again on the same
⭐      input. ⭐⭐ SO YOU MUST LOG THE FULL PROMPT AND THE FULL
⭐      RESPONSE AT THE TIME (DAY 455) — ⭐ there is no rerunning
⭐      it later.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ What to actually set, by task

```
⭐ ⭐⭐ EXTRACTION / CLASSIFICATION / STRUCTURED OUTPUT
⭐   ⭐ `temperature=0`, `top_p=1`
⭐   ⭐⭐ THERE IS ONE RIGHT ANSWER. ⭐ Any variance is pure
⭐     downside. ⭐ Add stop sequences and a tight `max_tokens`.

⭐ ⭐⭐ RAG / QUESTION ANSWERING OVER DOCUMENTS
⭐   ⭐ `temperature=0` to `0.2`
⭐   ⭐⭐ THE ANSWER IS SUPPOSED TO COME FROM THE CONTEXT, NOT FROM
⭐     THE MODEL'S IMAGINATION — ⭐ so a low temperature is
⭐     directly a ***hallucination control*** (Day 449).

⭐ ⭐ CODE GENERATION
⭐   ⭐ `temperature=0` to `0.2`. ⭐⭐ PEOPLE RAISE IT HOPING FOR
⭐     CLEVERNESS AND GET SUBTLY BROKEN CODE.

⭐ ⭐ SUMMARISATION
⭐   ⭐ `0.3` — ⭐ enough variation to read naturally, ⭐⭐ NOT
⭐     ENOUGH TO INVENT.

⭐ ⭐ CREATIVE WRITING / IDEATION / MARKETING COPY
⭐   ⭐ `0.7`–`1.0`, ⭐ `top_p` 0.9–0.95. ⭐⭐ THE ONE PLACE
⭐     VARIANCE IS THE PRODUCT.

⭐ ⭐⭐ "GENERATE N DIVERSE OPTIONS"
⭐   ⭐ Raise temperature ***or*** use `n>1` ⭐⭐ — AND `n>1` IS
⭐     USUALLY BETTER AND CHEAPER, BECAUSE THE PROMPT IS
⭐     PROCESSED ***ONCE*** AND ONLY DECODE IS REPEATED
⭐     (DAY 416). ⭐ People re-send the whole prompt N times and
⭐     pay N× for prefill they did not need.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE RULE THAT SETTLES MOST ARGUMENTS: ***SET
⭐   TEMPERATURE ***OR*** top-p, NOT BOTH.*** ⭐ Tuning two
⭐   interacting knobs at once means you cannot attribute a
⭐   change to either. ⭐⭐ HOLD `top_p=1` AND MOVE TEMPERATURE, OR
⭐   HOLD `temperature=1` AND MOVE top_p.
```

# Part 5 — Log-probabilities: the underused signal

```
⭐ ⭐⭐ MOST APIs CAN RETURN `logprobs` — THE MODEL'S PROBABILITY
⭐   FOR EACH TOKEN IT CHOSE. ⭐ Almost nobody uses them, and they
⭐   give you three genuinely useful things:

⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***A CHEAP UNCERTAINTY SIGNAL.*** ⭐ Mean token
⭐    probability across the answer correlates — imperfectly,
⭐    ⭐⭐ BUT USEFULLY — WITH WHETHER THE MODEL IS ON FIRM GROUND.
⭐    ⭐ A confident-sounding answer with low mean logprob is
⭐    ⭐⭐ EXACTLY THE CASE TO ROUTE TO A HUMAN OR TO A STRONGER
⭐    MODEL (DAY 457). ⭐ It is the closest thing to the
⭐    "confidence" that Day 414 said the text does not carry.

⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***CLASSIFICATION WITH ***ONE*** OUTPUT TOKEN.*** ⭐ Ask for
⭐    a single-token label and read the probabilities of each
⭐    candidate directly.
⭐    ⭐⭐ ⇒ ONE OUTPUT TOKEN INSTEAD OF FIFTY, SO IT IS FAST AND
⭐      NEARLY FREE, ***AND YOU GET CALIBRATED SCORES RATHER THAN
⭐      A BARE LABEL*** — ⭐ which means you can set a threshold
⭐      and abstain, instead of being forced to guess.

⭐ ③ ⭐ ***DEBUGGING A BAD GENERATION.*** ⭐ Find the token where
⭐    probability collapsed — ⭐⭐ THAT IS USUALLY THE EXACT POINT
⭐    THE ANSWER WENT WRONG, ⭐ and it is far more informative
⭐    than re-reading the output.
```

# Part 6 — Streaming, and the honest reason for it

```python
stream = client.chat.completions.create(
    model=MODEL, messages=msgs, stream=True,
    stream_options={"include_usage": True},   # ⭐⭐ OR YOU LOSE
)                                             # ⭐   TOKEN COUNTS
for chunk in stream:
    if chunk.choices and (d := chunk.choices[0].delta.content):
        yield d
```

```
⭐⭐ WHY: ***PERCEIVED*** LATENCY. ⭐ Time to first token is ~500 ms
⭐   while total time is 8 seconds — ⭐⭐ AND A USER WHO SEES TEXT
⭐   AT 500 ms EXPERIENCES A FAST SYSTEM, ⭐ while the same user
⭐   staring at a spinner for 8 seconds experiences a broken one.
⭐   ⭐ It costs nothing: decode is incremental anyway (Day 416).

⭐⭐ AND THE FOUR THINGS STREAMING BREAKS, WHICH PEOPLE DISCOVER
⭐   AFTER SHIPPING:
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ YOU CANNOT VALIDATE BEFORE THE USER SEES IT. ⭐ Guardrails
⭐    and schema checks (Days 424, 448) need the whole response.
⭐    ⭐ ⇒ Stream to a buffer and validate, ⭐ or accept it and
⭐    stream, ⭐⭐ AND FOR ANYTHING SAFETY-RELEVANT DO NOT STREAM.
⭐ ② ⭐ Token ***usage*** only arrives at the end, and only if you
⭐    ask for it — ⭐⭐ SO NAIVE STREAMING SILENTLY LOSES YOUR COST
⭐    DATA (DAY 421).
⭐ ③ ⭐ Errors can arrive ***mid-stream***, after you have already
⭐    sent 200 OK and half an answer. ⭐⭐ YOUR CLIENT NEEDS A
⭐    PROTOCOL FOR "THIS ANSWER IS ABANDONED".
⭐ ④ ⭐ Retrying is no longer transparent — ⭐⭐ YOU HAVE ALREADY
⭐    SHOWN THE USER THE FIRST ATTEMPT.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. ⭐⭐ "Explain temperature and top-p."**
> ⭐ The model outputs a distribution and a sampler picks a token — ⭐⭐ **the model doesn't pick, which
> means "the model was creative" really means "your sampler drew from the tail".** ⭐ Temperature divides
> the logits before softmax, so it reshapes the whole distribution including the long tail of nonsense
> — at high temperature you're explicitly buying a chance of drawing a token the model thought was
> almost certainly wrong. ⭐⭐ **Top-p keeps the smallest set of tokens summing to p and cuts the tail
> off entirely, and the reason it's better is that it's adaptive: where the model is confident the
> nucleus is one or two tokens and you get near-greedy behaviour, where it's genuinely uncertain the
> nucleus is fifty. It tightens exactly where correctness matters.** ⭐ And I'd set one or the other,
> never both, or I can't attribute a change to either.

**2. ⭐⭐ "Is `temperature=0` deterministic?"**
> ⭐ No — it's greedy, which is not the same thing. ⭐⭐ **Floating-point addition isn't associative, and
> GPU kernels reduce in whatever order the hardware schedules, which depends on the batch — which
> depends on what other people sent at the same moment. So identical requests can produce logits that
> differ in the last bits, and when the top two tokens are nearly tied the argmax flips and the two
> generations diverge completely from there.** ⭐ Three consequences: ⭐⭐ **never assert an exact output
> string in a test — it'll pass a hundred times and fail in CI on a Friday, so assert properties
> instead; caching by prompt hash is therefore safe, because you're choosing one sample and reusing
> it; and an incident may not reproduce, so you must log the full prompt and response at the time,
> because there's no rerunning it later.**

**3. "What temperature do you use?"**
> ⭐ It's a property of the task, not taste. ⭐⭐ **Zero for extraction, classification and structured
> output, because there's one right answer and any variance is pure downside. Zero to 0.2 for RAG,
> because the answer is supposed to come from the context rather than the model's imagination — so low
> temperature is directly a hallucination control.** ⭐ Zero to 0.2 for code, ⭐⭐ **because people raise it
> hoping for cleverness and get subtly broken code.** ⭐ Around 0.3 for summarisation, 0.7 to 1.0 for
> creative work — the one place variance is the product.

**4. ⭐⭐ "You need five diverse options. How?"**
> ⭐⭐ **Use `n=5` rather than five separate calls, because the prompt is processed once and only decode
> is repeated** — five separate calls pay for prefill five times, which on a long RAG prompt is most
> of the cost. ⭐ Raise temperature as well if the options are too similar. ⭐ It's the clearest case
> where knowing the prefill/decode split saves real money.

**5. ⭐⭐ "Any signal people underuse?"**
> ⭐ Log-probabilities. ⭐⭐ **Mean token probability across an answer correlates usefully with whether
> the model is on firm ground — so a confident-sounding answer with low mean logprob is exactly the
> case to route to a human or a stronger model. It's the closest thing to the confidence the text
> itself doesn't carry.** ⭐ They also give you single-token classification: ⭐⭐ **ask for a one-token
> label and read the probability of each candidate — one output token instead of fifty, and you get
> calibrated scores rather than a bare label, so you can set a threshold and abstain instead of being
> forced to guess.** ⭐ And for debugging, the token where probability collapsed is usually exactly where
> the answer went wrong.

**6. "Should you always stream?"**
> ⭐ For interactive text, yes — ⭐⭐ **time to first token at 500 ms feels fast even when total time is
> eight seconds, and it costs nothing because decode is incremental anyway.** ⭐ But it breaks four
> things people find after shipping: ⭐⭐ **you can't validate before the user sees it, so anything
> safety-relevant shouldn't stream; usage data only arrives at the end and only if you ask, so naive
> streaming silently loses your cost telemetry; errors can arrive mid-stream after you've already sent
> 200 OK; and retrying is no longer transparent because you've already shown the first attempt.**
