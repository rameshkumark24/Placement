# Day 412 💔

**P-412** · 💔 **Failure detection, gossip, split brain** — and replication at depth

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: you cannot distinguish a crashed node from a slow one — the only
> instrument you have is a timeout — so every failure detector is a bet about how long is too long, and
> tuning it is not reducing your error rate, it is choosing which of two error types you would rather
> have.**

---

# Part 1 — The impossibility, and the choice it forces

```
⭐⭐ ***A CRASHED NODE AND A SLOW NODE ARE OBSERVATIONALLY
⭐   IDENTICAL.*** ⭐ In both cases you sent something and heard
⭐   nothing. ⭐ There is no message that distinguishes them,
⭐   ⭐⭐ BECAUSE THE MESSAGE THAT WOULD HAVE IS THE ONE THAT DID
⭐   NOT ARRIVE (DAY 398).

⭐⭐ ⇒ SO FAILURE DETECTION IS A ***SUSPICION*** SYSTEM WITH TWO
⭐   ERROR TYPES, AND YOU MUST HAVE ONE OF THEM:

⭐   ⭐⭐ ***FALSE POSITIVE*** — ⭐ declaring a live node dead.
⭐     ⭐ Costs: an unnecessary failover, a rebalance (Day 402),
⭐     ⭐⭐ A SECOND PRIMARY, AND IN THE WORST CASE SPLIT BRAIN
⭐     (PART 4). ⭐ Timeout too ***short***.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ***FALSE NEGATIVE*** — ⭐ missing a genuinely dead node.
⭐     ⭐ Costs: requests hang, ⭐ traffic keeps being sent to a
⭐     black hole, ⭐⭐ AND YOUR OUTAGE LASTS AS LONG AS THE
⭐     TIMEOUT. ⭐ Timeout too ***long***.

⭐⭐ ⇒ ***TUNING A TIMEOUT DOES NOT REDUCE ERRORS. IT MOVES THEM
⭐   FROM ONE COLUMN TO THE OTHER.*** ⭐ So the real question is
⭐   which error your system survives better — ⭐⭐ AND FOR
⭐   ANYTHING WITH A SINGLE WRITER, FALSE POSITIVES ARE FAR MORE
⭐   EXPENSIVE, WHICH IS WHY DATABASE FAILOVER TIMEOUTS ARE TENS
⭐   OF SECONDS AND NOT ONE.

⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE PRACTICAL COROLLARY THAT SAVES YOU HOURS: ***MOST
⭐   "NETWORK PARTITIONS" ARE NOT NETWORK PARTITIONS.*** ⭐ They
⭐   are ⭐ a CFS-throttled container (Day 361), ⭐ a full
⭐   connection pool (Day 387), ⭐ a saturated worker pool
⭐   (Day 389), ⭐ a stop-the-world pause, ⭐ or a disk stall
⭐   (Day 410). ⭐⭐ THE NETWORK IS USUALLY FINE AND YOUR SERVICE
⭐   IS SLOW — ⭐ and the failure detector cannot tell, which is
⭐   the whole point.
```

# Part 2 — Heartbeats, and the phi-accrual detector

```
⭐ ***NAIVE HEARTBEAT:*** ⭐ ping every T, declare dead after k
⭐   missed. ⭐⭐ ONE FIXED THRESHOLD FOR ALL CONDITIONS.
⭐   ⭐ Which is wrong, because a 500 ms timeout that is generous
⭐     inside one AZ is aggressive across a region, ⭐⭐ AND
⭐     ***CATASTROPHIC*** DURING A TRAFFIC PEAK WHEN EVERY
⭐     RESPONSE IS SLOWER — ⭐ so your detector declares half the
⭐     fleet dead exactly when you can least afford it.
⭐     ⭐⭐ THE FAILURE DETECTOR FIRING DURING OVERLOAD IS ITSELF A
⭐     CASCADE AMPLIFIER (DAY 336).

⭐ ***PHI-ACCRUAL:*** ⭐ instead of a boolean, output a
⭐   ***suspicion level***.
⭐   ⭐ Keep a sliding window of inter-arrival times.
⭐   ⭐ Fit a distribution. ⭐⭐ φ = −log₁₀(P(a heartbeat is later
⭐     than the current silence)).
⭐   ⭐ φ = 1 → ~10% chance you are wrong. ⭐ φ = 8 → ~10⁻⁸.
⭐   ⭐ Act at a φ threshold.
⭐⭐ ⇒ THE VALUE: ***THE DETECTOR ADAPTS TO THE NETWORK IT IS
⭐   ACTUALLY ON.*** ⭐ A jittery link automatically gets more
⭐   slack; ⭐ a rock-steady one gets less. ⭐⭐ ONE THRESHOLD
⭐   INSTEAD OF A TIMEOUT PER ENVIRONMENT, ⭐ and no
⭐   re-tuning when you add a region. ⭐ Used by Cassandra and
⭐   Akka.
```

# Part 3 — Gossip, and SWIM's good idea

```
⭐ ***GOSSIP / EPIDEMIC DISSEMINATION:*** ⭐ each node picks k
⭐   random peers per round and exchanges state.
⭐   ⭐⭐ CONVERGES IN O(log N) ROUNDS. ⭐ No coordinator, ⭐ no
⭐     single point of failure, ⭐ robust to partial failure,
⭐     ⭐ scales to thousands of nodes.
⭐   ⭐ Cost: ⭐⭐ EVENTUAL — THERE IS A WINDOW WHERE NODES
⭐     DISAGREE ABOUT MEMBERSHIP — ⭐ and metadata grows with the
⭐     cluster.

⭐⭐ ***SWIM'S CONTRIBUTION IS THE ONE IDEA TO REMEMBER: INDIRECT
⭐   PROBES.***
⭐   ⭐ A cannot reach B. ⭐ Naively: B is dead.
⭐   ⭐⭐ INSTEAD, A ASKS C AND D TO PROBE B ON ITS BEHALF.
⭐   ⭐ If C and D reach B, ⭐⭐ THEN B IS ALIVE AND THE ***A–B
⭐     PATH*** IS BROKEN — ⭐ which is a completely different fact
⭐     and calls for a completely different response.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ THIS DIRECTLY ATTACKS THE FALSE-POSITIVE RATE FROM
⭐     PART 1, ⭐ and it is why SWIM-based membership (Consul's
⭐     Serf, HashiCorp's stack) is markedly more stable than
⭐     naive heartbeating on a real network.
⭐ ⭐ SWIM also adds a ***suspect*** state before ***dead***,
⭐   ⭐⭐ WITH A REFUTATION WINDOW — SO A NODE THAT IS WRONGLY
⭐   SUSPECTED CAN SAY "I AM HERE" BEFORE IT IS EVICTED.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Split brain, and the three defences

```
⭐ ***SPLIT BRAIN:*** ⭐ a partition where ***both*** sides
⭐   believe they are authoritative. ⭐ Both accept writes.
⭐   ⭐⭐ AND THE DAMAGE IS NOT THE DIVERGENCE — IT IS THAT
⭐   ***MERGING IS A BUSINESS PROBLEM, NOT A TECHNICAL ONE.***
⭐   ⭐ Two customers bought the same seat. ⭐ Two invoices carry
⭐     the number INV-4471. ⭐ The same order shipped twice.
⭐   ⭐⭐ NO ALGORITHM DECIDES WHO GETS THE SEAT. A PERSON DOES,
⭐     AND THEY WILL BE ANGRY.

⭐⭐ THE THREE DEFENCES:
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***QUORUM / MAJORITY.*** ⭐ Only the side holding a
⭐    majority may act; the minority ***stops***. ⭐ That is
⭐    Day 410's arithmetic and it is the primary defence.
⭐    ⭐⭐ AND IT IS WHY ODD CLUSTER SIZES MATTER: A 2-NODE CLUSTER
⭐    SPLIT 1–1 HAS NO MAJORITY, SO ***BOTH*** SIDES STOP — WHICH
⭐    IS SAFE AND USELESS. ⭐ For two data centres you need a
⭐    third location.
⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***FENCING.*** ⭐ Day 411's token, or STONITH — the old
⭐    hardware answer of literally powering off the other node.
⭐    ⭐ The principle is identical: ⭐⭐ MAKE THE DEPOSED SIDE
⭐    ***UNABLE*** TO ACT, RATHER THAN TRUSTING IT TO NOTICE.
⭐ ③ ⭐ ***A WITNESS / TIEBREAKER*** in a third failure domain —
⭐    ⭐⭐ A CHEAP VOTING-ONLY MEMBER THAT HOLDS NO DATA. ⭐ Two
⭐    real nodes plus a witness gives you a majority and costs
⭐    almost nothing.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE ANTI-PATTERN TO NAME: ***"BOTH SIDES KEEP SERVING
⭐   WRITES AND WE'LL RECONCILE LATER."*** ⭐ Unless you have
⭐   designed explicit merge semantics — CRDTs, or an
⭐   application-level merge somebody actually wrote — ⭐⭐ THE
⭐   DEFAULT RECONCILIATION IS "LAST WRITE WINS", WHICH IS
⭐   DAY 409's SILENT DATA LOSS, ⭐ chosen by accident.
```

# Part 5 — Replication topologies, at depth

```
⭐ ***SINGLE-LEADER*** (Day 328, Day 387) — ⭐ one writer, no
⭐   write conflicts by construction. ⭐⭐ THE FAILURE MODE IS
⭐   FAILOVER: CHOOSING A NEW LEADER, AND WHETHER THE OLD ONE
⭐   KNOWS. ⭐ Almost always the right answer.

⭐ ***MULTI-LEADER*** — ⭐ writes accepted in several places.
⭐   ⭐⭐ WRITE CONFLICTS ARE NOW ***NORMAL***, NOT EXCEPTIONAL,
⭐   ⭐ so you must have a resolution strategy ***before***
⭐   launch:
⭐   ⭐ LWW → ⭐⭐ LOSES DATA (DAY 409). ⭐ Only where losing is
⭐     acceptable.
⭐   ⭐ Application merge → correct, ⭐ and someone must write it.
⭐   ⭐ CRDTs → automatic, ⭐ with limits (below).
⭐   ⭐ When it is right: ⭐ multi-DC with local writes,
⭐     ⭐ offline-capable clients, ⭐ collaborative editing.

⭐ ***LEADERLESS (DYNAMO-STYLE)*** — ⭐ write to N, wait for W,
⭐   read from R.
⭐   ⭐⭐ R + W > N GIVES QUORUM INTERSECTION — THE SAME FACT AS
⭐     RAFT'S SAFETY PROOF (DAY 410).
⭐   ⭐⭐ AND DAY 348's CAVEAT REMAINS THE IMPORTANT ONE:
⭐     ***A SLOPPY QUORUM BREAKS IT.*** ⭐ If W writes land on
⭐     ***any*** N available nodes rather than the N ***home***
⭐     nodes, the intersection guarantee is gone — ⭐⭐ YOU HAVE
⭐     BOUGHT AVAILABILITY AND QUIETLY SOLD THE CONSISTENCY YOU
⭐     THOUGHT YOU WERE PAYING FOR.
⭐   ⭐ Repair mechanisms: ⭐ read repair (fix on read),
⭐     ⭐ hinted handoff, ⭐⭐ AND ANTI-ENTROPY WITH MERKLE TREES —
⭐     ⭐ compare hash trees to find differing ranges in O(log n)
⭐     comparisons instead of shipping everything.

⭐ ***CRDTs***, briefly and honestly:
⭐   ⭐ G-Counter, PN-Counter, OR-Set, LWW-Register, RGA for text.
⭐   ⭐⭐ THE PROPERTY: A MERGE THAT IS COMMUTATIVE, ASSOCIATIVE
⭐     AND IDEMPOTENT ⇒ ***ALL REPLICAS CONVERGE WITHOUT ANY
⭐     COORDINATION.*** ⭐ Which is a genuinely beautiful result.
⭐   ⭐⭐ AND THE TWO COSTS PEOPLE OMIT: ⭐ metadata and tombstones
⭐     that ***grow and are hard to garbage-collect***,
⭐     ⭐⭐ AND — THE DECISIVE ONE — ***NOT EVERY BUSINESS RULE CAN
⭐     BE A CRDT.*** ⭐ "The balance must never go negative" is an
⭐     invariant over the ***whole*** value, ⭐⭐ AND NO
⭐     COORDINATION-FREE MERGE CAN ENFORCE IT. ⭐ Convergence is
⭐     not correctness.
```

# Part 6 — What to instrument

```
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***REPLICATION LAG, IN SECONDS AND IN BYTES.*** ⭐ Seconds
⭐    for the human decision, ⭐ bytes because a paused replica
⭐    retains WAL and fills the primary's disk (Day 407).
⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***QUORUM HEALTH: HOW MANY FAILURES CAN YOU SURVIVE
⭐    ***RIGHT NOW***?*** ⭐ Not "is the cluster up" — ⭐⭐ A
⭐    THREE-NODE CLUSTER WITH ONE MEMBER DOWN IS ***UP*** AND HAS
⭐    ZERO REMAINING TOLERANCE, ⭐ and that is the alert you
⭐    want, hours before the outage.
⭐ ③ ⭐ ***LEADER CHANGES PER HOUR.*** ⭐⭐ A NON-ZERO STEADY RATE
⭐    MEANS YOUR FAILURE DETECTOR IS TOO AGGRESSIVE OR A NODE IS
⭐    SICK. ⭐ It is the earliest signal of both.
⭐ ④ ⭐ ***ISR / MEMBERSHIP SIZE*** (Day 403).
⭐ ⑤ ⭐⭐ ***PAUSE DETECTION IN YOUR OWN PROCESS***: ⭐ a thread
⭐    that sleeps 100 ms in a loop and logs whenever it wakes
⭐    late. ⭐⭐ TEN LINES, AND IT TURNS "MYSTERIOUS TIMEOUTS" INTO
⭐    "WE WERE DESCHEDULED FOR 1.8 SECONDS", ⭐ which is the
⭐    difference between guessing and knowing.
```

# Part 7 — Never build your own failover

```
⭐⭐ ***THE ADVICE, STATED PLAINLY: DO NOT WRITE YOUR OWN
⭐   FAILOVER LOGIC. USE A SYSTEM THAT HAS ALREADY LOST SOMEBODY
⭐   ELSE'S DATA AND LEARNED FROM IT.***

⭐ WHAT TO USE, AND WHAT EACH GETS RIGHT:
⭐   ⭐ ***PATRONI*** for Postgres — ⭐⭐ IT USES etcd/CONSUL AS THE
⭐     SOURCE OF TRUTH, SO LEADERSHIP IS A CONSENSUS DECISION
⭐     (DAY 410) RATHER THAN A SCRIPT'S OPINION, ⭐ and it
⭐     ***fences*** the demoted primary by demoting it to a
⭐     replica before promoting the new one.
⭐   ⭐ ***REDIS SENTINEL*** — ⭐ quorum-based, ⭐⭐ AND STILL SUBJECT
⭐     TO DAY 388's CLIENT-SIDE PROBLEM: THE FAILOVER SUCCEEDS
⭐     AND YOUR POOL DOES NOT NOTICE.
⭐   ⭐ ***RDS/Aurora Multi-AZ*** — someone else's problem
⭐     (Day 387).
⭐   ⭐ ***KUBERNETES OPERATORS*** for stateful systems.

⭐⭐ AND THE FOUR QUESTIONS TO ASK OF ***ANY*** FAILOVER SYSTEM
⭐   BEFORE YOU TRUST IT:
⭐   ⭐ ① What is the quorum, and what happens with no majority?
⭐   ⭐⭐ ② HOW IS THE OLD PRIMARY FENCED? ⭐ If the answer is "it
⭐      notices and steps down", ⭐⭐ THAT IS DAY 411's PAUSED
⭐      CLIENT AND IT IS NOT A FENCE.
⭐   ⭐ ③ What is the RPO — how many acknowledged writes can be
⭐      lost? ⭐⭐ AND HAVE YOU MEASURED IT, NOT READ IT?
⭐   ⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ HOW DOES THE ***CLIENT*** FIND THE NEW PRIMARY, AND
⭐      HOW LONG DOES ITS POOL TAKE TO NOTICE? ⭐ This is the
⭐      question that turns a 30-second failover into a
⭐      40-minute outage, ⭐⭐ AND IT IS THE ONE NOBODY ASKS
⭐      (DAY 387).
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. ⭐⭐ "How do you detect that a node has failed?"**
> ⭐ You can't, in the strict sense — ⭐⭐ **a crashed node and a slow node are observationally
> identical, because in both cases you sent something and heard nothing, and the message that would
> distinguish them is the one that didn't arrive.** ⭐ So it's a suspicion system with two error types
> and you must have one of them: ⭐⭐ **a false positive declares a live node dead, causing an
> unnecessary failover and possibly split brain; a false negative misses a dead node, so requests hang
> and the outage lasts as long as the timeout.** ⭐ Tuning the timeout doesn't reduce errors, ⭐⭐ **it
> moves them from one column to the other — and for anything with a single writer, false positives are
> far more expensive, which is why database failover timeouts are tens of seconds rather than one.**

**2. "What's better than a fixed timeout?"**
> ⭐ A phi-accrual detector. ⭐⭐ **Instead of a boolean it outputs a suspicion level based on the
> distribution of past inter-arrival times, so the detector adapts to the network it's actually on — a
> jittery link automatically gets more slack.** ⭐ One threshold instead of a timeout per environment.
> ⭐ And the reason it matters operationally: ⭐⭐ **a fixed timeout that's generous inside an AZ becomes
> catastrophic during a traffic peak when everything is slower, so your detector declares half the
> fleet dead exactly when you can least afford it — the failure detector firing during overload is
> itself a cascade amplifier.**

**3. ⭐⭐ "What's SWIM's good idea?"**
> ⭐ Indirect probes. ⭐⭐ **When A can't reach B, instead of concluding B is dead, A asks C and D to
> probe B on its behalf. If they reach it, B is alive and the A–B path is broken — which is a
> completely different fact needing a completely different response.** ⭐ That attacks the
> false-positive rate directly, which is why SWIM-based membership is noticeably more stable than
> naive heartbeating on a real network. ⭐ It also adds a suspect state with a refutation window, ⭐⭐ **so
> a wrongly-suspected node can say "I'm here" before it's evicted.**

**4. ⭐⭐ "How do you prevent split brain?"**
> ⭐ Three defences, and I'd start by naming what makes it so bad: ⭐⭐ **the damage isn't the
> divergence, it's that merging is a business problem rather than a technical one — two customers
> bought the same seat, and no algorithm decides who gets it.** ⭐ Quorum first: only the majority side
> may act and the minority stops. ⭐⭐ **Which is why odd cluster sizes matter, and why a two-node
> cluster split one-one has no majority so both sides stop — safe and useless. For two data centres you
> need a witness in a third location.** ⭐ Then fencing — a token, or STONITH — ⭐⭐ **the principle being
> to make the deposed side unable to act rather than trusting it to notice.** ⭐ And the anti-pattern to
> name is ⭐⭐ **"both sides keep serving and we'll reconcile later", because unless someone has written
> explicit merge semantics, the default reconciliation is last-write-wins, which is silent data loss
> chosen by accident.**

**5. "Talk about CRDTs."**
> ⭐ A data type whose merge is commutative, associative and idempotent, ⭐⭐ **which means all replicas
> converge without any coordination at all** — a genuinely beautiful result, and the basis of
> collaborative editing and offline-first apps. ⭐ Two costs get omitted: ⭐⭐ **metadata and tombstones
> that grow and are hard to garbage-collect, and the decisive one — not every business rule can be a
> CRDT.** ⭐ "The balance must never go negative" is an invariant over the whole value, ⭐⭐ **and no
> coordination-free merge can enforce it. Convergence is not correctness.**

**6. ⭐⭐ "What would you monitor on a replicated system?"**
> ⭐ Replication lag in both seconds and bytes — seconds for the human decision, bytes because a paused
> replica retains WAL and can fill the primary's disk. ⭐⭐ **Quorum health expressed as how many further
> failures you can survive right now — not "is the cluster up", because a three-node cluster with one
> member down is up and has zero remaining tolerance, and that's the alert you want hours before the
> outage.** ⭐ Leader changes per hour, ⭐⭐ **because a steady non-zero rate means the detector is too
> aggressive or a node is sick, and it's the earliest signal of both.** ⭐ And ⭐⭐ **pause detection
> inside my own process — a thread that sleeps 100 ms in a loop and logs when it wakes late. Ten lines,
> and it turns "mysterious timeouts" into "we were descheduled for 1.8 seconds"** — because most
> "network partitions" are actually a throttled container or a saturated pool.

**7. "Would you write your own failover?"**
> ⭐⭐ **No — use something that has already lost somebody else's data and learned from it.** ⭐ Patroni
> for Postgres, because ⭐⭐ **leadership is a consensus decision in etcd rather than a script's opinion,
> and it fences the demoted primary rather than hoping it notices.** ⭐ And I'd ask four questions of any
> failover system: what's the quorum, how is the old primary fenced — ⭐⭐ **if the answer is "it notices
> and steps down", that's a paused client and it isn't a fence** — what's the measured RPO, and ⭐⭐ **how
> does the client find the new primary and how long does its connection pool take to notice, which is
> the question that turns a 30-second failover into a 40-minute outage and the one nobody asks.**

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Start from the impossibility: **a crashed node and a slow node are observationally
identical — you sent something and heard nothing, and the message that would distinguish them is the
one that didn't arrive.** So detection is suspicion with two error types: **false positives cause
unnecessary failovers and split brain, false negatives leave requests hanging for the length of the
timeout — and tuning the timeout doesn't reduce errors, it moves them between columns. For a
single-writer system false positives are far more expensive, which is why database failover timeouts
are tens of seconds.** Add the practical correction: **most "network partitions" are a throttled
container, a full connection pool or a disk stall — the network is fine and your service is slow, and
the detector can't tell.** Then two improvements: **phi-accrual outputs a suspicion level from the
distribution of past arrivals, so it adapts to the network instead of needing a timeout per
environment; and SWIM's indirect probes ask two other nodes to check B before declaring it dead, which
distinguishes "B is dead" from "the A–B path is broken".** Then split brain, framed correctly: **the
damage isn't divergence, it's that merging is a business problem — two people bought the same seat and
no algorithm decides who gets it.** Three defences: **quorum, which is why a two-node cluster split
one-one has both sides stop; fencing, so the deposed side is unable to act rather than trusted to
notice; and a witness in a third location.** Close on operations: **monitor how many further failures
you can survive right now, not whether the cluster is up; alarm on leader changes per hour; and never
write your own failover — use something that has already lost somebody else's data. Then ask the
question nobody asks: how long does the client's connection pool take to notice.**
