# Day 334 🪵

**P-334** · 🪵 **Kafka fundamentals** — the log, the partition, and ⭐⭐ where ordering actually lives

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: Kafka is not a queue, it is a partitioned, replicated, append-only log
> that consumers read at their own pace — and almost every Kafka mistake comes from one fact:
> ordering exists inside a partition and nowhere else, so your partition key is your ordering
> guarantee and your parallelism ceiling at the same time.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ The log, and why it changes everything

```
⭐⭐ A CLASSIC QUEUE: a message is delivered, acknowledged, and
⭐   ***deleted.*** ⭐ The broker tracks per-message state.
⭐⭐ KAFKA: messages are ***appended to a file*** and kept for a
⭐   retention period. ⭐⭐ CONSUMERS TRACK THEIR OWN POSITION.

⭐⭐ FOUR CONSEQUENCES, AND THEY ARE THE ENTIRE DESIGN:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ ***READING DOES NOT DESTROY.*** N independent
⭐        consumer groups read the same data at different
⭐        speeds. ⭐ Adding an analytics consumer costs the
⭐        producer nothing and risks nothing.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ ***REPLAY IS FREE.*** Reset the offset to yesterday
⭐        and reprocess. ⭐ Day 333's missing replay procedure
⭐        is a one-line command here — ⭐⭐ WHICH IS THE SINGLE
⭐        BEST REASON TO CHOOSE KAFKA.
⭐   ③ ⭐ ***THE BROKER IS DUMB AND THEREFORE FAST.*** No
⭐        per-message state, sequential disk I/O, zero-copy
⭐        transfer. ⭐ Sequential disk writes are faster than
⭐        random memory access patterns, which is why a
⭐        disk-backed log outruns most in-memory brokers.
⭐   ④ ⭐⭐ ***A SLOW CONSUMER CANNOT SLOW THE PRODUCER OR THE
⭐        OTHER CONSUMERS.*** It just falls behind — ⭐ and if it
⭐        falls behind ***past retention***, it silently skips
⭐        data. ⭐⭐ THAT IS THE FAILURE MODE TO ALERT ON.
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ Partitions: ordering and parallelism are the same knob

```
⭐⭐ A TOPIC IS SPLIT INTO PARTITIONS. EACH PARTITION IS AN
⭐   INDEPENDENT, ORDERED LOG.

⭐⭐ THE CENTRAL FACT: ***ORDERING IS GUARANTEED WITHIN A
⭐   PARTITION AND NOWHERE ELSE.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THERE IS NO TOPIC-WIDE ORDER. THERE NEVER WAS. Any
⭐     design that assumes one is wrong, and it will look
⭐     correct in testing with one partition.

⭐⭐ THE PARTITIONER: `partition = hash(key) % num_partitions`.
⭐   ⭐ Same key ⇒ same partition ⇒ ***ordered relative to
⭐     itself.***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ NULL KEY ⇒ ROUND-ROBIN (sticky batching) ⇒ NO
⭐     ORDERING AT ALL. ⭐ This is the most common accidental
⭐     bug: forgetting the key and losing ordering silently.

⭐⭐ SO THE KEY DECIDES THREE THINGS AT ONCE — ⭐⭐ AND THIS IS
⭐   THE INSIGHT THE DAY IS BUILT ON:
⭐   ① ⭐ WHAT IS ORDERED (everything sharing the key).
⭐   ② ⭐ HOW EVENLY LOAD SPREADS (⭐⭐ SAME HOT-KEY PROBLEM AS
⭐        DAY 329 — one loud tenant melts one partition).
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ YOUR MAXIMUM CONSUMER PARALLELISM.

⭐⭐ BECAUSE: ***ONE PARTITION IS CONSUMED BY AT MOST ONE
⭐   CONSUMER IN A GROUP.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ 6 PARTITIONS ⇒ AT MOST 6 USEFUL CONSUMERS. A SEVENTH
⭐     SITS IDLE. ⭐ Scaling out stops working with no error
⭐     message.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Hence: ⭐⭐ OVER-PARTITION MODESTLY AT CREATION (Day
⭐     329's pre-split, again). ⭐ Increasing partitions later
⭐     ***changes the hash mapping and destroys your existing
⭐     ordering guarantee*** for keys that move.
```

```python
# ⭐⭐ THE ONE-LINE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN ORDERED AND NOT
await producer.send("orders", key=None, value=payload)          # ❌
await producer.send("orders", key=order_id.encode(), value=...)  # ✔
# ⭐ Same topic, same code path, entirely different guarantees —
# ⭐⭐ AND THE BUG IS INVISIBLE UNTIL TWO EVENTS FOR ONE ORDER
# ⭐   LAND ON DIFFERENT PARTITIONS AND ARE PROCESSED OUT OF
# ⭐   ORDER BY TWO CONSUMERS AT ONCE.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐ Replication, ISR, and the durability knobs

```
⭐⭐ EACH PARTITION HAS ONE ***LEADER*** AND N−1 ***FOLLOWERS***.
⭐   ⭐ Producers and consumers talk to the leader.
⭐   ⭐ ***ISR*** (in-sync replicas) = followers caught up
⭐     within a lag bound.

⭐⭐ THE THREE SETTINGS THAT DECIDE WHETHER YOU LOSE DATA:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ `acks`
⭐        ⭐ `0` — fire and forget. ⭐ Lose freely.
⭐        ⭐ `1` — leader only. ⭐⭐ LEADER DIES BEFORE
⭐          REPLICATION ⇒ THE MESSAGE IS GONE. ⭐ Same shape as
⭐          Day 328's async replication.
⭐        ⭐ `all` — ⭐⭐ EVERY ISR MEMBER. ⭐ The only durable
⭐          choice.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ `min.insync.replicas`
⭐        ⭐ `acks=all` with `min.insync=1` is ⭐⭐ A LIE THAT
⭐          LOOKS SAFE: if only the leader is in the ISR,
⭐          "all" means "one".
⭐        ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE CORRECT PAIRING: replication factor 3,
⭐          `min.insync.replicas=2`, `acks=all`. ⭐ Survives one
⭐          broker loss with zero data loss, and ⭐ blocks
⭐          writes when two are down — which is the CP choice,
⭐          made deliberately (Day 330).
⭐   ③ ⭐ `unclean.leader.election.enable`
⭐        ⭐⭐ `true` LETS AN OUT-OF-SYNC REPLICA BECOME LEADER
⭐          ⇒ AVAILABILITY, AT THE PRICE OF SILENTLY LOSING
⭐          COMMITTED MESSAGES. ⭐ Default false. Keep it false.

⭐⭐ ⇒ THESE THREE SETTINGS ARE A CAP DECISION EXPRESSED AS
⭐   CONFIGURATION, ⭐⭐ AND SAYING SO IS WORTH MORE THAN
⭐   RECITING THEM.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Consumer groups, offsets, and the rebalance that eats your lunch

```
⭐⭐ A CONSUMER GROUP SHARES A TOPIC'S PARTITIONS. ⭐ Each
⭐   partition to exactly one member. ⭐ Offsets are committed
⭐   per (group, topic, partition).

⭐⭐ COMMIT SEMANTICS — THE CHOICE THAT DEFINES YOUR SEMANTICS:
⭐   ⭐ ***COMMIT BEFORE PROCESSING*** ⇒ at-most-once. ⭐ A crash
⭐     loses the message.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ***COMMIT AFTER PROCESSING*** ⇒ AT-LEAST-ONCE. ⭐ A
⭐     crash reprocesses. ⭐⭐ THIS IS WHAT YOU WANT, AND IT IS
⭐     WHY IDEMPOTENCY IS MANDATORY (Day 335).
⭐   ⭐ `enable.auto.commit=true` ⇒ ⭐⭐ COMMITS ON A TIMER,
⭐     REGARDLESS OF WHETHER YOU FINISHED. ⭐ It is neither
⭐     semantics reliably. ⭐⭐ TURN IT OFF FOR ANYTHING THAT
⭐     MATTERS.

⭐⭐ THE REBALANCE — THE OPERATIONAL PROBLEM NOBODY WARNS YOU
⭐   ABOUT:
⭐   ⭐ A member joins, leaves, or ⭐⭐ FAILS TO CALL `poll()`
⭐     WITHIN `max.poll.interval.ms`.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Partitions are reassigned. ⭐⭐ WITH THE EAGER
⭐     PROTOCOL, ***EVERY*** CONSUMER STOPS — A STOP-THE-WORLD
⭐     PAUSE FOR THE WHOLE GROUP.

⭐⭐ THE CLASSIC PYTHON FAILURE, IN FULL:
⭐   ⭐ Your consumer polls 500 records and processes them one
⭐     at a time, each taking 700 ms.
⭐   ⭐ 350 seconds pass. `max.poll.interval.ms` is 300 s.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE BROKER CONCLUDES YOU ARE DEAD AND REBALANCES.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ YOUR OFFSET COMMIT IS THEN ***REJECTED***, SO ALL 500
⭐     RECORDS ARE REDELIVERED — ⭐ to a new consumer, which
⭐     also takes 350 s, ⭐⭐ AND THE GROUP REBALANCES FOREVER
⭐     WHILE MAKING NO PROGRESS.
⭐   ⇒ FIXES: ⭐ reduce `max.poll.records`; ⭐ raise
⭐     `max.poll.interval.ms`; ⭐⭐ OR DECOUPLE — poll fast, hand
⭐     work to an executor, and pause/resume partitions for
⭐     backpressure (Day 336).
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ And use ***cooperative sticky*** assignment, which
⭐     revokes only the partitions that actually move.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐ Retention, compaction, and the topic as a table

```
⭐⭐ TWO RETENTION POLICIES, AND THE SECOND IS THE INTERESTING
⭐   ONE:
⭐   ⭐ ***DELETE*** — drop segments older than `retention.ms`
⭐     or beyond `retention.bytes`. ⭐ The default. A stream of
⭐     facts with a shelf life.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ***COMPACT*** — keep the ***latest value per key***,
⭐     forever.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ A COMPACTED TOPIC IS A TABLE: replay it from the
⭐     start and you have the current state of every key. ⭐ It
⭐     is a durable, replicated key-value store with a change
⭐     feed attached — ⭐⭐ AND THAT IS THE FOUNDATION OF CDC
⭐     (Day 407) AND OF EVERY "MATERIALISE A VIEW FROM A
⭐     TOPIC" DESIGN.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐ Deletion in a compacted topic is a ***tombstone***:
⭐     a record with a null value.

⭐⭐ THE STREAM/TABLE DUALITY, IN ONE LINE:
⭐   ***A TABLE IS THE ACCUMULATED STATE OF A STREAM; A STREAM
⭐      IS THE SEQUENCE OF CHANGES TO A TABLE.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Once you see it, event sourcing (Day 406) and CQRS
⭐     stop being exotic.
```

# Part 6 — ⭐⭐ Sizing, and the numbers that keep you honest

```
⭐⭐ PARTITION COUNT — THE RULE OF THUMB:
⭐   ⭐ ***max(target throughput ÷ per-partition throughput,
⭐        desired consumer parallelism)***, ⭐ then round up
⭐     and add headroom.
⭐   ⭐ A partition sustains ~10 MB/s+ comfortably.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ 200 MB/s target and 20 consumers ⇒ max(20, 20) = 20,
⭐     ⭐ call it 30.

⭐⭐ AND THE COST OF TOO MANY, WHICH PEOPLE IGNORE:
⭐   ⭐ Each partition is open file handles, memory on every
⭐     broker, and ⭐⭐ LONGER LEADER ELECTION AFTER A BROKER
⭐     FAILURE. ⭐ Tens of thousands per cluster is a real
⭐     operational limit.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SO: ENOUGH TO PARALLELISE AND GROW, NOT "1000 JUST
⭐     IN CASE".

⭐⭐ THE ALERT THAT MATTERS: ⭐⭐ ***CONSUMER LAG IN TIME***
⭐   (Day 262, Day 328 — third appearance, one law). ⭐ Records
⭐   behind is meaningless; ⭐ "our consumer is 4 minutes behind"
⭐   is actionable.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND THE CRITICAL ONE: ***LAG APPROACHING RETENTION.***
⭐     ⭐ At that point you are not slow, ⭐⭐ YOU ARE ABOUT TO
⭐     SKIP DATA WITH NO ERROR ANYWHERE.
```

# Part 7 — ⭐ Kafka in Python, and when not to use it

```
⭐⭐ THE CLIENTS:
⭐   ⭐ `confluent-kafka` — librdkafka binding. ⭐⭐ FASTEST AND
⭐     MOST COMPLETE. Use it in production.
⭐   ⭐ `aiokafka` — native asyncio, pure Python. ⭐ Ergonomic in
⭐     an async service, slower.
⭐   ⭐ `kafka-python` — pure Python, ⭐ lagging on features.

⭐⭐ THE PYTHON-SPECIFIC TRAP, AND IT IS THE ONE THAT BITES:
⭐   ⭐⭐ A KAFKA CONSUMER IS A ***LONG-RUNNING PROCESS THAT
⭐     MUST POLL***. ⭐ Anything that blocks the loop — a slow
⭐     synchronous DB call inside an async consumer, a CPU-bound
⭐     parse, a `time.sleep` — ⭐⭐ DELAYS `poll()` AND
⭐     TRIGGERS PART 4'S REBALANCE SPIRAL.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Poll on one task, process on a bounded worker pool,
⭐     commit only what completed, ⭐⭐ AND USE `pause()` /
⭐     `resume()` AS YOUR BACKPRESSURE MECHANISM.

⭐⭐ WHEN ***NOT*** TO USE KAFKA — SAY THIS UNPROMPTED:
⭐   ⭐ ✘ Under a few thousand messages/second with no replay
⭐     need ⇒ ⭐⭐ POSTGRES OR SQS. Kafka is three ZooKeeper-or-
⭐     KRaft brokers, a schema registry, a monitoring stack and
⭐     an on-call rota.
⭐   ⭐ ✘ Per-message TTL, priorities, or complex routing ⇒
⭐     ⭐⭐ RABBITMQ. Kafka has none of these and faking them is
⭐     painful.
⭐   ⭐ ✘ Request/reply ⇒ that is HTTP (Day 333).
⭐   ⭐ ✘ Millions of low-volume topics ⇒ wrong shape entirely.
⭐   ⭐ ✔ ⭐⭐ USE IT FOR: HIGH VOLUME, REPLAY, MULTIPLE
⭐     INDEPENDENT CONSUMERS, RETENTION AS A FEATURE, AND
⭐     STREAM PROCESSING.
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ Assuming topic-wide ordering | Order exists inside a partition only. It looks fine with one partition in dev |
| ⭐⭐ Publishing with a null key | Round-robin, so ordering is silently gone and two consumers race the same entity |
| ⭐⭐ `acks=all` with `min.insync.replicas=1` | "All" can mean "one". A lie that looks safe on a dashboard |
| ⭐ `unclean.leader.election=true` | An out-of-sync replica becomes leader and committed messages vanish |
| ⭐⭐ `enable.auto.commit=true` | Commits on a timer regardless of progress. Neither at-least-once nor at-most-once, reliably |
| ⭐⭐ Long processing inside the poll loop | Exceeds `max.poll.interval.ms`, triggers a rebalance, offsets rejected, infinite redelivery |
| ⭐ Adding partitions to an existing topic | Rehashes keys and destroys the ordering guarantee for everything that moves |
| ⭐ Thousands of partitions "just in case" | File handles, broker memory, and slow leader election after a failure |
| ⭐⭐ Alerting on lag in records | Meaningless. Lag in time, and specifically lag approaching retention |
| ⭐ Falling behind retention silently | You don't error, you skip data. Nothing anywhere reports it |
| ⭐ Kafka for 50 messages a second | Three brokers, a registry and an on-call rota to replace one Postgres table |

## Interview questions

**1. "Where does Kafka guarantee ordering?"**
> ⭐⭐ **Inside a partition, and nowhere else** — there is no topic-wide order and there never was.
> ⭐ The key determines the partition, so the key is the ordering guarantee: all events for one order
> stay ordered relative to each other if I key by order id. ⭐⭐ And the trap is that a null key
> round-robins, **so forgetting the key removes ordering with no error and no test failure** — it only
> shows up when two consumers process the same entity's events out of order.

**2. "How many partitions?"**
> ⭐ The max of target throughput divided by per-partition throughput, and my desired consumer
> parallelism — because **one partition is consumed by at most one member of a group, so partitions
> are my parallelism ceiling.** ⭐⭐ Six partitions means a seventh consumer sits idle and scaling out
> stops working silently. ⭐ I'd over-provision modestly at creation, because adding partitions later
> rehashes keys and breaks existing ordering — ⭐ but not wildly, since each one costs file handles,
> broker memory and leader-election time.

**3. "Configure Kafka so you don't lose data."**
> ⭐ Replication factor 3, `min.insync.replicas=2`, `acks=all`, `unclean.leader.election=false`.
> ⭐⭐ The subtle one is the pairing: **`acks=all` with `min.insync=1` means "all" can be "one"**, which
> looks safe and isn't. ⭐ With 3 and 2 I survive one broker loss with no data loss and block writes
> when two are down — ⭐⭐ and I'd name that as a deliberate CP choice rather than a config accident.

**4. "Your consumer group keeps rebalancing and never progresses."**
> ⭐⭐ Classic: processing inside the poll loop exceeds `max.poll.interval.ms`, so the broker declares
> the member dead, rebalances, **and rejects the offset commit — so all the records are redelivered to
> a consumer that will also take too long.** ⭐ Fixes: fewer records per poll, a longer interval, or
> properly decoupling — poll on one task, process on a bounded pool, commit what finished, and use
> `pause`/`resume` for backpressure. ⭐ Plus cooperative sticky assignment so a rebalance doesn't stop
> the world.

**5. "What is log compaction for?"**
> ⭐ It keeps the latest value per key forever, so ⭐⭐ **a compacted topic is a table** — replay it
> from the beginning and you have the current state of every key, with a change feed attached. ⭐ It's
> the foundation of CDC and of materialising views from a topic, and deletion is a tombstone with a
> null value. ⭐ It's also the cleanest way to state the stream/table duality: a table is the
> accumulated state of a stream.

**6. "When would you not use Kafka?"**
> ⭐ Below a few thousand messages a second with no replay need — that's Postgres or SQS, and Kafka is
> a cluster, a schema registry, a monitoring stack and an on-call rota. ⭐ Per-message TTL, priorities
> or content routing is RabbitMQ. Request/reply is HTTP. ⭐⭐ **What Kafka is uniquely good at is
> replay, retention as a feature, and many independent consumers reading the same data at different
> speeds** — if I'm not using at least one of those, I'm paying for a log I don't need.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Correct the category first: **Kafka is not a queue, it's a partitioned append-only log
that consumers read at their own pace** — reading doesn't destroy, so N consumer groups read the same
data independently, and replay is a one-line offset reset, which is the single best reason to choose
it. Then the fact everything else hangs off: **ordering exists inside a partition and nowhere else**,
so the key is simultaneously your ordering guarantee, your load distribution and your parallelism
ceiling. Say the silent bug out loud: **a null key round-robins, so forgetting the key removes ordering
with no error and no failing test.** Give the durability triple as a CAP decision expressed in config —
**replication three, min-insync two, acks all** — and name the lie, which is that acks=all with
min-insync one means acks=one. Then the operational one, because it's the one you'll actually hit:
**processing inside the poll loop exceeds the max poll interval, the broker declares you dead, the
rebalance rejects your offset commit, and every record is redelivered to a consumer that will also
take too long** — so poll fast, process on a bounded pool, and pause partitions for backpressure. Close
on compaction — **a compacted topic is a table, and a table is the accumulated state of a stream** —
and on the alert that matters: lag in seconds, and lag approaching retention, because past that point
you don't error, you skip data.
