# Part 1 — Chaos engineering, done as an experiment

```
⭐⭐ IT IS NOT VANDALISM AND IT IS NOT A GAME DAY WITH A `kill`
⭐   COMMAND. ⭐ It is four steps, and the first one is the one
⭐   people skip:

⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***DEFINE STEADY STATE AS A ***METRIC***, NOT A FEELING.***
⭐    ⭐ "p99 under 400 ms and error rate under 0.1%, measured at
⭐      the edge." ⭐⭐ IF YOU CANNOT STATE IT AS A NUMBER YOU
⭐      CANNOT DETECT THE DAMAGE, AND THE EXPERIMENT IS JUST AN
⭐      OUTAGE YOU CAUSED.
⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***WRITE THE HYPOTHESIS DOWN BEFORE YOU START.***
⭐    ⭐ "If the recommendation service returns 500s, checkout
⭐      succeeds and p99 rises by less than 50 ms."
⭐    ⭐⭐ WRITING IT DOWN IS WHAT MAKES IT FALSIFIABLE, ⭐ and it
⭐      stops the "well, obviously that would happen"
⭐      rationalisation afterwards.
⭐ ③ ⭐ ***INJECT THE SMALLEST REALISTIC FAULT.***
⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***DECIDE THE ABORT CONDITION AND THE BLAST RADIUS
⭐    ***BEFORE*** YOU START.*** ⭐ "Abort if error rate exceeds
⭐      1% or after 10 minutes." ⭐ "One AZ, 5% of traffic."
⭐    ⭐⭐ AN EXPERIMENT WITH NO ABORT CONDITION IS AN INCIDENT
⭐      WITH A NICER NAME.

⭐ PROGRESSION: ⭐ staging → ⭐ production at 1% → ⭐ production
⭐   during business hours with the team watching → ⭐⭐ AUTOMATED
⭐   AND CONTINUOUS. ⭐ Skipping to the end is how chaos
⭐   engineering gets banned at a company.

⭐⭐ AND THE REFRAME THAT MAKES IT SELLABLE INTERNALLY: ***YOU ARE
⭐   NOT INTRODUCING RISK, YOU ARE ***RESCHEDULING*** IT.***
⭐   ⭐ The failure will happen. ⭐⭐ THE ONLY CHOICE IS WHETHER IT
⭐   HAPPENS AT 3 P.M. WITH THE TEAM WATCHING AND AN ABORT BUTTON
⭐   READY, OR AT 3 A.M. DURING A SALE.
```

# Part 2 — The fault menu, ranked by information per unit of risk

```
⭐ ① ⭐ ***KILL ONE INSTANCE / TASK.*** ⭐⭐ CHEAP, AND YOU SHOULD
⭐    ALREADY KNOW THE ANSWER. ⭐ If you do not, start here.
⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***ADD 200 ms OF LATENCY TO ONE DEPENDENCY. THIS IS THE
⭐    HIGHEST-VALUE EXPERIMENT THERE IS.***
⭐    ⭐ It finds every missing timeout at once (Day 398), ⭐ every
⭐      missing bulkhead, ⭐ and every place a slow dependency
⭐      consumes your whole worker pool.
⭐    ⭐⭐ AND IT IS FAR MORE REVEALING THAN KILLING THE DEPENDENCY,
⭐      BECAUSE A DEAD DEPENDENCY FAILS FAST AND A ***SLOW*** ONE
⭐      IS WHAT ACTUALLY TAKES YOU DOWN (DAY 336).
⭐ ③ ⭐ ***RETURN ERRORS FROM A DEPENDENCY*** — 500s, then 429s.
⭐    ⭐⭐ DOES YOUR RETRY BUDGET HOLD, OR DO YOU AMPLIFY?
⭐ ④ ⭐ ***EXHAUST A CONNECTION POOL*** (Day 387). ⭐⭐ WATCH
⭐    WHETHER THE SYMPTOM IS RECOGNISABLE FROM YOUR DASHBOARDS.
⭐ ⑤ ⭐ ***PARTITION TWO SERVICES*** — asymmetrically, so A can
⭐    reach B but not vice versa. ⭐⭐ THE NASTIEST REAL-WORLD
⭐    PARTITION AND ALMOST NOBODY TESTS IT.
⭐ ⑥ ⭐ ***KILL AN ENTIRE AZ*** (Day 397).
⭐ ⑦ ⭐⭐ ***AND THE PYTHON-SPECIFIC ONE ALMOST NOBODY RUNS:
⭐    `SIGSTOP` A PROCESS FOR 40 SECONDS, THEN `SIGCONT`.***
⭐    ⭐ That is Day 411's lock expiry, Day 402's rebalance, and
⭐      Day 412's false positive, ⭐⭐ ALL IN ONE COMMAND. ⭐ If your
⭐      system survives it, your fencing works. ⭐⭐ IF IT DOES
⭐      NOT, YOU HAVE JUST FOUND A DOUBLE-WRITE BUG THAT WOULD
⭐      OTHERWISE HAVE FOUND YOU.

⭐ ⭐ Tools: `toxiproxy` (latency and partition injection, easy),
⭐   ⭐ `tc netem`, ⭐ AWS FIS, ⭐ Chaos Mesh. ⭐⭐ START WITH
⭐   `toxiproxy` — IT IS AN AFTERNOON.
```

# Part 3 — Load shedding, with the counterintuitive part

```
⭐⭐ THE RULE FROM DAY 335, RESTATED: ***UNDER OVERLOAD, SERVING
⭐   SOME REQUESTS WELL BEATS SERVING ALL OF THEM BADLY*** —
⭐   ⭐ because at saturation every request times out and you get
⭐   0% success instead of 70%.

⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***SHED ON QUEUE DEPTH OR WAIT TIME, NOT ON CPU.***
⭐    ⭐ CPU is a lagging indicator and an I/O-bound Python
⭐      service saturates its workers at 30% CPU (Day 389).
⭐    ⭐⭐ THE HONEST SIGNAL IS "HOW LONG HAS THE OLDEST QUEUED
⭐      REQUEST BEEN WAITING" — ⭐ and if that exceeds the client's
⭐      timeout, ⭐⭐ REJECTING IT IS ***FREE***: NOBODY IS WAITING
⭐      FOR THE ANSWER ANY MORE.
⭐ ② ⭐ ***PRIORITY CLASSES.*** ⭐ Health checks and payments beat
⭐    recommendations and analytics. ⭐⭐ WITHOUT CLASSES YOU SHED
⭐    UNIFORMLY AND DROP THE PAYMENT.
⭐ ③ ⭐ ***SHED AT THE EDGE***, cheaply, before the request has
⭐    consumed a worker.
⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***AND THE COUNTERINTUITIVE ONE: UNDER OVERLOAD, SERVE
⭐    THE ***NEWEST*** REQUESTS FIRST (LIFO), NOT THE OLDEST.***
⭐    ⭐ Because the oldest requests have probably ***already
⭐      timed out on the client***, ⭐⭐ SO FIFO SPENDS YOUR
⭐      SCARCEST RESOURCE COMPUTING ANSWERS NOBODY WILL RECEIVE.
⭐    ⭐ LIFO under overload converts a queue that is uniformly
⭐      useless into one that is mostly useful.
⭐    ⭐⭐ IT FEELS UNFAIR AND IT IS STRICTLY BETTER, AND SAYING SO
⭐      IN AN INTERVIEW IS A STRONG SIGNAL.
⭐ ⑤ ⭐ ***DEGRADE RATHER THAN FAIL*** — the popular list instead
⭐    of personalised recommendations. ⭐⭐ A PRODUCT DECISION YOU
⭐    MUST GO AND GET IN ADVANCE.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ The senior-track conversation drill

> The roadmap promised "senior-track conversations". **These are them.** Each should sustain ten
> minutes without notes.

```
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***"HOW WOULD YOU MAKE THIS EXACTLY-ONCE?"***
⭐    ⭐ → It cannot be done end to end (Day 335). ⭐ At-least-once
⭐      delivery plus an idempotency key written in the ***same
⭐      transaction*** as the effect gives effectively-once.
⭐      ⭐⭐ NAME THE DEDUP WINDOW AND SAY WHY IT MUST OUTLAST A
⭐      HUMAN REPLAY.

⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***"HOW DO YOU KNOW YOUR REPLICAS/PROJECTIONS AGREE?"***
⭐    ⭐ → ⭐⭐ YOU MEASURE DIVERGENCE; YOU DO NOT ASSERT IT.
⭐      ⭐ A comparison job, a row-count check, a sampled diff, and
⭐      a metric. ⭐ Day 406, Day 337.

⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***"WHAT HAPPENS DURING A NETWORK PARTITION?"***
⭐    ⭐ → Per-operation, not per-system (Day 329). ⭐ "Reads stay
⭐      available and may be stale; writes to the minority side
⭐      are rejected." ⭐⭐ AND: MOST PARTITIONS ARE ACTUALLY A
⭐      THROTTLED CONTAINER (DAY 412).

⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***"HOW DO YOU ROLL THIS OUT?"***
⭐    ⭐ → Expand/contract, ⭐⭐ AND THE PRECONDITION THAT TWO
⭐      VERSIONS MUST COEXIST AGAINST ONE DATABASE AND ONE SET OF
⭐      QUEUES (DAY 371) — ⭐ including a message published by v2
⭐      and consumed by v1 after a rollback (Day 398's ninth
⭐      fallacy).

⭐ ⑤ ⭐⭐ ***"WHERE IS THE ORDERING GUARANTEE?"***
⭐    ⭐ → Per partition, per aggregate, per entity —
⭐      ⭐⭐ NEVER GLOBAL, AND "WE NEED GLOBAL ORDERING" IS ALMOST
⭐      ALWAYS "WE NEED PER-ENTITY ORDERING" SAID IMPRECISELY
⭐      (DAYS 401, 409).

⭐ ⑥ ⭐⭐ ***"WHAT FAILURE HAVE YOU NOT DESIGNED FOR?"***
⭐    ⭐ → ⭐⭐ ANSWER IT HONESTLY. ⭐ "A full regional outage — our
⭐      RTO would be four hours and we accepted that." ⭐⭐ THE
⭐      CANDIDATE WHO NAMES A REAL GAP AND ITS ACCEPTED COST IS
⭐      MORE CREDIBLE THAN THE ONE WHO CLAIMS THERE ISN'T ONE,
⭐      ⭐ every single time.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐⭐ The five proofs

> Day 379's and Day 397's rule again: **demonstrations, not claims.** Each produces a number, a
> timestamp or a screenshot.

```
⭐⭐ ① ***INJECT 200 ms INTO ONE DEPENDENCY AND SHOW IT DOES NOT
⭐    CASCADE.*** ⭐ The proof is the p99 of every service
⭐    ***above*** it, and the fact that your timeout budget
⭐    absorbed it instead of propagating it (Day 398).

⭐⭐ ② ***`SIGSTOP` A LOCK HOLDER PAST ITS TTL AND SHOW THE
⭐    FENCING TOKEN REJECTS ITS WRITE.*** ⭐ The proof is the
⭐    rejected write in the log ⭐⭐ AND THE ABSENCE OF A DUPLICATE
⭐    EFFECT (DAY 411).

⭐⭐ ③ ***KILL A BROKER UNDER LOAD AND SHOW ZERO MESSAGE LOSS.***
⭐    ⭐ Count produced and consumed. ⭐⭐ THIS ONLY PASSES IF
⭐    `min.insync.replicas=2` (DAY 401) AND YOUR PRODUCER FLUSHES
⭐    ON SIGTERM.

⭐⭐ ④ ***REBUILD A PROJECTION FROM THE LOG AND SHOW IT MATCHES —
⭐    BY MEASUREMENT.*** ⭐ Row counts and a sampled diff against
⭐    the live one. ⭐⭐ A DIVERGENCE OF ZERO IS THE PROOF; "IT
⭐    SHOULD MATCH" IS NOT (DAY 406).

⭐⭐ ⑤ ***REPLAY A DUPLICATE MESSAGE AND SHOW NOTHING DOUBLES.***
⭐    ⭐ Then replay it ***a week later*** ⭐⭐ AND SHOW THE DEDUP
⭐    WINDOW STILL HOLDS (DAY 335).
```

⭐    ⭐ A remote call succeeded, failed, or you cannot tell — and
⭐    the third case is the subject. ⭐⭐ ⇒ IDEMPOTENCY, READ-BACK,
⭐    OR COMPENSATION. THERE IS NO FOURTH ANSWER.
⭐    ⭐ (398 · 400's DEADLINE_EXCEEDED · 403 · 407 · 408)

⭐    ⭐ Per partition, per aggregate, per key. ⭐⭐ ⇒ AND
⭐    "WE NEED GLOBAL ORDERING" IS ALMOST ALWAYS A REQUIREMENT

⭐⭐ ③ ***CORRECTNESS LIVES IN THE RESOURCE, NOT IN THE
⭐    COORDINATOR.***
⭐    ⭐ The lock can be wrong; the storage cannot. ⭐⭐ ⇒ FENCING
⭐    TOKENS, UNIQUE CONSTRAINTS, CONDITIONAL WRITES, OPTIMISTIC
⭐    VERSIONS. ⭐ (411 · 405 · 407 · 402)

⭐⭐ ④ ***EVERY GUARANTEE IS A QUORUM INTERSECTION OR A TIMING
⭐    ASSUMPTION.***
⭐    ⭐ Raft's safety is two majorities overlapping; Raft's
⭐    ***liveness*** is a randomised timeout. ⭐⭐ ⇒ IF YOU CANNOT

⭐    IT GUARANTEES. ⭐ (410 · 403 · 412 · 398's FLP)

⭐⭐ ⑤ ***DERIVED STATE MUST BE REBUILDABLE, AND YOU MUST HAVE
⭐    REBUILT IT.***
⭐    ⭐ A rebuild procedure never run is a procedure that does
⭐    not work. ⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE MOMENT A PROJECTION HOLDS A FACT
⭐    THAT EXISTS NOWHERE ELSE, IT IS A SECOND SOURCE OF TRUTH.
⭐    ⭐ (405 · 406 · 403's COMPACTION · 359)

⭐⭐ ⑥ ***COMPENSATION IS A NEW FACT, NOT AN UNDO.***
⭐    ⭐ A refund is not an un-charge. ⭐⭐ ⇒ SO EVERY SAGA HAS A
⭐    PIVOT, EVERYTHING AFTER IT MUST BE RETRIABLE, AND SOME
⭐    THINGS CANNOT BE COMPENSATED AT ALL. ⭐ (408 · 405 · 358)

⭐⭐ AND THE MOVE THAT UNDERLIES HALF OF THEM (DAY 407): ***WHEN
⭐   TWO THINGS MUST HAPPEN TOGETHER AND ONLY ONE SYSTEM HAS
⭐   TRANSACTIONS, PUT BOTH IN THAT SYSTEM AND MAKE THE SECOND A
⭐   CONSEQUENCE OF THE FIRST.***

## 🎙️ Articulation drill — the stage close

**Three minutes.** Open with what the stage was about: **Stage 9 gave me the shapes — use a quorum,
use an outbox, use a saga. Stage 12 gave me the mechanisms, so I can say *why* a quorum is safe and
therefore when it isn't.** Then the six shapes, as the transferable content.

**The unknown outcome is the whole problem.** A remote call succeeded, failed, or you can't tell — and
you can never distinguish a lost request from a lost response, so there are exactly three answers:
idempotency, read-back, or compensation. **A system that hasn't chosen one has chosen silent
inconsistency.**

**Ordering is always per-something, never global** — per partition, per aggregate, per key — **and "we
need global ordering" is nearly always a requirement stated imprecisely.**

**Correctness lives in the resource, not the coordinator.** The lock can be wrong; the storage can't.
That's the fencing token, the unique constraint, the conditional write — **and it's why a unique
constraint on (job, date) beats every distributed lock: no TTL, no clock, correct under arbitrary
pauses.**

**Every guarantee is a quorum intersection or a timing assumption.** Raft's safety is two majorities
overlapping; its liveness is a randomised timeout. **If you can't name which one your system rests on,
you don't know what it guarantees.**

**Derived state must be rebuildable, and you must have rebuilt it** — a procedure never run is a
procedure that doesn't work.

**And compensation is a new fact, not an undo** — a refund isn't an un-charge, which is why every saga
has a pivot and everything after it must be retriable.

Then close on proof rather than claim: **I injected 200 ms into one dependency and it didn't cascade,
because the timeout budget absorbed it. I `SIGSTOP`-ed a lock holder past its TTL and the fencing token
rejected its write. I killed a broker under load with zero message loss. I rebuilt a projection from
the log with a measured divergence of zero. And I replayed a week-old duplicate and nothing doubled.**
Finish with the honest line: **the failure I haven't designed for is a full regional outage — our RTO
would be four hours and we accepted that** — because naming a real gap and its accepted cost is more
credible than claiming there isn't one.
