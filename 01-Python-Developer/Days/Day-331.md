# Day 331 🧊

**P-331** · 🧊 **Consistency models** — ⭐⭐ the four session guarantees, and what users actually notice

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: "strong" and "eventual" are the two ends of a ladder with useful rungs in
> between, and almost every product that thinks it needs linearizability actually needs the four
> session guarantees — which are dramatically cheaper, because they only promise consistency to the
> person who is looking.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ Two different words that both got called "consistency"

```
⭐⭐ UNTANGLE THIS BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE — INTERVIEWERS PROBE IT:
⭐   ⭐ ***ACID's C*** — the database will not let you violate a
⭐     constraint. It is about ***invariants*** and it is,
⭐     honestly, the least interesting letter in ACID.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ***DISTRIBUTED C*** — what a read may return given
⭐     concurrent writes on multiple copies. It is about
⭐     ***copies***.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND A THIRD, WHICH CAUSES MOST OF THE CONFUSION:
⭐     ***ISOLATION LEVELS*** (Day 189) are about concurrent
⭐     transactions on ***one*** copy.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Serializable ≠ linearizable. ⭐⭐ SERIALIZABLE SAYS
⭐     TRANSACTIONS APPEAR IN ***SOME*** ORDER; LINEARIZABLE
⭐     SAYS OPERATIONS APPEAR IN ***REAL-TIME*** ORDER.
⭐     ⭐ Both together is ***strict serializability***, which is
⭐     what Spanner sells and what people usually mean by
⭐     "strong consistency".
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The ladder, strongest to weakest

```
⭐⭐ ① ***LINEARIZABLE*** (atomic / strong)
⭐   ⭐ Every operation appears to take effect at a single
⭐     instant between its call and its return.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE TEST: IF A WRITE COMPLETES AND ***THEN*** A READ
⭐     BEGINS — even from a different client, on a different
⭐     node — THE READ SEES IT.
⭐   ⭐ Cost: a quorum round trip per operation, minimum.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ etcd, ZooKeeper, Spanner, a single Postgres primary.

⭐⭐ ② ***SEQUENTIAL***
⭐   ⭐ All nodes see all operations in the ***same*** order, but
⭐     that order need not match real time.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Cheaper. ⭐⭐ AND SUBTLY WRONG FOR HUMANS: you can post
⭐     a comment, phone a friend, and they legitimately still
⭐     don't see it — ⭐ because "real time" was the thing
⭐     dropped.

⭐⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***CAUSAL*** — THE SWEET SPOT.
⭐   ⭐ Operations that are causally related appear in order
⭐     everywhere. Concurrent ones may be seen in any order.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ QUESTIONS ALWAYS PRECEDE THEIR ANSWERS. A REPLY
⭐     NEVER APPEARS BEFORE THE THING IT REPLIES TO.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE STRONGEST MODEL ACHIEVABLE WITHOUT
⭐     GIVING UP AVAILABILITY UNDER PARTITION — a genuinely
⭐     important result, and worth saying.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Implemented with version vectors, Lamport/hybrid
⭐     logical clocks (Day 405).

⭐⭐ ④ ***EVENTUAL***
⭐   ⭐ If writes stop, all replicas eventually agree.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ IT PROMISES NOTHING WHATSOEVER ABOUT ***WHEN***, AND
⭐     NOTHING ABOUT WHAT YOU SEE ON THE WAY. ⭐ It is a
⭐     liveness property, not a safety one — ⭐ which is why
⭐     "eventually consistent" alone is never an answer.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The four session guarantees — the actual product answer

```
⭐⭐ THE INSIGHT: ***MOST "CONSISTENCY" COMPLAINTS ARE ABOUT
⭐   WHAT ***ONE USER*** SEES ACROSS ***THEIR OWN*** SEQUENCE OF
⭐   ACTIONS.*** ⭐ Nobody notices that another user's write
⭐   took 400 ms to reach them. ⭐⭐ EVERYBODY NOTICES THEIR OWN
⭐   EDIT VANISHING.

⭐⭐ ① ***READ YOUR WRITES*** — you see your own writes.
⭐      ⇒ ⭐ Without it: you save, reload, see the old value,
⭐        and save again. ⭐⭐ THE #1 CAUSE OF DUPLICATE
⭐        SUBMISSIONS.
⭐⭐ ② ***MONOTONIC READS*** — you never see time go backwards.
⭐      ⇒ ⭐ Without it: refresh shows a comment, refresh again
⭐        and it is gone. ⭐⭐ USERS FIND THIS MORE ALARMING
⭐        THAN BEING SIMPLY OUT OF DATE.
⭐⭐ ③ ***MONOTONIC WRITES*** — your writes apply in the order
⭐      you made them.
⭐      ⇒ ⭐ Without it: you set the name then the avatar; the
⭐        avatar lands first and the name write is lost to a
⭐        conflict resolver.
⭐⭐ ④ ***WRITES FOLLOW READS*** (session causality) — if you
⭐      read X and then write Y, everyone who sees Y sees X.
⭐      ⇒ ⭐ Without it: your reply appears above the message it
⭐        replies to. ⭐ This is Day 328's consistent-prefix
⭐        anomaly from the writer's side.

⭐⭐ THE PAYOFF, AND IT IS LARGE:
⭐   ***ALL FOUR CAN BE PROVIDED BY A ***CLIENT-SIDE TOKEN***
⭐      (a version, an LSN, a vector) CARRIED IN THE SESSION —
⭐      WITH NO GLOBAL COORDINATION AT ALL.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE SYSTEM STAYS EVENTUALLY CONSISTENT AND AVAILABLE.
⭐     ⭐ The ***session*** is consistent. ⭐⭐ THAT IS THE WHOLE
⭐     TRICK, AND IT IS WHY THIS DAY MATTERS MORE THAN CAP DOES.
```

```python
# ⭐⭐ ALL FOUR GUARANTEES, IN ~20 LINES
# ⭐ The token is a monotonic version the client carries.
async def read(key: str, session: Session) -> Value:
    node = pick_replica(session.pin)          # ⭐ ② monotonic reads
    v = await node.get(key)
    if v.version < session.seen.get(key, 0):  # ⭐⭐ ① READ-YOUR-WRITES
        v = await leader.get(key)             # ⭐ …or wait/retry
    session.seen[key] = max(session.seen.get(key, 0), v.version)
    return v

async def write(key: str, val: Value, session: Session) -> None:
    v = await leader.put(
        key, val,
        after=session.last_write_version,     # ⭐ ③ monotonic writes
        depends_on=dict(session.seen),        # ⭐⭐ ④ WRITES FOLLOW READS
    )
    session.last_write_version = v
    session.seen[key] = v
# ⭐⭐ NOTE WHAT IS ABSENT: ANY GLOBAL LOCK, QUORUM OR CONSENSUS.
# ⭐   The guarantees are per-session, so they cost per-session.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ What users actually notice — the perception table

```
⭐⭐ STALENESS BUDGETS FROM REAL PRODUCTS. ⭐ Memorise the
⭐   ***shape***, not the numbers:
⭐   ⭐ Like count on a post          ⇒ ⭐⭐ MINUTES. Nobody cares.
⭐   ⭐ Someone else's new post       ⇒ seconds to a minute.
⭐   ⭐ ***YOUR OWN*** new post       ⇒ ⭐⭐ ZERO. IMMEDIATELY.
⭐   ⭐ A chat message you sent       ⇒ ⭐⭐ ZERO, AND IN ORDER.
⭐   ⭐ Someone else's chat message   ⇒ ~1 s.
⭐   ⭐ Account balance after ***your*** transfer ⇒ ⭐⭐ ZERO.
⭐   ⭐ Account balance, general      ⇒ ⭐ often minutes, and
⭐     banks openly show "pending".
⭐   ⭐ Seat availability             ⇒ ⭐ stale is fine to
⭐     ***display***; ⭐⭐ THE BOOKING ITSELF MUST BE
⭐     LINEARIZABLE (Day 316).

⭐⭐ THE PATTERN, AND IT IS THE WHOLE DAY IN ONE LINE:
⭐   ***USERS DEMAND STRONG CONSISTENCY FOR ***THEIR OWN***
⭐      ACTIONS AND TOLERATE SECONDS OR MINUTES FOR EVERYONE
⭐      ELSE'S.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ WHICH IS EXACTLY WHAT THE SESSION GUARANTEES PROVIDE,
⭐     AND EXACTLY WHY LINEARIZABILITY IS ALMOST ALWAYS
⭐     OVERKILL.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ And the UI trick that buys the rest: ⭐⭐ OPTIMISTIC
⭐     RENDERING. Show the user's own action instantly from
⭐     local state, reconcile in the background, and the
⭐     staleness becomes invisible — ⭐ but you now owe a
⭐     visible, honest failure path for when reconciliation
⭐     fails, or you have simply moved the lie to the frontend.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐ Where to enforce which model

```
⭐⭐ A DECISION TABLE YOU CAN DEFEND:
⭐   ⭐ ***LINEARIZABLE*** ⇒ uniqueness (a username), locks and
⭐     leader election, inventory decrement, money movement,
⭐     ⭐⭐ ANY CHECK-THEN-ACT (Day 310 — and note this is the
⭐     same rule at a different scale).
⭐   ⭐ ***CAUSAL*** ⇒ messaging, comments, collaborative
⭐     documents, anything with reply-to semantics.
⭐   ⭐ ***SESSION GUARANTEES*** ⇒ ⭐⭐ EVERY USER-FACING CRUD
⭐     PATH YOU WILL EVER WRITE. This is the default.
⭐   ⭐ ***EVENTUAL*** ⇒ counters, analytics, recommendations,
⭐     search indexes, feeds, caches.

⭐⭐ AND THE RULE FOR MIXING THEM:
⭐   ***PUT THE LINEARIZABLE PART IN THE SMALLEST POSSIBLE BOX.***
⭐   ⭐ Day 316's seat booking: the ***hold*** is one
⭐     linearizable conditional UPDATE; the seat ***map*** is
⭐     cached and stale, and that is stated in the UI.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE ART IS NOT PICKING A MODEL FOR THE SYSTEM. IT IS
⭐     SHRINKING THE PART THAT NEEDS THE EXPENSIVE ONE UNTIL IT
⭐     IS ONE ROW AND ONE STATEMENT.
```

# Part 6 — ⭐⭐ Measuring consistency, so it stops being a vibe

```
⭐⭐ IF YOU SAY "EVENTUALLY CONSISTENT" YOU MUST BE ABLE TO
⭐   ANSWER: ***HOW EVENTUAL, AT p99, AND HOW DO YOU KNOW?***

⭐⭐ THREE MEASUREMENTS, ALL CHEAP:
⭐   ① ⭐ REPLICATION LAG, in ***time*** (Day 328).
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ END-TO-END CONVERGENCE: a canary writer stamps a
⭐      value every second; a canary reader on each replica
⭐      records when it appears. ⭐ Export p50/p99/max.
⭐      ⇒ ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE NUMBER TO PUT IN THE SLO, because it
⭐        includes the cache, the queue and the index — not
⭐        just the database.
⭐   ③ ⭐ ANOMALY COUNTERS: how many times per day did a read
⭐      return a version older than one this session already
⭐      saw? ⭐⭐ THAT IS MONOTONIC-READS VIOLATIONS, COUNTED,
⭐      AND MOST TEAMS HAVE NO IDEA WHAT THEIR NUMBER IS.

⭐⭐ AND THE TESTING TOOL WORTH NAMING: ***JEPSEN.*** ⭐ It
⭐   partitions a real cluster and checks histories against a
⭐   model. ⭐⭐ ITS RESULTS ARE WHY SEVERAL DATABASES' MARKETING
⭐   CLAIMS CHANGED. ⭐ You will not run it; knowing what it does
⭐   is the point.
```

# Part 7 — ⭐ The Python angle: where your ORM hides this

```
⭐⭐ THREE PLACES THE MODEL IS DECIDED WITHOUT YOU NOTICING:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ THE IDENTITY MAP (Day 301). Within one SQLAlchemy
⭐      session, reading the same row twice returns the ***same
⭐      object*** — ⭐ so you get read-your-writes ***inside the
⭐      session*** for free, and none at all across sessions.
⭐      ⇒ ⭐⭐ WHICH IS WHY A BUG "ONLY HAPPENS ON THE SECOND
⭐        REQUEST": the first request was consistent by
⭐        accident.
⭐   ② ⭐ `expire_on_commit=True` (the default) forces a reload
⭐      after commit — ⭐⭐ AND IF YOUR READ ROUTING SENDS THAT
⭐      RELOAD TO A REPLICA, YOU HAVE BUILT A READ-YOUR-WRITES
⭐      VIOLATION INTO YOUR ORM CONFIGURATION.
⭐   ③ ⭐ Any cache read (Day 326) is an eventual read wearing a
⭐      synchronous function signature. ⭐⭐ `get_user(id)` LOOKS
⭐      LINEARIZABLE AND IS NOT.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE HABIT WORTH BUILDING: ***NAME THE CONSISTENCY
⭐     MODEL IN THE FUNCTION NAME OR THE DOCSTRING.***
⭐     `get_user_cached()` vs `get_user_authoritative()`.
⭐     ⭐ Two words, and the entire class of bug becomes visible
⭐     at the call site.
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ Confusing ACID's C with CAP's C | Constraints versus copies. Completely different claims |
| ⭐⭐ Treating serializable and linearizable as synonyms | Serializable is *some* order; linearizable is *real-time* order. Together they're strict serializability |
| ⭐⭐ "Eventually consistent" with no number | A liveness property with no bound. Give a p99 convergence figure or say nothing |
| ⭐ Reaching for linearizability everywhere | A quorum round trip per operation, when session guarantees would have been enough |
| ⭐⭐ Ignoring the session guarantees entirely | They fix the four anomalies users actually notice, with no global coordination |
| ⭐ Assuming users notice everyone's staleness | They notice *their own*. Budget accordingly |
| ⭐⭐ Optimistic UI with no failure path | You moved the inconsistency to the frontend and called it fixed |
| ⭐ Making the whole system strongly consistent | The skill is shrinking the strong part to one row and one statement |
| ⭐⭐ Replica reads after commit with `expire_on_commit` | Your ORM config is now a read-your-writes violation |
| ⭐ Cache reads that look like database reads | `get_user()` reads eventual data through a synchronous signature. Name it |
| ⭐ Never measuring convergence | If you can't state p99 convergence, you don't know your own model |

## Interview questions

**1. "What's the difference between linearizable and serializable?"**
> ⭐ Serializable is an isolation property — transactions appear in *some* serial order. Linearizable
> is a recency property — a single operation appears to happen at one instant in **real time**, so a
> read that starts after a write completes must see it. ⭐⭐ Serializable alone permits a transaction to
> read a stale snapshot; **both together is strict serializability**, which is what people usually mean
> by "strong consistency" and what Spanner sells.

**2. "Explain the session guarantees."**
> ⭐ Read-your-writes, monotonic reads, monotonic writes, and writes-follow-reads. ⭐⭐ The reason they
> matter is economic: **all four can be delivered with a version token carried in the client session,
> with no global coordination at all** — the system stays eventually consistent and available, and the
> *session* is consistent. ⭐ And they map exactly onto the four things users actually notice: their
> edit vanishing, time running backwards, their writes reordering, and their reply appearing above the
> message.

**3. "How stale can a feed be?"**
> ⭐ Someone else's post, tens of seconds — nobody notices. ⭐⭐ **Your own post, zero, always** —
> that's the whole asymmetry, and it's why session guarantees beat linearizability for feeds. ⭐ The
> like count can be minutes stale. ⭐ I'd deliver the user's own item optimistically from local state
> and reconcile in the background, with a visible failure path so I'm not just relocating the lie.

**4. "What's the strongest model you can have while staying available under partition?"**
> ⭐⭐ Causal consistency. That's a real result and worth stating: causally related operations appear
> in order everywhere, concurrent ones may be seen in either order, and it survives partitions. ⭐ In
> practice it's version vectors or hybrid logical clocks, and it's what you want for chat, comments
> and collaborative editing — **because the property users care about is that a reply never precedes
> the thing it replies to.**

**5. "Where would you demand linearizability?"**
> ⭐ Uniqueness, locks and leader election, inventory decrement, money movement — ⭐⭐ in general, **any
> check-then-act**, which is Day 310's rule at a different scale. ⭐ And I'd add the design move: put
> the linearizable part in the smallest possible box. In seat booking, the hold is one conditional
> update on one row; the seat map is cached and stale and the UI says so.

**6. "How do you know your system is as consistent as you claim?"**
> ⭐ Three measurements. Replication lag in time, not bytes. ⭐⭐ A canary writer and per-replica canary
> readers giving **end-to-end p99 convergence, which includes the cache, the queue and the index** —
> that's the number that belongs in an SLO. ⭐ And anomaly counters: how many reads per day returned a
> version older than one this session already saw. ⭐ Most teams have never measured the third.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Start by untangling the word, because three different things are called consistency:
**ACID's C is about constraints, isolation levels are about concurrent transactions on one copy, and
distributed consistency is about what a read may return when there are several copies** — and
serializable is not linearizable, since one is *some* order and the other is *real-time* order. Then
walk the ladder quickly, and stop on causal: **it's the strongest model you can have while staying
available under partition**, which is why chat and comments live there. Then make the argument that
actually changes designs: **most consistency complaints are about what one user sees across their own
sequence of actions** — nobody notices someone else's write taking four hundred milliseconds, everybody
notices their own edit vanishing. Name the four session guarantees and the symptom of each, and give
the economics: **all four come from a version token carried in the session, with no global
coordination**, so the system stays available and the session stays consistent. Close on the design
rule and the measurement. The rule: **shrink the linearizable part until it's one row and one
statement** — in seat booking, the hold is a conditional update and the seat map is openly stale. The
measurement: **a canary writer and per-replica readers giving p99 end-to-end convergence**, because
"eventually" without a number is not an answer.
