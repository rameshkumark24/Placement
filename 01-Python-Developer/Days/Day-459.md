# Part 2 — The confidence ladder

```
⭐ ① ***OFFLINE EVAL*** (Days 425/435) — ⭐ necessary, ⭐⭐ NEVER
⭐   SUFFICIENT. ⭐ Your eval set is 150 cases; production is a
⭐   distribution you did not sample. ⭐⭐ IT CATCHES BREAKAGE,
⭐   NOT DRIFT.

⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***SHADOW.*** ⭐ Run the new config on ***real
⭐   production traffic***, discard the output, compare.
⭐   ⭐ CATCHES: ⭐⭐ COST, LATENCY, SCHEMA FAILURE RATE, REPAIR
⭐     RATE, ABSTENTION SHIFT, OUTPUT-LENGTH SHIFT, ERROR RATES
⭐     — ⭐ on your ***actual*** input distribution, including
⭐     the 200-page PDF you never put in the eval set.
⭐   ⭐ MISSES: ⭐ user reaction. ⭐ Costs 2× on the shadowed
⭐     fraction, so shadow 5–10%, not everything.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THIS RUNG IS SKIPPED CONSTANTLY AND IT IS THE BEST
⭐     VALUE ON THE LADDER: ⭐ full production realism, ***zero
⭐     user risk***.

⭐ ③ ***CANARY*** — 1–5% of real users. ⭐ Now user-visible.
⭐   ⭐ Watch the online proxies (Day 455), ⭐⭐ NOT "ACCURACY" —
⭐     ⭐ you have no labels in production.

⭐ ④ ***A/B*** — ⭐ when you need to compare two ***good***
⭐   options rather than detect a regression. ⭐ Part 4.

⭐ ⑤ ***FULL ROLLOUT***, ⭐ with the kill switch still wired.
```

# Part 3 — What moves, and when

```
⭐ ***THE TIMESCALES ARE DIFFERENT BY ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE, AND
⭐   PEOPLE READ THE SLOW SIGNALS TOO EARLY.***

⭐ ***MINUTES*** — ⭐ deterministic, high volume, trustworthy:
⭐   ⭐ cost per request, latency, ⭐⭐ SCHEMA VALIDITY, REPAIR
⭐     RATE, `finish_reason == length`, ⭐ output length
⭐     distribution, ⭐⭐ ABSTENTION RATE.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ IF ANY OF THESE MOVES, STOP. ⭐ You do not need
⭐     statistics for a repair rate going 2% → 11%.

⭐ ***HOURS*** — ⭐ behavioural, noisier, the real quality signal:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ REPHRASE RATE, ESCALATION RATE, ⭐ citation
⭐     click-through, ⭐ abandonment.

⭐ ***DAYS TO WEEKS*** — ⭐ business: retention, deflection rate,
⭐   ticket volume. ⭐⭐ TOO SLOW TO GATE A ROLLOUT ON, ⭐ and the
⭐   thing that actually matters — ⭐⭐ SO THEY ARE A ***REVIEW***
⭐   SIGNAL, NOT A ***RELEASE*** SIGNAL. ⭐ Say that explicitly
⭐   or someone will ask you to wait two weeks per prompt tweak.

⭐ ***AND THE ONE THAT NEEDS NO DEPLOY AT ALL:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE PROVIDER CHANGES THE MODEL UNDER A STABLE NAME.
⭐     ⭐ You shipped nothing and behaviour moved.
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ pin model ***versions*** wherever the provider offers
⭐     them, ⭐⭐ AND RUN THE NIGHTLY EVAL AGAINST PRODUCTION
⭐     CONFIG (D455) — ⭐ it is the only detector you have for a
⭐     change you did not make.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ The statistics people skip

```
⭐ ***THE SCENE:*** ⭐ "we A/B tested the new prompt on 200
⭐   requests, satisfaction went from 71% to 74%, ship it."
⭐   ⭐⭐ THAT DIFFERENCE IS NOISE AND THE TEAM IS ABOUT TO SHIP A
⭐     COIN FLIP.

⭐ ***THE INTUITION YOU NEED, ROUGHLY:***
⭐   ⭐ to detect a ***3-point*** change around 70%, at 95%
⭐     confidence with 80% power, ⭐⭐ YOU NEED ROUGHLY 3,500
⭐     SAMPLES ***PER ARM***.
⭐   ⭐ to detect ***1 point***: ⭐⭐ ~30,000 PER ARM.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ SMALLER EFFECTS COST QUADRATICALLY MORE TRAFFIC, ⭐ so
⭐     halving the effect you want to detect quadruples the
⭐     sample.

⭐ ⇒ ***THE PRACTICAL CONSEQUENCES:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ MOST TEAMS CANNOT A/B TEST QUALITY AT ALL — ⭐ they
⭐     do not have the traffic. ⭐⭐ SAYING SO IS HONEST, NOT
⭐     DEFEATIST.
⭐   ⭐ ② ⇒ ⭐⭐ LEAN ON THE OFFLINE EVAL (WHERE YOU ***HAVE***
⭐     LABELS AND CAN RUN 150 PAIRED CASES) ⭐ and use production
⭐     only to detect ***breakage***, which is a large effect and
⭐     needs little data.
⭐   ⭐ ③ ***PAIRED COMPARISON BEATS A/B*** when you can do it —
⭐     ⭐⭐ RUN BOTH CONFIGS ON THE ***SAME*** INPUTS AND COMPARE
⭐     PER-CASE. ⭐ Removes input variance, which is most of the
⭐     noise, and cuts the sample size dramatically.
⭐     ⭐⭐ THAT IS EXACTLY WHAT SHADOW MODE GIVES YOU FOR FREE.
⭐   ⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ NEVER PEEK-AND-STOP. ⭐ Checking every hour and
⭐     stopping when it looks good manufactures significance out
⭐     of pure noise. ⭐ Fix the sample size in advance.
```

# Part 5 — Gates, switches and the loop that closes

```
⭐ ***THE CI GATE*** (Days 425/435/457) — ⭐ what blocks a merge:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ `leak_count == 0` — HARD, NO TOLERANCE BAND;
⭐   ⭐ `recall@k` ≥ last release − 0.02;
⭐   ⭐ `faithfulness` ≥ 0.95;
⭐   ⭐ `abstention` ≥ 0.80 on the unanswerable set;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ MEAN COST PER CASE ≤ +15%;
⭐   ⭐ p95 latency ≤ budget.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ NOTE THE TWO KINDS: ⭐ security invariants are
⭐     absolute; quality metrics get a band above measured
⭐     run-to-run noise, ⭐ or sampling variance blocks your
⭐     builds and the team learns to ignore the gate.

⭐ ***KILL SWITCHES*** — ⭐ per feature, per tenant, and one that
⭐   ⭐⭐ DROPS THE WHOLE FEATURE TO THE NON-AI PATH (D456 RUNG
⭐   ⑥). ⭐ Test them on a normal Tuesday.

⭐   ⭐ every canary failure and every rollback ⭐⭐ BECOMES AN
⭐     EVAL CASE (D435/455), ⭐ desensitised at capture (D450);
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE GATE THAT DID NOT CATCH IT THIS TIME WILL CATCH

⭐     THE EVAL SET IS THE ASSET AND THE PROMPT IS NOT.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. "How do you ship a prompt change?"**

⭐ Offline eval gate, then shadow, then a canary, then full — with the kill switch left wired. ⭐⭐ The
offline eval is necessary and never sufficient, because 150 cases aren't the production distribution;
it catches breakage, not drift. ⭐ Shadow is the rung people skip and it's the best value on the
ladder: run the new config on real production traffic, throw the output away, and compare cost, latency,
schema failure rate, repair rate and abstention on my *actual* inputs — including the 200-page PDF that
was never in my eval set. ⭐⭐ Full production realism, zero user risk, at two times cost on the
shadowed fraction. ⭐ Then 1 to 5% of users, watching the fast proxies rather than "accuracy", because
production has no labels.

**2. "Which signals do you watch, and how fast?"**

⭐ Three timescales, and reading the slow ones too early is a common mistake. ⭐⭐ Minutes: cost,
latency, schema validity, repair rate, `finish_reason == length`, output length distribution and
abstention rate — deterministic and high volume, so a repair rate going from two percent to eleven needs
no statistics at all, it just means stop. ⭐ Hours: rephrase rate, escalation rate, citation
click-through — the real quality signals, noisier. ⭐⭐ Days to weeks: retention and deflection, which
matter most and are far too slow to gate a rollout on. ⭐ I'd say that explicitly, or someone will ask
me to wait two weeks per prompt tweak.

**3. "How big does an A/B test need to be?"**

⭐ Much bigger than people assume, and this is where a lot of teams fool themselves. ⭐⭐ To detect a
three-point change around seventy percent at normal confidence and power you need roughly three and a
half thousand samples **per arm**; for one point it's about thirty thousand. ⭐ Effects get
quadratically more expensive to detect, so halving the effect quadruples the traffic. ⭐⭐ Which means
most teams can't A/B test quality at all — they don't have the traffic — and saying that is honest
rather than defeatist. ⭐ So I lean on the offline eval, where I have labels and can run paired cases,
and use production to detect breakage, which is a large effect that needs little data. ⭐⭐ And paired
comparison beats A/B whenever it's possible: run both configs on the *same* inputs and compare per case.
That removes input variance, which is most of the noise — and it's exactly what shadow mode gives me for
free.

**4. "What's the most dangerous change to ship?"**

⭐ A chunking or embedding-model change, and it's dangerous for a reason that isn't obvious. ⭐⭐ It's
not that the change is risky — it's that the **rollback** is a reindex, minutes to hours, so a bad
decision is expensive to undo. ⭐ Everything else in the system is configuration: a prompt, k, the
rerank floor, temperature, even the model roll back in one second because they're a content-hashed
config value. ⭐⭐ So I rank changes by rollback cost rather than by how risky they feel, and the two
slow rows get the caution I save everywhere else — an alias swap into a new index, evaluated before any
traffic sees it, with the old index kept for a week.

**5. "Behaviour changed and you didn't deploy anything."**

⭐ That's the provider updating the model behind a stable name, and it's a real class of incident with no
change of mine to bisect. ⭐⭐ Two defences: pin model *versions* wherever the provider offers them, and
run the nightly eval against **production config**. ⭐ That nightly run is the only detector I have for
a change I didn't make, and it's also the only measurement in the whole stack that actually knows the
correct answer. ⭐⭐ Output length distribution is the fastest proxy that catches it in between runs.

**6. "What's in the CI gate?"**

⭐ Cross-tenant leak count at hard zero with no tolerance band, recall within two points of the last
release, faithfulness above 0.95, abstention above 0.8 on the unanswerable set, mean cost per case
within fifteen percent, and p95 latency under budget. ⭐⭐ The important structural point is that there
are two kinds of gate: security invariants are absolute, and quality metrics get a band above measured
run-to-run noise — because if sampling variance blocks builds, the team learns to ignore the gate, and
then it protects nothing. ⭐ And every canary failure and rollback becomes a new eval case, desensitised
at capture. ⭐⭐ That's the mechanism by which the system gets more reliable over months, and it's why
the eval set is the asset rather than the prompt.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

⭐⭐ **Three minutes, out loud: "how do you ship changes to a system you can't unit-test?"**

Open with the organising idea, because it's not the one they expect. **I rank changes by rollback cost
rather than by how risky they feel. Almost everything in this system is configuration — the prompt, k,
the rerank floor, temperature, even the model — and because the task config is content-hashed, rollback
is one value and it's total. One second, no rebuild, no migration. That makes aggressive iteration safe.
But a chunking change or an embedding model change rolls back through a reindex, which is forty minutes
for us. So those two rows get an alias swap, evaluated before any traffic, with the old index kept for a
week — and they get the caution I save everywhere else.**

Then the ladder. **Offline eval, shadow, canary, full, kill switch still wired. The offline eval is
necessary and never sufficient — a hundred and fifty cases aren't the production distribution, so it
catches breakage rather than drift. Shadow is the rung people skip and it's the best value on the
ladder: run the new config on real production traffic, discard the output, and compare cost, latency,
schema validity, repair rate and abstention on the actual input distribution — including the
two-hundred-page PDF that was never in the eval set. Full realism, zero user risk, two times cost on the
shadowed slice.**

Then the timescales, which is where people misread their own data. **Cost, latency, schema validity,
repair rate and abstention move in minutes and are trustworthy — a repair rate going from two percent to
eleven needs no statistics, it just means stop. Rephrase and escalation rate take hours. Retention and
deflection take weeks, and they're the ones that matter most, which is exactly why they're a review
signal rather than a release signal.**

Close on the statistics and the loop. **And I'd be honest about A/B testing: detecting a three-point
change around seventy percent needs roughly three and a half thousand samples per arm, and one point
needs thirty thousand. Most teams don't have that traffic, so "we tested on two hundred requests and it
went from 71 to 74" is shipping a coin flip. So I lean on paired offline evals where I have labels, and
use production to detect breakage, which is a large effect needing little data — and shadow mode gives
me paired comparison for free, because both configs see the same inputs. Then every canary failure and
every rollback becomes a new eval case. That's how the gate that missed it this time catches it next
time, and it's why the eval set is the asset and the prompt isn't.**
