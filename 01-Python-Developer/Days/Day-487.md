# Day 487 📊

**P-487** · 📊 **Levelling** — ⭐⭐ scope, not difficulty

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: level is not a measure of how hard the code you can write is — it is a
> measure of how large and how ambiguous a problem you can be handed and still produce a good outcome,
> which is why a brilliant engineer who has only ever been given well-specified tasks levels below a
> mediocre one who has owned something end to end.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ The axis that actually decides it

```
⭐ ***THE ASSUMPTION:*** ⭐ senior means "writes harder code" or
⭐   "knows more".
⭐ ***THE REALITY — THE LADDER IS ABOUT ***SCOPE AND
⭐   AMBIGUITY***:***

⭐  ┌──────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────┐
⭐  │ ***JUNIOR***  │ ⭐ given a ***task***. "Add this        │
⭐  │          │ endpoint." ⭐⭐ SOMEONE ELSE DECIDED WHAT   │
⭐  │          │ AND WHY.                                  │
⭐  │ ***MID***     │ ⭐ given a ***feature***. "Users need to│
⭐  │          │ export their data." ⭐⭐ YOU DECIDE HOW.    │
⭐  │ ***SENIOR***  │ ⭐ given a ***problem***. "Support cost │
⭐  │          │ is too high." ⭐⭐ YOU DECIDE ***WHAT TO    │
⭐  │          │ BUILD***, INCLUDING WHETHER TO BUILD      │
⭐  │          │ ANYTHING.                                 │
⭐  │ ***STAFF***   │ ⭐ given an ***area***. ⭐⭐ YOU DECIDE   │
⭐  │          │ WHICH PROBLEMS ARE WORTH SOLVING AND      │
⭐  │          │ CONVINCE OTHER TEAMS.                     │
⭐  └──────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────┘

⭐ ⭐⭐ THE UNCOMFORTABLE COROLLARY: ⭐ an engineer who writes
⭐   beautiful code but has only ever been handed well-specified
⭐   tasks ⭐⭐ LEVELS BELOW ONE WHO WRITES ADEQUATE CODE AND HAS
⭐   OWNED A PROBLEM END TO END. ⭐ That feels unfair and it is
⭐   consistent, ⭐⭐ BECAUSE THE THING THAT IS SCARCE IN ANY
⭐   ENGINEERING ORGANISATION IS NOT PEOPLE WHO CAN IMPLEMENT — IT
⭐   IS PEOPLE WHO CAN DECIDE ***WHAT*** TO IMPLEMENT AND BE
⭐   RIGHT.

⭐ ***AND THE SECOND AXIS, WHICH RUNS ALONGSIDE:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ***HOW MUCH ***OTHER PEOPLE'S*** OUTPUT DO YOU
⭐     AFFECT?*** ⭐ Reviews, design docs, a pattern others adopt,
⭐     a junior who became productive faster because of you.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ SENIORITY IS PARTLY MEASURED IN WORK YOU DID NOT DO
⭐     YOURSELF, ⭐ and candidates almost never mention this.
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ Task-scope stories versus problem-scope stories

```
⭐ ***THE SAME PROJECT, TOLD TWO WAYS. ⭐⭐ ONE LEVELS YOU AT MID
⭐   AND ONE AT SENIOR, AND THE FACTS ARE IDENTICAL.***

⭐ ❌ ***TASK-SCOPE:*** ⭐ "I was asked to build a RAG system over
⭐   our documentation. ⭐ I used FastAPI and pgvector, chunked
⭐   the documents, and got recall to 0.91."
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ EVERY VERB IS AN IMPLEMENTATION VERB. ⭐ Someone else
⭐     decided a RAG system was the answer. ⭐⭐ THE DEBRIEF WRITES
⭐     "SOLID EXECUTION" — WHICH IS A MID SIGNAL.

⭐ ✅ ***PROBLEM-SCOPE:*** ⭐ "Support agents were spending twenty
⭐   minutes finding answers that already existed. ⭐⭐ I SPENT THE
⭐   FIRST WEEK READING ACTUAL TICKETS RATHER THAN BUILDING, ⭐ and
⭐   found a third of them were aggregations — ***counts and
⭐   totals*** — which retrieval fundamentally cannot answer.
⭐   ⭐⭐ SO I SPLIT THE PROBLEM: A ROUTER, RETRIEVAL FOR LOOKUP
⭐   QUESTIONS, AND TEXT-TO-SQL FOR THE REST. ⭐ Recall on the
⭐   retrieval half is 0.91, and the SQL half wouldn't have
⭐   worked at all under the original framing."
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ SAME PROJECT. ⭐ But now: you questioned the brief, you
⭐     gathered evidence before building, ⭐⭐ AND YOU CHANGED WHAT
⭐     GOT BUILT. ⭐ The debrief writes "defined the problem, not
⭐     just the solution" — ⭐⭐ WHICH IS THE SENIOR SIGNAL.

⭐ ***THE FIVE PHRASES THAT ***DEMONSTRATE*** SCOPE:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ "BEFORE BUILDING, I WENT AND LOOKED AT…"
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ "THE BRIEF SAID X, BUT THE DATA SAID…"
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ "I DECIDED ***NOT*** TO BUILD…" ⭐ ← the strongest one
⭐     available, because deciding not to build something is the
⭐     purest form of owning a problem
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ "I CONVINCED [PERSON] BY…"
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ "THE TRADE-OFF I CHOSE, AND WHAT IT COST US, WAS…"

⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE HONESTY CONSTRAINT (DAY 463): ⭐ do not narrate a
⭐   problem-scope story you did not live. ⭐⭐ IF YOU WERE HANDED
⭐   A SPEC, SAY SO — AND THEN SAY WHAT YOU ***DID*** DECIDE
⭐   INSIDE IT. ⭐ There is almost always something: a rejected
⭐   approach, a scope cut, a thing you measured that nobody
⭐   asked you to measure.
```

# Part 3 — Reading the ladder you are being measured against

```
⭐ ***"SENIOR" MEANS DIFFERENT THINGS AND THE GAP IS ENORMOUS:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AT A 30-PERSON STARTUP: ⭐ 3–4 years, ships features
⭐     alone, ⭐ nobody has time to specify anything.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AT A LARGE TECH COMPANY: ⭐ 7–10 years, ⭐ owns a
⭐     system, ⭐ influences a roadmap, ⭐⭐ AND IS EXPECTED TO
⭐     MENTOR.
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE SAME PERSON IS "SENIOR" AT ONE AND "MID" AT THE
⭐     OTHER, WITH NO CHANGE IN ABILITY. ⭐ Titles are not
⭐     transferable currency and treating them as such causes
⭐     avoidable disappointment.

⭐ ***HOW TO FIND OUT ***BEFORE*** THE LOOP*** — ⭐ and this is a
⭐   normal question, not a presumptuous one:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ "WHAT LEVEL IS THIS ROLE MAPPED TO, AND WHAT WOULD
⭐     SOMEONE AT THAT LEVEL BE EXPECTED TO OWN IN THEIR FIRST
⭐     YEAR?"
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE SECOND HALF IS THE USEFUL HALF. ⭐ "Own a service"
⭐     versus "own a domain" versus "close well-defined tickets"
⭐     tells you exactly which stories to lead with.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐ Many companies publish their engineering ladder. ⭐⭐ READ
⭐     IT AND MAP YOUR STORIES ONTO THEIR VOCABULARY — ⭐ it is
⭐     free calibration.

⭐ ***AND THE SIGNALS THEY WILL PROBE FOR SENIOR SPECIFICALLY:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ DID YOU CHANGE YOUR MIND WITH EVIDENCE? ⭐ (Day 468's
⭐     deletions.)
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ CAN YOU NAME WHAT YOUR DESIGN COST? ⭐ (The `price`
⭐     field.)
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ HAVE YOU SAID NO TO SOMETHING, AND HOW?
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ DO YOU KNOW YOUR SYSTEM'S LIMITS WITHOUT LOOKING THEM
⭐     UP? ⭐ (Day 471's six-clause sentence.)
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Down-levelling is a decision, not an insult

```
⭐ ***THE SITUATION:*** ⭐ you applied for senior; ⭐ they offer
⭐   mid, or "senior with a lower band than expected".

⭐ ***WHAT IT USUALLY MEANS — ⭐⭐ AND ONLY ONE OF THESE IS ABOUT
⭐   YOUR ABILITY:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THEIR LADDER IS CALIBRATED DIFFERENTLY ⭐ (Part 3);
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ YOUR STORIES WERE TASK-SCOPE ⭐ (Part 2) — ⭐ a
⭐     ***fixable presentation problem***, and the most common
⭐     cause;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ONE ROUND WAS WEAK ⭐ and they are hedging rather than
⭐     rejecting;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE HEADCOUNT IS BUDGETED AT THAT LEVEL ⭐ and has
⭐     nothing to do with you at all.

⭐ ***THE FOUR QUESTIONS TO ASK — AND ASK THEM CALMLY, BECAUSE
⭐   THE ANSWERS ARE GENUINELY INFORMATIVE:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ "WHAT WOULD I NEED TO DEMONSTRATE TO BE AT THE NEXT
⭐     LEVEL?" ⭐ A good company answers concretely. ⭐⭐ A VAGUE
⭐     ANSWER IS ITSELF DATA — IT USUALLY MEANS PROMOTION IS
⭐     POLITICAL RATHER THAN CRITERIA-BASED.
⭐   ⭐ ② ***"How long does a promotion typically take, and how
⭐     many people did it last cycle?"*** ⭐⭐ THE SECOND NUMBER IS
⭐     THE HONEST ONE.
⭐   ⭐ ③ ***"Is the level or the compensation the constraint?"***
⭐     ⭐ Sometimes they can pay senior money at a mid title,
⭐     ⭐⭐ AND SOMETIMES THE TITLE IS FIXED AND THE MONEY IS NOT.
⭐   ⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ "WHICH SIGNAL WAS WEAKEST?" ⭐ Even if you accept,
⭐     this tells you what to work on.

⭐ ***WHEN ACCEPTING IS RIGHT:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE WORK IS GENUINELY BIGGER THAN YOUR CURRENT SCOPE
⭐     ⭐ — a title is a label and a year of larger problems is an
⭐     actual change in what you can claim next time;
⭐   ⭐ the promotion criteria are concrete and the cycle is
⭐     short;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE COMPENSATION IS ACCEPTABLE REGARDLESS OF THE
⭐     TITLE.

⭐ ***WHEN IT IS NOT:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE WORK IS ***SMALLER*** THAN YOUR CURRENT SCOPE —
⭐     ⭐ you will be bored, you will not build new stories, ⭐⭐ AND
⭐     YOU WILL LEVEL ***DOWN*** FOR THE NEXT SEARCH TOO;
⭐   ⭐ the criteria are vague or the cycle is annual and
⭐     capped;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ YOU ARE ACCEPTING BECAUSE YOU ARE TIRED OF
⭐     INTERVIEWING, ⭐ which is the most expensive reason there
⭐     is and the most common one.

⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE FRAMING THAT KEEPS IT USEFUL: ⭐ a level is a bet
⭐   the company is making about what you can handle. ⭐⭐ IF THE
⭐   BET IS LOW AND THE WORK IS BIG, TAKE IT AND RESET IT IN A
⭐   YEAR. ⭐ If the bet is low ***because*** the work is small,
⭐   the title is the least of the problem.
```

# Part 5 — Building scope from where you are

```
⭐ ***THE TRAP:*** ⭐ "I can't get senior roles because I've only
⭐   had task-scope work, ⭐⭐ AND I CAN'T GET PROBLEM-SCOPE WORK
⭐   WITHOUT A SENIOR ROLE." ⭐ It is a real loop and it is
⭐   escapable.

⭐ ***FOUR WAYS OUT, ALL AVAILABLE WITHOUT A PROMOTION:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***MEASURE SOMETHING NOBODY ASKED YOU TO MEASURE.***
⭐     ⭐ Cost per request. The p99 nobody looked at. The
⭐     abstention rate. ⭐⭐ THAT IS AN OWNERSHIP STORY AND IT
⭐     REQUIRES NOBODY'S PERMISSION — ⭐ it is the cheapest scope
⭐     available and almost nobody takes it.
⭐   ⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***WRITE THE DESIGN DOC FOR THE THING YOU WERE
⭐     HANDED.*** ⭐ Even if it was specified for you, ⭐⭐ WRITING
⭐     THE ALTERNATIVES AND THE REJECTED OPTIONS TURNS A TASK
⭐     INTO A DECISION YOU CAN DEFEND (D468).
⭐   ⭐ ③ ***say no to something, in writing, with reasoning.***
⭐     ⭐⭐ ONE ARGUED SCOPE CUT IS A BETTER SENIOR STORY THAN TEN
⭐     DELIVERED FEATURES.
⭐   ⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***AFFECT SOMEONE ELSE'S OUTPUT.*** ⭐ A review that
⭐     changed a design. A pattern three people adopted. ⭐ A
⭐     runbook that stopped a recurring page.

⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE THING THIS TRACK ITSELF PRODUCED: ⭐ Stages 13/14
⭐   were 48 days of ***problem-scope*** work by construction —
⭐   ⭐⭐ YOU CHOSE WHAT TO MEASURE, DECIDED A THIRD OF THE
⭐   QUESTIONS SHOULD NOT USE RETRIEVAL AT ALL, DELETED
⭐   TECHNIQUES THAT DID NOT EARN THEIR PLACE, AND NAMED THE
⭐   LIMITS NOBODY ASKED ABOUT. ⭐ Those are not implementation
⭐   stories, ⭐⭐ AND TELLING THEM AS IMPLEMENTATION STORIES IS
⭐   THE MOST COMMON WAY TO LEVEL YOURSELF DOWN.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. "What level do you see yourself at?"**

⭐ I'd answer in scope terms rather than years, because that's the axis they're actually measuring.
⭐⭐ "I'm comfortable being handed a problem rather than a specification — the retrieval work started as
'support costs too much', not as 'build a RAG system', and part of what I delivered was the finding that
a third of the questions shouldn't use retrieval at all." ⭐ Then I'd ask theirs: what level is the role
mapped to, and what would someone at that level be expected to own in their first year? ⭐⭐ The second
half of that question is the useful half, because "own a service" and "close well-defined tickets" are
different jobs with the same title.

**2. "What actually determines level?"**

⭐ Scope and ambiguity, not code difficulty. ⭐⭐ Junior is given a task, mid a feature, senior a problem,
staff an area — and the ladder is about how much someone else has already decided before it reaches you.
⭐ The uncomfortable corollary is that an engineer who writes beautiful code but has only been handed
well-specified tasks levels below one who writes adequate code and has owned something end to end.
⭐⭐ That feels unfair and it's consistent, because what's scarce in an organisation isn't people who can
implement — it's people who can decide what to implement and be right. ⭐ There's a second axis too: how
much of *other people's* output you affect, which candidates almost never mention.

**3. "Tell me about your project." — and how would you level that answer?"**

⭐ The same project can be told two ways with identical facts. ⭐⭐ Task-scope: "I was asked to build a RAG
system, I used FastAPI and pgvector, recall is 0.91." Every verb is an implementation verb, someone else
decided RAG was the answer, and the debrief writes "solid execution" — a mid signal. ⭐ Problem-scope:
"Support agents were losing twenty minutes per ticket. I spent the first week reading actual tickets
rather than building, and found a third were aggregations that retrieval fundamentally can't answer — so
I split it into a router, retrieval, and text-to-SQL." ⭐⭐ Now I questioned the brief, gathered evidence
before building, and changed what got built. ⭐ Same project, different level.

**4. "They offered you a level below what you applied for."**

⭐ I'd treat it as information rather than an insult, because only one of the four usual causes is about
my ability. ⭐⭐ Their ladder may be calibrated differently; my stories may have been task-scope, which is
a fixable presentation problem and the most common cause; one round may have been weak and they're
hedging; or the headcount is simply budgeted at that level. ⭐ So I'd ask four things calmly: what would I
need to demonstrate for the next level, how long does promotion take and how many people made it last
cycle, is the level or the compensation the constraint, and which signal was weakest. ⭐⭐ A vague answer
to the first is itself data — it usually means promotion is political rather than criteria-based.

**5. "Would you accept it?"**

⭐ It depends on one thing: whether the *work* is bigger than my current scope. ⭐⭐ If it is, I'd take it,
because a title is a label and a year of larger problems changes what I can honestly claim next time.
⭐ If the work is *smaller* than what I do now, then no — I'd be bored, I'd build no new stories, and I'd
level down for the next search too, which compounds. ⭐⭐ The reason I'd most distrust in myself is
accepting because I'm tired of interviewing, which is the most expensive reason there is and the most
common one. ⭐ The framing I use is that a level is a bet about what I can handle: if the bet is low and
the work is big, take it and reset it in a year.

**6. "You've mostly had task-scope work. How do you get out of that?"**

⭐ Four ways, none of which needs a promotion first. ⭐⭐ Measure something nobody asked you to measure —
cost per request, a p99 nobody looked at, an abstention rate. That's an ownership story requiring nobody's
permission, and it's the cheapest scope available. ⭐ Write the design doc for the thing you were handed,
including the alternatives you rejected — that turns a task into a decision you can defend. ⭐⭐ Say no to
something in writing with reasoning; one argued scope cut is a better senior story than ten delivered
features. ⭐ And affect someone else's output: a review that changed a design, a pattern others adopted, a
runbook that stopped a recurring page.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

⭐⭐ **Two minutes, out loud: tell your main project as a problem-scope story.**

Do not start with what you built. **"The problem was that support agents were spending about twenty
minutes per ticket finding answers that already existed somewhere in eight thousand documents. That's
what I was given — not a specification, a cost."**

Then show the evidence-gathering, because that is the part that reads as senior. **"The first thing I did
was not build anything. I spent a week reading actual tickets, and the finding changed the project: about
a third of the questions were aggregations — how many, what's the total, which is largest — and those are
questions a vector index fundamentally cannot answer. Top-k returns eight documents when the answer needs
four thousand rows, so any number the model produces is fabricated. If I'd built what was implied, a third
of the traffic would have got confident wrong answers with citations attached."**

Then the decision, including the negative one. **"So I split it: a router, retrieval for lookup questions,
and validated text-to-SQL for the analytical ones, with the generated query shown to the user because
that's the only citation an aggregate can have. And I decided *not* to build the conversational memory
layer that was in the original sketch — it would have taken three weeks and the ticket data said almost
nobody asks follow-up questions in that flow."**

Close with cost and limits, which is where the level is confirmed. **"Recall on the retrieval half went
from 0.62 to 0.91, and two-thirds of that was free — heading prefixes and hybrid search, before I spent
anything. What it cost was that permission revocation takes up to thirty seconds, because ACL changes
propagate through an outbox rather than being resolved live, and I chose that deliberately for latency. I
can tell you the four conditions under which I'd change it."**
