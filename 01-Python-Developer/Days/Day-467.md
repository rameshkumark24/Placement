# Day 467 🎬

**P-467** · 🎬 **Project defence I** — ⭐⭐ thirty seconds, three minutes, thirty minutes

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: you do not get to choose which length you give — the interviewer decides
> that from your first two sentences, so the thirty-second version is not a summary of the long one, it
> is the audition for it.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ Three lengths, three different jobs

```
⭐ ***THEY ARE NOT NESTED VERSIONS OF ONE ANSWER.*** ⭐ Each has a
⭐   different goal, and preparing only the long one is why
⭐   people get cut off at ninety seconds.

⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***THIRTY SECONDS — THE HOOK.***
⭐   ⭐ GOAL: ⭐⭐ MAKE THEM ASK A SECOND QUESTION.
⭐   ⭐ Used in: the recruiter screen, "so tell me about
⭐     yourself", the first minute of every round, and every
⭐     conversation you will ever have at a meetup.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ CONTAINS: THE PROBLEM, THE HARD PART, ONE NUMBER.
⭐     ⭐ Nothing else. No stack list.

⭐ ② ***THREE MINUTES — THE SHAPE.***
⭐   ⭐ GOAL: ⭐⭐ ESTABLISH THAT THIS IS A REAL SYSTEM, ⭐ and hand
⭐     them three or four places to probe.
⭐   ⭐ Used in: "walk me through a project", the opening of a
⭐     deep dive, the behavioural round when a story needs
⭐     grounding.
⭐   ⭐ ***CONTAINS:*** ⭐ problem → constraint → the one hard
⭐     decision → the measurement → ⭐⭐ WHAT IT CANNOT DO.

⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***THIRTY TO FORTY-FIVE MINUTES — THE DEEP DIVE.***
⭐   ⭐ GOAL: ⭐⭐ SURVIVE SUSTAINED INTERROGATION ⭐ and come out
⭐     the other side having said something they had not thought
⭐     of.
⭐   ⭐ Day 471 builds this whole round.

⭐ ⭐⭐ THE FAILURE THIS PREVENTS: ⭐ a candidate who only prepared
⭐   ③ gives ninety seconds of architecture to a recruiter,
⭐   ⭐⭐ AND A THIRTY-SECOND SHRUG TO THE PRINCIPAL ENGINEER WHO
⭐   HAD FORTY MINUTES BOOKED.
```

---

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The hook, and how to build one

```
⭐ ***A HOOK IS NOT A SUMMARY. IT IS A ***QUESTION-GENERATOR***.***

⭐ ❌ "I built a RAG chatbot over our internal docs using
⭐   FastAPI, LangChain and Pinecone."
⭐   ⭐⭐ THE HONEST REACTION IS "SO HAS EVERYONE." ⭐ It generates
⭐     no question because it contains no tension.

⭐ ✅ ⭐⭐ "I BUILT A RETRIEVAL SYSTEM OVER EIGHT THOUSAND
⭐   INTERNAL DOCUMENTS. ⭐ The interesting part was that a third
⭐   of the questions users actually asked ***couldn't be
⭐   answered by retrieval at all*** — they were counts and
⭐   totals — ⭐⭐ SO THE MOST IMPORTANT COMPONENT ENDED UP BEING
⭐   THE ROUTER THAT SENDS THOSE SOMEWHERE ELSE."
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THAT GENERATES THREE OBVIOUS FOLLOW-UPS ⭐ and it
⭐     signals judgement before you have said anything technical.

⭐ ***THE FOUR HOOK PATTERNS THAT WORK:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***THE COUNTERINTUITIVE FINDING*** — "two-thirds of
⭐     the quality gain was free; the thing I expected to matter
⭐     mattered least."
⭐   ⭐ ② ***THE INVISIBLE FAILURE*** — "the bug I'm proudest of
⭐     catching produced no error and no latency change."
⭐   ⭐ ③ ***THE CONSTRAINT NOBODY EXPECTS*** — "the corpus was
⭐     twenty million tokens, so 'put it in the prompt' wasn't a
⭐     slow option, it wasn't an option."
⭐   ⭐ ④ ***THE THING YOU DELETED*** — "the most useful decision
⭐     was removing a technique after measuring it."

⭐ ⭐⭐ ALL FOUR SHARE ONE PROPERTY: ***THEY CONTAIN TENSION.***
⭐   ⭐ A hook with no tension is a description, and descriptions
⭐     do not generate questions. ⭐⭐ IF YOUR OPENING COULD APPEAR
⭐     IN A README, IT IS NOT A HOOK.
```

---

# Part 3 — The three-minute version, assembled

```
⭐ ***FIVE BEATS. NOTHING ELSE FITS IN THREE MINUTES.***

⭐ ① ***THE PROBLEM, IN USER TERMS*** (20s) — ⭐⭐ NOT IN
⭐   ARCHITECTURE TERMS. ⭐ "Support agents were spending twenty
⭐   minutes finding an answer that existed in a document
⭐   somewhere."

⭐ ② ***THE CONSTRAINT THAT MADE IT HARD*** (30s) — ⭐⭐ THIS IS
⭐   WHERE MOST PEOPLE SKIP STRAIGHT TO THE STACK AND LOSE THE
⭐   ROOM. ⭐ "Twenty million tokens, so no context window holds
⭐   it. And per-team permissions, which turned out to be the
⭐   hard part."

⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***THE ONE DECISION YOU MOST WANT PROBED*** (60s) — with
⭐   the alternative you rejected. ⭐ Not three decisions. One.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ YOU ARE ROUTING THEM, THE SAME WAY THE RESUME DOES
⭐     (DAY 462).

⭐ ④ ***THE MEASUREMENT*** (30s) — ⭐ what you measured, what it
⭐   said, ⭐⭐ AND WHAT YOU CHANGED BECAUSE OF IT. ⭐ A number
⭐   with no consequent decision is trivia.

⭐ ⑤ ⭐⭐ ***WHAT IT CANNOT DO*** (20s) — ⭐ volunteered, unasked.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THIS BEAT IS WHY THE ANSWER IS BELIEVED. ⭐ Nobody
⭐     trusts a system with no limits, and stating them
⭐     unprompted transfers credibility to everything else you
⭐     said.

⭐ ⭐⭐ NOTE WHAT IS ***NOT*** IN THE FIVE BEATS: ⭐ the tech stack.
⭐   ⭐ It comes out naturally in ③, ⭐⭐ AND A LIST OF
⭐     TECHNOLOGIES WITH NO DECISION ATTACHED IS THE MOST
⭐     FORGETTABLE THING YOU CAN SAY.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Choosing which project

```
⭐ ***THE MISTAKE:*** ⭐ leading with the biggest project.
⭐ ***THE RULE:*** ⭐⭐ LEAD WITH THE ONE WHERE YOU MADE THE MOST
⭐   DECISIONS ***YOU CAN DEFEND***. ⭐ Size is irrelevant;
⭐   ⭐⭐ DECISION DENSITY IS EVERYTHING.

⭐ ***SCORE EACH CANDIDATE PROJECT OUT OF 5 ON:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ HOW MANY REAL DECISIONS DID ***YOU*** MAKE?
⭐   ⭐ ② Do you have ***numbers***?
⭐   ⭐ ③ Did something ***go wrong*** that you fixed?
⭐   ⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ CAN YOU NAME FIVE THINGS IT CANNOT DO?
⭐   ⭐ ⑤ Does it overlap the job you are applying for?
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE WINNER IS USUALLY NOT THE BIGGEST ONE.

⭐ ***AND THE HONEST FRAME FOR A PERSONAL PROJECT*** — ⭐ said
⭐   once, up front, without apology, then never again:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ "THIS IS A PERSONAL SYSTEM RATHER THAN A PRODUCTION
⭐     ONE — WHAT I CAN'T CLAIM IS THAT I'VE BEEN PAGED FOR IT AT
⭐     3AM OR NEGOTIATED SCOPE WITH A PM. ⭐ What it does have is
⭐     an eval harness, a CI gate I've watched fail, and a cost
⭐     model."
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THEN PROCEED EXACTLY AS IF IT WERE PRODUCTION. ⭐ The
⭐     apology is one sentence; the defence is thirty minutes.

⭐ ***HAVE A SECOND PROJECT READY.*** ⭐ "Tell me about a
⭐   different one" is a standard probe, ⭐⭐ AND IT IS CHECKING
⭐   WHETHER YOU HAVE ONE EXPERIENCE OR SEVERAL.
```

# Part 5 — Tailoring the same project to three audiences

```
⭐ ***ONE PROJECT, THREE ROOMS. SAME FACTS, DIFFERENT SPINE.***

⭐ ***TO A BACKEND TEAM:*** ⭐ lead on the ***service***.
⭐   ⭐ "It's a FastAPI service over Postgres — the interesting
⭐     engineering was that requests take three seconds instead
⭐     of fifty milliseconds, ⭐⭐ WHICH INVALIDATES YOUR WORKER
⭐     MODEL, YOUR AUTOSCALING SIGNAL AND YOUR DEPLOY
⭐     PROCEDURE."

⭐ ***TO AN AI/ML TEAM:*** ⭐ lead on the ***measurement***.
⭐   ⭐ "The part I'd defend hardest is the eval harness. Recall
⭐     and faithfulness measured separately, because a bad answer
⭐     has two causes with opposite fixes, ⭐⭐ AND TWENTY
⭐     UNANSWERABLE QUESTIONS WHERE THE ONLY CORRECT BEHAVIOUR IS
⭐     ABSTENTION."

⭐ ***TO A PLATFORM / INFRA TEAM:*** ⭐ lead on the
⭐   ***operations***.
⭐   ⭐ "Rate limits are on tokens, not requests, so we hit the
⭐     ceiling at half a request per second. ⭐⭐ AND OUR OWN
⭐     NIGHTLY EVAL JOB COULD STARVE PRODUCTION TRAFFIC, WHICH IS
⭐     WHY WORKLOAD CLASSES HAVE SEPARATE KEYS."

⭐ ⭐⭐ THE POINT IS NOT SPIN — ⭐ it is that a system has several
⭐   true centres of gravity, ⭐⭐ AND LEADING WITH THE ONE THIS
⭐   ROOM CARES ABOUT IS THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN INTERESTING AND
⭐   IRRELEVANT. ⭐ Prepare all three openings; the body is the
⭐   same.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. "Tell me about a project you've worked on."**

⭐ I'd give the three-minute version and let them pull. ⭐⭐ "Support agents were spending twenty minutes
finding answers that already existed in a document somewhere, across about eight thousand documents.
That's twenty million tokens, so 'put it in the prompt' wasn't a slow option — it wasn't an option. And
the documents have per-team permissions, which turned out to be the hardest part of the whole thing."
⭐ Then the one decision I most want probed, the measurement, and what it can't do. ⭐⭐ What I wouldn't
do is list the stack, because a list of technologies with no decision attached is the most forgettable
thing I could say.

**2. "Give me the short version."**

⭐ "I built a retrieval system over eight thousand internal documents. ⭐⭐ The interesting part was that
about a third of the questions users actually asked couldn't be answered by retrieval at all — they were
counts and totals — so the most important component ended up being the router that sends those to SQL
instead." ⭐ That's thirty seconds and it's designed to generate a follow-up rather than to summarise.
⭐⭐ A hook with no tension in it is a description, and descriptions don't get questions.

**3. "How do you choose which project to talk about?"**

⭐ Decision density, not size. ⭐⭐ I score candidates on how many real decisions I made, whether I have
numbers, whether something went wrong that I fixed, whether I can name five things it can't do, and
whether it overlaps the role. ⭐ The winner is usually not the biggest project — a small system where I
made eight defensible decisions beats a large one where I implemented someone else's design.
⭐⭐ And I keep a second one ready, because "tell me about a different project" is a standard probe
checking whether I have one experience or several.

**4. "It's a personal project though, isn't it?"**

⭐ It is, and I'd say so in the first thirty seconds rather than let it be discovered. ⭐⭐ What I can't
claim is that I've been paged for it at 3am or negotiated scope with a product manager under a real
deadline. ⭐ What it has is an eval harness with a gold set, a CI gate I've watched go red, a cost model
with a p99 I investigated, and a documented list of limitations. ⭐⭐ Then I proceed exactly as if it
were production — the caveat is one sentence, the defence is thirty minutes. ⭐ Repeating the apology is
what actually devalues it.

**5. "You've told this to three different teams. Does the story change?"**

⭐ The spine does, the facts don't. ⭐⭐ To a backend team I lead on the service — three-second requests
invalidate your worker model, your autoscaling signal and your deploy procedure. To an AI team I lead on
the measurement — recall and faithfulness separated because a bad answer has two causes with opposite
fixes. To a platform team I lead on operations — token-per-minute limits, and our own nightly eval job
being able to starve production. ⭐ It's not spin; a real system has several true centres of gravity, and
⭐⭐ leading with the one this room cares about is the difference between interesting and irrelevant.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

⭐⭐ **Three minutes, out loud: the hook, then the three-minute version, back to back.**

Hook first — thirty seconds, and stop. **"I built a retrieval system over eight thousand internal
documents. The interesting part was that about a third of the questions people actually asked couldn't
be answered by retrieval at all — they were counts and totals, and top-k similarity search can't count.
So the component that mattered most ended up being the router that sends those somewhere else."** Then
stop talking and let them ask.

Then the three-minute version, in five beats. **Problem in user terms: "Support agents were spending
twenty minutes finding an answer that already existed in a document."** **Constraint: "Twenty million
tokens, so no context window holds it — and per-team permissions, which turned out to be the hard part."**

**One decision, with the alternative:** **"The decision I'd most like to be pushed on is putting the
permission filter inside the query rather than after the search. Filtering afterwards is one line and it
makes the test pass — but it means the other tenant's vectors were searched, scored and pulled into my
process, with one line of application code between them and the prompt. So it went inside the SQL, with
row-level security underneath so the database enforces it whether or not my code remembers."**

**Measurement, with the consequence:** **"Recall@5 went from 0.62 to 0.91, measured on thirty questions
with known-correct sources — and two-thirds of that was free, which changed how I sequenced the rest of
the work: free things first, always."**

**Limits, unprompted:** **"What it can't do: nine percent of questions have no correct chunk, so no
prompt fixes those. Aggregations route to SQL. Permission revocation takes up to thirty seconds. And a
full rebuild is thirty-eight minutes, which is our worst-case recovery time."**
