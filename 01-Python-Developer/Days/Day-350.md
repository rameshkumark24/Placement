# Day 350 📣

**P-350** · 📣 **Design 6 — a notification system at scale** — ⭐⭐ the broadcast

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** BUILD

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: sending one notification is trivial and sending fifty million in ten
> minutes is a different system — the design is dominated by fan-out, by third-party providers whose
> rate limits you do not control, and by the fact that the notification is often what causes the
> traffic spike that then takes down the service it points at.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐ 0–8: scope and the numbers that matter

```
⭐⭐ FUNCTIONAL:
⭐   ① Send to a user across channels: push, email, SMS, in-app.
⭐   ② ⭐ Templates with variables and localisation.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ USER PREFERENCES AND UNSUBSCRIBE — ⭐ legally
⭐        required, and it constrains the pipeline order.
⭐   ④ ⭐⭐ BULK / BROADCAST TO MILLIONS.
⭐   ⑤ Delivery status and retries.
⭐   ⭐⭐ OUT: designing the templates, analytics dashboards,
⭐     campaign scheduling UI.

⭐⭐ NON-FUNCTIONAL — ⭐⭐ AND THE TWO WORKLOADS ARE DIFFERENT
⭐   SYSTEMS SHARING A PIPELINE:
⭐   ⭐ ***TRANSACTIONAL*** — "your order shipped". ⭐ Low volume,
⭐     ⭐⭐ LATENCY-CRITICAL (seconds), MUST NOT BE LOST.
⭐   ⭐ ***BROADCAST*** — "flash sale starts now". ⭐ 50 M
⭐     recipients, ⭐⭐ THROUGHPUT-CRITICAL, INDIVIDUAL LOSS
⭐     TOLERABLE.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SAYING THIS SPLIT AT MINUTE FIVE IS THE SINGLE MOST
⭐     IMPORTANT MOVE IN THIS DESIGN, BECAUSE IT GIVES YOU
⭐     SEPARATE QUEUES AND SEPARATE PRIORITIES, AND EVERY
⭐     FAILURE MODE LATER TRACES BACK TO MIXING THEM.

⭐⭐ ESTIMATE:
⭐   ⭐ 100 M users, ~5 notifications/user/day ⇒ 5 × 10^8/day
⭐     ⇒ ⭐ 5,000/s average.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ BUT A BROADCAST TO 50 M IN 10 MINUTES IS 83,000/s —
⭐     ***17× THE AVERAGE, ARRIVING INSTANTLY.*** ⭐ That is the
⭐     number the system must be built for.
⭐   ⭐ Storage: 5 × 10^8/day × 200 B of status = ⭐ 100 GB/day
⭐     ⇒ ⭐⭐ SO STATUS ROWS NEED A RETENTION POLICY FROM DAY
⭐     ONE, NOT AS AN AFTERTHOUGHT.
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The pipeline, and why its order is correctness

```
⭐⭐ THE STAGES, IN THE ONLY CORRECT ORDER:
⭐   ① ⭐ ***INGEST*** — an event or an API call arrives.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ ***RESOLVE AUDIENCE*** — who receives this? ⭐ For a
⭐        broadcast this is a query returning millions of rows.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ ***CHECK PREFERENCES AND CONSENT*** — per user, per
⭐        channel, per category.
⭐   ④ ⭐ ***DEDUPLICATE / RATE LIMIT PER USER***.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐ ***RENDER*** the template with user data.
⭐   ⑥ ⭐ ***DISPATCH*** to the channel provider.
⭐   ⑦ ⭐ ***TRACK*** status; retry or DLQ.

⭐⭐ THE ORDERING RULES THAT ARE ***CORRECTNESS***, NOT STYLE:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ CONSENT (③) BEFORE RENDER (⑤). ⭐ Render first and
⭐     you have copied personal data into a queue for a message
⭐     that must not be sent — ⭐⭐ WHICH IS A DATA-PROTECTION
⭐     PROBLEM EVEN IF NOTHING IS DELIVERED.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ CONSENT CHECKED AT ***SEND*** TIME, NOT AT ENQUEUE
⭐     TIME. ⭐ A broadcast queued at 09:00 and delivered over
⭐     forty minutes must respect an unsubscribe made at 09:15.
⭐     ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE SINGLE MOST COMMONLY MISSED REQUIREMENT
⭐     IN THIS DESIGN, AND IT IS THE ONE WITH LEGAL WEIGHT.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ DEDUP KEY DERIVED FROM THE ***EVENT***, NOT
⭐     GENERATED AT SEND. ⭐ `notify:{event_id}:{user}:{channel}`
⭐     ⇒ a retried event never double-sends (Day 335).
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ Fan-out: the deep dive

```
⭐⭐ THE NAIVE BROADCAST: `SELECT id FROM users WHERE …` then
⭐   loop and enqueue.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ 50 M rows into application memory. ⭐⭐ THE PROCESS
⭐     DIES, AND YOU HAVE NO IDEA HOW FAR IT GOT.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ 50 M individual queue publishes at maybe 10k/s = ⭐⭐ 83
⭐     MINUTES ***JUST TO ENQUEUE***, before a single message is
⭐     sent.

⭐⭐ ✔ ***HIERARCHICAL FAN-OUT*** — the pattern:
⭐   ① ⭐ ONE ***CAMPAIGN*** ROW: `(id, segment, template,
⭐      status, cursor)`.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ A ***SPLITTER*** TURNS THE AUDIENCE INTO ***CHUNK***
⭐      MESSAGES: `(campaign_id, user_id_range)` — 50 M users
⭐      becomes 5,000 chunks of 10,000.
⭐      ⇒ ⭐⭐ 5,000 MESSAGES INSTEAD OF 50 MILLION, AND THE
⭐        SPLITTER IS RESUMABLE FROM ITS CURSOR.
⭐   ③ ⭐ ***CHUNK WORKERS*** each expand their range, apply
⭐      preferences, render, and dispatch — ⭐ in batches, with
⭐      the provider's batch API where one exists.
⭐   ④ ⭐ Progress is `campaign.chunks_done / chunks_total`.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ IDEMPOTENT AT THE CHUNK LEVEL: A REDELIVERED CHUNK
⭐     RE-SENDS, WHICH THE PER-USER DEDUP KEY THEN SUPPRESSES.
⭐     ⭐ Two layers, and both are needed.

⭐⭐ AND THE THING THAT MAKES CHUNKS WORK — ⭐⭐ RANGES, NOT
⭐   LISTS. ⭐ A chunk message carries `user_id BETWEEN a AND b`,
⭐   not ten thousand ids, ⭐ so the message stays tiny and the
⭐   expansion happens where the data is.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE CONNECTION WORTH NAMING: ⭐⭐ THIS IS EXACTLY DAY
⭐   351'S FAN-OUT-ON-WRITE PROBLEM, WITH THE SAME SOLUTION
⭐   SHAPE — and the same celebrity problem, since a broadcast
⭐   is one "author" with fifty million "followers".
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Providers: the constraint you do not control

```
⭐⭐ EVERY CHANNEL IS A THIRD PARTY WITH ITS OWN LIMITS, ⭐ and
⭐   this is where real systems actually break:
⭐   ⭐ APNs / FCM ⇒ per-connection throughput, ⭐⭐ AND TOKENS
⭐     THAT EXPIRE OR BECOME INVALID.
⭐   ⭐ SES/SendGrid ⇒ a send rate ***and*** a daily quota,
⭐     ⭐⭐ AND A REPUTATION SCORE THAT DROPS IF YOU SEND TO DEAD
⭐     ADDRESSES.
⭐   ⭐ Twilio ⇒ per-number rate limits, ⭐ per-country rules,
⭐     ⭐⭐ AND REAL MONEY PER MESSAGE.

⭐⭐ FIVE CONSEQUENCES:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ RATE LIMIT ***OUTBOUND***, PER PROVIDER, GLOBALLY
⭐      ACROSS ALL YOUR WORKERS. ⭐ This is Day 347's limiter,
⭐      pointed outward — ⭐ and it is the same allocator design.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ A PROVIDER OUTAGE MUST NOT LOSE MESSAGES. ⭐ Queue,
⭐      back off, and ⭐⭐ FAIL ***OVER*** FOR CRITICAL CHANNELS
⭐      (two SMS providers, two email providers) — ⭐ with the
⭐      caveat that failover must not double-send, which is
⭐      what the dedup key is for.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ HANDLE BOUNCES AND INVALID TOKENS AS ***FEEDBACK***.
⭐      ⭐ A hard bounce must mark the address dead
⭐      ***immediately***; ⭐⭐ CONTINUING TO SEND TO DEAD
⭐      ADDRESSES IS HOW YOU LOSE SENDER REPUTATION AND STOP
⭐      BEING ABLE TO DELIVER ***ANYTHING***, INCLUDING PASSWORD
⭐      RESETS. ⭐ That failure is invisible until it is
⭐      catastrophic.
⭐   ④ ⭐ Cost is per message, so ⭐⭐ A BUG THAT SENDS TWICE HAS
⭐      A PRICE TAG YOU CAN COMPUTE — say it in dollars.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐ Delivery is ***asynchronous and uncertain***: accepted
⭐      ≠ delivered ≠ seen. ⭐⭐ THREE DIFFERENT STATES, AND
⭐      WEBHOOKS DELIVER THE LATER ONES OUT OF ORDER.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐⭐ The self-inflicted DDoS

```
⭐⭐ THE FAILURE MODE THAT DEFINES THIS SYSTEM, AND ALMOST
⭐   NOBODY MENTIONS IT:
⭐   ⭐ You push "FLASH SALE — 50% OFF" to 50 M devices.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ 5–10% TAP IT WITHIN ***SIXTY SECONDS***.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ 2.5–5 MILLION USERS HIT YOUR APP IN ONE MINUTE —
⭐     ***40,000–80,000 REQUESTS PER SECOND, ARRIVING WITH NO
⭐     RAMP AT ALL.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE NOTIFICATION SYSTEM WORKED PERFECTLY AND TOOK
⭐     DOWN THE COMPANY. ⭐ Autoscaling reacts in minutes; the
⭐     spike is over in ninety seconds.

⭐⭐ THE DEFENCES — ⭐ and the first one is the real answer:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ ***SPREAD THE SEND DELIBERATELY.*** ⭐ Deliver over
⭐      30–60 minutes rather than 10, in jittered waves.
⭐      ⭐⭐ THE MARKETING TEAM WILL RESIST THIS, AND THE
⭐      CONVERSATION IS THE JOB.
⭐   ② ⭐ Pre-scale before a known campaign — ⭐⭐ WHICH MEANS
⭐      THE NOTIFICATION SYSTEM MUST ***TELL*** THE PLATFORM A
⭐      BROADCAST IS COMING. ⭐ That integration is a design
⭐      decision, not an operational courtesy.
⭐   ③ ⭐ Make the landing page static and CDN-cached, so the
⭐      spike hits the edge and not your origin.
⭐   ④ ⭐ Warm caches before the send.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐ Load shedding with priority (Day 336): ⭐⭐ SHED THE
⭐      CAMPAIGN TRAFFIC BEFORE THE CHECKOUT TRAFFIC.

⭐⭐ ⇒ SAYING THIS UNPROMPTED IS THE STRONGEST SINGLE MOVE
⭐   AVAILABLE IN THIS DESIGN, BECAUSE IT SHOWS YOU ARE
⭐   THINKING ABOUT THE SYSTEM THE NOTIFICATION ***POINTS AT***,
⭐   NOT JUST THE ONE THAT SENDS IT.
```

# Part 6 — ⭐ Preferences, quiet hours and per-user limits

```
⭐⭐ THE PREFERENCE MODEL — ⭐ a three-dimensional lookup:
⭐   `(user, category, channel) -> enabled`
⭐   ⭐ Plus global unsubscribe, ⭐⭐ AND A HARD DISTINCTION:
⭐     ***TRANSACTIONAL MESSAGES (PASSWORD RESET, SECURITY
⭐     ALERT) IGNORE MARKETING PREFERENCES*** — ⭐ and getting
⭐     that boundary wrong in either direction is a serious bug:
⭐     one way you spam people, the other way you fail to
⭐     deliver a security alert.

⭐⭐ QUIET HOURS — ⭐ and the trap:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THEY ARE IN THE ***USER'S*** TIMEZONE, SO A SINGLE
⭐     CAMPAIGN IS DELIVERED ACROSS A 24-HOUR WINDOW, NOT AT
⭐     ONE INSTANT. ⭐ Schedule per timezone bucket.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ WHICH ALSO CONVENIENTLY SOLVES PART 5, AND SAYING
⭐     "THESE TWO REQUIREMENTS HELP EACH OTHER" IS A NICE
⭐     OBSERVATION.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐ DST and half-hour offsets are real; store an IANA
⭐     timezone name, never a UTC offset (Day 270).

⭐⭐ PER-USER RATE LIMITING — ⭐⭐ THE FEATURE THAT SAVES YOUR
⭐   APP FROM BEING UNINSTALLED:
⭐   ⭐ "At most 3 marketing pushes per user per day; at most 1
⭐     per hour."
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Enforced ***centrally***, so no individual team can
⭐     exceed it — ⭐⭐ BECAUSE EVERY TEAM BELIEVES THEIR
⭐     NOTIFICATION IS THE IMPORTANT ONE.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AND A COLLAPSE/DIGEST RULE: five "someone liked your
⭐     post" become one "five people liked your post". ⭐ This is
⭐     a product feature that is also a capacity feature.
```

# Part 7 — ⭐ Status, storage and bottlenecks

```
⭐⭐ THE STATUS MODEL — ⭐ four states, and they are not linear:
⭐   `queued → sent → delivered → opened`
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ PLUS `failed` AND `suppressed` (consent, bounce,
⭐     rate limit) — ⭐ and `suppressed` must record ***why***,
⭐     or support cannot answer "why didn't I get it?", which
⭐     is the most common support question this system
⭐     generates.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ WEBHOOKS ARRIVE OUT OF ORDER AND MORE THAN ONCE,
⭐     SO STATUS TRANSITIONS MUST BE MONOTONIC: NEVER MOVE
⭐     `delivered` BACK TO `sent`.

⭐⭐ STORAGE: 100 GB/day of status.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Hot (7 days) in Postgres/Cassandra for support;
⭐     ⭐ cold in object storage for analytics;
⭐     ⭐⭐ AND PARTITION BY DAY SO EXPIRY IS `DROP PARTITION`
⭐     RATHER THAN A `DELETE` OF A BILLION ROWS (Day 260).

⭐⭐ WHAT BREAKS:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ PROVIDER RATE LIMITS — ⭐ the real ceiling, and it is
⭐      not yours to raise. ⭐ Multiple providers, warmed IPs,
⭐      and negotiated limits.
⭐   ② ⭐ The audience query for a complex segment ⇒ precompute
⭐      segments, ⭐⭐ AND NEVER RUN A 50-MILLION-ROW SCAN
⭐      AGAINST THE PRIMARY.
⭐   ③ ⭐ Status write volume ⇒ batch, and pre-aggregate the
⭐      campaign counters (Day 345's pattern).
⭐   ④ ⭐⭐ AND THE ONE OUTSIDE YOUR SYSTEM: THE APP THE
⭐      NOTIFICATION POINTS AT. ⭐ Part 5.
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ One queue for transactional and broadcast | A 50 M campaign delays every password reset behind it |
| ⭐⭐ Consent checked at enqueue rather than send | An unsubscribe at 09:15 doesn't stop a message queued at 09:00. Legal weight |
| ⭐⭐ Rendering before checking consent | Personal data copied into a queue for a message that must not be sent |
| ⭐⭐ Enqueueing 50 M individual messages | 83 minutes just to enqueue, unresumable, and the process dies with no idea how far it got |
| ⭐ Chunk messages carrying id lists | Huge messages. Carry ranges and expand where the data is |
| ⭐⭐ No outbound provider rate limiting | You get throttled or banned, and the failure mode is silent |
| ⭐⭐ Ignoring hard bounces | Sender reputation collapses and you stop delivering *anything*, including password resets |
| ⭐ Generated idempotency keys | A retried event double-sends. Derive the key from the event |
| ⭐⭐ Sending a broadcast in ten minutes | 5% tapping in 60 seconds is 40–80k rps at your app with no ramp |
| ⭐ Quiet hours in server time | They're in the user's timezone, which means a 24-hour delivery window |
| ⭐ Marketing preferences applied to security alerts | You fail to deliver a security alert. Wrong in the other direction, you spam |
| ⭐⭐ Non-monotonic status transitions | Webhooks arrive out of order; `delivered` must never revert to `sent` |
| ⭐ `suppressed` with no reason | Support cannot answer the most common question this system produces |

## Interview questions

**1. "How do you send to 50 million users?"**
> ⭐⭐ Hierarchical fan-out. One campaign row, a splitter that turns the audience into chunk messages
> carrying **ranges rather than id lists** — fifty million users becomes five thousand chunks of ten
> thousand — and chunk workers that expand, filter by consent, render and dispatch in batches. ⭐⭐ **Five
> thousand messages instead of fifty million**, the splitter is resumable from a cursor, and progress
> is chunks-done over chunks-total. ⭐ Idempotency at two layers: the chunk can be redelivered, and the
> per-user dedup key suppresses the duplicate.

**2. "Where does consent get checked?"**
> ⭐ At send time, and before rendering. ⭐⭐ **At send time, because a broadcast queued at nine and
> delivered over forty minutes must respect an unsubscribe made at nine fifteen** — that's the most
> commonly missed requirement here and it's the one with legal weight. ⭐ Before rendering, because
> rendering first copies personal data into a queue for a message that must not be sent, which is a
> data-protection problem even if nothing is delivered.

**3. "What's the failure mode people don't think about?"**
> ⭐⭐ **The notification system working perfectly and taking down the company.** ⭐ Push to fifty
> million devices, five to ten percent tap within sixty seconds, and that's two and a half to five
> million users arriving at your app in one minute — **forty to eighty thousand requests per second
> with no ramp**, while autoscaling reacts in minutes. ⭐ The primary defence isn't technical: **spread
> the send over thirty to sixty minutes in jittered waves**, and have that conversation with marketing.
> ⭐ Then pre-scale, static CDN-cached landing page, warm caches, and priority shedding that drops
> campaign traffic before checkout.

**4. "How do you handle provider limits?"**
> ⭐ Outbound rate limiting per provider, enforced globally across all workers — which is the same
> allocator design as a rate-limiting service, pointed outward. ⭐ Queue and back off on outages, with
> failover to a second provider for critical channels, ⭐⭐ **and the dedup key is what makes failover
> safe rather than double-sending.** ⭐ And treat bounces as feedback: **a hard bounce marks the address
> dead immediately, because continuing to send to dead addresses destroys sender reputation and then
> you can't deliver anything at all, including password resets** — a failure that's invisible until
> it's catastrophic.

**5. "Transactional and broadcast in one system?"**
> ⭐ Same pipeline, ⭐⭐ **separate queues and separate priorities, decided at minute five** — otherwise
> a fifty-million campaign puts every password reset behind it. ⭐ Transactional is latency-critical
> and must not be lost; broadcast is throughput-critical and individual loss is tolerable. ⭐ And
> transactional messages bypass marketing preferences entirely — that boundary is a correctness
> question in both directions.

**6. "How do you stop users uninstalling the app?"**
> ⭐⭐ Per-user rate limits enforced centrally — three marketing pushes a day, one an hour — **because
> every team believes their notification is the important one**, so it cannot be left to individual
> senders. ⭐ Plus collapsing: five "someone liked your post" become one "five people liked your post",
> ⭐⭐ which is a product feature that is simultaneously a capacity feature. ⭐ And quiet hours in the
> user's timezone, which conveniently also spreads the send.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Split the problem at minute one, because everything follows from it: **transactional
is low-volume, latency-critical and must not be lost; broadcast is fifty million recipients where
individual loss is tolerable** — separate queues, separate priorities, or one campaign puts every
password reset behind it. Then the pipeline, and give the two ordering rules as correctness: **consent
before render, or you've copied personal data into a queue for a message that must not be sent — and
consent at send time, not enqueue time, because a campaign delivered over forty minutes must respect an
unsubscribe made fifteen minutes in.** Then the fan-out, with arithmetic: enqueueing fifty million
individually takes over an hour just to enqueue and dies unresumably, **so chunk it — five thousand
messages carrying ranges rather than id lists, resumable from a cursor, with per-user dedup keys derived
from the event.** Then providers, and the failure that's invisible until it's catastrophic: **ignoring
hard bounces destroys your sender reputation, and then you can't deliver anything, including password
resets.** Close on the move that wins the round: **the notification system working perfectly is what
takes down the company** — five percent of fifty million tapping within a minute is forty to eighty
thousand requests a second with no ramp, and the primary defence is to **spread the send over an hour
in jittered waves**, which means having the conversation with marketing, and which quiet-hours
bucketing gives you almost for free.
