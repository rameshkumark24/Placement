# Day 353 🎬

**P-353** · 🎬 **Design 9 — video streaming** — ⭐⭐ adaptive bitrate, and egress as the business

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** BUILD

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: video streaming is not a streaming problem, it is a file-serving problem —
> the player downloads a sequence of small static files over ordinary HTTP, which is why a CDN can
> serve it at all, and the entire economics of the business is one number: bytes of egress.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐ 0–8: scope and the numbers that dominate

```
⭐⭐ FUNCTIONAL:
⭐   ① Upload a video. ② Transcode into multiple qualities.
⭐   ③ Stream with adaptive quality. ④ ⭐ Thumbnails, seek.
⭐   ⭐⭐ OUT: recommendations, comments, live streaming
⭐     (⭐ mention: live changes ***everything***, because you
⭐     cannot pre-transcode and latency becomes a hard
⭐     constraint — it is a different design), DRM.

⭐⭐ NON-FUNCTIONAL:
⭐   ⭐ 10 M DAU, ⭐ 30 min watched/user/day.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ START-UP TIME < 2 s AND ***REBUFFER RATIO*** < 0.5%
⭐     — ⭐⭐ REBUFFERING IS THE METRIC USERS ACTUALLY FEEL, AND
⭐     IT MATTERS MORE THAN RESOLUTION. ⭐ Say this early: it
⭐     licenses every quality-vs-continuity trade later.
⭐   ⭐ Upload: 500k videos/day, average 10 min.

⭐⭐ ⇒ THE ESTIMATE THAT ***IS*** THE BUSINESS:
⭐   ⭐ 10 M × 30 min = 5 M viewing-hours/day.
⭐   ⭐ At 1080p ≈ 5 Mbps ⇒ 2.25 GB/hour.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ 5 M × 2.25 GB = ***11 PETABYTES OF EGRESS PER DAY.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AT $0.09/GB THAT IS ***$1 MILLION PER DAY.*** ⭐ At a
⭐     negotiated CDN rate of ~$0.005/GB it is ~$55,000/day.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SO THE ***CDN CONTRACT IS THE ARCHITECTURE***, AND
⭐     EVERY 10% SAVED IN BITRATE IS MILLIONS A YEAR. ⭐ That
⭐     sentence reframes the entire design, and almost nobody
⭐     says it.

⭐⭐ STORAGE:
⭐   ⭐ 500k videos/day × 10 min. ⭐⭐ ONE SOURCE VIDEO BECOMES
⭐     ~6 RENDITIONS, TOTALLING ROUGHLY 1.5–2× THE SOURCE SIZE.
⭐   ⭐ ~1 GB source ⇒ ~2.5 GB stored ⇒ ⭐ 1.25 PB/day.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SO TIERING IS MANDATORY: 90% OF VIEWS GO TO A TINY
⭐     FRACTION OF VIDEOS, SO THE LONG TAIL MOVES TO COLD
⭐     STORAGE — ⭐ and old, never-watched renditions can be
⭐     ***deleted and regenerated on demand***, which is a real
⭐     technique.
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ It's files, not streams

```
⭐⭐ THE INSIGHT THAT MAKES EVERYTHING ELSE POSSIBLE:
⭐   ***THE VIDEO IS CHOPPED INTO 2–10 SECOND SEGMENTS, EACH A
⭐      SEPARATE FILE, LISTED IN A MANIFEST. THE PLAYER
⭐      DOWNLOADS THEM WITH ORDINARY HTTP `GET`s.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ NO PERSISTENT CONNECTION. NO STATEFUL SERVER. ⭐ Which
⭐     is why a plain CDN — a cache for static files — can serve
⭐     the entire product.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND IT MEANS EVERY DAY-327 LESSON APPLIES DIRECTLY:
⭐     immutable content-addressed URLs, long TTLs, signed URLs
⭐     for private content.

⭐⭐ HLS (Apple) / DASH (everyone else) — same idea:
⭐   ⭐ A ***master manifest*** lists available renditions.
⭐   ⭐ Each rendition has a ***media manifest*** listing its
⭐     segments.
⭐   ⭐ The player picks a rendition, fetches segments in order,
⭐     ⭐⭐ AND MAY SWITCH RENDITION AT ***ANY SEGMENT
⭐     BOUNDARY***.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE PROPERTY THAT MAKES SWITCHING SEAMLESS:
⭐   ***EVERY RENDITION MUST BE SEGMENTED AT THE ***SAME***
⭐      TIMESTAMPS, WITH ALIGNED KEYFRAMES.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ IF SEGMENT BOUNDARIES DIFFER BETWEEN RENDITIONS, THE
⭐     PLAYER CANNOT SWITCH WITHOUT A VISIBLE GLITCH OR A GAP.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Concretely: force a keyframe every N seconds during
⭐     transcode (`-force_key_frames`), identically for all
⭐     renditions. ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE SINGLE MOST COMMON
⭐     TRANSCODING BUG IN REAL PIPELINES, AND KNOWING IT IS A
⭐     STRONG SIGNAL.

⭐⭐ SEGMENT LENGTH IS A TRADE:
⭐   ⭐ Short (2 s) ⇒ faster start, faster adaptation, ⭐⭐ MORE
⭐     REQUESTS AND WORSE COMPRESSION.
⭐   ⭐ Long (10 s) ⇒ better compression, ⭐ slower reaction to
⭐     a bandwidth drop ⇒ ⭐⭐ MORE REBUFFERING.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ 4–6 s is the usual answer, ⭐ shorter for live.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The transcoding pipeline

```
⭐⭐ THE FLOW:
⭐   ① ⭐ Presigned multipart upload direct to object storage
⭐      (Day 327) — ⭐⭐ A 4 GB FILE MUST NEVER TOUCH YOUR API.
⭐   ② ⭐ Storage event ⇒ validate: real container/codec by
⭐      probing, duration, ⭐⭐ AND REJECT MALFORMED FILES
⭐      BEFORE SPENDING CPU ON THEM.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ ***SPLIT THE SOURCE INTO CHUNKS AND TRANSCODE THEM IN
⭐      PARALLEL.*** ⭐ This is the key scaling move: a 10-minute
⭐      video split into 60 ten-second chunks × 6 renditions =
⭐      ⭐⭐ 360 INDEPENDENT JOBS, so a 40-minute serial
⭐      transcode finishes in ***two minutes*** on a wide fleet.
⭐   ④ ⭐ Assemble segments, write manifests.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐ Generate thumbnails and the seek-preview sprite.
⭐   ⑥ ⭐ Mark `ready`, invalidate/publish.

⭐⭐ THE FOUR THINGS THAT MAKE THE PIPELINE SURVIVABLE:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ EVERY STAGE IDEMPOTENT AND KEYED BY
⭐      `(video_id, rendition, chunk)`. ⭐ A retried chunk
⭐      overwrites the same object. ⭐ Day 335, again.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ A DURABLE JOB STATE MACHINE (Day 342's saga table) —
⭐      transcoding takes minutes to hours, and workers die.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ PRIORITY: a 30-second clip must not queue behind a
⭐      3-hour film. ⭐ Separate queues by expected duration —
⭐      ⭐ Day 350's fairness lesson, again.
⭐   ④ ⭐ Spot/preemptible instances for transcoding — ⭐⭐ IT IS
⭐      BATCH, RESUMABLE AND CHUNKED, WHICH IS EXACTLY THE
⭐      WORKLOAD SPOT PRICING IS FOR. ⭐ Often 60–70% cheaper,
⭐      and saying it shows cost awareness.

⭐⭐ THE LADDER — ⭐ which renditions?
⭐   ⭐ 240p/400k · 360p/800k · 480p/1.4M · 720p/2.8M ·
⭐     1080p/5M · (4K/16M).
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE MODERN ANSWER: ***PER-TITLE ENCODING.*** A
⭐     cartoon needs far less bitrate than a fast sports clip
⭐     for the same perceived quality.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ ANALYSE COMPLEXITY AND CHOOSE THE LADDER PER VIDEO —
⭐     ⭐ typically 20% egress savings, ⭐⭐ WHICH ON PART 1'S
⭐     NUMBERS IS MILLIONS OF DOLLARS A YEAR. ⭐ This is the
⭐     highest-value optimisation in the whole system.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Adaptive bitrate: the player's algorithm

```
⭐⭐ THE PLAYER DECIDES, NOT THE SERVER. ⭐ It measures and
⭐   picks the next segment's rendition.

⭐⭐ ① ***THROUGHPUT-BASED*** — estimate bandwidth from recent
⭐   segment downloads, pick the highest rendition below it.
⭐   ⭐ ✘ ⭐⭐ NOISY, AND IT OSCILLATES: pick high ⇒ buffer drains
⭐     ⇒ pick low ⇒ buffer fills ⇒ pick high. ⭐ The user sees
⭐     quality flickering, which is worse than steady mediocrity.

⭐⭐ ② ***BUFFER-BASED*** — decide from ***how much buffer you
⭐   have***, not from measured bandwidth.
⭐   ⭐ Buffer > 20 s ⇒ go up. Buffer < 10 s ⇒ go down.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE INSIGHT: ***THE BUFFER LEVEL ALREADY ENCODES
⭐     BANDWIDTH HISTORY, AND IT IS THE THING YOU ACTUALLY CARE
⭐     ABOUT.*** ⭐ You are not optimising bitrate; you are
⭐     avoiding an empty buffer.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Much more stable. ⭐ Slow to start (no buffer yet).

⭐⭐ ③ ***HYBRID*** — throughput while the buffer is small
⭐   (start-up), buffer-based in steady state. ⭐⭐ THIS IS WHAT
⭐   REAL PLAYERS DO, and naming the transition is the answer.

⭐⭐ THREE DETAILS THAT SEPARATE ANSWERS:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ START LOW AND CLIMB. ⭐ Starting at 1080p means a
⭐     slow first segment and a blank screen — ⭐⭐ AND STARTUP
⭐     TIME PREDICTS ABANDONMENT MORE THAN QUALITY DOES.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ BE ASYMMETRIC: DROP QUALITY FAST, RAISE IT SLOWLY.
⭐     ⭐ A rebuffer costs far more than a few seconds of 480p.
⭐   ⭐ Prefetch the next 1–2 segments only; ⭐⭐ AGGRESSIVE
⭐     PREFETCH WASTES EGRESS ON VIDEO NOBODY WATCHES —
⭐     ⭐ and on Part 1's numbers, that waste is measured in
⭐     millions.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐⭐ The CDN, which is most of the system

```
⭐⭐ WHY IT WORKS SO WELL HERE: ⭐ segments are ***immutable***
⭐   and ***content-addressed***, so `max-age=31536000,
⭐   immutable` is safe (Day 327). ⭐⭐ THERE IS NEVER ANYTHING
⭐   TO INVALIDATE.

⭐⭐ HIT RATIO IS THE WHOLE COST MODEL:
⭐   ⭐ Popular video ⇒ ~99% hit.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ LONG TAIL ⇒ ***EVERY VIEW IS AN ORIGIN FETCH***, and
⭐     origin egress is 10–20× CDN egress.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ FIXES: ⭐⭐ TIERED CACHING (edge → regional shield →
⭐     origin) so ten edges cause ***one*** origin fetch rather
⭐     than ten; ⭐ pre-warm predictable hits (a new release);
⭐     ⭐ multi-CDN with cost/performance steering.

⭐⭐ PRIVATE CONTENT: ⭐ signed CDN URLs or signed cookies —
⭐   ⭐⭐ COOKIES ARE BETTER HERE, BECAUSE A VIDEO IS HUNDREDS OF
⭐   SEGMENT REQUESTS AND SIGNING EACH URL IS BOTH EXPENSIVE AND
⭐   FIDDLY. ⭐ One signed cookie authorises a path prefix.

⭐⭐ AND THE ANTI-PIRACY REALITY, stated honestly:
⭐   ⭐ Signed URLs stop casual hotlinking.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THEY DO NOT STOP SOMEONE WHO CAN PLAY THE VIDEO FROM
⭐     KEEPING IT. ⭐ That is DRM (Widevine/FairPlay), which is
⭐     a licensing and key-server problem, ⭐⭐ AND IT IS
⭐     EXPENSIVE AND STILL NOT ABSOLUTE. ⭐ Saying "signed URLs
⭐     are access control, not copy protection" is the honest,
⭐     scoring answer.
```

# Part 6 — ⭐ Measuring quality of experience

```
⭐⭐ THE FOUR METRICS THAT MATTER — ⭐ and the order is the
⭐   point:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ ***REBUFFER RATIO*** — time stalled ÷ time watched.
⭐        ⭐⭐ THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT NUMBER. Target < 0.5%.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ ***STARTUP TIME*** — click to first frame. ⭐ Every
⭐        extra second measurably increases abandonment.
⭐   ③ ⭐ ***AVERAGE BITRATE DELIVERED*** — quality actually
⭐        received.
⭐   ④ ⭐ ***SWITCH FREQUENCY*** — ⭐⭐ FLICKERING IS ITS OWN
⭐        DEFECT, and a throughput-only ABR produces it.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE TENSION TO STATE OUT LOUD: ***① AND ③ TRADE
⭐   AGAINST EACH OTHER, AND ① WINS.*** ⭐ Users forgive 480p;
⭐   they do not forgive a spinner.

⭐⭐ COLLECTION: the player beacons events (start, stall,
⭐   switch, complete).
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THAT IS A HIGH-VOLUME, LOSSY-TOLERANT WRITE PATH —
⭐     ***EXACTLY DAY 345'S CLICK PIPELINE***: batch, bounded
⭐     queue, pre-aggregate. ⭐ Same shape, different domain.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AND SEGMENT BY CDN, ISP, REGION AND DEVICE — ⭐ "the
⭐     rebuffer ratio is 4% on one ISP in one region" is the
⭐     finding you are actually looking for, and an aggregate
⭐     number hides it completely.
```

# Part 7 — ⭐ Bottlenecks and the summary

```
⭐⭐ WHAT BREAKS:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ EGRESS COST. ⭐ Not capacity — ***cost***. Per-title
⭐      encoding, better codecs (⭐ AV1 is ~30% smaller than
⭐      H.264 at equal quality, ⭐⭐ AT ROUGHLY 5–10× THE
⭐      ENCODING CPU — a straight trade of one-time compute
⭐      against perpetual egress, and at scale the egress wins),
⭐      ⭐ tiered caching, multi-CDN steering.
⭐   ② ⭐ Transcoding fleet at upload spikes ⇒ ⭐ queue depth,
⭐      spot capacity, priority by duration.
⭐   ③ ⭐ Origin storage cost ⇒ tiering, ⭐⭐ AND DELETING
⭐      RARELY-WATCHED RENDITIONS, REGENERATING ON DEMAND.
⭐   ④ ⭐ Manifest generation for very long videos ⇒ cache the
⭐      manifest too; it is requested once per session per
⭐      rendition switch.

⭐⭐ THE SUMMARY:
⭐   ***"It isn't a streaming problem, it's a file-serving
⭐      problem — segments plus a manifest over plain HTTP,
⭐      which is why a CDN serves the whole product. Transcode
⭐      by splitting the source into chunks so a forty-minute
⭐      job finishes in two, with every rendition segmented at
⭐      identical keyframes or the player can't switch cleanly.
⭐      Adaptation happens in the player, buffer-based in steady
⭐      state, dropping fast and rising slowly, because a
⭐      rebuffer costs far more than a few seconds of 480p. And
⭐      the number that governs everything is egress: eleven
⭐      petabytes a day, so per-title encoding saving twenty
⭐      percent is millions a year, and the CDN contract is
⭐      genuinely part of the architecture."***
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ Treating it as a persistent-connection streaming problem | It's static files over HTTP. That's what makes CDN delivery possible |
| ⭐⭐ Renditions with unaligned keyframes | The player cannot switch cleanly. The most common real transcoding bug |
| ⭐⭐ Serial transcoding | Chunk and parallelise: 360 independent jobs turn 40 minutes into 2 |
| ⭐ Uploading through the API | A 4 GB file must never touch your application |
| ⭐⭐ Throughput-only ABR | Oscillates visibly. Quality flicker is worse than steady mediocrity |
| ⭐ Starting at the highest rendition | Slow first segment and a blank screen; startup time predicts abandonment |
| ⭐⭐ Symmetric quality changes | Drop fast, rise slowly. A rebuffer costs far more than 480p |
| ⭐ Aggressive prefetch | Wasted egress on video nobody watches — measured in millions at this scale |
| ⭐⭐ Ignoring the long tail's CDN miss rate | Every tail view is an origin fetch at 10–20× the egress price |
| ⭐ Signing every segment URL | Hundreds of requests per video. Use a signed cookie on a path prefix |
| ⭐⭐ Claiming signed URLs prevent piracy | They're access control, not copy protection. That's DRM, and it's not absolute |
| ⭐ Reporting a single aggregate rebuffer ratio | It hides "4% on one ISP in one region", which is the finding you want |
| ⭐ One queue for all transcode jobs | A 30-second clip waits behind a 3-hour film |

## Interview questions

**1. "How does adaptive streaming actually work?"**
> ⭐⭐ The video is chopped into a few-second segments, each a separate file, listed in a manifest — **so
> the player just makes ordinary HTTP GETs, with no persistent connection and no stateful server**,
> which is exactly why a CDN can serve the entire product. ⭐ Each rendition has its own segment list,
> and the player can switch at any boundary — ⭐⭐ **provided every rendition is cut at identical
> timestamps with aligned keyframes, which is the most common real transcoding bug.**

**2. "How do you transcode at this volume?"**
> ⭐⭐ Split the source into chunks and transcode them in parallel — **a ten-minute video is sixty
> chunks times six renditions, three hundred and sixty independent jobs, so a forty-minute serial
> transcode finishes in two minutes.** ⭐ Every job idempotent and keyed by video, rendition and chunk;
> a durable state machine because workers die mid-job; separate queues by duration so a clip doesn't
> wait behind a film; ⭐ and spot instances, because this is batch, chunked and resumable, which is
> exactly what spot pricing is for.

**3. "Which ABR algorithm?"**
> ⭐ Hybrid. ⭐⭐ Throughput-based during startup when there's no buffer, then **buffer-based in steady
> state — because the buffer level already encodes bandwidth history, and what you're actually
> optimising isn't bitrate, it's not running out.** ⭐ Throughput-only oscillates and quality flicker is
> its own defect. ⭐ Then the asymmetry: **drop fast, rise slowly**, and start low, because startup time
> predicts abandonment more than quality does.

**4. "What's the dominant cost?"**
> ⭐⭐ Egress, by an enormous margin. ⭐ Five million viewing hours a day at 1080p is about **eleven
> petabytes a day — a million dollars a day at list price, tens of thousands at a negotiated CDN
> rate.** ⭐⭐ Which means **the CDN contract is part of the architecture**, and every ten percent of
> bitrate saved is millions a year. ⭐ So: per-title encoding, which typically saves twenty percent;
> AV1, which is thirty percent smaller for five to ten times the encoding CPU — a one-time compute cost
> against perpetual egress, and at scale the egress wins.

**5. "How do you handle the long tail?"**
> ⭐ Popular videos get ninety-nine percent CDN hit rates; the tail gets an origin fetch on nearly every
> view, and origin egress is ten to twenty times CDN egress. ⭐⭐ So **tiered caching — edge to regional
> shield to origin — so ten edges cause one origin fetch rather than ten.** ⭐ Pre-warm predictable
> hits. ⭐ And on the storage side, tier the tail to cold storage and **delete rarely-watched renditions
> entirely, regenerating them on demand**, which is a real technique.

**6. "How do you know the experience is good?"**
> ⭐ Four metrics, in order: ⭐⭐ **rebuffer ratio first, because that's the one users feel** — target
> under half a percent; then startup time; then average delivered bitrate; then switch frequency, since
> flicker is its own defect. ⭐ And I'd state the tension: **rebuffering and bitrate trade against each
> other and rebuffering wins** — users forgive 480p, they don't forgive a spinner. ⭐⭐ Collection is a
> player beacon pipeline — the same shape as click analytics: batched, bounded, pre-aggregated — ⭐ and
> **segmented by CDN, ISP, region and device, because an aggregate number hides "four percent on one
> ISP".**

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Reframe it in the first sentence: **this isn't a streaming problem, it's a file-serving
problem** — the video is chopped into a few-second segments listed in a manifest, and the player makes
ordinary HTTP GETs, which is exactly why a CDN can serve the whole product. Give the constraint that
makes adaptation work: **every rendition must be cut at identical timestamps with aligned keyframes, or
the player can't switch without a visible glitch** — and that's the most common bug in real pipelines.
On transcoding, give the scaling move with numbers: **split the source into chunks so sixty chunks times
six renditions is three hundred and sixty independent jobs, turning forty minutes into two**, on spot
instances, since it's batch and resumable. On adaptation, say where the decision lives and why:
**the player decides, buffer-based in steady state, because the buffer already encodes bandwidth history
and what you're really optimising is not running out** — drop fast, rise slowly, start low, because a
rebuffer costs far more than a few seconds of 480p. Close on the number that governs the business:
**eleven petabytes of egress a day, which is a million dollars a day at list price — so the CDN contract
is part of the architecture, per-title encoding saving twenty percent is millions a year, and AV1 trades
one-time encoding CPU against perpetual bandwidth, which at this scale is a trade you take.**
