# Day 396 💰

**P-396** · 💰 ⭐⭐ **Cost as an engineering constraint**

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: cost is a non-functional requirement exactly like latency — and the only
> reason it is not treated as one is that nobody gets paged for it, so the engineering work is not
> "being frugal", it is building the feedback loop that makes cost visible on the timescale of a
> decision rather than three weeks after it.**

---

# Part 1 — The reframe

```
⭐ COMPARE THE TWO NON-FUNCTIONALS:

⭐   LATENCY .... ⭐ has a dashboard, ⭐ an SLO, ⭐ an alert,
⭐               ⭐ an owner, ⭐ and a review when it regresses.
⭐               ⭐⭐ FEEDBACK IN SECONDS.
⭐   COST ....... ⭐ has a PDF, ⭐ which arrives three weeks after
⭐               the decision that caused it, ⭐⭐ AND GOES TO
⭐               SOMEBODY WHO CANNOT READ THE ARCHITECTURE.
⭐               ⭐⭐ FEEDBACK IN WEEKS, TO THE WRONG PERSON.

⭐⭐ ⇒ ***THAT ASYMMETRY IS THE ENTIRE PROBLEM.*** ⭐ Engineers
⭐   are not wasteful; ⭐⭐ THEY ARE UNINFORMED ON A DELAY LONG
⭐   ENOUGH THAT NOTHING CONNECTS CAUSE TO EFFECT.

⭐⭐ ⇒ SO THE WORK IS DAYS 373–374's MACHINERY POINTED AT
⭐   DOLLARS: ***A NUMBER, A DASHBOARD, AN ALERT, AND AN
⭐   OWNER.*** ⭐ Everything else in this day is downstream of
⭐   that sentence.

⭐⭐ AND THE COUNTERWEIGHT, STATED FIRST SO IT IS NOT AN
⭐   AFTERTHOUGHT: ***ENGINEER TIME IS MORE EXPENSIVE THAN MOST
⭐   INFRASTRUCTURE.*** ⭐ A week of work to save $50/month is a
⭐   ***bad trade*** and you should say so out loud. ⭐⭐ THE
⭐   PERSON WHO OPTIMISES A $30 LINE ITEM FOR THREE DAYS IS NOT
⭐   BEING RIGOROUS — ⭐ they are avoiding the $4,000 one because
⭐   it is harder. ⭐⭐ COST DISCIPLINE MEANS KNOWING WHICH NUMBER
⭐   IS WORTH YOUR WEEK.
```

# Part 2 — Where the money actually is

```
⭐ THE TYPICAL SHAPE OF A SMALL-TO-MID AWS BILL:
⭐   ⭐ COMPUTE ............... 40–60%
⭐   ⭐ DATABASE .............. 15–25%
⭐   ⭐⭐ DATA TRANSFER ........ 5–15% ⭐⭐ AND ALWAYS HIGHER THAN
⭐                             ANYONE GUESSES
⭐   ⭐ STORAGE ............... 5–15%
⭐   ⭐ OBSERVABILITY ......... 5–15% (Day 394)
⭐   ⭐ everything else ....... the rest

⭐⭐ AND THE FINDING THAT SURPRISES EVERY TEAM THE FIRST TIME
⭐   THEY LOOK PROPERLY: ***THE TOP LINES ON A SMALL BILL ARE
⭐   OFTEN NOT YOUR APPLICATION AT ALL.***
⭐   ⭐ NAT gateways ⭐⭐ (~$100/MO FOR THREE, BEFORE TRAFFIC —
⭐     DAY 383)
⭐   ⭐ Unattached EBS volumes and forgotten snapshots
⭐   ⭐ CloudWatch logs with no retention (Day 394)
⭐   ⭐ A dev environment nobody turned off
⭐   ⭐ Idle load balancers ($16–20/mo each, ⭐⭐ AND THEY
⭐     ACCUMULATE)
⭐   ⭐ Old RDS snapshots from instances deleted a year ago
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ SO THE FIRST HOUR OF COST WORK IS NEVER OPTIMISATION.
⭐     ***IT IS AN INVENTORY.***
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The elimination ladder

> Ranked by **return per hour of effort**. Work top down and stop when the next rung costs more than
> it saves.

```
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***DELETE THINGS.*** ⭐ Unattached volumes, orphan
⭐    snapshots, idle balancers, unused Elastic IPs, empty
⭐    services, ⭐⭐ AND NON-PROD RUNNING AT NIGHT AND AT WEEKENDS
⭐    (that alone is a ***70%*** cut on those accounts — 168
⭐    hours a week becomes 50).
⭐    ⭐⭐ TYPICALLY 5–15% OF THE BILL FOR ONE AFTERNOON. ⭐ Nothing
⭐    else has this ratio.
⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***COMMIT.*** ⭐ A Compute Savings Plan at 60–70% of
⭐    steady baseline (Day 385). ⭐⭐ 20–30% OFF COMPUTE, ZERO CODE
⭐    CHANGE, ONE AFTERNOON. ⭐ Covers Fargate and Lambda too.
⭐ ③ ⭐ ***RIGHT-SIZE — WITH EVIDENCE.*** ⭐ From measured
⭐    utilisation over weeks, ⭐ including the peak, ⭐⭐ AND
⭐    INCLUDING BURST CREDITS (DAY 385). ⭐⭐ RIGHT-SIZING WITHOUT
⭐    MEASUREMENT IS HOW YOU CAUSE AN INCIDENT AND LOSE
⭐    PERMISSION TO DO COST WORK AT ALL.
⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***CHANGE THE ARCHITECTURE*** — ⭐ each of these is a
⭐    day from this stage, and each is a one-off with a
⭐    permanent effect:
⭐    ⭐ S3 gateway endpoint → NAT processing on S3 goes to zero
⭐      (Day 384)
⭐    ⭐ CloudFront in front of S3 → cheaper egress ***and*** a
⭐      cache (Day 392)
⭐    ⭐ Graviton → ~20% off compute (Day 385)
⭐    ⭐ gp2 → gp3 → ~20% off block storage (Day 385)
⭐    ⭐ Two lifecycle rules per bucket (Day 386)
⭐    ⭐ Log retention and log class (Day 394)
⭐    ⭐⭐ CO-LOCATE CHATTY SERVICES IN ONE AZ (Day 380)
⭐ ⑤ ⭐ ***REWRITE CODE.*** ⭐⭐ LAST, BECAUSE IT IS THE MOST
⭐    EFFORT PER DOLLAR — ⭐ with one exception: ⭐⭐ WHEN THE COST
⭐    IS ***ALGORITHMIC***. ⭐ An N+1 that becomes a million extra
⭐    DynamoDB reads, ⭐ a loop doing 50M small S3 PUTs
⭐    (Day 386), ⭐ a query scanning a whole unpartitioned table
⭐    in Athena. ⭐⭐ THOSE ARE BUGS THAT HAPPEN TO ARRIVE AS AN
⭐    INVOICE, AND THEY GO TO THE TOP OF THE LADDER, NOT THE
⭐    BOTTOM.
```

# Part 4 — The tools, and the prerequisite

```
⭐⭐ ***TAGGING IS THE PREREQUISITE FOR EVERY OTHER TOOL.***
⭐   ⭐ Without `service`, `env` and `owner` on every resource,
⭐     ⭐⭐ COST EXPLORER CAN TELL YOU THAT EC2 COSTS $8,000 AND
⭐     NOTHING ELSE. ⭐ That is not actionable.
⭐   ⭐ Cost allocation tags must be ⭐⭐ ***ACTIVATED*** IN THE
⭐     BILLING CONSOLE AND TAKE ~24 HOURS TO APPEAR — ⭐ and they
⭐     are ⭐⭐ NOT RETROACTIVE, ⭐ which is the real reason Day 380
⭐     said to tag from the first resource.
⭐   ⭐ Enforce with a tag policy or a Terraform
⭐     `default_tags` block ⭐⭐ SO IT CANNOT BE FORGOTTEN.

⭐ COST EXPLORER — ⭐ group by service, tag, usage type. ⭐⭐ START
⭐   WITH "GROUP BY USAGE TYPE" ON YOUR BIGGEST SERVICE; ⭐ that
⭐   is where the surprise lives.
⭐ ⭐⭐ COST ANOMALY DETECTION — ***FREE, AND THE CLOSEST THING TO
⭐   AN ALERT. TURN IT ON TODAY.*** ⭐ It catches the forgotten
⭐   `p4d` instance and the runaway Lambda ***this week***
⭐   instead of next month. ⭐⭐ IF YOU DO ONE THING FROM THIS DAY,
⭐   DO THIS.
⭐ ⭐ BUDGETS ***WITH ACTIONS*** — ⭐ not just a notification:
⭐   ⭐⭐ A BUDGET THAT STOPS NON-PROD INSTANCES OR DETACHES AN IAM
⭐   POLICY AT 100%. ⭐ For sandbox accounts this is the guard
⭐   rail that makes them safe to hand out.
⭐ ⭐ CUR → ATHENA — ⭐ the hourly, line-item truth. ⭐⭐ THE ONLY
⭐   WAY TO ANSWER "WHAT DOES ***THIS TENANT*** COST US?"
```

# Part 5 — ⭐⭐ Unit economics: the only number that survives growth

```
⭐⭐ ***TOTAL COST IS NOT A METRIC. IT IS A CONSEQUENCE OF TWO
⭐   THINGS — EFFICIENCY AND GROWTH — AND IT CANNOT DISTINGUISH
⭐   THEM.***
⭐   ⭐ "Our bill doubled" → ⭐⭐ ALARMING AND MEANINGLESS.
⭐   ⭐ "Our cost per order fell 30% while volume tripled" →
⭐     ⭐⭐ THE ACTUAL STORY, AND IT IS A GOOD ONE.

⭐ PICK ***ONE*** DENOMINATOR THAT MATCHES YOUR BUSINESS:
⭐   ⭐ cost per 1,000 requests
⭐   ⭐ cost per monthly active user
⭐   ⭐ cost per order / per document / per GB ingested
⭐   ⭐ cost per tenant ⭐⭐ (AND THIS ONE ANSWERS "IS THIS
⭐     CUSTOMER PROFITABLE?", WHICH IS A QUESTION ENGINEERING
⭐     CAN SUDDENLY ANSWER AND FINANCE CANNOT)

⭐⭐ THEN DO THE THING THAT MAKES IT REAL: ***EMIT IT AS A
⭐   METRIC, ON THE SAME DASHBOARD AS YOUR LATENCY SLO.***
⭐   ⭐ Monthly cost ÷ monthly units, published daily.
⭐   ⭐⭐ IT IS CRUDE, IT IS LAGGY, AND IT IS INFINITELY BETTER
⭐     THAN A PDF, ⭐ because it sits where engineers already
⭐     look.

⭐⭐ AND THE DECISIONS IT UNLOCKS, WHICH IS THE POINT:
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ IS THIS FEATURE PROFITABLE? ⭐ A free tier costing
⭐      $0.40/user/month against $0 revenue is a product
⭐      decision, ⭐⭐ AND IT IS ***YOUR*** NUMBER THAT MAKES IT
⭐      ONE.
⭐   ⭐ ② Should this be a managed service? ⭐ Now comparable.
⭐   ⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ DID THAT OPTIMISATION WORK? ⭐ Total cost cannot
⭐      tell you during growth. ⭐⭐ UNIT COST CAN.
⭐   ⭐ ④ What should we price at? ⭐⭐ ENGINEERING SUDDENLY HAS
⭐      SOMETHING TO SAY IN A COMMERCIAL CONVERSATION, ⭐ which
⭐      is unusual and valuable.
```

# Part 6 — Cost in the development loop

```
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***`infracost` IN THE PULL REQUEST.*** ⭐ A comment
⭐    saying "+$340/month" next to a Terraform diff, ⭐⭐ AT THE
⭐    MOMENT OF THE DECISION AND IN FRONT OF THE PERSON MAKING
⭐    IT. ⭐ This is the single highest-leverage thing in the
⭐    whole day: ⭐⭐ IT COLLAPSES THE FEEDBACK LOOP FROM THREE
⭐    WEEKS TO THREE MINUTES.
⭐ ② ⭐ ***A BUDGET PER ENVIRONMENT***, with an action on
⭐    non-prod.
⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***THE "WHAT DOES THIS QUERY COST" HABIT.*** ⭐ Athena
⭐    reports bytes scanned; ⭐ DynamoDB reports consumed
⭐    capacity; ⭐ S3 reports request counts. ⭐⭐ READ THEM. ⭐ A
⭐    query scanning 2 TB instead of 20 GB because it ignored
⭐    the partition is a ***bug***, ⭐ and it is visible in the
⭐    same console that ran it.
⭐ ④ ⭐ ***A MONTHLY 30-MINUTE REVIEW*** — top five line items,
⭐    biggest movers, unit cost trend. ⭐⭐ THIRTY MINUTES, WITH
⭐    THE PEOPLE WHO CAN CHANGE THE NUMBERS IN THE ROOM.
⭐ ⑤ ⭐⭐ AND THE CULTURAL ONE, WHICH DECIDES WHETHER ANY OF THE
⭐    ABOVE WORKS: ***MAKE THE NUMBER VISIBLE TO THE TEAM THAT
⭐    CAN CHANGE IT.*** ⭐ A bill that goes only to finance
⭐    produces nothing, ⭐ every time, ⭐⭐ REGARDLESS OF HOW GOOD
⭐    THE TOOLING IS.
```

# Part 7 — The traps, with numbers

```
⭐ ① ⭐ THE FORGOTTEN ENVIRONMENT. ⭐⭐ A DEMO STACK FROM A
⭐    PROOF-OF-CONCEPT: $400/MO × 36 MONTHS = $14,400 FOR
⭐    SOMETHING NOBODY HAS OPENED SINCE.
⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***CROSS-AZ CHATTER.*** ⭐ Two services split across AZs
⭐    doing 50 calls per request. ⭐ $0.01/GB ***each way***
⭐    (Day 380). ⭐⭐ IT NEVER APPEARS AS A LINE CALLED "OUR
⭐    ARCHITECTURE"; ⭐ it appears as `DataTransfer-Regional-Bytes`
⭐    ⭐⭐ AND PEOPLE ASSUME IT IS UNAVOIDABLE.
⭐ ③ ⭐ LOG RETENTION "FOREVER" (Day 394).
⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***THE OVER-PROVISIONED DATABASE NOBODY DARES TOUCH.***
⭐    ⭐ Often the single largest over-spend, ⭐ and it survives
⭐    because it is scary. ⭐⭐ THE ANSWER IS NOT COURAGE, IT IS A
⭐    MEASUREMENT: ⭐ Performance Insights for four weeks, ⭐ then
⭐    a resize in a maintenance window ⭐⭐ WITH THE ROLLBACK
⭐    REHEARSED.
⭐ ⑤ ⭐ IDLE PROVISIONED CONCURRENCY, ⭐ idle Aurora ACU floors,
⭐    ⭐ idle OpenSearch domains.
⭐ ⑥ ⭐⭐ ***"WE'LL OPTIMISE LATER" THAT BECOMES STRUCTURAL.***
⭐    ⭐ A region chosen badly, ⭐ a single-table design (Day 332),
⭐    ⭐ a per-tenant cluster. ⭐⭐ THESE STOP BEING COST DECISIONS
⭐    AND BECOME MIGRATIONS — ⭐ which is why cost belongs in the
⭐    design review, ⭐⭐ NOT IN A QUARTERLY CLEAN-UP.
⭐ ⑦ ⭐⭐ AND THE INVERSE TRAP, WHICH IS JUST AS REAL: ***COST
⭐    THEATRE.*** ⭐ Three days shaving $30 while ignoring a
⭐    $4,000 line, ⭐ or an availability regression traded for a
⭐    small saving. ⭐⭐ IF A CHANGE SAVES MONEY AND COSTS YOU AN
⭐    SLO, IT IS NOT AN OPTIMISATION — IT IS A DOWNGRADE WITH A
⭐    SPREADSHEET ATTACHED.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. ⭐⭐ "How do you think about cloud cost?"**
> ⭐ As a non-functional requirement like latency, ⭐⭐ **and the reason it isn't treated as one is
> feedback speed: latency has a dashboard, an SLO, an alert and an owner, while cost has a PDF that
> arrives three weeks after the decision and goes to someone who can't read the architecture.**
> ⭐ Engineers aren't wasteful, they're uninformed on a delay long enough that nothing connects cause
> to effect. ⭐⭐ **So the work is building the loop: a number, a dashboard, an alert and an owner.**
> ⭐ And I'd say the counterweight up front — ⭐⭐ **engineer time is more expensive than most
> infrastructure, so a week of work to save $50 a month is a bad trade, and knowing which number
> deserves your week is the actual discipline.**

**2. ⭐⭐ "Where would you start on a bill you've never seen?"**
> ⭐ With an inventory, not an optimisation. ⭐⭐ **The top lines on a small bill are frequently not the
> application at all: NAT gateways at roughly $100 a month for three before any traffic, unattached
> EBS volumes, snapshots from instances deleted a year ago, CloudWatch logs with no retention, idle
> load balancers, and a dev environment nobody turned off.** ⭐ Deleting things is the highest
> return-per-hour rung on the ladder — typically 5 to 15% of the bill for one afternoon — ⭐⭐ **and
> scheduling non-prod to run only in working hours cuts those accounts by about 70%, because 168 hours
> a week becomes 50.** ⭐ Then commit with a Savings Plan, then right-size with evidence, then change
> the architecture.

**3. "What's the most valuable cost tool?"**
> ⭐⭐ **Cost Anomaly Detection, because it's free and it's the closest thing to an alert** — it catches
> the forgotten GPU instance or the runaway Lambda this week rather than next month. ⭐ Then
> `infracost` in the pull request: ⭐⭐ **a comment saying "+$340/month" next to a Terraform diff, at the
> moment of the decision, in front of the person making it — that collapses the feedback loop from
> three weeks to three minutes.** ⭐ And underneath all of it, tagging, ⭐⭐ **because without
> `service`, `env` and `owner`, Cost Explorer can only tell you that EC2 costs $8,000, which isn't
> actionable — and cost allocation tags aren't retroactive, which is why you tag from the first
> resource.**

**4. ⭐⭐ "Your bill doubled. Is that bad?"**
> ⭐ I can't tell from that number, and that's the point. ⭐⭐ **Total cost is a consequence of
> efficiency and growth and can't distinguish them — so the metric is cost per unit: per thousand
> requests, per active user, per order, per tenant.** ⭐ "Our bill doubled" is alarming and
> meaningless; ⭐⭐ **"our cost per order fell 30% while volume tripled" is the actual story.** ⭐ I'd
> publish that daily on the same dashboard as the latency SLO — crude and laggy, ⭐⭐ **and infinitely
> better than a PDF, because it sits where engineers already look.** ⭐ It also unlocks decisions
> nothing else can: whether a feature is profitable, whether a specific tenant is profitable, and
> whether an optimisation actually worked during a growth period.

**5. "How do you handle the over-provisioned database nobody wants to touch?"**
> ⭐ It's often the single largest over-spend and it survives because it's scary. ⭐⭐ **The answer isn't
> courage, it's measurement: Performance Insights and utilisation over four weeks including the peak,
> then a resize in a maintenance window with the rollback rehearsed.** ⭐ I'd also be explicit that
> ⭐⭐ **right-sizing without measurement is how you cause an incident and lose permission to do cost
> work at all** — one bad resize sets the whole programme back a year.

**6. "When is cost optimisation the wrong thing to do?"**
> ⭐ When it's theatre. ⭐⭐ **Three days shaving $30 while a $4,000 line goes unexamined is avoidance
> dressed as rigour** — usually because the big one is harder. ⭐ And when it trades away an SLO:
> ⭐⭐ **if a change saves money and costs you availability, it isn't an optimisation, it's a downgrade
> with a spreadsheet attached.** ⭐ The genuinely urgent cases are the ones where the cost is
> algorithmic — an N+1 producing a million extra reads, a loop doing 50 million small S3 PUTs, an
> Athena query ignoring the partition — ⭐⭐ **those are bugs that happen to arrive as an invoice, and
> they jump to the top of the ladder rather than the bottom.**

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Open with the reframe and the diagnosis: **cost is a non-functional requirement like
latency, and the only reason it isn't treated as one is feedback speed — latency has a dashboard, an
SLO, an alert and an owner; cost has a PDF that arrives three weeks after the decision and goes to
someone who can't read the architecture. Engineers aren't wasteful, they're uninformed on a long
delay.** So the work is building the loop. Say the counterweight immediately, because it shows
judgement: **engineer time is more expensive than most infrastructure — a week to save $50 a month is
a bad trade, and someone spending three days on a $30 line while a $4,000 line goes unexamined is
avoiding the hard one.** Then the ladder, ordered by return per hour: **delete things first — NAT
gateways, unattached volumes, orphan snapshots, idle balancers, and non-prod running at nights and
weekends, which is a 70% cut because 168 hours becomes 50. Then commit with a Savings Plan. Then
right-size with evidence, because right-sizing without measurement causes an incident and loses you
permission to do cost work at all. Then architecture — gateway endpoints, CloudFront, Graviton,
lifecycle rules. Code last, unless the cost is algorithmic, in which case it's a bug that arrived as
an invoice and it goes first.** Then the metric that matters: **total cost can't distinguish
efficiency from growth. "Our bill doubled" is meaningless; "cost per order fell 30% while volume
tripled" is the story — and I'd publish unit cost daily next to the latency SLO, because it sits where
engineers already look and it answers whether a feature or a tenant is profitable.** Close on the two
things I'd do first: **Cost Anomaly Detection, free, five minutes; and `infracost` in the pull request,
which collapses the feedback loop from three weeks to three minutes.**
