# Day 464 🗣️

**P-464** · 🗣️ **Behavioural I** — ⭐⭐ ten stories cover every question they can ask

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: there are not fifty behavioural questions, there are about ten underlying
> stories asked fifty ways — so the work is not preparing answers, it is building a story bank and
> learning to route any question to the right one in three seconds.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ What this round is actually scoring

```
⭐ ***THE MISCONCEPTION:*** ⭐ "they want to know if I'm nice."
⭐ ***THE REALITY:*** ⭐ the behavioural round is a
⭐   ⭐⭐ ***RISK ASSESSMENT AND A LEVELLING EXERCISE***, ⭐ and it
⭐   is often the round that decides your offer band (Day 487).

⭐ ***THE FOUR THINGS BEING SCORED:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***SCOPE*** — ⭐ how big was the problem you owned?
⭐     ⭐ A task, a feature, a system, a team's direction?
⭐     ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE LEVELLING SIGNAL AND IT IS READ FROM YOUR
⭐       STORIES, NOT YOUR TITLE.
⭐   ⭐ ② ***JUDGEMENT*** — ⭐ did you make a reasonable decision
⭐     under uncertainty, ⭐⭐ AND CAN YOU EXPLAIN THE TRADE-OFF
⭐     YOU REJECTED?
⭐   ⭐ ③ ***RISK*** — ⭐ will you blame people, hide problems,
⭐     go quiet under pressure, or be impossible to disagree
⭐     with? ⭐⭐ EVERY "TELL ME ABOUT A CONFLICT" IS THIS
⭐     QUESTION.
⭐   ⭐ ④ ***COMMUNICATION*** — ⭐ can you make a complicated
⭐     thing understandable to someone who was not there?
⭐     ⭐⭐ WHICH IS MOST OF THE JOB.

⭐ ⭐⭐ ⇒ SO A STORY WITH NO DECISION IN IT SCORES NOTHING, ⭐ no
⭐   matter how impressive the outcome. ⭐⭐ "IT WENT WELL" IS NOT
⭐   A STORY. ⭐ "It was going badly and here is what I chose"
⭐   is.
```

# Part 2 — STAR, with the proportions corrected

```
⭐ ***THE STANDARD ADVICE IS RIGHT AND THE PROPORTIONS PEOPLE
⭐   USE ARE WRONG.***

⭐  ┌──────────────┬──────────┬──────────┬─────────────────────┐
⭐  │              │ TYPICAL  │ ⭐⭐ RIGHT │ WHY                 │
⭐  ├──────────────┼──────────┼──────────┼─────────────────────┤
⭐  │ ***SITUATION*** │ ⭐ 50%  │ ⭐⭐ 15%  │ ⭐⭐ NOBODY NEEDS THE │
⭐  │              │          │          │ ORG CHART. ⭐ Two    │
⭐  │              │          │          │ sentences of context│
⭐  │ ***TASK***      │ 20%     │ 10%      │ ⭐ what was at stake,│
⭐  │              │          │          │ and why it was hard │
⭐  │ ⭐⭐ ***ACTION*** │ 25%    │ ⭐⭐ 55%  │ ⭐⭐ WHAT ***YOU***   │
⭐  │              │          │          │ DID AND ***WHY***.   │
⭐  │              │          │          │ The whole answer.   │
⭐  │ ***RESULT***    │ 5%      │ 20%      │ ⭐ number + ⭐⭐ WHAT  │
⭐  │              │          │          │ YOU LEARNED         │
⭐  └──────────────┴──────────┴──────────┴─────────────────────┘

⭐ ⭐⭐ THE SINGLE MOST COMMON FAILURE IN THIS ROUND: ⭐ spending
⭐   ninety seconds on background, ⭐⭐ SO THE INTERVIEWER
⭐   INTERRUPTS BEFORE YOU REACH ANY DECISION — ⭐ and a story
⭐   with no decision in it scores zero.

⭐ ***THE ACTION SECTION IS NOT A NARRATIVE OF EVENTS.*** ⭐ It is
⭐   ⭐⭐ ***DECISION → ALTERNATIVE CONSIDERED → WHY YOU CHOSE ***
⭐   ⭐ repeated two or three times. ⭐⭐ "I DID X" IS WEAK. ⭐ "I
⭐   chose X over Y because Z, and I was wrong about Z" is
⭐   strong.

⭐ ***AND THE FIFTH LETTER NOBODY TEACHES — ⭐⭐ "L" FOR LEARNED:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ "WHAT I'D DO DIFFERENTLY IS…" ⭐ turns a good story
⭐     into a senior one, ⭐ because it demonstrates the thing
⭐     they cannot test directly: ⭐⭐ THAT YOU UPDATE.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The ten-story bank

```
⭐ ***BUILD THESE TEN AND YOU CAN ANSWER ANY BEHAVIOURAL
⭐   QUESTION.*** ⭐ Most map to two or three.

⭐  ① ⭐⭐ ***THE HARDEST TECHNICAL PROBLEM*** — the debugging or
⭐     design problem where the answer was not obvious.
⭐  ② ⭐⭐ ***THE FAILURE*** — you broke something, or shipped the
⭐     wrong thing. ⭐ Day 465. ⭐⭐ THE MOST IMPORTANT ONE.
⭐  ③ ⭐⭐ ***THE CONFLICT*** — you disagreed with someone and it
⭐     mattered. ⭐ Day 465.
⭐  ④ ***THE DEADLINE*** — you had to cut scope. ⭐⭐ WHAT DID YOU
⭐     CUT, AND HOW DID YOU DECIDE?
⭐  ⑤ ***THE AMBIGUITY*** — the requirements were unclear and you
⭐     had to choose anyway.
⭐  ⑥ ***THE INFLUENCE*** — you changed a decision without
⭐     authority to change it.
⭐  ⑦ ***THE HELP*** — you unblocked, mentored or reviewed
⭐     someone. ⭐⭐ SENIORITY IS PARTLY MEASURED IN OTHER
⭐     PEOPLE'S OUTPUT.
⭐  ⑧ ***THE LEARNING*** — you were out of your depth and got
⭐     competent fast.
⭐  ⑨ ***THE OWNERSHIP*** — you fixed something that was nobody's
⭐     job.
⭐  ⑩ ***THE TRADE-OFF*** — you chose the worse-looking option
⭐     for a good reason. ⭐⭐ THE MOST UNDER-PREPARED AND ONE OF
⭐     THE MOST REVEALING.

⭐ ⭐⭐ THE ROUTING SKILL: ⭐ when a question arrives, you are not
⭐   inventing — you are asking ***"which of my ten is this?"***
⭐   ⭐ "Tell me about a time you had to make a decision without
⭐     enough information" → ⑤, and ⑩ if ⑤ is thin.
⭐   ⭐ "When did you disagree with your manager?" → ③.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ "TELL ME ABOUT A PROJECT YOU'RE PROUD OF" → ⭐ ① or ⑨,
⭐     ***and never a story where nothing went wrong.***
```

# Part 4 — Sourcing stories when your experience is thin

```
⭐ ***THE OBJECTION:*** ⭐ "I have no professional stories."
⭐ ⭐⭐ YOU HAVE 488 DAYS OF THEM. ⭐ You just have not been
⭐   labelling them as stories.

⭐   ⭐ ***THE FAILURE (②):*** ⭐ the post-filter leak you
⭐     reproduced deliberately — ⭐⭐ "I BUILT A RETRIEVER, IT
⭐     PASSED EVERY TEST, AND THEN I WROTE A CANARY TEST AND
⭐     FOUND IT RETURNED ANOTHER TENANT'S DOCUMENTS WITH NO ERROR
⭐     ANYWHERE."
⭐   ⭐ ***THE TRADE-OFF (⑩):*** ⭐ choosing pgvector over a
⭐     dedicated vector database, ⭐⭐ AND BEING ABLE TO NAME THE
⭐     FOUR CONDITIONS UNDER WHICH YOU'D CHANGE YOUR MIND.
⭐   ⭐ ***THE AMBIGUITY (⑤):*** ⭐ picking a chunk size with no
⭐     ground truth — ⭐⭐ SO YOU BUILT THE GROUND TRUTH FIRST.
⭐   ⭐ ***THE LEARNING (⑧):*** ⭐ the first time you measured
⭐     abstention and it was 0.15.
⭐   ⭐ ***THE OWNERSHIP (⑨):*** ⭐ nobody asked you to measure
⭐     cost per request. ⭐⭐ YOU DID, AND FOUND THE p99 WAS A BUG.

⭐ ⭐⭐ AND SAY THE FRAME OUT LOUD, ONCE, WITHOUT APOLOGY: ⭐ "this
⭐   is from a personal project rather than a production system —
⭐   ⭐⭐ WHAT I CAN'T CLAIM IS THAT I'VE BEEN PAGED FOR IT AT
⭐   3AM." ⭐ Then continue normally.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ NAMING THE LIMIT COSTS YOU ALMOST NOTHING AND BUYS
⭐     CREDIBILITY FOR THE REST; ⭐ letting them discover it costs
⭐     you the round.
```

# Part 5 — The mechanics that make a story land

```
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***LENGTH: 90 SECONDS TO 2 MINUTES.*** ⭐ Beyond three,
⭐   you have lost them. ⭐ Time yourself — ⭐⭐ EVERYONE IS LONGER
⭐   THAN THEY THINK.

⭐ ② ***OPEN WITH THE HOOK, NOT THE CONTEXT.***
⭐   ⭐ ❌ "So in my second year, the team was structured as…"
⭐   ⭐ ✅ ⭐⭐ "I SHIPPED A CHANGE THAT SILENTLY LEAKED DATA
⭐     BETWEEN CUSTOMERS FOR TWO DAYS." ⭐ Now they are listening.

⭐ ③ ***PRESENT TENSE FOR THE HARD PART.*** ⭐ It puts the
⭐   interviewer in the room and makes the decision feel live.

⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***NAME THE ALTERNATIVE YOU REJECTED.*** ⭐ Without it
⭐   there is no judgement to score — ⭐ a decision with no
⭐   alternative is just an event.

⭐ ⑤ ***END ON THE NUMBER, THEN THE LEARNING.*** ⭐⭐ NOT ON A
⭐   MORAL. ⭐ "…so the leak test now runs on every commit. What
⭐   I'd do differently is write it before the feature, not
⭐   after."

⭐ ⑥ ⭐⭐ ***DO NOT REUSE ONE STORY TWICE IN A LOOP.*** ⭐ The
⭐   panel debriefs together. ⭐⭐ TWO INTERVIEWERS HEARING THE
⭐   SAME STORY READS AS ONE EXPERIENCE STRETCHED THIN — ⭐ which
⭐   is exactly why you need ten and not three.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. "Tell me about a project you're proud of."**

⭐ I'd deliberately not pick one where everything went well, because a story with no decision in it
scores nothing. ⭐⭐ Mine is the retrieval system, and the part I'm proud of isn't the recall number —
it's that I built the eval set before the index. ⭐ I spent the first two weeks arguing about chunk
sizes by reading outputs, got nowhere, then wrote thirty questions with known-correct sources in an
afternoon and had the answer in ninety minutes. ⭐⭐ And it wasn't the chunk size at all, it was the
splitting strategy. ⭐ The lesson I took was that measurement isn't the polish at the end, it's the thing
that unblocks everything else — you can't change what you can't see.

**2. "How do you handle a question you have no story for?"**

⭐ I route rather than invent. ⭐⭐ There aren't fifty behavioural questions, there are about ten
underlying stories asked fifty ways, so when something arrives I'm asking "which of my ten is this?"
rather than searching my whole career. ⭐ If genuinely none fits, I'd say so and offer the nearest —
"I haven't had exactly that, but the closest is X, and here's the part that transfers." ⭐⭐ That's far
better than a manufactured story, which collapses under follow-up because it has no specific details in
it.

**3. "You keep saying 'we'."**

⭐ Fair, and it's the more common failure than over-claiming — ⭐⭐ an interviewer can't score a "we", so
I've made myself invisible in my own story. ⭐ The shape I use is team-scope first, then my part: "on a
four-person project, I owned the retrieval service — chunking, hybrid search, the eval harness and the
isolation work." ⭐⭐ Generous about the team, precise about myself. ⭐ Naming where my work ended tends
to increase trust rather than reduce it.

**4. "Most of your examples are from a personal project."**

⭐ They are, and I'd say that up front rather than let it be discovered. ⭐⭐ What I can't claim is that
I've been paged for it at 3am or negotiated scope with a product manager under a real deadline. ⭐ What
it does have is the things that make the stories real: an eval harness, a CI gate I've watched fail, a
cost model with a p99 I investigated and found to be a bug, and a documented list of what the system
can't do. ⭐⭐ Naming the limit costs almost nothing and buys credibility for everything after it.

**5. "What makes a strong answer in this round, in your view?"**

⭐ A decision with an alternative attached. ⭐⭐ "I did X" is an event; "I chose X over Y because Z, and I
was wrong about Z" is judgement, and judgement is what's actually being scored. ⭐ The other thing is
proportion — most people spend half the answer on situation and get interrupted before any decision
appears. ⭐⭐ I aim for about fifteen percent situation and more than half on action-and-why, then close
on the number and what I'd do differently. ⭐ That last part is the only way to demonstrate that I
update, which they can't test directly.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

⭐⭐ **Two minutes, out loud: "tell me about a time something you built went wrong."**

Open on the hook, in the present tense. **"I'd built a retrieval service, all the tests passed, and I
was about to call it done. Then I wrote one more test — seed a second tenant with a document containing
a unique token, ask every question in my eval set as the first tenant, and assert that token never
appears. It failed immediately."**

Then the decision, with the alternative. **"The obvious fix was to filter the results after the search —
one line, and it made the test pass. I nearly did that. What stopped me was thinking about what
post-filtering actually means: the other tenant's vectors were searched, scored and pulled into my
process, and one line of application code was the only thing between them and the prompt. That's a
security control living in a place any refactor can move. So I put the predicate inside the SQL instead,
and then added row-level security underneath so the database enforces it whether or not my code
remembers."**

Then the part that makes it a real story. **"The thing that actually unsettled me was what the failure
looked like. There was no exception, no error-rate change, no latency change — just a fluent, well-cited
paragraph built from the wrong customer's data. If I hadn't written that specific test, nothing in the
system would ever have told me."**

Close on the number and the learning. **"The canary now runs on every commit, and I've deliberately
broken it twice to prove it catches the leak — once with the predicate removed, where it went red in
forty seconds, and once with RLS enabled, where it didn't leak at all. What I'd do differently is write
that test before the feature rather than after. I got lucky in the order I did things, and I'd rather
not rely on that again."**
