# Day 463 🔍

**P-463** · 🔍 **Defending the page** — ⭐⭐ never write a number you cannot reconstruct

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: the follow-up to every number is "from what to what, measured how, over
> what period" — and the only safe rule is that if you cannot answer that in sixty seconds, the number
> comes off the page, because your interviewer has built the same thing and knows what is plausible.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ Four legitimate sources of a number

```
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***MEASURED.*** ⭐ You ran it, you have the artefact.
⭐   ⭐ "recall@5 went 0.62 → 0.91 across 40 configurations on a
⭐     30-question eval set" ⭐⭐ — AND YOU HAVE THE PLOT.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THIS IS WHY STAGES 13/14 MADE YOU MEASURE EVERYTHING.
⭐     ⭐ Those numbers were never really about the system.

⭐ ② ***ESTIMATED, WITH THE METHOD STATED.*** ⭐ Legitimate, and
⭐   you must volunteer the method ***before*** being asked.
⭐   ⭐ "About 1.2p per question — that's 4,600 tokens at the
⭐     published input rate plus 500 out, ⭐⭐ AVERAGED OVER A
⭐     WEEK OF REAL TRAFFIC."
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ SAYING "ESTIMATED" OUT LOUD MAKES YOU MORE CREDIBLE,
⭐     NOT LESS. ⭐ It signals you know the difference.

⭐ ③ ***RELATIVE.*** ⭐ "Three times faster", "half the cost".
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ SAFE ONLY IF YOU KNOW BOTH ABSOLUTE ENDS, ⭐ because
⭐     the first follow-up is "from what to what?" and
⭐     ⭐⭐ "3× FASTER, FROM 90ms TO 30ms" IS A DIFFERENT
⭐     ACHIEVEMENT FROM "FROM 9s TO 3s".

⭐ ④ ***SCOPE.*** ⭐ Not an outcome — a ***fact***.
⭐   ⭐ "12 endpoints · 40k rows/day · 8,000 documents ·
⭐     4 engineers · 8 months · 3 tenants"
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ALWAYS AVAILABLE, ALWAYS DEFENSIBLE, AND FAR BETTER
⭐     THAN A PERCENTAGE YOU CANNOT RECONSTRUCT.

⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE FIFTH, WHICH IS NOT LEGITIMATE: ⭐ ***THE NUMBER
⭐   THAT SOUNDED RIGHT WHEN YOU WROTE THE BULLET AT 11PM.***
⭐   ⭐ It will be asked about. ⭐⭐ IT IS ALWAYS THE ONE THAT GETS
⭐     ASKED ABOUT, BECAUSE IT IS THE MOST IMPRESSIVE LINE ON THE
⭐     PAGE.
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The honesty line, and why it is drawn here

```
⭐ ***THE LINE:*** ⭐⭐ ***YOU MAY PRESENT YOUR WORK IN ITS BEST
⭐   HONEST LIGHT. YOU MAY NOT STATE A FACT THAT IS NOT ONE.***

⭐ ***ON THE RIGHT SIDE OF IT:***
⭐   ⭐ leading with your strongest project even if it was small;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ SAYING "I OWNED X" WHEN YOU OWNED X ON A TEAM OF FOUR;
⭐   ⭐ using scope when you have no outcome metric;
⭐   ⭐ describing a personal project as a personal project ⭐ and
⭐     still putting its measured numbers on the page;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ COUNTING WORK YOU DID BADLY THE FIRST TIME AND FIXED.

⭐ ***OVER IT:***
⭐   ⭐ a percentage nobody measured;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ "LED" WHERE YOU MEANT "PARTICIPATED IN";
⭐   ⭐ a teammate's work in the first person;
⭐   ⭐ a technology you read about listed as a skill;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ A DATE RANGE THAT HIDES A GAP.

⭐ ***WHY THE LINE IS DRAWN ***HERE*** AND NOT SOMEWHERE MORE
⭐   COMFORTABLE — THREE REASONS, IN INCREASING ORDER OF
⭐   PRACTICALITY:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ***IT IS THE TRUTH.*** ⭐ That is sufficient on its own.
⭐   ⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***YOUR INTERVIEWER HAS BUILT THE SAME THING.*** ⭐ A
⭐     claimed 10× latency improvement on a CRUD endpoint is
⭐     ⭐⭐ IMPLAUSIBLE TO SOMEONE WHO HAS OPTIMISED ONE, ⭐ and
⭐     they will find the seam in two questions.
⭐   ⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***A DISCOVERED EXAGGERATION ENDS THE PROCESS
⭐     IMMEDIATELY AND SILENTLY.*** ⭐ Nobody tells you why. ⭐ It
⭐     is not a scoring deduction — ⭐⭐ IT IS A CATEGORY CHANGE
⭐     FROM "CANDIDATE" TO "RISK", ⭐ and it can follow you
⭐     through a referral network.
```

# Part 3 — The "we" problem

```
⭐ ***THE TWO FAILURE MODES, AND BOTH COST YOU:***
⭐   ⭐ ❌ ***OVER-CLAIMING:*** ⭐ "Migrated the platform to
⭐     Kubernetes." ⭐⭐ ONE FOLLOW-UP ABOUT THE INGRESS
⭐     CONTROLLER AND IT COLLAPSES.
⭐   ⭐ ❌ ***SELF-ERASING:*** ⭐ "We built…", "the team
⭐     delivered…", "I helped with…" ⭐⭐ THE INTERVIEWER CANNOT
⭐     SCORE A "WE". ⭐ You have made yourself invisible on your
⭐     own resume, and this is much more common than
⭐     over-claiming.

⭐ ✅ ***THE SHAPE THAT WORKS — SCOPE THE TEAM, THEN CLAIM YOUR
⭐   PART:***
⭐   ⭐ "On a four-person migration to EKS, ⭐⭐ I OWNED THE
⭐     DEPLOYMENT PIPELINE AND THE READINESS/LIVENESS PROBES —
⭐     ⭐ including the deregistration-delay bug that was causing
⭐     502s during every deploy."
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THAT SENTENCE IS SIMULTANEOUSLY MODEST AND SPECIFIC,
⭐     ⭐ and the specific detail is what makes the modesty
⭐     credible.

⭐ ⭐⭐ THE GENERAL RULE: ***BE GENEROUS ABOUT THE TEAM AND
⭐   PRECISE ABOUT YOURSELF.*** ⭐ Interviewers trust candidates
⭐   who name their boundaries, ⭐⭐ BECAUSE SOMEONE WHO KNOWS
⭐   WHERE THEIR WORK ENDED PROBABLY KNOWS WHAT THEY ACTUALLY
⭐   DID INSIDE IT.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ The line-by-line defence drill

```
⭐ ***THE DRILL, PER BULLET. THIS IS THE WHOLE DAY.***
⭐   ⭐ ① write the bullet;
⭐   ⭐ ② write ***the three questions it invites***;
⭐   ⭐ ③ answer each in ***60 seconds, out loud, timed***;
⭐   ⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ IF ANY ANSWER IS THIN, ***FIX THE BULLET, NOT THE
⭐     ANSWER.*** ⭐ Rewrite it to invite a question you can
⭐     answer well.

⭐ ***WORKED EXAMPLE:***
⭐   ⭐ BULLET: "Raised retrieval recall@5 from 0.62 to 0.91 on
⭐     an 8,000-document corpus."
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ Q1: "HOW DID YOU MEASURE RECALL?"
⭐     ⭐ → 30 questions with known-correct source documents,
⭐       generated backwards from chunks and human-reviewed;
⭐       ⭐⭐ LABELLED BY DOC ID AND ANSWER SPAN RATHER THAN CHUNK
⭐       ID, so re-chunking didn't invalidate the set.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ Q2: "WHAT ACTUALLY MOVED IT?"
⭐     ⭐ → prefix 0.62→0.75 free, hybrid →0.82 for 15ms, rerank
⭐       →0.91 for 150ms. ⭐⭐ TWO-THIRDS OF THE GAIN WAS FREE.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ Q3: "WHY NOT HIGHER?"
⭐     ⭐ → the remaining 9% are multi-hop questions and split
⭐       tables. ⭐⭐ I KNOW BECAUSE I READ THE FAILURES.

⭐ ⭐⭐ NOTE WHAT Q3 DOES: ⭐ a candidate who knows ***why their
⭐   own number is not higher*** is in a different category from
⭐   one who only knows the number. ⭐⭐ PREPARE Q3 FOR EVERY
⭐   BULLET. IT IS THE ONE THAT WINS ROUNDS.
```

# Part 5 — Gaps, changes and the non-defensive answer

```
⭐ ***THE PATTERN FOR ALL OF THEM — THREE BEATS, THEN STOP:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ***the fact***, plainly, without apology;
⭐   ⭐ ② ***what you did with the time*** (one sentence);
⭐   ⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***A PIVOT BACK TO THE WORK.***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THEN STOP TALKING. ⭐ Over-explaining converts a
⭐     non-issue into the topic of the interview — ⭐ the length
⭐     of the answer signals how much you think it matters.

⭐ ***EXAMPLES:***
⭐   ⭐ ***GAP:*** ⭐ "I took nine months out for family reasons.
⭐     I used part of it to work through distributed systems and
⭐     LLM engineering properly — ⭐⭐ THE RETRIEVAL PROJECT ON MY
⭐     RESUME IS FROM THAT PERIOD."
⭐   ⭐ ***CAREER CHANGE:*** ⭐ don't apologise for the prior
⭐     career; ⭐⭐ NAME THE ONE THING IT GAVE YOU THAT A
⭐     CS-GRADUATE COLLEAGUE LACKS ⭐ — domain knowledge, dealing
⭐     with users, working to a deadline that had money on it.
⭐   ⭐ ***SHORT TENURE:*** ⭐ "The role wasn't what was described
⭐     — I was hired for backend and the work was manual data
⭐     entry. ⭐⭐ I LEFT AFTER FIVE MONTHS AND I'D MAKE THE SAME
⭐     DECISION AGAIN." ⭐ Never criticise the people. ⭐⭐ NAMING
⭐     THE MISMATCH IS FINE; BLAMING INDIVIDUALS READS AS A
⭐     PREVIEW OF HOW YOU WILL DESCRIBE ***THEM***.
⭐   ⭐ ***NO PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE:*** ⭐⭐ LEAD WITH THE
⭐     PROJECT AND ITS MEASUREMENTS. ⭐ A personal project with a
⭐     real eval harness, a CI gate and a cost model is worth
⭐     more than a job title, ⭐⭐ AND IT IS MORE THAN MOST
⭐     JUNIORS BRING.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. "You claim recall went from 0.62 to 0.91. How did you measure that?"**

⭐ Thirty questions with known-correct source documents. ⭐⭐ I built them backwards — sampled chunks
stratified by document type, had a model write the question each one answers in the words a user would
type, then human-reviewed about a fifth to delete the ambiguous and trivially keyword-matched ones.
⭐ Three hours. ⭐⭐ One detail mattered: I labelled the doc id and a verbatim answer span rather than the
chunk id, because chunk ids stop meaning anything the moment you change the chunker — and changing the
chunker was the whole experiment. ⭐ Then recall@5 with no generation involved, so nothing could
confound it.

**2. "Why isn't it higher?"**

⭐ Because I read the failures. ⭐⭐ The remaining nine percent are two things: multi-hop questions that
need two documents, where a single embedding of a two-subject question lands between both and matches
neither well; and content that was in split tables before I made tables atomic. ⭐ Knowing that also told
me what *not* to do — those aren't prompt problems, so tuning the prompt was never going to touch them.
⭐⭐ Recall@8 is a ceiling on end-to-end accuracy, so nine percent of questions are unanswerable no
matter what model or prompt I use, and I stopped optimising the prompt once I measured that.

**3. "This bullet says 'led'. Who did you lead?"**

⭐ If I've written it, I can answer it — and if I couldn't, the word would be "built" instead. ⭐⭐ Being
one level too generous with a verb is the most common way to lose a round you'd otherwise pass, and it's
not because the claim is big — it's because the follow-up exposes it in two questions. ⭐ My rule is the
strongest verb that's *true*: contributed, built, owned, led, and I use the one that survives "who,
how many, and what did you decide?"

**4. "This was a team project. What did you actually do?"**

⭐ I'd have scoped that on the page already, because a "we" can't be scored — the interviewer literally
has nothing to grade. ⭐⭐ The shape I use is: name the team, then claim my part precisely. "On a
four-person project over eight months, I owned the retrieval service end to end — chunking, hybrid
search, the eval harness and per-tenant isolation. Generation and the frontend were other people."
⭐ Being generous about the team and precise about myself tends to increase trust rather than reduce it,
⭐⭐ because someone who knows where their work ended probably knows what they did inside it.

**5. "There's a nine-month gap here."**

⭐ Family reasons. ⭐ I used part of it to work through distributed systems and LLM engineering properly,
and the retrieval project on my resume is from that period. ⭐⭐ Then I'd stop, because the length of the
answer signals how much I think it matters — over-explaining a non-issue is what turns it into the
topic of the interview.

**6. "Everything on your resume is a personal project."**

⭐ It is, and I'd rather be specific about that than blur it. ⭐⭐ What I'd point at is that it has the
things production projects usually don't: an eval harness with a gold set, a CI gate that fails on a
cross-tenant leak — and I've seen it fail, which is why I know it works — a cost model with p50 and p99,
and a documented list of what the system can't do. ⭐ I can't claim I've been on-call for it at 3am, and
I won't. ⭐⭐ But I can tell you its recall, its abstention rate, its rebuild time and its five known
limitations, and that's a more useful conversation than most job titles produce.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

⭐⭐ **Two minutes, out loud: defend your single strongest bullet under hostile questioning.**

Take the number and pre-empt the first question. **"Recall@5 went from 0.62 to 0.91. I measured it on
thirty questions with known-correct source documents — generated backwards from chunks, stratified by
document type, human-reviewed to drop the ambiguous ones. Recall only, no generation involved, so
nothing could confound it."**

Then break it down rather than defending it as a lump, because the breakdown is the credibility.
**"The gain wasn't one thing. Putting the heading path into the text before embedding took it from 0.62
to 0.75 for a string concatenation. Hybrid search with reciprocal rank fusion took it to 0.82 for
fifteen milliseconds. A cross-encoder reranker took it to 0.91 for a hundred and fifty. Two-thirds of it
was free, which was the opposite of what I expected — I'd assumed the reranker would be the win."**

Then answer the question they haven't asked yet. **"And it's not higher because of two things I can
name. Multi-hop questions that need two documents, where one embedding of a two-subject question lands
between both and matches neither. And a long tail of content that was in split tables before I made
tables atomic. I know that because I read the failures rather than just the score."**

Close by stating the limit as a decision, not an excuse. **"Recall@8 is a ceiling on end-to-end
accuracy, so nine percent of questions are unanswerable regardless of model or prompt. Measuring that is
what stopped me spending another two weeks on prompt engineering for a problem prompts can't reach."**
