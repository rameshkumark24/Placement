# Day 451 🎚️

**P-451** · 🎚️ **Fine-tuning, honestly** — ⭐⭐ you are forking somebody else's model

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: fine-tuning has exactly one case where it clearly and repeatedly wins —
> making a small model behave like a big one on a narrow task — and the reason people reach for it
> instead is that they are trying to teach it facts, which is the one thing it does worst.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ What it changes, and therefore what it can fix

```
⭐ ***THE MECHANISM (DAY 417):*** ⭐ gradient updates to weights
⭐   on your examples. ⭐⭐ IT ADJUSTS ***THE DISTRIBUTION OVER
⭐   BEHAVIOURS***, NOT A STORE OF FACTS.

⭐ ⇒ ***WHAT IT IS GENUINELY GOOD AT:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***MAKING A SMALL MODEL IMITATE A BIG ONE ON ONE
⭐     NARROW TASK.*** ⭐ Distillation. ⭐⭐ THE ONLY CASE WITH A
⭐     RELIABLE BUSINESS ARGUMENT, AND IT IS A STRONG ONE.
⭐   ⭐ ② ***FORMAT AND STYLE THAT PROMPTING CANNOT HOLD*** —
⭐     ⭐ a house voice, a rigid report layout, a domain register
⭐     ⭐⭐ THAT TAKES 2,000 TOKENS OF INSTRUCTION AND STILL
⭐     DRIFTS.
⭐   ⭐ ③ ***A CLASSIFICATION OR EXTRACTION TASK WITH A CLOSED
⭐     OUTPUT SPACE*** — ⭐ often a fine-tuned encoder beats a
⭐     frontier LLM at 1/100th the cost.
⭐   ⭐ ④ ***UNUSUAL INPUT DISTRIBUTIONS*** — ⭐ OCR noise, chat
⭐     shorthand, a low-resource language, a proprietary DSL.

⭐ ⇒ ***WHAT IT IS BAD AT — AND ① IS WHY MOST PROJECTS FAIL:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***FACTS.*** ⭐ They are absorbed lossily, cannot be
⭐     cited, cannot be updated without retraining, ⭐⭐ AND THE
⭐     MODEL BECOMES ***MORE*** CONFIDENT ABOUT THEM WHILE BEING
⭐     NO MORE CORRECT (D429/449). ⭐ Use retrieval.
⭐   ⭐ ② ***REASONING DEPTH.*** ⭐ You cannot fine-tune a 7B into
⭐     a frontier reasoner with 3,000 examples.
⭐   ⭐ ③ ***ANYTHING THAT CHANGES WEEKLY.***
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ Distillation, with the arithmetic

```
⭐ ***THE WORKFLOW — FOUR STEPS, AND STEP 3 IS THE ONE PEOPLE
⭐   SKIP:***
⭐   ⭐ ① run the ***expensive*** model over real production
⭐     inputs, with your best prompt, and keep the outputs;
⭐   ⭐ ② ⭐⭐ FILTER WITH YOUR EVAL HARNESS AND VALIDATORS
⭐     (D425/435) — ⭐ keep only outputs that ***passed***;
⭐   ⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ HAND-REVIEW A SAMPLE. ⭐ You are about to bake these
⭐     into weights permanently. ⭐⭐ EVERY ERROR YOU LEAVE IN IS
⭐     AN ERROR YOU ARE ***TRAINING FOR***, AND IT WILL BE
⭐     REPRODUCED CONFIDENTLY AND CONSISTENTLY.
⭐   ⭐ ④ train the small model on the survivors; ⭐ evaluate on a
⭐     ***held-out*** set, ⭐⭐ NOT ON ANYTHING THE TEACHER SAW.

⭐ ***THE ARITHMETIC THAT JUSTIFIES IT:***
⭐   ⭐ frontier model: $2.50/M in, $10/M out
⭐   ⭐ small fine-tuned: ~$0.15/M in, $0.60/M out
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ≈ 16× CHEAPER, AND 2–4× FASTER
⭐   ⭐ at 5M tokens/day: ⭐ $12,500/mo → ⭐⭐ $780/mo
⭐   ⭐ minus: training runs, data prep (⭐⭐ THE REAL COST — 2–4
⭐     WEEKS OF SOMEONE'S TIME), hosting or per-token fine-tune
⭐     surcharges, and re-tuning forever (Part 5).
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ IT PAYS AT HIGH, ***STABLE*** VOLUME ON A ***NARROW,
⭐     STABLE*** TASK. ⭐ Below roughly $3–5k/month of spend on
⭐     that one task, it does not pay — ⭐⭐ THE ENGINEERING TIME
⭐     COSTS MORE THAN THE TOKENS.

⭐ ***AND THE PRECONDITION, WHICH IS ABSOLUTE:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ YOU CANNOT FINE-TUNE WITHOUT AN EVAL SET. ⭐ Not
⭐     "should not" — ***cannot***, ⭐⭐ BECAUSE YOU WILL HAVE NO
⭐     WAY TO KNOW WHETHER THE RESULT IS BETTER, AND "IT FEELS
⭐     BETTER" IS HOW TEAMS SHIP REGRESSIONS WITH CONFIDENCE.
```

# Part 3 — Data: quality, quantity, and what you are actually teaching

```
⭐ ***QUANTITY*** — ⭐ far less than people fear:
⭐   ⭐ style/format with LoRA: ⭐⭐ 500–2,000 GOOD EXAMPLES
⭐   ⭐ narrow extraction/classification: 1,000–5,000
⭐   ⭐ broad behaviour change: 10,000+
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ 200 EXCELLENT EXAMPLES BEAT 5,000 SCRAPED ONES,
⭐     RELIABLY.

⭐ ***QUALITY — FIVE THINGS THAT DECIDE IT:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ EVERY EXAMPLE TEACHES ***EVERYTHING*** IN IT. ⭐ If
⭐     10% of your outputs hedge unnecessarily, ⭐⭐ YOU ARE
⭐     TRAINING A HEDGING MODEL.
⭐   ⭐ ② ***DIVERSITY BEATS VOLUME*** — ⭐ 500 examples covering
⭐     20 input shapes beat 5,000 covering three.
⭐   ⭐ ③ ***DEDUPE.*** ⭐⭐ NEAR-DUPLICATES OVERWEIGHT A PATTERN
⭐     AND YOU WILL NOT NOTICE UNTIL PRODUCTION.
⭐   ⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ INCLUDE THE ***HARD*** AND ***ABSTAINING*** CASES.
⭐     ⭐ If no training example says "the document does not
⭐       contain this", ⭐⭐ YOU HAVE TRAINED THE ABSTENTION PATH
⭐       ***OUT*** OF THE MODEL (D422/449). ⭐ This is the single
⭐       most common self-inflicted wound in fine-tuning.
⭐   ⭐ ⑤ ***MATCH PRODUCTION FORMATTING EXACTLY*** — ⭐ same
⭐     system prompt shape, same field order. ⭐⭐ A MISMATCH AT
⭐     INFERENCE IS A SILENT QUALITY DROP.

⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE PRIVACY GATE (DAY 450): ⭐ training data is a
⭐   permanent, non-deletable copy of whatever it contains.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ***WEIGHTS CANNOT HONOUR AN ERASURE REQUEST.*** ⭐ Redact
⭐   before training, or you have created a store you can never
⭐   delete from.
```

# Part 4 — LoRA, and why it is almost always the answer

```
⭐ ***FULL FINE-TUNING:*** ⭐ update every weight. ⭐ Needs the
⭐   optimiser state in memory — ⭐⭐ ROUGHLY 12–16× THE MODEL
⭐   SIZE IN GPU RAM. ⭐ A full copy of the model per task.

⭐ ***LoRA:*** ⭐ freeze the base; ⭐ inject small trainable
⭐   low-rank matrices (`A` is d×r, `B` is r×d, r ≈ 8–64) into
⭐   the attention projections. ⭐⭐ TRAIN ~0.1–1% OF THE
⭐   PARAMETERS.
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ trains on one consumer GPU;
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE ADAPTER IS A FEW MB, SO YOU CAN HOST ***ONE***
⭐     BASE MODEL AND HOT-SWAP MANY ADAPTERS — ⭐ which turns
⭐     "one fine-tune per customer" from absurd into routine;
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ LESS CATASTROPHIC FORGETTING, BECAUSE THE BASE IS
⭐     UNTOUCHED;
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ***QLoRA*** = LoRA on a 4-bit quantised base. ⭐ Fits a
⭐     70B on a single 48GB card. ⭐ Slightly worse, dramatically
⭐     cheaper.

⭐ ***THE COST YOU MUST MEASURE ANYWAY — CAPABILITY
⭐   REGRESSION:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE MODEL GETS BETTER AT YOUR TASK AND WORSE AT
⭐     EVERYTHING ELSE — ⭐ instruction-following, refusals,
⭐     other formats, ⭐⭐ AND SOMETIMES ITS SAFETY BEHAVIOUR.
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ EVAL ***BROADLY***, NOT ONLY ON THE TARGET TASK.
⭐     ⭐ Keep a "did it forget how to be a model" suite: general
⭐       instruction following, refusal on obviously bad
⭐       requests, and one unrelated format.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE STEP EVERYONE SKIPS, AND IT IS HOW A
⭐     FINE-TUNE PASSES ITS OWN EVAL AND DEGRADES THE PRODUCT.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐⭐ The maintenance trap

```
⭐ ***YOU NOW OWN A FORK OF SOMEBODY ELSE'S MODEL, AND UPSTREAM
⭐   KEEPS MOVING.***

⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ THE BASE MODEL GETS DEPRECATED. ⭐ Providers retire
⭐     fine-tunable base models on ***their*** timetable. ⭐ Your
⭐     adapter is worthless against a new base — ⭐⭐ RE-TUNE AND
⭐     RE-EVAL, ON THEIR SCHEDULE, NOT YOURS.
⭐   ⭐ ② ⭐⭐ THE NEXT GENERATION OF BASE MODEL BEATS YOUR
⭐     FINE-TUNE ***WITH A GOOD PROMPT***. ⭐ This has happened
⭐     repeatedly and it will happen to you. ⭐ ⇒ re-run the
⭐     prompt-only baseline every time a new model ships, ⭐⭐ AND
⭐     BE WILLING TO DELETE THE FINE-TUNE. ⭐ Sunk cost is
⭐     powerful here because someone spent a month on it.
⭐   ⭐ ③ ***YOUR DATA DRIFTS.*** ⭐ The fine-tune is frozen at
⭐     its training distribution; ⭐⭐ IT DEGRADES SILENTLY AS
⭐     INPUTS MOVE, ⭐ with no error and no alert. ⭐ Only the
⭐     nightly eval catches it (Day 427).
⭐   ⭐ ④ ***YOU MUST VERSION EVERYTHING*** — data, base, hyper-
⭐     parameters, adapter, eval results — ⭐⭐ OR YOU CANNOT
⭐     REPRODUCE THE MODEL YOU ARE SERVING.

⭐ ***SO THE HONEST ORDER OF ATTEMPTS:***
⭐   ⭐ ① a better prompt → ⭐ ② few-shot → ⭐ ③ better retrieval
⭐   → ⭐ ④ a stronger model → ⭐ ⑤ decomposition →
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ⑥ FINE-TUNE.
⭐   ⭐⭐ MOST TEAMS ATTEMPT ⑥ AT POSITION ②, AND THE TELL IS
⭐     THAT THEY CANNOT SAY WHAT THEIR CURRENT PROMPT SCORES.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. "When would you fine-tune?"**

⭐ One case clearly and repeatedly: making a small model behave like a big one on a narrow, stable task.
⭐⭐ That's about 16× cheaper per token and two to four times faster, so at five million tokens a day
it's twelve and a half thousand a month down to under a thousand. ⭐ I'd also fine-tune for format or
style that prompting can't hold — where you've got two thousand tokens of instructions and it still
drifts — and for closed-output classification, where a fine-tuned encoder often beats a frontier LLM at
a hundredth of the cost. ⭐⭐ What I wouldn't do is fine-tune to teach facts. ⭐ They're absorbed
lossily, can't be cited, go stale the day you ship, and the model becomes *more* confident without
becoming more correct. ⭐ That's retrieval.

**2. "What's the precondition?"**

⭐ An eval set — and I mean *cannot*, not *shouldn't*. ⭐⭐ Without one you have no way to know whether
the fine-tune is better, and "it feels better" is how teams ship regressions with confidence. ⭐ It's
also required mechanically for distillation, because step two of the workflow is filtering the teacher's
outputs through your validators and keeping only what passed. ⭐⭐ And I'd hand-review a sample on top of
that, because I'm about to bake these into weights permanently — every error I leave in is an error I'm
*training for*, and it'll be reproduced confidently and consistently.

**3. "How much data?"**

⭐ Less than people fear and better than people manage. ⭐⭐ Five hundred to two thousand good examples
for style with LoRA; one to five thousand for narrow extraction. ⭐ Two hundred excellent examples
reliably beat five thousand scraped ones. ⭐ What decides it is diversity over volume, deduplication —
near-duplicates overweight a pattern invisibly — and ⭐⭐ including the hard and *abstaining* cases.
⭐ That last one is the most common self-inflicted wound: if no training example says "the document
doesn't contain this", you've trained the abstention path out of the model, and you've done it right
after spending a stage learning how valuable abstention is.

**4. "LoRA or full fine-tuning?"**

⭐ LoRA almost always. ⭐⭐ You freeze the base and inject small low-rank matrices into the attention
projections, training a fraction of a percent of the parameters — so it fits on one consumer GPU, and
the adapter is a few megabytes. ⭐ That last part is the operationally interesting bit: one hosted base
model with hot-swappable adapters turns "a fine-tune per customer" from absurd into routine. ⭐⭐ It also
suffers less catastrophic forgetting, because the base is untouched. ⭐ QLoRA on a 4-bit base fits a 70B
on a single 48GB card — slightly worse, dramatically cheaper.

**5. "What breaks after you fine-tune?"**

⭐ Everything you didn't train for. ⭐⭐ The model gets better at your task and worse at
instruction-following, at other formats, at refusing obviously bad requests — sometimes its safety
behaviour degrades. ⭐ So I evaluate broadly, not just on the target task: a small "did it forget how to
be a model" suite covering general instruction following, refusal, and one unrelated format. ⭐⭐ That's
the step everyone skips, and it's how a fine-tune passes its own eval while degrading the product.

**6. "What's the long-term cost?"**

⭐ You now own a fork of somebody else's model and upstream keeps moving. ⭐⭐ Base models get retired on
the provider's timetable, and your adapter is worthless against a new base — so you re-tune and re-eval
on their schedule, not yours. ⭐ Your data drifts, and the fine-tune is frozen at its training
distribution, so it degrades silently with no error and no alert; only the nightly eval catches it.
⭐⭐ And the one that stings: the next generation of base model regularly beats your fine-tune *with a
good prompt*. ⭐ So I re-run the prompt-only baseline every time a new model ships, and I'm willing to
delete the fine-tune — which is hard, because someone spent a month on it. ⭐⭐ There's also a privacy
point: weights can't honour an erasure request, so training data is a permanent copy of whatever it
contains, and redaction has to happen before training or you've built a store you can never delete from.

⭐ ② ***DO THE ARITHMETIC*** for your own task: monthly spend
⭐   now, projected spend on a small model, data-prep weeks.

⭐   500 real inputs, ⭐ filter through your validators,

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

⭐⭐ **Two minutes, out loud: "would you fine-tune for this?"**

Start by narrowing it to the case that works. **There's one case where fine-tuning clearly and
repeatedly wins: making a small model behave like a big one on a narrow, stable task. That's roughly
sixteen times cheaper per token and two to four times faster — at five million tokens a day it's twelve
and a half thousand a month down to under a thousand. Format and style that prompting can't hold is a
second real case, and closed-output classification is a third, where a fine-tuned encoder often beats a
frontier model at a hundredth of the cost.**

Then name the case people actually bring, and refuse it. **What I wouldn't do is fine-tune to teach
facts, which is why most of these projects fail. Facts are absorbed lossily, can't be cited, go stale on
the day you ship, and the model gets more confident without getting more correct. That's retrieval. And
the precondition for any of it is an eval set — not should, *cannot*, because without one you have no
way to tell whether the result is better, and "it feels better" is how teams ship regressions
confidently.**

Then the data, with the trap. **Less data than people fear: five hundred to two thousand good examples
for a LoRA style task, and two hundred excellent ones beat five thousand scraped. Diversity over volume,
dedupe hard, match production formatting exactly. And include the abstaining cases — if no example says
"the document doesn't contain this", you've trained the abstention path out of the model, which is the
most common self-inflicted wound in this work.**

Close on ownership, because that's the part that decides it. **You end up owning a fork of somebody
else's model while upstream keeps moving. Base models get retired on the provider's schedule, so you
re-tune on their timetable. Your data drifts and the fine-tune degrades silently with no error — only
the nightly eval catches it. And the next generation of base model regularly beats your fine-tune with a
good prompt, so I re-run the prompt-only baseline on every model release and stay willing to delete a
month of someone's work. Plus weights can't honour an erasure request, so redaction happens before
training or you've built a store you can never delete from. So the order is: better prompt, few-shot,
better retrieval, stronger model, decomposition — and fine-tuning sixth.**
