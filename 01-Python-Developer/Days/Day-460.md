**P-460** · 🎤 **The AI Engineer interview** — ⭐⭐ demo or system?

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: every interviewer in this field is testing exactly one thing — whether you
> can tell the difference between something that worked once and something you can operate — and the
> whole difference is numbers, limits and failure modes you can name without being prompted.**

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ The one signal

```
⭐ ***A DEMO:*** ⭐ "I built a RAG chatbot over our docs. It uses
⭐   LangChain and Pinecone. It works really well."
⭐ ***A SYSTEM:*** ⭐ "Recall@8 is 0.91, so about 9% of questions
⭐   have no correct chunk and no prompt fixes those. Abstention
⭐   on unanswerable questions is 0.85. It costs 1.2p a question,
⭐   p99 is 40p and I know why. A third of user questions were
⭐   aggregations so those route to SQL. Cross-tenant leak count
⭐   is gated at zero in CI."
⭐   ⭐⭐ SAME PROJECT. ⭐ The second one gets the offer.

⭐ ***WHAT THE SECOND ANSWER PROVES WITHOUT CLAIMING IT:***
⭐   ⭐ you measured, so you can improve;
⭐   ⭐ you know the limits, so you will not oversell to a
⭐     stakeholder;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ YOU KNOW THE COST, SO YOU CAN BE TRUSTED WITH A
⭐     BUDGET;
⭐   ⭐ you found the failure modes, so you have run it in anger;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AND YOU ROUTED A THIRD OF TRAFFIC AWAY FROM THE
⭐     TECHNOLOGY YOU WERE EXCITED ABOUT, ⭐ which is the single
⭐     clearest seniority signal available.

⭐ ⭐⭐ THE THREE ROLE ARCHETYPES — ⭐ know which room you are in:
⭐   ⭐ ① ***PRODUCT / APPLICATION AI ENGINEER*** — ⭐⭐ 80% OF
⭐     ROLES. ⭐ This entire stage. RAG, agents, evals, cost,
⭐     safety.
⭐   ⭐ ② ***PLATFORM / SERVING*** — ⭐ Days 452–458. vLLM,
⭐     throughput, quotas, multi-provider.
⭐   ⭐ ③ ***APPLIED RESEARCH*** — ⭐ fine-tuning, data curation,
⭐     eval methodology. ⭐⭐ ASKS FOR ML DEPTH THIS TRACK DOES
⭐     NOT CLAIM — ⭐ say so, and point at what you ***can*** do.
```

# Part 2 — The six questions, and the shape of a strong answer

```
⭐ ① ***"DESIGN A RAG SYSTEM FOR X."***
⭐   ⭐ Weak: pipeline diagram, done.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ STRONG: PIPELINE ***PLUS*** THE FOUR THINGS NOBODY
⭐     VOLUNTEERS — ⭐ how you evaluate it, ⭐ the permission
⭐     filter, ⭐ cost per question, ⭐ and which questions it
⭐     ***cannot*** answer.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ OPEN WITH "WHAT'S THE CORPUS AND WHO CAN SEE WHAT?"
⭐     ⭐ — it reframes the whole conversation in one sentence.

⭐ ② ***"HOW DO YOU KNOW IT WORKS?"***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE HIGHEST-SIGNAL QUESTION IN THE FIELD. ⭐ Answer:
⭐     the two-stage split (recall vs faithfulness), the gold set
⭐     built backwards in an afternoon, the unanswerable set, and
⭐     the CI gates. ⭐⭐ "WE READ THE OUTPUTS AND THEY LOOKED
⭐     GOOD" IS A REJECTION.

⭐ ③ ***"WHAT DOES IT COST?"***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ IF YOU CANNOT ANSWER IN ONE SENTENCE, THE INTERVIEW
⭐     IS ALREADY DECIDED. ⭐ Unit cost, p50 vs p99, and what the
⭐     p99 turned out to be (a bug).

⭐ ④ ***"WHAT COULD GO WRONG?"***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ FOUR, IN THIS ORDER: ⭐ cross-tenant leak (silent),
⭐     prompt injection (no complete fix, so the trifecta),
⭐     confident fabrication (measured, not assumed), unbounded
⭐     cost (user-controlled).

⭐ ⑤ ***"IT'S TOO SLOW / TOO EXPENSIVE."***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE LADDER, FREE RUNGS FIRST — ⭐ and the line that
⭐     lands: ***"which of these requests didn't need a model at
⭐     all?"***

⭐ ⑥ ***"SHOULD WE FINE-TUNE?"***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ "WHAT KIND OF GAP IS IT?" ⭐ Facts → retrieval. Form →
⭐     prompting, then fine-tuning. Skill → a better model or
⭐     decomposition. Cost/latency on a narrow task → ⭐⭐ THE ONE
⭐     CASE WHERE FINE-TUNING CLEARLY WINS.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ Five sentences that mark seniority

```
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***"WE MEASURED IT."*** ⭐ …followed by a number, and by
⭐   the ***method*** that produced it.

⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***"HERE'S WHAT IT CAN'T DO."*** ⭐ Stated unprompted.
⭐   ⭐ Recall ceiling, abstention rate, revocation window,
⭐     rebuild time. ⭐⭐ NOBODY BELIEVES A SYSTEM WITH NO LIMITS,
⭐     AND VOLUNTEERING THEM IS THE FASTEST WAY TO BE BELIEVED
⭐     ABOUT EVERYTHING ELSE.

⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***"THAT'S A PRODUCT DECISION, NOT AN ENGINEERING
⭐   ONE."*** ⭐ "80% cheaper, accuracy 91→86, four hundred more
⭐   wrong answers a day, nine thousand a month saved — ⭐⭐ HERE
⭐   ARE BOTH NUMBERS, YOU CHOOSE."

⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***"WE TRIED IT AND DELETED IT."*** ⭐ HyDE was flat.
⭐   Semantic caching false-hit at 4%. Multi-agent lost to one
⭐   agent. ⭐⭐ A DELETION WITH A MEASUREMENT BEHIND IT IS
⭐   STRONGER EVIDENCE THAN ANY ADOPTION.

⭐ ⑤ ⭐⭐ ***"THAT ISN'T A RETRIEVAL QUESTION."*** ⭐ Or "that's a
⭐   check, not a boundary." ⭐ Or "a role label is a hint, not a
⭐   trust boundary." ⭐⭐ ONE PRECISE DISTINCTION, DELIVERED
⭐   CALMLY, RESETS AN INTERVIEWER'S ESTIMATE OF YOU.

⭐ ***AND THE TELLS THAT GO THE OTHER WAY:***
⭐   ⭐ ❌ naming frameworks instead of mechanisms;
⭐   ⭐ ❌ ⭐⭐ "WE USE RAG SO IT DOESN'T HALLUCINATE" — ⭐ wrong,
⭐     and Day 429 explains exactly why;
⭐   ⭐ ❌ no numbers anywhere;
⭐   ⭐ ❌ ⭐⭐ "WE PROMPT IT TO ALWAYS BE ACCURATE";
⭐   ⭐ ❌ describing a guardrail as a security boundary;
⭐   ⭐ ❌ ⭐⭐ "TEMPERATURE ZERO MAKES IT DETERMINISTIC";
⭐   ⭐ ❌ a system with no stated limits.
```

# Part 4 — The rounds you will actually sit

```
⭐ ***① THE PROJECT DEEP-DIVE*** — ⭐⭐ THE MOST IMPORTANT ONE.
⭐   ⭐ Structure every project answer as six beats:
⭐     ⭐ ***problem → constraint → what you measured →
⭐       what you chose → what it cost → what it can't do.***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE LAST TWO BEATS ARE WHERE EVERYONE STOPS EARLY AND
⭐     THEY CARRY MOST OF THE SIGNAL.

⭐ ***② LIVE CODING*** — ⭐ smaller than you fear:
⭐   ⭐ retry with exponential backoff ***and jitter***;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ CHUNK A DOCUMENT SENSIBLY (structure, not characters);
⭐   ⭐ cosine similarity / top-k without a library;
⭐   ⭐ parse and ***validate*** a model's JSON safely;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ WRITE `recall_at_k` — ⭐ six lines, and it shows you
⭐     have actually built an eval;
⭐   ⭐ a tool-calling loop with a step cap.

⭐ ***③ SYSTEM DESIGN*** — ⭐ Stage 9 plus five axes:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ COST PER REQUEST ⭐ · ⭐⭐ THE EVAL STRATEGY ⭐ ·
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE TRUST BOUNDARY (INJECTION) ⭐ ·
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE PERMISSION FILTER ⭐ · ⭐⭐ THE FALLBACK LADDER.
⭐   ⭐ Everything else — queues, caches, replicas, idempotency —
⭐     ⭐⭐ IS STAGES 9–12 UNCHANGED, AND SAYING THAT OUT LOUD IS
⭐     ITSELF A GOOD ANSWER.

⭐ ***④ DEBUGGING*** — ⭐ "it worked yesterday". ⭐⭐ WALK THE
⭐   LADDER: ⭐ did the provider change the model (output length
⭐   distribution)? did the index rebuild (retrieval scores)?
⭐   did someone change the chunker? is the repair rate up? ⭐⭐
⭐   NAME THE ***METRIC*** YOU WOULD LOOK AT FOR EACH.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. "Tell me about an AI system you've built."**

⭐ Six beats, and I make sure I reach the last two. ⭐⭐ Problem: internal support answers were scattered
across eight thousand documents. Constraint: twenty million tokens, so no context window holds it, and
documents have per-team permissions. What I measured: recall@5 on thirty real questions, dense-only at
0.62. What I chose: structure-aware chunking with heading prefixes, hybrid search with reciprocal rank
fusion, a cross-encoder reranker with an absolute floor — 0.91. What it cost: 1.2p a question at p50.
⭐ And what it can't do: nine percent of questions have no correct chunk, so no prompt fixes them;
aggregations route to SQL because top-k can't count; and permission revocation takes up to thirty
seconds because ACLs propagate through an outbox. ⭐⭐ That last beat is where most people stop early,
and it's where most of the signal is.

**2. "What separates a demo from a system?"**

⭐ Numbers, limits and failure modes you can name without being asked. ⭐⭐ A demo is "it works really
well". A system is "recall is 0.91 so nine percent is unreachable, abstention is 0.85, p99 cost is 40p
and I know why". ⭐ Same project — the second answer proves I measured it so I can improve it, I know
the limits so I won't oversell it to a stakeholder, I know the cost so I can be trusted with a budget,
and I've run it in anger. ⭐⭐ And the strongest single thing I can say is that I routed a third of
traffic *away* from the technology I was excited about, because those questions were aggregations and a
vector index can't count.

**3. "What's the question you'd most want to be asked?"**

⭐ "How do you know it works?" ⭐⭐ Because the answer distinguishes people immediately: a wrong answer
has two causes with opposite fixes, so I measure retrieval and generation separately — recall@k as a
ceiling and faithfulness for generation. ⭐ The gold set came from generating questions backwards from
chunks in an afternoon, labelled by doc id and answer span rather than chunk id so re-chunking doesn't
invalidate it. ⭐⭐ And twenty questions the corpus genuinely can't answer, where the only correct
behaviour is abstention — most systems score near zero there and find out from a customer. ⭐ "We read
the outputs and they looked good" is the answer that ends an interview.

**4. "What would you push back on in a design review?"**

⭐ Three things, calmly. ⭐⭐ A guardrail described as a security boundary — a classifier has a
false-negative rate, and a false negative is a bypass, so boundaries go underneath and checks go on top.
⭐ "Temperature zero makes it deterministic" — it's greedy sampling, not determinism, and it is not an
idempotency mechanism. ⭐⭐ And "we use RAG so it won't hallucinate" — retrieval changes *what* it
hallucinates about and introduces its own modes: retrieve nothing and it answers from memory, retrieve a
stale chunk and it's confidently wrong **with a citation**, which is worse than without one. ⭐ One
precise distinction delivered without point-scoring usually shifts the whole conversation.

**5. "What are you weakest on?"**

⭐ Training models. ⭐⭐ I can fine-tune with LoRA, curate a dataset and evaluate the result honestly, and
I know when fine-tuning is the wrong tool — which is most of the time people reach for it. ⭐ But I
haven't done pretraining, architecture work or serious ML research, and I wouldn't claim it. ⭐⭐ What I
do have is everything from the API boundary outward: retrieval, evals, agents, the trust boundary, cost,
serving and rollout. ⭐ For an applied-research role I'd be the wrong hire and I'd say so early rather
than waste both our time.

**6. "What would you do in your first month here?"**

⭐ Find out whether there's an eval set, and build one if there isn't — because every other improvement
is blocked on it. ⭐⭐ Without one you can't move to a cheaper model, change a prompt, swap providers or
change chunking, since you can't see the effect. ⭐ In parallel I'd get cost attribution by feature and
tenant, read twenty p99 requests by hand — they're usually bugs rather than power users — and check
whether the permission filter is inside the query and whether there's a cross-tenant test in CI.
⭐⭐ That's roughly two weeks of unglamorous work that makes the next six months possible.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

⭐⭐ **Three minutes, out loud: the project story, told as a system.**

Lead with the constraint, not the technology. **Internal support answers were spread across eight
thousand documents, about twenty million tokens — so no context window holds it, and "just put it in the
prompt" isn't a slow option, it isn't an option. And the documents have per-team permissions, which
turned out to be the hardest part.**

Then the measurement, because it licenses everything after. **The first thing I built wasn't the index,
it was thirty questions with known-correct sources, including six the corpus genuinely couldn't answer.
Dense-only recall@5 was 0.62. Prepending the heading path before embedding took it to 0.75 for free.
Hybrid search with reciprocal rank fusion — because identifiers and rare terms are where embeddings
reliably lose — took it to 0.82 for fifteen milliseconds. A cross-encoder reranker with an absolute
floor took it to 0.91. Two-thirds of the gain arrived before I spent anything, and getting that ordering
right is most of the skill. I tried HyDE, measured it flat, and deleted it.**

Then the part I'd want them to remember. **The security work is where I'd spend the most time again. A
missing tenant filter in SQL shows forty thousand rows and someone notices in minutes. The same bug in
retrieval returns a fluent, well-cited paragraph built from another customer's data — no exception, no
error-rate change, no latency change, because generation launders it into original prose. So the tenant
is a required keyword-only argument, the filter is inside the query, there's row-level security
underneath, and there's a canary in CI: tenant B holds a unique token in a topically identical document,
and every eval question asked as A must never surface it. We proved it by deleting the predicate in a
branch — CI went red in forty seconds — and again with RLS on, where it didn't leak at all.**

Close on limits and cost, unprompted. **What it can't do: recall@8 is 0.91, so nine percent of questions
have no correct chunk and no prompt fixes those — knowing that stopped us tuning prompts for a problem
prompts can't touch. About a third of the questions users actually asked were aggregations, which a
vector index can't answer at all, so those route to validated text-to-SQL and we show the generated
query. Permission revocation takes up to thirty seconds. Full rebuild is thirty-eight minutes, which is
our worst-case recovery time. And it costs 1.2p a question at p50 with a p99 of 40p — I read twenty of
those p99s and they were bugs, not power users.**
