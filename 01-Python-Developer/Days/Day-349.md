# Day 349 🕷️

**P-349** · 🕷️ **Design 5 — a web crawler** — ⭐⭐ the frontier, and politeness by structure

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** BUILD

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: a crawler is a distributed BFS over a hostile, infinite, adversarial graph
> — and the entire design lives in one component, the frontier, which must simultaneously prioritise
> what to fetch next and guarantee that no single host ever receives two concurrent requests, which
> is a scheduling constraint disguised as a queue.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐ 0–8: scope and the numbers

```
⭐⭐ FUNCTIONAL:
⭐   ① Crawl from seed URLs, extract links, follow them.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ RESPECT `robots.txt` AND CRAWL DELAYS — non-negotiable.
⭐   ③ Recrawl based on change frequency.
⭐   ④ Store raw pages for downstream processing.
⭐   ⭐⭐ OUT: indexing, ranking, rendering JavaScript
⭐     (⭐ mention it: a headless browser is ***50–100× the CPU
⭐     and memory of an HTTP fetch***, which changes the fleet
⭐     size by two orders of magnitude — ⭐⭐ SO IT IS A
⭐     SEPARATE, SMALLER, TARGETED CRAWLER, NEVER THE DEFAULT).

⭐⭐ NON-FUNCTIONAL:
⭐   ⭐ 1 billion pages/month ⇒ 10^9/2.5×10^6 s = ⭐⭐ ~400
⭐     PAGES/S SUSTAINED, call it 1,000/s with headroom.
⭐   ⭐ Average page 100 KB compressed ⇒ ⭐ 100 TB/month raw.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ POLITENESS IS A HARD CONSTRAINT, NOT A FEATURE: ONE
⭐     REQUEST AT A TIME PER HOST, AND OBEY `Crawl-delay`.
⭐   ⭐ Freshness: news in minutes, most sites in days/weeks.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE OBSERVATION THAT SHAPES EVERYTHING:
⭐   ***AT 1,000 PAGES/S ACROSS THE WEB, YOU ARE NOT
⭐      THROUGHPUT-BOUND — YOU ARE ***POLITENESS***-BOUND.***
⭐   ⭐ A single machine can open 1,000 concurrent connections
⭐     easily (Day 070's asyncio). ⭐⭐ THE HARD PART IS THAT
⭐     THOSE 1,000 MUST BE 1,000 ***DIFFERENT HOSTS***.
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The frontier: two queues, and why

```
⭐⭐ THE NAIVE FRONTIER: one big priority queue of URLs.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ FAILS IMMEDIATELY. ⭐ A page on `bbc.co.uk` yields
⭐     200 links, ***all to `bbc.co.uk`***, all high priority.
⭐     ⭐ Your next 200 fetches hammer one host. ⭐⭐ YOU HAVE
⭐     WRITTEN A DENIAL-OF-SERVICE TOOL WITH GOOD INTENTIONS.

⭐⭐ ***THE MERCATOR DESIGN — TWO STAGES***:
⭐   ⭐ ***FRONT QUEUES*** — ⭐ N queues by ***priority***. A URL
⭐     is assigned by importance (domain authority, change rate,
⭐     depth). ⭐ Selecting from them biases toward important
⭐     pages.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ***BACK QUEUES*** — ⭐ M queues, and ***EACH HOLDS
⭐     URLS FOR EXACTLY ONE HOST.*** ⭐ A worker takes a whole
⭐     back queue, so ⭐⭐ ONE-REQUEST-PER-HOST IS GUARANTEED
⭐     ***STRUCTURALLY***, NOT BY A LOCK OR A CHECK.
⭐   ⭐ A ***HEAP*** of (next_allowed_time, queue_id) says which
⭐     back queue is eligible next.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE WHOLE INSIGHT OF THE DAY: ***POLITENESS
⭐     IS ENFORCED BY THE SHAPE OF THE DATA STRUCTURE, SO IT
⭐     CANNOT BE FORGOTTEN, RACED, OR CONFIGURED WRONG.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Day 312's elevator lesson, in a different costume: put
⭐     the constraint in the structure, not in a check.

⭐⭐ DISTRIBUTION ACROSS MACHINES:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ PARTITION BY `hash(registrable_domain)`, NOT BY URL.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ ALL URLS FOR A HOST GO TO ONE CRAWLER, SO POLITENESS
⭐     NEEDS ***NO CROSS-MACHINE COORDINATION AT ALL***. ⭐ That
⭐     is the payoff, and it is worth saying explicitly.
⭐   ⭐ Use the ***registrable domain*** (eTLD+1) so
⭐     `a.example.com` and `b.example.com` share a budget —
⭐     ⭐⭐ OTHERWISE A SITE WITH 10,000 SUBDOMAINS GETS 10,000×
⭐     THE POLITENESS BUDGET, WHICH IS A REAL EVASION.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ Deduplication at two levels

```
⭐⭐ ① ***URL DEDUPLICATION*** — have I seen this URL?
⭐   ⭐ 10^9+ URLs. ⭐ A hash set of 64-bit hashes = 8 GB —
⭐     ⭐ borderline, and it grows.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ ***BLOOM FILTER*** IN FRONT: ~1.2 GB FOR 10^9 URLS
⭐     AT 1% FALSE POSITIVE.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND THE ERROR DIRECTION IS EXACTLY RIGHT: A FALSE
⭐     POSITIVE MEANS YOU ***SKIP*** A PAGE YOU HAVEN'T SEEN.
⭐     ⭐ You lose 1% coverage. ⭐ A false negative — refetching —
⭐     is impossible. ⭐⭐ THE CHEAP ERROR IS THE ONE THE
⭐     STRUCTURE MAKES, AND SAYING THAT IS THE POINT.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ URL NORMALISATION FIRST, OR THE DEDUP IS WORTHLESS:
⭐     lowercase host, strip default ports, sort query params,
⭐     ⭐ drop tracking params (`utm_*`, `fbclid`), ⭐ resolve
⭐     `.`/`..`, ⭐ decide on the trailing slash, ⭐ strip
⭐     fragments.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Without it, `example.com/a?b=1&c=2` and
⭐     `example.com/a?c=2&b=1` are two pages forever.

⭐⭐ ② ***CONTENT DEDUPLICATION*** — is this page a duplicate of
⭐   one I have?
⭐   ⭐ Exact: `sha256(normalised body)` ⇒ catches mirrors and
⭐     the same article on ten URLs.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ NEAR-DUPLICATE: ***SIMHASH***. ⭐ Documents differing
⭐     only in a timestamp or an ad slot produce hashes within a
⭐     small Hamming distance, ⭐ so you can detect
⭐     near-duplicates without comparing documents pairwise.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THIS MATTERS MORE THAN IT SOUNDS: A LARGE FRACTION
⭐     OF THE WEB IS NEAR-DUPLICATE, AND STORING IT ALL IS
⭐     PAYING FOR THE SAME PAGE HUNDREDS OF TIMES.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ The traps: how the web fights back

```
⭐⭐ ① ***CRAWLER TRAPS / INFINITE SPACES.***
⭐   ⭐ A calendar with a "next month" link — ⭐⭐ INFINITELY
⭐     MANY VALID, DISTINCT, NEVER-BEFORE-SEEN URLS.
⭐   ⭐ Session ids in URLs ⇒ every fetch yields a "new" page.
⭐   ⭐ Faceted search ⇒ combinatorially many filter URLs.
⭐   ⇒ DEFENCES, ⭐ and you need several:
⭐      ⭐ ⭐⭐ A ***PER-DOMAIN PAGE BUDGET*** — the single most
⭐        effective control. ⭐ No site gets more than N pages
⭐        per cycle, however many links it offers.
⭐      ⭐ Max path depth; ⭐ max URL length; ⭐ a cap on
⭐        distinct query parameters.
⭐      ⭐ ⭐⭐ DETECT LOW ***YIELD***: if the last 1,000 pages
⭐        from a domain produced almost no new content by
⭐        simhash, deprioritise the whole domain.

⭐⭐ ② ***`robots.txt` IS NOT SIMPLE.***
⭐   ⭐ Fetch it, ⭐⭐ CACHE IT (per host, ~24 h) — otherwise you
⭐     fetch it before every page and it becomes your highest-
⭐     traffic request.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ WHAT IF IT IS UNREACHABLE? ⭐ 404 ⇒ allow. ⭐ 5xx or
⭐     timeout ⇒ ***assume disallow*** and retry later —
⭐     ⭐⭐ FAILING OPEN HERE MEANS CRAWLING A SITE THAT ASKED
⭐     YOU NOT TO, WHICH IS THE ONE FAILURE WITH LEGAL AND
⭐     REPUTATIONAL COST.
⭐   ⭐ Honour `Crawl-delay` and `Sitemap:` — ⭐⭐ SITEMAPS ARE
⭐     FREE, HIGH-QUALITY FRONTIER SEEDS AND MOST CANDIDATES
⭐     NEVER MENTION THEM.

⭐⭐ ③ ***PATHOLOGICAL RESPONSES.***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ A 10 GB RESPONSE — CAP THE READ AND ABORT THE
⭐     CONNECTION, don't just set a timeout.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ A DECOMPRESSION BOMB — 1 KB of gzip expanding to
⭐     10 GB. ⭐ Cap the ***decompressed*** size (Day 267).
⭐   ⭐ Redirect loops ⇒ cap the chain at ~5.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ SSRF-STYLE TARGETS: A CRAWLED LINK POINTING AT
⭐     `http://169.254.169.254/` OR `http://localhost:6379/`.
⭐     ⭐⭐ YOUR CRAWLER IS A CONFUSED DEPUTY INSIDE YOUR OWN
⭐     NETWORK — ⭐ block private ranges after DNS resolution,
⭐     and re-check after every redirect.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AND SLOW-DRIP RESPONSES: A SERVER SENDING ONE BYTE
⭐     PER SECOND HOLDS A WORKER FOREVER. ⭐ Enforce a
⭐     ***total*** deadline, not just a read timeout (Day 336).
```

# Part 5 — ⭐ Freshness: recrawling without wasting the fleet

```
⭐⭐ THE PROBLEM: recrawling everything monthly wastes most of
⭐   your capacity on pages that never change, ⭐ and misses news
⭐   entirely.

⭐⭐ THE APPROACH:
⭐   ⭐ Track per-URL change history. ⭐⭐ ESTIMATE A CHANGE RATE
⭐     (a Poisson λ) AND SCHEDULE THE NEXT CRAWL SO THE
⭐     ***EXPECTED STALENESS*** IS BOUNDED.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ADAPT MULTIPLICATIVELY: unchanged ⇒ ×1.5 the
⭐     interval (cap at ~30 days); changed ⇒ ÷2 (floor at ~5
⭐     minutes). ⭐ Converges quickly and needs almost no state.
⭐   ⭐ Weight by importance: ⭐⭐ A HOMEPAGE THAT CHANGES DAILY
⭐     IS WORTH MORE THAN A FORUM POST THAT CHANGES DAILY.
⭐   ⭐ Use ***conditional requests***: `If-Modified-Since` /
⭐     `If-None-Match` ⇒ ⭐⭐ A 304 COSTS ~1 KB INSTEAD OF
⭐     100 KB, SO YOU CAN CHECK 100× MORE OFTEN FOR THE SAME
⭐     BANDWIDTH. ⭐ This is the cheapest freshness win available
⭐     and it is one header.

⭐⭐ AND THE STORAGE DESIGN:
⭐   ⭐ Raw pages ⇒ ⭐⭐ OBJECT STORAGE, IN ***BATCHED*** FILES
⭐     (WARC-style), NOT ONE OBJECT PER PAGE. ⭐ A billion tiny
⭐     objects costs ~$5,000 in PUT requests alone (Day 327)
⭐     ⭐ and makes listing impossible.
⭐   ⭐ Metadata ⇒ a KV store keyed by URL hash: last crawl,
⭐     etag, simhash, status, next_crawl_at.
```

# Part 6 — ⭐⭐ Python specifics: why this is a good asyncio problem

```
⭐⭐ CRAWLING IS ***ENTIRELY*** I/O-BOUND, ⭐ which is the case
⭐   where Python is genuinely excellent (Day 070).
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ONE PROCESS WITH `aiohttp` HANDLES 1,000+
⭐     CONCURRENT FETCHES ON ONE CORE.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THEN PARSING IS CPU-BOUND AND ***DESTROYS THE
⭐     EVENT LOOP***. ⭐ `BeautifulSoup` on a 100 KB page is
⭐     30–80 ms of pure CPU — ⭐⭐ AT 1,000 PAGES/S THAT IS 30–80
⭐     CORE-SECONDS PER SECOND, WHICH IS NOT A TUNING PROBLEM,
⭐     IT IS AN ARCHITECTURE PROBLEM.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SO: ***FETCH IN THE EVENT LOOP, PARSE IN A PROCESS
⭐     POOL.*** ⭐ Or better — hand raw bytes to a queue and let
⭐     a separate parser fleet consume it, so the two scale
⭐     independently.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ And use `selectolax` or `lxml` rather than
⭐     `BeautifulSoup` — ⭐⭐ ROUGHLY 10× FASTER, AND AT THIS
⭐     VOLUME THAT IS TENS OF MACHINES.

⭐⭐ THREE MORE THAT BITE:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ DNS IS A BOTTLENECK AND A BLOCKING CALL. ⭐ Cache
⭐     resolutions aggressively; use an async resolver.
⭐     ⭐ A synchronous `getaddrinfo` in an async crawler stalls
⭐     ***every*** concurrent fetch.
⭐   ⭐ Connection limits: `aiohttp`'s `limit_per_host` is a
⭐     second line of defence — ⭐ the frontier is the first.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ MEMORY: 1,000 CONCURRENT RESPONSES × 10 MB CAP =
⭐     10 GB IF THEY ALL ARRIVE LARGE. ⭐ Stream to disk above a
⭐     threshold rather than holding bodies in RAM.
```

# Part 7 — ⭐ Bottlenecks, ethics, and the summary

```
⭐⭐ WHAT BREAKS:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ THE FRONTIER'S SIZE. ⭐ 10^10 pending URLs × 100 B =
⭐      1 TB — ⭐ it does not fit in memory, so the back queues
⭐      spill to disk/Redis with only the heads resident.
⭐   ② ⭐ Politeness at the ***registrable domain*** level means
⭐      ⭐⭐ ONE HUGE SITE IS ONE QUEUE AND CRAWLS SLOWLY
⭐      FOREVER. ⭐ That is correct behaviour, and the fix is
⭐      commercial (an agreement, or their sitemap), not
⭐      technical.
⭐   ③ ⭐ Parser fleet CPU, per Part 6.

⭐⭐ ETHICS AND LEGALITY — ⭐ thirty seconds, and it is scored:
⭐   ⭐ Identify yourself in the `User-Agent` with a contact URL.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ OBEY `robots.txt` EVEN WHEN IT COSTS COVERAGE.
⭐   ⭐ Back off aggressively on 429/503 — ⭐⭐ AND TREAT A
⭐     RISING ERROR RATE FROM ONE HOST AS A SIGNAL THAT YOU ARE
⭐     THE PROBLEM.
⭐   ⭐ Provide a way to be contacted and to be excluded.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ A CRAWLER IS THE ONE SYSTEM WHERE YOUR BUGS LAND ON
⭐     ***OTHER PEOPLE'S*** INFRASTRUCTURE, AND SAYING THAT
⭐     UNPROMPTED IS A MATURITY SIGNAL.

⭐⭐ THE SUMMARY:
⭐   ***"It's a distributed BFS over a hostile graph, and the
⭐      whole design is the frontier. Front queues give
⭐      priority; back queues hold exactly one host each, so
⭐      one-request-per-host is structural rather than checked;
⭐      and partitioning by registrable domain means politeness
⭐      needs no cross-machine coordination at all. Dedup at two
⭐      levels — a Bloom filter for URLs, where the false
⭐      positive costs coverage rather than correctness, and
⭐      simhash for near-duplicate content. Then the traps:
⭐      per-domain budgets for infinite spaces, decompression
⭐      and SSRF limits on responses, and adaptive recrawl with
⭐      conditional requests so a freshness check costs a
⭐      kilobyte instead of a hundred."***
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ One global priority queue | 200 links from one page all point at one host. You built a DoS tool with good intentions |
| ⭐⭐ Politeness by lock or check rather than structure | It can be forgotten, raced or misconfigured. Put it in the queue shape |
| ⭐⭐ Partitioning by URL rather than domain | Politeness now needs cross-machine coordination it should never need |
| ⭐ Per-subdomain politeness | 10,000 subdomains means 10,000× the budget. Use the registrable domain |
| ⭐⭐ No URL normalisation | Reordered query params are two pages forever, and dedup is worthless |
| ⭐ Fetching `robots.txt` per page | It becomes your highest-traffic request. Cache per host |
| ⭐⭐ Allowing on a `robots.txt` 5xx | You crawl a site that asked you not to — the one failure with legal cost |
| ⭐ No per-domain page budget | A calendar with a "next month" link is an infinite crawl |
| ⭐⭐ No decompressed-size cap | 1 KB of gzip expands to 10 GB and kills the worker |
| ⭐⭐ Not blocking private IP ranges after DNS | A crawled link becomes SSRF against your own metadata service |
| ⭐ Read timeout without a total deadline | One byte per second holds a worker forever |
| ⭐⭐ Parsing in the event loop | 30–80 ms of CPU per page at 1,000 pages/s. Architecture problem, not a tuning one |
| ⭐ One S3 object per page | ~$5,000 in PUT requests for a billion pages, and listing becomes impossible |

## Interview questions

**1. "What's the hardest part?"**
> ⭐⭐ The frontier, and specifically that **politeness is a scheduling constraint disguised as a
> queue.** ⭐ A naive priority queue fails instantly: one page yields two hundred links to the same
> host, so your next two hundred fetches hammer it. ⭐⭐ The fix is structural — **front queues for
> priority, back queues holding exactly one host each, so a worker taking a whole back queue can't
> violate one-request-per-host** — plus a heap of next-allowed times to pick the eligible queue. ⭐ It's
> the same principle as putting an invariant in a type: make it unforgettable.

**2. "How do you distribute it?"**
> ⭐⭐ Partition by hash of the **registrable domain**, not the URL — then all of a host's URLs land on
> one crawler and **politeness needs no cross-machine coordination at all**, which is the whole payoff.
> ⭐ Registrable domain rather than hostname, or a site with ten thousand subdomains gets ten thousand
> times the politeness budget, which is a real evasion.

**3. "How do you avoid recrawling the same page?"**
> ⭐ Normalise first — lowercase host, sort query params, drop tracking parameters, strip fragments —
> or reordered parameters are two distinct pages forever. ⭐⭐ Then a Bloom filter: about 1.2 gigabytes
> for a billion URLs at one percent false positive, ⭐⭐ **and the error direction is exactly right — a
> false positive skips a page you haven't seen, costing coverage, while refetching is impossible.**
> ⭐ Then content-level dedup with simhash, because a large fraction of the web is near-duplicate and
> storing it is paying for the same page hundreds of times.

**4. "What's a crawler trap and how do you escape one?"**
> ⭐ An infinite valid URL space — a calendar with a "next month" link, session ids in paths, faceted
> search. ⭐⭐ You can't escape it by crawling harder, so the defence is a budget: **a per-domain page
> cap per cycle is the single most effective control**, plus depth and URL-length limits. ⭐ And a yield
> check: if the last thousand pages from a domain produced almost no new content by simhash,
> deprioritise the whole domain.

**5. "What if `robots.txt` returns a 500?"**
> ⭐⭐ Assume disallow and retry later. ⭐ A 404 means allow, but a 5xx or timeout means I don't know —
> and **failing open there means crawling a site that asked me not to, which is the one failure with
> legal and reputational cost.** ⭐ I'd also cache it per host for about a day, or fetching it before
> every page makes it my highest-traffic request, ⭐ and I'd read the `Sitemap:` directive, because
> sitemaps are free high-quality frontier seeds.

**6. "Why is this a good asyncio problem, and where does that break?"**
> ⭐ Fetching is purely I/O-bound, so one process with `aiohttp` handles a thousand concurrent
> connections on one core — this is where Python is genuinely excellent. ⭐⭐ It breaks at parsing:
> **BeautifulSoup on a hundred-kilobyte page is thirty to eighty milliseconds of CPU, so at a thousand
> pages a second that's thirty to eighty core-seconds per second** — an architecture problem, not a
> tuning one. ⭐ So fetch in the loop, hand raw bytes to a separate parser fleet, and use `selectolax`
> or `lxml`, which is roughly ten times faster and at this volume is tens of machines. ⭐ Also: a
> synchronous DNS lookup stalls every concurrent fetch.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Open with the reframing that decides the design: **at a thousand pages a second you
are not throughput-bound, you are politeness-bound** — a single machine opens a thousand connections
easily, but they must be a thousand *different* hosts. Then the frontier, which is the whole system:
a naive priority queue fails instantly because one page yields two hundred links to one host, **so it's
front queues for priority and back queues holding exactly one host each — politeness enforced by the
shape of the data structure, so it can't be forgotten or raced.** Add the distribution payoff:
**partition by registrable domain and politeness needs no cross-machine coordination at all.** Then
dedup at two levels, and make the Bloom filter argument properly: **the false positive skips a page you
haven't seen, costing coverage, while refetching is impossible — the cheap error is the one the
structure makes.** Then the hostility: **infinite calendars and faceted search, where a per-domain page
budget is the only defence that works**, decompression bombs, and crawled links pointing at your own
metadata service. Close on Python honestly: **fetching is where asyncio shines, and parsing is where it
dies — thirty to eighty milliseconds of CPU per page at a thousand pages a second is an architecture
problem** — and on the maturity point, that a crawler is the one system where your bugs land on other
people's infrastructure.
