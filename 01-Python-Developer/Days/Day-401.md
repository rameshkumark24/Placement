# Day 401 🪵

**P-401** · 🪵 **Kafka I** — the log, partitions, and the producer

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: Kafka is not a queue — it is a replicated append-only log with a cursor
> per consumer group — and every difference from a queue follows from the single fact that reading
> does not remove anything.**

---

# Part 1 — The log, and what follows from it

```
⭐ THE DATA STRUCTURE: ⭐ an append-only, ordered, immutable
⭐   sequence per partition. ⭐ Each record gets an ***offset***.
⭐   ⭐ Records are retained by ***time or size***, ⭐⭐ NOT BY
⭐   WHETHER ANYONE HAS READ THEM.

⭐⭐ FIVE CONSEQUENCES, AND EACH ONE IS A CAPABILITY A QUEUE DOES
⭐   NOT HAVE:
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***MANY INDEPENDENT CONSUMERS.*** ⭐ Each group holds its
⭐    own cursor, so adding a sixth consumer of the same stream
⭐    costs the producer nothing and changes nothing (Day 393's
⭐    fan-out, structurally).
⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***REPLAY.*** ⭐ Seek to an offset or a timestamp and read
⭐    it all again. ⭐ This is what makes a Kafka topic a
⭐    ***rebuildable source*** for derived stores (Day 359 shape
⭐    ⑦, Day 406).
⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***THE BROKER DOES NO PER-MESSAGE BOOKKEEPING.*** ⭐ It
⭐    does not track who read what; ⭐ the consumer does. ⭐⭐ THAT
⭐    IS WHY IT IS FAST: THE BROKER'S WORK IS SEQUENTIAL DISK
⭐    APPENDS AND `sendfile()` ZERO-COPY READS, ⭐ and both are
⭐    things the kernel is extremely good at.
⭐ ④ ⭐ Throughput is a function of disk sequential I/O and
⭐    network, ⭐ not of message count.
⭐ ⑤ ⭐⭐ ***AND THE COST: NO PER-MESSAGE ACK, NO PER-MESSAGE
⭐    RETRY, NO DELAY, NO PRIORITY.*** ⭐ A poison message blocks
⭐    its partition or gets skipped. ⭐⭐ THAT IS THE TRADE, AND IT
⭐    IS WHY DAY 404 EXISTS.
```

# Part 2 — Partitions: parallelism and ordering, in one object

```
⭐⭐ ***A PARTITION IS SIMULTANEOUSLY THE UNIT OF PARALLELISM AND
⭐   THE UNIT OF ORDERING. THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT SENTENCE
⭐   IN KAFKA.***

⭐ ⭐⭐ ORDERING IS GUARANTEED ***WITHIN A PARTITION ONLY.***
⭐   ⭐⭐ THERE IS NO TOTAL ORDER ACROSS A TOPIC AND THERE NEVER
⭐   WILL BE — ⭐ that is not a missing feature, ⭐⭐ IT IS THE
⭐   PRICE OF HORIZONTAL SCALE (DAY 334).

⭐ ROUTING: ⭐ `partition = murmur2(key) % num_partitions`
⭐   ⭐ (null key → sticky batching across partitions).
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ ***SAME KEY, SAME PARTITION, SAME ORDER.*** ⭐ So you
⭐     choose your ordering guarantee by choosing your key:
⭐     ⭐ key = `user_id` → that user's events are ordered
⭐     ⭐ key = `order_id` → that order's lifecycle is ordered
⭐     ⭐⭐ AND THAT IS ***ALWAYS*** WHAT THE BUSINESS ACTUALLY
⭐       NEEDS. ⭐ "We need global ordering" is nearly always
⭐       "we need per-entity ordering" said imprecisely
⭐       (Day 393).

⭐⭐ AND THE TRAP THAT MAKES PARTITION COUNT A ONE-WAY DOOR:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ***ADDING PARTITIONS CHANGES `% num_partitions`, SO KEYS
⭐   MOVE.*** ⭐ A key that was on partition 3 lands on partition
⭐   7 — ⭐⭐ AND ITS OLD EVENTS ARE STILL ON PARTITION 3, BEING
⭐   READ BY A DIFFERENT CONSUMER AT A DIFFERENT SPEED. ⭐ Your
⭐   ordering guarantee breaks across the resize, silently, for
⭐   in-flight keys.
⭐   ⭐⭐ AND YOU CANNOT ***REMOVE*** PARTITIONS AT ALL.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ SO OVER-PROVISION UP FRONT. ⭐ Pick from: peak
⭐     throughput ÷ per-partition throughput, ⭐ and desired
⭐     consumer parallelism (Day 402 — ⭐⭐ CONSUMERS PER GROUP ARE
⭐     CAPPED BY PARTITION COUNT).
⭐   ⭐ But not absurdly: ⭐⭐ EACH PARTITION IS FILE HANDLES,
⭐     MEMORY, LONGER REBALANCES AND MORE END-TO-END LATENCY.
⭐     ⭐ A few hundred to a few thousand per broker is the
⭐     practical band; ⭐ start at 6–12 for a normal topic.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The producer, and the one config fact that matters most

```
⭐ BATCHING: ⭐ `linger.ms` (wait this long to fill a batch) and
⭐   `batch.size`. ⭐⭐ `linger.ms=0` IS LOWEST LATENCY AND WORST
⭐   THROUGHPUT; ⭐ 5–20 ms typically multiplies throughput
⭐   several-fold for latency nobody notices.
⭐ COMPRESSION: ⭐ `lz4` or `zstd`, ⭐⭐ APPLIED PER ***BATCH***, SO
⭐   IT ONLY WORKS IF YOU ARE BATCHING. ⭐ Compression also
⭐   reduces disk and network, so it usually pays twice.

⭐⭐ ***AND NOW THE ACKS SETTING, WHICH IS THE MOST IMPORTANT
⭐   CONFIGURATION FACT IN KAFKA:***

⭐   ⭐ `acks=0` — ⭐ fire and forget. ⭐⭐ YOU WILL LOSE DATA AND
⭐     YOU WILL NOT KNOW.
⭐   ⭐ `acks=1` — ⭐ the leader wrote it. ⭐⭐ THE FAILURE: LEADER
⭐     ACKS, LEADER DIES BEFORE A FOLLOWER FETCHES, ⭐ a new
⭐     leader is elected without that record. ⭐⭐ THE PRODUCER WAS
⭐     TOLD "OK". THE RECORD IS GONE. ⭐ Silently.
⭐   ⭐ `acks=all` — ⭐ every ***in-sync replica*** has it.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND HERE IS THE LIE PEOPLE SHIP (DAY 334, NOW WITH ITS
⭐   MECHANISM): ***`acks=all` WITH `min.insync.replicas=1` IS
⭐   EXACTLY `acks=1`.***
⭐   ⭐ Because if the ISR has shrunk to just the leader, ⭐⭐ "ALL
⭐     IN-SYNC REPLICAS" ***IS*** THE LEADER. ⭐ The setting is
⭐     satisfied and the guarantee is not.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ THE CORRECT COMBINATION, AND IT IS THREE SETTINGS THAT
⭐     ONLY WORK TOGETHER:
⭐     ⭐⭐ `replication.factor = 3`
⭐     ⭐⭐ `min.insync.replicas = 2`   ← ***TOPIC-LEVEL***
⭐     ⭐⭐ `acks = all`                ← ***PRODUCER-LEVEL***
⭐   ⭐ Now: two replicas must have it before you are told OK,
⭐     ⭐ so one broker failure loses nothing, ⭐⭐ AND IF TWO
⭐     BROKERS ARE DOWN THE PRODUCER GETS
⭐     `NotEnoughReplicasException` INSTEAD OF SILENTLY LOSING
⭐     DATA. ⭐ That error ***is*** the feature (Day 403).
```

# Part 4 — The idempotent producer: what it does and does not cover

```
⭐ `enable.idempotence=true` ⭐ (default since Kafka 3.0, and it
⭐   implies `acks=all`, `retries=MAX`,
⭐   `max.in.flight.requests.per.connection<=5`).

⭐ MECHANISM: ⭐ the producer gets a ***producer id***, and each
⭐   record carries a ***sequence number per partition***. ⭐ The
⭐   broker rejects a duplicate sequence.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ SO A NETWORK-LEVEL RETRY — THE CLASSIC "I SENT IT, THE
⭐     ACK WAS LOST, I SEND AGAIN" (DAY 398's OUTCOME ③) — ⭐ no
⭐     longer produces two records.

⭐⭐ AND WHAT IT DOES ***NOT*** COVER, WHICH IS WHERE PEOPLE
⭐   OVER-CLAIM:
⭐   ⭐ ❌ Your application calling `produce()` twice because
⭐      ***your*** logic retried. ⭐⭐ THE BROKER CANNOT KNOW THOSE
⭐      ARE THE SAME EVENT.
⭐   ⭐ ❌ A producer ***restart*** — a new producer id, so the
⭐      sequence resets. ⭐⭐ MESSAGES BUFFERED AND UNCONFIRMED AT
⭐      CRASH TIME MAY BE RE-SENT AS NEW.
⭐   ⭐ ❌ Anything about the ***consumer*** side.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ SO IT REMOVES ONE SOURCE OF DUPLICATES, NOT ALL OF
⭐     THEM. ⭐ You still need an application-level key
⭐     (Day 335), ⭐⭐ AND SAYING SO IS THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN
⭐     UNDERSTANDING IT AND HAVING READ THE FLAG NAME.

⭐⭐ ORDERING NOTE: ***WITHOUT IDEMPOTENCE, `max.in.flight > 1`
⭐   PLUS A RETRY REORDERS MESSAGES.*** ⭐ Batch 1 fails, batch 2
⭐   succeeds, batch 1 is retried and lands after. ⭐⭐ WITH
⭐   IDEMPOTENCE, THE BROKER ENFORCES SEQUENCE ORDER UP TO 5
⭐   IN-FLIGHT, ⭐ so you keep both ordering and throughput.
```

# Part 5 — The Python producer, and the bug everyone ships once

```python
from confluent_kafka import Producer

p = Producer({
    "bootstrap.servers": BROKERS,
    "enable.idempotence": True,      # ⭐⭐ implies acks=all
    "compression.type": "lz4",
    "linger.ms": 10,                 # ⭐ batch for 10 ms
    "batch.size": 262144,
})

def _on_delivery(err, msg):
    if err:                          # ⭐⭐ THE ***ONLY*** PLACE
        log.error("produce_failed", extra={   # YOU LEARN IT FAILED
            "topic": msg.topic(), "key": msg.key(), "err": str(err)})
        FAILED.inc()
    # ⭐ else: msg.offset() is now known

try:
    for ev in events:
        p.produce(TOPIC, key=ev.aggregate_id.encode(),
                  value=ev.serialize(), on_delivery=_on_delivery)
        p.poll(0)                    # ⭐⭐ SERVES DELIVERY CALLBACKS
finally:
    p.flush(30)                      # ⭐⭐ NON-NEGOTIABLE
```

```
⭐⭐ ***THE #1 PYTHON KAFKA BUG, AND IT IS SILENT:***
⭐   ⭐⭐ `produce()` IS ***ASYNCHRONOUS***. ⭐ It appends to a local
⭐     buffer and returns immediately. ⭐⭐ IT DOES NOT RAISE ON
⭐     FAILURE AND IT DOES NOT MEAN "SENT".
⭐   ⭐⭐ A PROCESS THAT EXITS WITHOUT `flush()` DROPS EVERYTHING
⭐     STILL IN THE BUFFER. ⭐ No exception. ⭐ No log line.
⭐   ⭐⭐ AND IT HAPPENS AT EXACTLY THE WORST TIME: ***DURING A
⭐     DEPLOY***, WHEN SIGTERM ARRIVES (DAY 378) AND YOUR
⭐     SHUTDOWN PATH FORGOT THE PRODUCER.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ `flush()` IN A `finally`, ***AND*** IN YOUR SIGTERM
⭐     HANDLER, ***AND*** AN `on_delivery` CALLBACK THAT LOGS AND
⭐     COUNTS. ⭐ Without the callback you have no error path at
⭐     all.

⭐ CLIENT CHOICE, HONESTLY:
⭐   ⭐⭐ `confluent-kafka` — ***USE THIS.*** ⭐ librdkafka
⭐     underneath: fast, complete, ⭐⭐ AND THE ONLY ONE WITH
⭐     TRANSACTIONS (DAY 403).
⭐   ⭐ `aiokafka` — decent asyncio option; ⭐ fewer features.
⭐   ⭐ `kafka-python` — pure Python, ⭐⭐ MUCH SLOWER, LAGGING ON
⭐     PROTOCOL FEATURES. ⭐ Fine for a script, ⭐⭐ NOT FOR A
⭐     PIPELINE.
```

# Part 6 — Serialisation and the registry

```
⭐⭐ ***JSON IS THE DEFAULT AND IT IS THE WRONG DEFAULT FOR A
⭐   TOPIC THAT WILL OUTLIVE YOUR TEAM.***
⭐   ⭐ No schema, ⭐ no compatibility checking, ⭐ 3–10× the bytes,
⭐   ⭐⭐ AND THE NINTH FALLACY (DAY 398) ARRIVES AS A `KeyError`
⭐   IN A CONSUMER AT 2 A.M.

⭐ ⇒ ⭐ Avro or Protobuf (Day 400) with a ***schema registry***:
⭐   ⭐ the message carries a schema ***id***, not the schema;
⭐   ⭐⭐ THE REGISTRY ENFORCES A COMPATIBILITY MODE AT
⭐     ***REGISTRATION*** TIME, ⭐ so an incompatible change fails
⭐     in CI rather than in a consumer;
⭐   ⭐ `BACKWARD` (new consumer reads old data) is the usual
⭐     mode — ⭐⭐ BUT IF CONSUMERS UPGRADE BEFORE PRODUCERS YOU
⭐     WANT `FORWARD`, ⭐ and if you replay history you want
⭐     `FULL`. ⭐⭐ CHOOSE IT DELIBERATELY; THE DEFAULT IS A
⭐     DECISION YOU DID NOT MAKE.

⭐ ⭐ And put a ***header*** on every message with the trace
⭐   context (Day 394) and the event id ⭐⭐ — SO THE CONSUMER CAN
⭐   DEDUPE AND SO THE TRACE SURVIVES THE HOP.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. ⭐⭐ "Is Kafka a message queue?"**
> ⭐ No — it's a replicated append-only log with a cursor per consumer group, ⭐⭐ **and every difference
> follows from the fact that reading doesn't remove anything.** ⭐ That gives you many independent
> consumers for free, replay from any offset, and a broker that does no per-message bookkeeping — which
> is why it's fast, since its work is sequential appends and zero-copy reads. ⭐⭐ **And it costs you
> exactly what a queue gives: no per-message ack, no per-message retry, no delay, no priority — a
> poison message blocks its partition or gets skipped.** ⭐ That trade is the reason RabbitMQ and SQS
> still exist.

**2. ⭐⭐ "How does Kafka guarantee ordering?"**
> ⭐ It doesn't, at the topic level — ⭐⭐ **ordering is guaranteed within a partition only, and there's
> no total order across a topic and never will be. That isn't a missing feature; it's the price of
> horizontal scale.** ⭐ You choose your ordering guarantee by choosing your key, since the same key
> always hashes to the same partition. ⭐⭐ **Key by `user_id` and that user's events are ordered; key by
> `order_id` and that order's lifecycle is ordered — and that's always what the business actually
> needs. "We need global ordering" is nearly always "we need per-entity ordering" said imprecisely.**

**3. "How many partitions, and can you change it later?"**
> ⭐ From two numbers: peak throughput divided by per-partition throughput, and the consumer
> parallelism you want — since consumers in a group are capped by partition count. ⭐ And I'd
> over-provision, ⭐⭐ **because adding partitions changes the modulo, so keys move to different
> partitions while their old events sit on the original one being read at a different speed — the
> ordering guarantee breaks silently across the resize. And you can't remove partitions at all.**
> ⭐ Not absurdly, though: each partition is file handles, memory, longer rebalances and more
> end-to-end latency. ⭐ Six to twelve is a reasonable starting point for a normal topic.

**4. ⭐⭐ "What's the most important Kafka configuration?"**
> ⭐ The durability triple, and specifically the fact that ⭐⭐ **`acks=all` with `min.insync.replicas=1`
> is exactly `acks=1` — because if the ISR has shrunk to just the leader, "all in-sync replicas" is
> the leader.** ⭐ The setting is satisfied and the guarantee isn't. ⭐⭐ **The correct combination is
> replication factor 3, `min.insync.replicas=2` at the topic level, and `acks=all` at the producer** —
> and they only work together. ⭐ Then one broker failure loses nothing, ⭐⭐ **and if two are down the
> producer gets `NotEnoughReplicasException` instead of silently losing data. That error is the
> feature.**

**5. "Does the idempotent producer give you exactly-once?"**
> ⭐ No, and it's worth being precise. ⭐⭐ **It gives each producer a producer id and each record a
> per-partition sequence number, so the broker rejects a duplicate sequence — which removes duplicates
> caused by network-level retries, the classic "I sent it, the ack was lost, I sent again".** ⭐ What it
> doesn't cover: ⭐⭐ **your application calling produce twice because your own logic retried — the
> broker can't know those are the same event — a producer restart, which resets the sequence, and
> anything at all on the consumer side.** ⭐ So it removes one source of duplicates, not all of them,
> and you still need an application-level idempotency key.

**6. ⭐⭐ "What's the commonest Kafka bug in Python?"**
> ⭐ Not calling `flush()`. ⭐⭐ **`produce()` is asynchronous — it appends to a local buffer and returns
> immediately, it doesn't raise on failure, and it doesn't mean "sent". A process that exits without
> flushing drops everything still buffered, with no exception and no log line.** ⭐ And it happens at
> the worst possible time: during a deploy, when SIGTERM arrives and the shutdown path forgot the
> producer. ⭐⭐ **So: flush in a `finally`, flush in the SIGTERM handler, and an `on_delivery` callback
> that logs and counts — because without the callback there is no error path at all.**

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Open by refusing the framing: **Kafka isn't a queue — it's a replicated append-only
log with a cursor per consumer group, and every difference follows from reading not removing
anything.** That buys independent consumers, replay, and a broker doing no per-message bookkeeping —
**which is why it's fast, and also why there's no per-message ack, retry, delay or priority.** Then
the sentence that matters most: **a partition is simultaneously the unit of parallelism and the unit
of ordering, so ordering exists within a partition only — there's no total order across a topic and
never will be. You pick your guarantee by picking your key, and per-entity ordering is always what the
business actually needed.** Warn about the one-way door: **adding partitions moves keys while their
history stays behind, so ordering breaks silently across the resize, and you can never remove
partitions.** Then the durability fact, which is the one people get wrong: **`acks=all` with
`min.insync.replicas=1` is exactly `acks=1`, because if the ISR has shrunk to the leader then "all
in-sync replicas" is the leader — the setting is satisfied and the guarantee isn't. You need
replication factor 3, min.insync 2, acks all, and then two brokers down gives you an error instead of
silent loss, and that error is the feature.** Be precise about idempotence: **it removes duplicates
from network retries, not from your own logic retrying and not across a producer restart.** Close on
the Python one: **`produce()` is asynchronous and doesn't raise — a process that exits without
`flush()` silently drops its buffer, and it happens during deploys when SIGTERM arrives and the
shutdown path forgot the producer.**
