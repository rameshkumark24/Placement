# Day 357 💳

**P-357** · 💳 **Design 13 — the payment system** — ⭐⭐ the double-entry ledger

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** BUILD

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: a payment system is not a service that moves money, it is a service that
> records irrefutable claims about money that other people move — so the ledger is append-only and
> double-entry, every external call is idempotent, and reconciliation against the provider is a
> first-class component rather than a monthly spreadsheet.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐ 0–8: scope and the requirements that are absolute

```
⭐⭐ FUNCTIONAL:
⭐   ① Accept a payment for an order.
⭐   ② ⭐ Handle authorise / capture / refund / partial refund.
⭐   ③ ⭐ Support multiple providers and payment methods.
⭐   ④ ⭐⭐ RECONCILE WITH THE PROVIDER.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐ Payouts to sellers (marketplace).
⭐   ⭐⭐ OUT: fraud scoring (⭐ a separate system that this one
⭐     calls), PCI card storage (⭐⭐ USE THE PROVIDER'S
⭐     TOKENISATION — SAYING "WE NEVER TOUCH THE PAN" IS THE
⭐     CORRECT AND CHEAPEST ANSWER), currency conversion.

⭐⭐ NON-FUNCTIONAL — ⭐ and these are unusually absolute:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ NEVER DOUBLE-CHARGE. NEVER LOSE A PAYMENT RECORD.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ EVERY BALANCE MUST BE EXPLAINABLE FROM ENTRIES.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ FULL AUDIT TRAIL, IMMUTABLE, FOR YEARS (7 in many
⭐     jurisdictions).
⭐   ⭐ Volume is ***low***: 1,000 payments/s is a large
⭐     business. ⭐⭐ THIS IS NOT A THROUGHPUT PROBLEM AND SAYING
⭐     SO EARLY IS IMPORTANT — ⭐ it licenses you to choose the
⭐     boring, correct, slower option everywhere.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE FRAMING THAT SHAPES EVERYTHING:
⭐   ***YOU DO NOT HOLD THE MONEY. THE BANK AND THE PROVIDER DO.
⭐      YOUR SYSTEM HOLDS ***CLAIMS***, AND ITS JOB IS TO MAKE
⭐      THOSE CLAIMS PROVABLY CONSISTENT WITH REALITY.***
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ Double-entry, and why it is not bookkeeping trivia

```
⭐⭐ THE RULE: ***EVERY TRANSACTION IS TWO OR MORE ENTRIES THAT
⭐   SUM TO ZERO.*** ⭐ Money always moves ***from*** an account
⭐   ***to*** an account.

⭐⭐ A £100 CARD PAYMENT WITH A £3 FEE:
⭐     account                    debit    credit
⭐     customer_receivable        100
⭐     revenue                              97
⭐     processor_fee_expense        3
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SUM = 0. ⭐ Always. ⭐⭐ AND THAT INVARIANT IS
⭐     ENFORCEABLE BY A CONSTRAINT, WHICH MEANS A WHOLE CLASS
⭐     OF BUG BECOMES ***IMPOSSIBLE*** RATHER THAN UNLIKELY
⭐     (Day 187, Day 297).

⭐⭐ WHY IT MATTERS HERE — ⭐ four reasons, and the fourth is the
⭐   one people miss:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ ERRORS ARE ***DETECTABLE***: if the ledger doesn't
⭐      balance, something is wrong ***and you know it now***.
⭐      ⭐ A single-entry system is silently wrong forever.
⭐   ② ⭐ Every balance is a `SUM` over entries ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND IS
⭐      THEREFORE ***EXPLAINABLE***: "why do I have £340?" has
⭐      an answer (Day 315).
⭐   ③ ⭐ It composes: fees, taxes, refunds, chargebacks,
⭐      payouts and FX are all just more entries.
⭐   ④ ⭐⭐ IT MAKES ***TIME*** EXPLICIT. A balance is always
⭐      "as of" a point, so you can reconstruct any historical
⭐      state — ⭐ which is exactly what an auditor asks for and
⭐      what a stored balance can never provide.

⭐⭐ THE STRUCTURE:
⭐   `accounts(id, type, owner_id, currency)`
⭐   `entries(id, transaction_id, account_id, amount, currency,
⭐            created_at)`  ⭐⭐ APPEND-ONLY, NEVER UPDATED
⭐   `transactions(id, kind, external_ref, idempotency_key,
⭐                 created_at)`
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ NO `UPDATE`. NO `DELETE`. ⭐ A mistake is corrected by
⭐     a ***reversing entry***, not by an edit — ⭐ which is Day
⭐     342's compensation, and Day 315's ledger, at their most
⭐     literal.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Enforce with database permissions, not convention:
⭐     ⭐⭐ REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE ON entries.

⭐⭐ AND THE MONEY TYPE (Day 304):
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ INTEGER MINOR UNITS. NEVER FLOAT. `NUMERIC` IF YOU
⭐     MUST, BUT `BIGINT` PENCE IS BETTER.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ CURRENCY TRAVELS WITH THE AMOUNT, ALWAYS, AND
⭐     ADDING TWO DIFFERENT CURRENCIES MUST BE A ***TYPE
⭐     ERROR***, not a runtime surprise.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The payment state machine

```
⭐⭐ `initiated → authorised → captured → settled`
⭐   ⭐ Plus `failed`, `cancelled`, `refunded`,
⭐     `partially_refunded`, `disputed`, `charged_back`,
⭐     ⭐⭐ AND `unknown`.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ `unknown` IS NOT A DEFECT — IT IS DAY 317'S THIRD
⭐     OUTCOME, AND ***OMITTING IT IS THE SINGLE MOST COMMON
⭐     DESIGN ERROR IN PAYMENT SYSTEMS.***

⭐⭐ AUTHORISE VS CAPTURE — ⭐ and why the split exists:
⭐   ⭐ ***AUTHORISE*** reserves funds on the card. ⭐ It is a
⭐     hold with an expiry (typically ~7 days).
⭐   ⭐ ***CAPTURE*** actually takes them.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AUTHORISE AT CHECKOUT, CAPTURE AT ***SHIPMENT*** —
⭐     because you should not take money for something you have
⭐     not sent, ⭐ and because a cancellation before capture is
⭐     a void rather than a refund (⭐⭐ WHICH IS CHEAPER,
⭐     INSTANT, AND INVISIBLE ON THE CUSTOMER'S STATEMENT).
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND IT IS RESERVE-THEN-CONFIRM ***AGAIN*** — the
⭐     seat hold, the ATM, the driver offer, and now the card
⭐     authorisation. ⭐ Fifth appearance; say it.

⭐⭐ THE STATES THAT ARRIVE DAYS LATER — ⭐ the part people
⭐   forget entirely:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ SETTLEMENT (funds actually arrive) IS T+1 TO T+3.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ CHARGEBACKS CAN ARRIVE ***MONTHS*** LATER, and they
⭐     reverse money you have already spent and goods you have
⭐     already shipped.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SO THE SYSTEM MUST ACCEPT STATE CHANGES ON RECORDS
⭐     THAT ARE LONG "COMPLETE". ⭐ Anything that archives or
⭐     immutably freezes a payment after 30 days is wrong.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Idempotency at every layer

```
⭐⭐ THREE LAYERS, THREE KEYS, ALL REQUIRED:

⭐⭐ ① ***CLIENT → YOUR API.*** ⭐ The client sends
⭐   `Idempotency-Key`; you store `(key, request_hash,
⭐   response)`.
⭐   ⭐ Same key + same body ⇒ ⭐⭐ RETURN THE STORED RESPONSE.
⭐   ⭐ Same key + ***different*** body ⇒ ⭐⭐ 422. ⭐ That is a
⭐     bug on their side and silently accepting it is worse than
⭐     failing.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THIS IS WHERE IDEMPOTENCY ACTUALLY STARTS — THE
⭐     DOUBLE-CLICKED BUTTON, NOT THE QUEUE (Day 335).

⭐⭐ ② ***YOUR SERVICE → THE PROVIDER.*** ⭐ Stripe et al. accept
⭐   an idempotency key.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ DERIVE IT FROM YOUR DOMAIN: `charge:{payment_id}`.
⭐     ***NEVER `uuid4()`***, or a retry generates a new key and
⭐     charges twice. ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE SINGLE HIGHEST-VALUE LINE
⭐     OF CODE IN THE INTEGRATION.

⭐⭐ ③ ***PROVIDER → YOUR WEBHOOK.*** ⭐ Webhooks are
⭐   at-least-once and arrive ***out of order***.
⭐   ⭐ Dedupe on the provider's event id.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AND MAKE TRANSITIONS MONOTONIC: A LATE
⭐     `payment.authorised` MUST NOT OVERWRITE `captured`.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ VERIFY THE SIGNATURE. ⭐ An unauthenticated webhook
⭐     endpoint is an API for marking arbitrary payments as
⭐     succeeded, ⭐ and it is a real, exploited class of bug.

⭐⭐ AND THE INTERNAL ONE THAT MAKES ALL THREE WORK:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ `UNIQUE(idempotency_key)` ON `transactions`, SO THE
⭐     ***DATABASE*** ARBITRATES, NOT A `SELECT` FOLLOWED BY AN
⭐     `INSERT` (Day 310).
```

# Part 5 — ⭐⭐ Reconciliation: the component that proves you are right

```
⭐⭐ THE PREMISE: ***YOUR RECORDS AND THE PROVIDER'S ***WILL***
⭐   DIVERGE.*** ⭐ Timeouts, dropped webhooks, manual refunds
⭐   issued in their dashboard, provider-side corrections,
⭐   chargebacks.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ RECONCILIATION IS NOT ERROR HANDLING. IT IS A DAILY,
⭐     AUTOMATED, ALERTED PROCESS — AND IT IS THE ONLY THING
⭐     THAT MAKES YOUR LEDGER ***TRUSTWORTHY*** RATHER THAN
⭐     MERELY SELF-CONSISTENT.

⭐⭐ THE PROCESS:
⭐   ① ⭐ Fetch the provider's settlement report for the day.
⭐   ② ⭐ Match on ***your*** reference (which is why every
⭐      request carries one), ⭐ then amount and date.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ CLASSIFY EVERY DIFFERENCE:
⭐      ⭐ In yours, not theirs ⇒ ⭐⭐ DID WE RECORD A PAYMENT
⭐        THAT NEVER HAPPENED? ⭐ Investigate immediately.
⭐      ⭐ In theirs, not yours ⇒ ⭐⭐ WE TOOK MONEY AND HAVE NO
⭐        RECORD. ⭐ The worst case, and usually a lost webhook
⭐        plus a timed-out call.
⭐      ⭐ Amount mismatch ⇒ fees, partial refunds, FX.
⭐      ⭐ Timing ⇒ crossed a day boundary. ⭐ Benign, and it is
⭐        most of your volume, ⭐⭐ SO THE MATCHER MUST HANDLE IT
⭐        OR EVERY DAY LOOKS BROKEN.
⭐   ④ ⭐ Auto-resolve the mechanical cases; ⭐⭐ QUEUE THE REST
⭐      FOR A HUMAN, WITH A NAMED OWNER AND AN SLA.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐⭐ EXPORT `unreconciled_count` AND `unreconciled_value`
⭐      AS METRICS AND ALERT ON THEM. ⭐ That pair is the
⭐      single best health indicator a payment system has.

⭐⭐ AND THE DESIGN CONSEQUENCE PEOPLE MISS:
⭐   ***RECONCILIATION IS ONLY POSSIBLE IF EVERY REQUEST CARRIES
⭐      YOUR OWN REFERENCE AND EVERY RESPONSE IS STORED
⭐      VERBATIM.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SO STORE THE RAW PROVIDER PAYLOAD ON EVERY CALL AND
⭐     WEBHOOK, FOREVER. ⭐ It is the evidence, and it costs
⭐     almost nothing.
```

# Part 6 — ⭐ Multi-provider, payouts and the money-movement rules

```
⭐⭐ MULTIPLE PROVIDERS — ⭐ three real reasons:
⭐   ⭐ Redundancy (⭐⭐ A PROVIDER OUTAGE IS A REVENUE OUTAGE);
⭐   ⭐ cost and acceptance-rate optimisation by geography and
⭐     card type; ⭐ regulatory coverage.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND THE ABSTRACTION MUST BE ***NARROW***: authorise,
⭐     capture, refund, void, plus a normalised status. ⭐ Do not
⭐     try to abstract every provider feature — ⭐⭐ THE LEAKY
⭐     PARTS (3-D SECURE FLOWS, DISPUTE APIs, LOCAL METHODS)
⭐     SHOULD BE EXPLICITLY PROVIDER-SPECIFIC RATHER THAN
⭐     BADLY GENERALISED (Day 318's declined abstraction).
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND FAILOVER MUST NOT DOUBLE-CHARGE: if provider A
⭐     timed out, you do ***not*** know it failed. ⭐ Reconcile
⭐     first, or route only ***new*** payments to B.

⭐⭐ MARKETPLACE PAYOUTS:
⭐   ⭐ Seller balance is ⭐⭐ A SUM OVER LEDGER ENTRIES, NEVER A
⭐     STORED NUMBER.
⭐   ⭐ Payout = debit seller balance, credit payout-pending
⭐     ⇒ then a bank transfer ⇒ then confirm or reverse.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND THE RULE: ***NEVER PAY OUT MONEY YOU HAVE NOT
⭐     SETTLED.*** ⭐ Paying from authorised-but-unsettled funds
⭐     means a chargeback leaves you funding a seller's payout
⭐     from your own capital — ⭐⭐ WHICH IS EXACTLY HOW
⭐     MARKETPLACE FRAUD WORKS.

⭐⭐ AND THREE RULES THAT ARE NOT NEGOTIABLE:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ NEVER LOG A CARD NUMBER, CVV, OR FULL PAN — ⭐ and
⭐     assume anything in a log ***will*** be indexed and
⭐     searchable by many people.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ NEVER DERIVE A REFUND AMOUNT FROM A CLIENT REQUEST
⭐     ALONE. ⭐ Refunds are computed from the ledger and capped
⭐     by what was actually captured.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ NEVER LET A RETRY PATH CREATE A ***NEW*** PAYMENT
⭐     RECORD. ⭐ It resumes the existing one, or it is a
⭐     double-charge waiting for a slow network.
```

# Part 7 — ⭐ Bottlenecks and the summary

```
⭐⭐ WHAT BREAKS — ⭐⭐ AND NOTE THAT NONE OF IT IS THROUGHPUT:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ PROVIDER LATENCY AND AVAILABILITY. ⭐ Your p99 is
⭐      theirs. ⭐ Timeouts, circuit breakers, and a queued
⭐      "we'll confirm shortly" path rather than a spinner
⭐      (Day 339).
⭐   ② ⭐ Webhook delivery gaps ⇒ ⭐⭐ ***POLL AS A BACKSTOP.***
⭐      ⭐ Never depend on a webhook alone for a state you care
⭐      about; a webhook is an optimisation over polling.
⭐   ③ ⭐ Ledger growth ⇒ partition by month; ⭐ it is
⭐      append-only, so partitioning is easy and pruning is
⭐      never allowed.
⭐   ④ ⭐ Hot account rows for a marketplace seller ⇒
⭐      ⭐⭐ ENTRIES ARE APPENDED, NOT UPDATED, SO THERE IS NO
⭐      HOT ROW AT ALL — ⭐ the balance is a rollup, refreshed
⭐      periodically. ⭐⭐ THE LEDGER DESIGN SOLVED A CONTENTION
⭐      PROBLEM AS A SIDE EFFECT, AND POINTING THAT OUT IS A
⭐      NICE CLOSE.

⭐⭐ THE SUMMARY:
⭐   ***"This isn't a throughput problem — a thousand payments a
⭐      second is a large business — so I take the boring
⭐      correct option everywhere. An append-only double-entry
⭐      ledger where entries sum to zero, enforced by a
⭐      constraint and by revoking UPDATE, so errors are
⭐      detectable rather than silent and every balance is
⭐      explainable. Authorise then capture, which is
⭐      reserve-then-confirm for the fifth time in this
⭐      curriculum. Idempotency at three layers, with the
⭐      provider key derived from my domain rather than a fresh
⭐      UUID. An explicit unknown state, because a timeout is
⭐      the absence of information. And reconciliation as a
⭐      daily automated component with an alerted unreconciled
⭐      count — because that's the only thing that makes the
⭐      ledger trustworthy rather than merely self-consistent."***
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ Storing a balance instead of entries | "Why do I have £340?" has no answer, and errors are silent forever |
| ⭐⭐ `UPDATE` on ledger entries | Destroys the audit trail. Correct with a reversing entry; revoke UPDATE at the database |
| ⭐⭐ Floats for money | Never. Integer minor units, currency travelling with the amount |
| ⭐⭐ No `unknown` state | A timeout is the absence of information. Omitting it is the classic payment design error |
| ⭐⭐ `uuid4()` as the provider idempotency key | The retry gets a new key and charges twice |
| ⭐ Idempotency only at the queue | The double-clicked button already made a duplicate |
| ⭐⭐ Unverified webhook endpoint | An API for marking arbitrary payments succeeded. Real and exploited |
| ⭐ Non-monotonic webhook handling | A late `authorised` overwrites `captured` |
| ⭐⭐ Treating reconciliation as a monthly spreadsheet | It's a daily automated component with an alerted metric |
| ⭐ Not storing raw provider payloads | You've thrown away the evidence reconciliation depends on |
| ⭐⭐ Capturing at checkout rather than shipment | You took money for something unsent, and a cancel becomes a refund instead of a void |
| ⭐⭐ Paying out unsettled funds | A chargeback leaves you funding the payout. This is how marketplace fraud works |
| ⭐ Failing over to provider B after a timeout | You don't know A failed. Reconcile first, or route only new payments |
| ⭐ Refund amounts taken from the client | Compute from the ledger, capped by what was captured |
| ⭐ Archiving payments after 30 days | Chargebacks arrive months later and must be applicable |

## Interview questions

**1. "Why double-entry?"**
> ⭐⭐ Four reasons, and the first is the point: **errors become detectable.** If entries don't sum to
> zero something is wrong and you know it now, whereas a single-entry system is silently wrong forever.
> ⭐ Every balance is a sum over entries, so it's explainable — "why do I have £340" has an answer.
> ⭐ It composes: fees, refunds, chargebacks, FX and payouts are all just more entries. ⭐⭐ And it makes
> time explicit, so you can reconstruct any historical balance — **which is precisely what an auditor
> asks for and what a stored number can never provide.**

**2. "Where does idempotency live?"**
> ⭐ Three layers. Client to my API with a stored key, request hash and response — same key with a
> different body is a 422, because silently accepting it is worse than failing. ⭐⭐ My service to the
> provider with a key **derived from my domain — `charge:{payment_id}`, never a fresh UUID, or a retry
> charges twice.** ⭐ And provider to my webhook, deduped on their event id, with monotonic transitions
> so a late `authorised` can't overwrite `captured`, and the signature verified — ⭐⭐ **an
> unauthenticated webhook endpoint is an API for marking arbitrary payments as succeeded.**

**3. "The provider call times out. Now what?"**
> ⭐⭐ You don't know whether the customer was charged, so you record `unknown` — **which is a state,
> not a defect, and omitting it is the most common design error here.** ⭐ Then reconcile: query the
> provider by *my* reference, which is why every request carries one. ⭐ Do not retry with a new key, do
> not fail over to another provider, and do not release whatever the payment was holding — ⭐⭐ because
> **releasing something you may have charged for is the worst outcome available.**

**4. "What is reconciliation and why is it a component?"**
> ⭐⭐ Because **your records and the provider's will diverge** — dropped webhooks, timeouts, refunds
> issued manually in their dashboard, chargebacks. ⭐ So daily: fetch the settlement report, match on my
> reference, and classify every difference. ⭐⭐ In mine but not theirs means I recorded a payment that
> may not have happened; **in theirs but not mine means I took money and have no record**, which is the
> worst case. ⭐ Auto-resolve the mechanical cases, queue the rest for a named human with an SLA, and
> ⭐⭐ **alert on unreconciled count and value — that pair is the best health indicator this system
> has.**

**5. "Why authorise and capture separately?"**
> ⭐ Authorise reserves funds with an expiry; capture takes them. ⭐⭐ Authorise at checkout and capture
> at shipment, **because you shouldn't take money for something you haven't sent** — and a cancellation
> before capture is a void, which is cheaper, instant, and never appears on the customer's statement.
> ⭐⭐ It's also reserve-then-confirm for the fifth time in this curriculum: the seat hold, the ATM, the
> driver offer, the inventory hold, and now the card authorisation.

**6. "Marketplace payouts — what's the trap?"**
> ⭐⭐ **Never pay out money you haven't settled.** ⭐ Authorised isn't settled, and settlement is T+1 to
> T+3 while chargebacks arrive months later. ⭐⭐ If you pay a seller from unsettled funds, a chargeback
> leaves you funding their payout from your own capital — **which is exactly how marketplace fraud
> works.** ⭐ And the seller balance is a sum over ledger entries, never a stored number, ⭐ which
> incidentally removes the hot-row problem entirely, because entries are appended rather than updated.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Frame it correctly first: **this isn't a service that moves money, it's a service that
records irrefutable claims about money other people move** — and it isn't a throughput problem either,
since a thousand payments a second is a large business, which licenses the boring correct option
everywhere. Give the ledger and the reason that matters: **append-only double-entry where entries sum
to zero, enforced by a constraint and by revoking UPDATE — so errors are detectable rather than
silently wrong forever, and every balance is explainable from the entries that produced it.** Mistakes
are corrected by reversing entries, never edits. Then authorise-and-capture, with both reasons: **you
shouldn't take money for something you haven't shipped, and a pre-capture cancellation is a void rather
than a refund** — and note it's reserve-then-confirm for the fifth time. Then idempotency at three
layers, and single out the one that costs real money: **the provider key must be derived from your
domain, never a fresh UUID, or the retry charges twice.** Say the state people omit: **`unknown`,
because a timeout is the absence of information, not a failure.** Close on the component that makes the
whole thing trustworthy: **reconciliation as a daily automated job, matching on your own reference,
classifying every difference, with unreconciled count and value alerted** — because a ledger that only
agrees with itself is self-consistent, and reconciliation is what makes it *true*.
