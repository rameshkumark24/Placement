# Day 351 📰

**P-351** · 📰 **Design 7 — the news feed** — ⭐⭐ fan-out on write vs read

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** BUILD

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: the feed is the canonical read-vs-write trade — you can do the work once
> per post and multiply it by the follower count, or once per read and multiply it by the read count,
> and the correct answer is neither, because the follower distribution is so skewed that any single
> strategy is wrong for one end of it.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐ 0–8: scope and the load parameter that matters

```
⭐⭐ FUNCTIONAL:
⭐   ① Post content. ② View a feed of people you follow.
⭐   ③ Follow / unfollow. ④ ⭐ Pagination, infinite scroll.
⭐   ⭐⭐ OUT: ranking/ML (⭐ mention it as a re-ranking layer on
⭐     top of retrieval), ads, comments, stories, DMs.

⭐⭐ NON-FUNCTIONAL:
⭐   ⭐ 500 M DAU. ⭐ Each user posts ~0.5×/day, reads the feed
⭐     ~10×/day.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ FEED LOAD p99 < 200 ms — this is the whole product;
⭐     a slow feed is a dead app.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ EVENTUAL CONSISTENCY IS FINE FOR ***OTHERS'*** POSTS
⭐     (seconds), AND ***ZERO*** FOR YOUR OWN (Day 331's
⭐     asymmetry). ⭐ Say this at minute five — it licenses
⭐     everything else.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE LOAD PARAMETER, WHICH IS NOT QPS:
⭐   ***FOLLOWERS PER POSTING USER.*** ⭐ Day 322 promised this
⭐   day, and here it is.
⭐   ⭐ Median user: ~200 followers.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ p99.9 USER: 100,000+. ⭐ Celebrity: ***100 MILLION.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ A SIX-ORDER-OF-MAGNITUDE SPREAD IN THE ***COST OF ONE
⭐     WRITE***, AND THAT SPREAD ***IS*** THE DESIGN PROBLEM.
```

---

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The estimate that decides it

```
⭐⭐ WRITES: 500 M × 0.5 = 250 M posts/day = ⭐ 2,500/s;
⭐   peak ~7,500/s.
⭐⭐ READS: 500 M × 10 = 5 × 10^9 feed loads/day = ⭐⭐ 50,000/s;
⭐   peak 150,000/s.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Read:write = ***20:1***.

⭐⭐ FAN-OUT ON WRITE — THE ARITHMETIC:
⭐   ⭐ 2,500 posts/s × 200 median followers = ⭐⭐ 500,000
⭐     TIMELINE INSERTS/S. ⭐ Large but tractable.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ONE CELEBRITY POST WITH 100 M FOLLOWERS = 100 MILLION
⭐     INSERTS FOR ***ONE ACTION***.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ At 1 M inserts/s of dedicated capacity that is
⭐     ***100 seconds*** — ⭐⭐ AND DURING THOSE 100 SECONDS
⭐     EVERY OTHER USER'S FAN-OUT IS QUEUED BEHIND IT.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THAT IS THE PROBLEM, AND IT IS NOT A CAPACITY
⭐     PROBLEM — IT IS A ***FAIRNESS*** PROBLEM.

⭐⭐ FAN-OUT ON READ — THE ARITHMETIC:
⭐   ⭐ 50,000 feed loads/s × 200 followees = ⭐⭐ 10 MILLION
⭐     POST-LIST FETCHES/S, then a merge.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND A USER FOLLOWING 5,000 ACCOUNTS DOES 5,000
⭐     LOOKUPS ***PER FEED LOAD***, ON THE LATENCY-CRITICAL
⭐     PATH.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Scatter-gather is as slow as its slowest shard (Day
⭐     329) ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE 200 ms BUDGET IS GONE.

⭐⭐ STORAGE FOR PRECOMPUTED TIMELINES:
⭐   ⭐ 500 M users × 500 entries × ~50 B (post id + author +
⭐     score) = ⭐⭐ ~12.5 TB.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Feasible in a Redis cluster, ⭐⭐ AND CRITICALLY: ONLY
⭐     FOR ***ACTIVE*** USERS. ⭐ If only 20% log in weekly,
⭐     that is 2.5 TB — ⭐⭐ AND SAYING THAT TURNS AN EXPENSIVE
⭐     ANSWER INTO A CHEAP ONE.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The hybrid, and the rule that makes it work

```
⭐⭐ ***FAN-OUT ON WRITE (PUSH)*** for normal users:
⭐   ⭐ On post, insert the post id into each follower's
⭐     precomputed timeline (a Redis sorted set, capped at ~800).
⭐   ⭐ ✔ ⭐⭐ FEED READ IS ***ONE*** `ZREVRANGE` — SUB-MILLISECOND.
⭐   ⭐ ✘ Write amplification; ⭐ wasted work for inactive users.

⭐⭐ ***FAN-OUT ON READ (PULL)*** for celebrities:
⭐   ⭐ Do ***not*** fan out. Keep their posts in their own
⭐     timeline only.
⭐   ⭐ ✔ One write. ⭐ ✘ Every reader must fetch them.

⭐⭐ ***THE HYBRID*** — ⭐⭐ AND THIS IS THE ANSWER:
⭐   ⭐ Users above a follower threshold (~10k–100k) are
⭐     ***pull***; everyone else is ***push***.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ A FEED READ = `ZREVRANGE` OF THE PRECOMPUTED
⭐     TIMELINE ***MERGED WITH*** A SMALL PULL FROM THE HANDFUL
⭐     OF CELEBRITIES YOU FOLLOW.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE NUMBER THAT MAKES IT WORK: ***ALMOST NOBODY
⭐     FOLLOWS MORE THAN ~50 CELEBRITIES***, so the pull side is
⭐     50 cached lookups — ⭐ and those 50 lists are the hottest
⭐     objects in the system, so they are ***always*** in cache.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE PULL SET IS SMALL ***AND*** HOT. THAT IS WHY THE
⭐     HYBRID IS CHEAP RATHER THAN A COMPROMISE, AND IT IS THE
⭐     SENTENCE TO SAY.

⭐⭐ THREE REFINEMENTS THAT COST ALMOST NOTHING:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ONLY FAN OUT TO ***ACTIVE*** FOLLOWERS (logged in
⭐     within ~30 days). ⭐ Inactive users get a pull-built feed
⭐     on their next login. ⭐⭐ THIS ALONE CUTS FAN-OUT VOLUME
⭐     BY 60–80%.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ INSERT THE AUTHOR'S OWN POST INTO THEIR OWN TIMELINE
⭐     ***SYNCHRONOUSLY***. ⭐ Read-your-writes for free, and it
⭐     is the only consistency guarantee users actually notice.
⭐   ⭐ Cap timelines; ⭐ trim on insert.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Deep dive: the fan-out pipeline

```
⭐⭐ THE FLOW:
⭐   ① Post written to the posts store (⭐ sharded by post_id).
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ AUTHOR'S OWN TIMELINE UPDATED ***SYNCHRONOUSLY***.
⭐   ③ ⭐ A `PostCreated` event via the outbox (Day 263).
⭐   ④ ⭐⭐ FAN-OUT WORKERS EXPAND FOLLOWERS ***IN CHUNKS***
⭐      (Day 350's pattern — ranges, resumable, idempotent).
⭐   ⑤ ⭐ Pipelined `ZADD` into each follower's timeline.

⭐⭐ THE FIVE THINGS THAT MAKE THIS SURVIVABLE:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ PRIORITY QUEUES BY FOLLOWER COUNT. ⭐ A 200-follower
⭐      post must not queue behind a 5-million-follower post.
⭐      ⭐⭐ WITHOUT THIS, ONE CELEBRITY DELAYS EVERYONE — THE
⭐      FAIRNESS PROBLEM FROM PART 2, SOLVED BY SEPARATING THE
⭐      QUEUES.
⭐   ② ⭐ Pipelining/batching: 1,000 `ZADD`s per round trip, not
⭐      1,000 round trips.
⭐   ③ ⭐ Idempotent: a sorted set keyed by post id ⇒ ⭐⭐ A
⭐      REDELIVERED CHUNK IS A NO-OP BY CONSTRUCTION.
⭐   ④ ⭐⭐ BACKPRESSURE: IF FAN-OUT LAG EXCEEDS A THRESHOLD,
⭐      DEGRADE ***MORE*** USERS TO PULL RATHER THAN LET THE
⭐      QUEUE GROW. ⭐ The threshold becomes dynamic, and that is
⭐      a genuinely good answer.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐ Timeline entries are ***post ids***, not post content
⭐      (Day 262) ⇒ ⭐⭐ AN EDITED OR DELETED POST NEEDS NO
⭐      TIMELINE REWRITE, WHICH WOULD OTHERWISE BE 100 MILLION
⭐      UPDATES.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE HYDRATION STEP, WHICH IS WHERE READS ACTUALLY
⭐   SPEND THEIR TIME:
⭐   ⭐ `ZREVRANGE` gives 50 post ids ⇒ ⭐⭐ ONE MULTI-GET
⭐     AGAINST THE POST CACHE ⇒ hydrate authors, media, counts.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THIS IS AN N+1 WAITING TO HAPPEN (Day 197). ⭐ Batch
⭐     every hydration: posts, authors, like counts, and the
⭐     viewer's own like/follow state — ⭐ four multi-gets, not
⭐     four hundred.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐⭐ The problems the hybrid creates

```
⭐⭐ ① ***THE FOLLOW EVENT.*** ⭐ You follow someone: does their
⭐   history appear in your timeline?
⭐   ⭐ Backfilling means inserting their last N posts — ⭐ fine.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ UNFOLLOWING MEANS ***REMOVING*** THEIR POSTS FROM
⭐     YOUR TIMELINE, WHICH IS A SCAN-AND-DELETE OVER A SORTED
⭐     SET.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE CHEAP ANSWER: ***FILTER AT READ TIME*** AGAINST
⭐     YOUR (CACHED) FOLLOW SET, AND LET THE ENTRIES AGE OUT OF
⭐     THE CAPPED TIMELINE NATURALLY. ⭐ Correct, cheap, and
⭐     eventually self-cleaning.

⭐⭐ ② ***DELETED AND BLOCKED CONTENT.*** ⭐ Ids, not content,
⭐   means hydration returns a tombstone and the reader
⭐   silently drops it.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ WHICH MEANS A PAGE OF 50 IDS CAN HYDRATE TO 43
⭐     POSTS. ⭐ Over-fetch and top up — ⭐⭐ AND THIS IS WHY
⭐     OFFSET PAGINATION IS BROKEN HERE AND CURSORS ARE
⭐     MANDATORY (Day 194).

⭐⭐ ③ ***RANKING BREAKS PURE CHRONOLOGY.*** ⭐ A ranked feed is
⭐   retrieval (a few hundred candidates from the timeline) then
⭐   ⭐ re-ranking by a model.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SO THE TIMELINE BECOMES A ***CANDIDATE STORE***, NOT
⭐     THE FEED ITSELF — ⭐ and the cursor must then encode the
⭐     ranking session, or refreshing reshuffles and the user
⭐     sees the same post three times.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ That is Day 293's retrieve-then-rerank, in a completely
⭐     different domain — worth naming.

⭐⭐ ④ ***THE THUNDERING CELEBRITY READ.*** ⭐ A celebrity posts
⭐   ⇒ millions pull their timeline within seconds.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ It is one key ⇒ ⭐⭐ HOT KEY (Day 326). ⭐ Near-cache in
⭐     the app process with a 1–5 s TTL, ⭐ and replicate that
⭐     key across cache nodes.
```

# Part 6 — ⭐ The data model

```
⭐⭐ `posts` — sharded by `post_id` (⭐ a Snowflake id: time
⭐   ordered ***and*** unique across shards, Day 329).
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ TIME-ORDERED IDS MEAN THE TIMELINE'S SORT SCORE CAN
⭐     BE THE ID ITSELF — no separate timestamp needed, and
⭐     sorting is free.

⭐⭐ `follows` — ⭐⭐ STORED ***TWICE***:
⭐   ⭐ `(follower_id, followee_id)` ⇒ "who do I follow" — the
⭐     read path.
⭐   ⭐ `(followee_id, follower_id)` ⇒ "who follows me" — ⭐⭐ THE
⭐     FAN-OUT PATH, AND THE ONE PEOPLE FORGET TO INDEX.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ TWO COPIES, WRITTEN TOGETHER. ⭐ This is
⭐     denormalisation as a deliberate act, and it is exactly
⭐     Day 332's "shape the data to the query".

⭐⭐ `timeline:{user_id}` — ⭐ a Redis sorted set, score =
⭐   post_id, capped at ~800, ⭐ TTL for inactive users so
⭐   memory is reclaimed automatically.

⭐⭐ AND THE SHARD-KEY NOTE: ⭐⭐ SHARD `follows` BY
⭐   `followee_id` FOR THE FAN-OUT COPY, SO EXPANDING ONE
⭐   AUTHOR'S FOLLOWERS IS A ***SINGLE-SHARD RANGE SCAN***
⭐   RATHER THAN A SCATTER-GATHER OVER EVERY SHARD.
```

# Part 7 — ⭐ Bottlenecks and the summary

```
⭐⭐ WHAT BREAKS:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ FAN-OUT WORKER THROUGHPUT DURING A CELEBRITY BURST ⇒
⭐      priority queues, dynamic thresholds, ⭐ and accepting
⭐      that a celebrity's post reaches followers over minutes
⭐      rather than seconds. ⭐⭐ THAT IS A PRODUCT DECISION AND
⭐      IT IS FINE — nobody can tell.
⭐   ② ⭐ Redis memory ⇒ ⭐⭐ ACTIVE USERS ONLY, CAPPED
⭐      TIMELINES, TTLs.
⭐   ③ ⭐ Hydration ⇒ the post cache is the busiest thing you
⭐      own; ⭐ it must be a cluster, and hot posts need a
⭐      near-cache.
⭐   ④ ⭐ The follow graph for someone following 50,000 accounts
⭐      ⇒ ⭐⭐ CAP IT, OR ACCEPT A SLOWER FEED FOR THAT USER AND
⭐      SAY SO.

⭐⭐ THE SUMMARY:
⭐   ***"The load parameter isn't QPS, it's followers per
⭐      posting user — and that spans six orders of magnitude,
⭐      which is why no single strategy works. So: push for
⭐      normal users, giving a one-operation feed read; pull for
⭐      accounts above a threshold; and merge at read time. It
⭐      works because almost nobody follows more than about
⭐      fifty large accounts, and those fifty lists are the
⭐      hottest objects in the system, so the pull side is small
⭐      and always cached. Fan out only to active followers,
⭐      which removes most of the volume; write the author's own
⭐      post synchronously, which buys read-your-writes for
⭐      free; store ids rather than content, so deleting a post
⭐      isn't a hundred million updates; and if fan-out lag
⭐      grows, lower the threshold and degrade more users to
⭐      pull rather than let a queue build."***
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ Pure fan-out on write | One celebrity post is 100 M inserts, and every other user queues behind it |
| ⭐⭐ Pure fan-out on read | A user following 5,000 accounts does 5,000 lookups on the latency path |
| ⭐⭐ No priority queues for fan-out | A 200-follower post waits behind a 5-million-follower one. It's fairness, not capacity |
| ⭐⭐ Fanning out to inactive users | 60–80% of the work is for people who won't log in |
| ⭐ Author's own post fanned out asynchronously | You lose read-your-writes, the one guarantee users actually notice |
| ⭐⭐ Storing post content in timelines | Deleting or editing a post becomes 100 million updates |
| ⭐ Offset pagination | Hydration drops deleted and blocked posts, so page sizes vary. Cursors are mandatory |
| ⭐⭐ Not storing the follow graph twice | The fan-out direction becomes a scatter-gather over every shard |
| ⭐ Hydrating per post | Classic N+1 at 150,000 feed loads/s. Batch every lookup |
| ⭐⭐ Ignoring the celebrity read hot key | Millions pull one key within seconds of a post |
| ⭐ Ranked feed with a plain offset cursor | Refreshing reshuffles and the user sees the same post three times |
| ⭐ Expecting instant delivery for a celebrity post | Minutes is fine and nobody can tell. Say so as a decision |

## Interview questions

**1. "Fan-out on write or read?"**
> ⭐⭐ Neither alone, and the reason is the load parameter: **followers per posting user spans six orders
> of magnitude**, from two hundred at the median to a hundred million for a celebrity. ⭐ Push gives a
> one-operation feed read but makes one celebrity post a hundred million inserts that block everyone
> else's fan-out. ⭐ Pull makes a post cheap but puts thousands of lookups on the latency-critical path.
> ⭐⭐ So hybrid: push below a follower threshold, pull above it, merge at read.

**2. "Why is the hybrid cheap rather than a compromise?"**
> ⭐⭐ Because **almost nobody follows more than about fifty large accounts**, so the pull side is
> fifty lookups — and those fifty lists are the hottest objects in the entire system, so they're always
> in cache. ⭐ The pull set is small *and* hot, which is why the merge costs almost nothing. ⭐ If the
> distribution were flat, the hybrid wouldn't help.

**3. "How do you stop one celebrity blocking everyone?"**
> ⭐⭐ Priority queues by follower count — **a two-hundred-follower post must not queue behind a
> five-million-follower one, and that's a fairness problem, not a capacity one.** ⭐ Plus chunked,
> resumable, idempotent fan-out; batched pipelined writes; and ⭐⭐ **backpressure that lowers the
> push/pull threshold when fan-out lag grows, degrading more users to pull rather than letting a queue
> build.** ⭐ And I'd accept that a celebrity's post reaches followers over minutes — nobody can tell.

**4. "What cuts the most work for free?"**
> ⭐⭐ **Only fanning out to active followers** — logged in within thirty days — which typically removes
> sixty to eighty percent of the volume, and inactive users get a pull-built feed on next login. ⭐ Then
> storing post ids rather than content, so a deleted post costs nothing instead of a hundred million
> updates. ⭐ And writing the author's own post to their own timeline synchronously, which buys
> read-your-writes for free and is the only consistency users actually notice.

**5. "What happens when someone unfollows?"**
> ⭐ Removing their posts from your timeline is a scan-and-delete over a sorted set, which is expensive
> and needless. ⭐⭐ **Filter at read time against your cached follow set and let the entries age out of
> the capped timeline naturally** — correct, cheap and self-cleaning. ⭐ Same mechanism handles blocks.
> ⭐ The consequence is that a page of fifty ids might hydrate to forty-three posts, which is exactly
> why cursors rather than offsets.

**6. "How does ranking change this?"**
> ⭐ The timeline stops being the feed and becomes a **candidate store**: retrieve a few hundred, then
> re-rank with a model. ⭐⭐ That's retrieve-then-rerank, the same shape as a retrieval system.
> ⭐ The consequence people miss is pagination — **the cursor has to encode the ranking session, or a
> refresh reshuffles and the user sees the same post three times**, which reads as a bug even though
> every component worked.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Start by naming the load parameter, because it isn't QPS: **followers per posting
user, and it spans six orders of magnitude** — two hundred at the median, a hundred million for a
celebrity. Then do both arithmetics out loud. **Push: two and a half thousand posts a second times two
hundred followers is half a million timeline inserts a second, tractable — until one celebrity post is
a hundred million inserts, and every other user's fan-out queues behind it, which is a fairness problem
rather than a capacity one.** **Pull: fifty thousand feed loads a second times two hundred followees is
ten million lookups a second, and a user following five thousand accounts blows the two-hundred
millisecond budget on its own.** So the hybrid — and give the reason it's cheap rather than a
compromise: **almost nobody follows more than about fifty large accounts, and those fifty lists are the
hottest objects in the system, so the pull side is small and always cached.** Then the three refinements
that cost nothing: **fan out only to active followers, which removes most of the volume; write the
author's own post synchronously, which buys read-your-writes; and store ids rather than content, so a
deleted post isn't a hundred million updates.** Close on the adaptive part, which is the senior answer:
**when fan-out lag grows, lower the threshold and push fewer users — degrade to pull rather than let a
queue build** — and on accepting that a celebrity's post lands over minutes, because nobody can tell.
