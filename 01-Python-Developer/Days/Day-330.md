# Day 330 ⚖️

**P-330** · ⚖️ **CAP done correctly** — and ⭐⭐ PACELC, which matters more

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: CAP is not "pick two" — partitions are not a choice you make, they are
> weather, so the theorem says only this: while a partition is happening, you must choose between
> refusing requests and returning possibly-stale answers. And since partitions are rare, the question
> that actually shapes your system is PACELC's second half: what you trade on a normal Tuesday.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ What the theorem actually says

```
⭐⭐ THE THREE TERMS, PRECISELY:
⭐   ⭐ C — ***LINEARIZABILITY.*** Every read returns the most
⭐     recent completed write. ⭐⭐ NOT "the database has
⭐     constraints". NOT ACID's C. ⭐ It is a statement about
⭐     ***a single register appearing to be a single copy.***
⭐   ⭐ A — ***EVERY NON-FAILING NODE RETURNS A NON-ERROR
⭐     RESPONSE.*** ⭐⭐ NOT "99.99% uptime". ⭐ A node that
⭐     replies "I'm not sure, try later" has ***failed*** A.
⭐   ⭐ P — ***THE NETWORK MAY DROP OR DELAY ARBITRARY
⭐     MESSAGES.***

⭐⭐ AND NOW THE PART THAT MAKES "PICK TWO" NONSENSE:
⭐   ***P IS NOT A PROPERTY YOU CHOOSE. IT IS A PROPERTY OF
⭐      NETWORKS, WHICH IS TO SAY IT IS A FACT.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Cables get cut. Switches reboot. A GC pause is
⭐     indistinguishable from a partition. ⭐⭐ AWS HAS
⭐     PARTITIONS. YOUR RACK HAS PARTITIONS.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SO "CA" IS NOT AN OPTION FOR ANY DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM.
⭐     ⭐ A single-node database is CA — because it is not
⭐     distributed. That is the only honest CA system.

⭐⭐ THE THEOREM, RESTATED CORRECTLY, IN ONE LINE:
⭐   ***WHEN A PARTITION OCCURS, YOU MUST CHOOSE: REFUSE
⭐      REQUESTS (CP) OR SERVE POSSIBLY-STALE ONES (AP).***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THAT IS ALL IT SAYS. It says nothing about what
⭐     happens the rest of the time — ⭐ which is 99.9% of the
⭐     time, and which is Part 4.
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The three-line proof

```
⭐⭐ SAY THIS AND YOU HAVE DEMONSTRATED YOU UNDERSTAND IT
⭐   RATHER THAN MEMORISED THE TRIANGLE:

⭐   ① Two nodes, N1 and N2, replicating value v. The network
⭐      between them is cut.
⭐   ② A client writes v=2 to N1. N1 cannot reach N2.
⭐   ③ Another client reads from N2.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ N2 HAS EXACTLY TWO CHOICES:
⭐      ⭐ Return v=1 ⇒ ***NOT LINEARIZABLE*** (chose A).
⭐      ⭐ Refuse to answer ⇒ ***NOT AVAILABLE*** (chose C).
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THERE IS NO THIRD OPTION, AND NO CLEVERNESS
⭐     ANYWHERE CREATES ONE. It is not an engineering limitation;
⭐     it is information: ⭐ N2 cannot know something nobody
⭐     told it.

⭐⭐ AND THE COROLLARY THAT MATTERS OPERATIONALLY:
⭐   ***N1 CANNOT TELL WHETHER N2 IS DEAD, SLOW, OR
⭐      UNREACHABLE.*** ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE SAME IMPOSSIBILITY THAT
⭐   MAKES FAILOVER DETECTION A GUESS (Day 328) AND CONSENSUS
⭐   NECESSARY (Day 343). ⭐ One fact, three days.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ CP and AP as behaviours, not labels

```
⭐⭐ CP — REFUSE RATHER THAN BE WRONG.
⭐   ⭐ The minority side of a partition ***stops serving.***
⭐   ⭐ Examples: ZooKeeper, etcd, Consul, ⭐ HBase, ⭐ MongoDB
⭐     with majority write concern, ⭐ a Postgres primary with
⭐     synchronous replication.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Correct choice for: ⭐⭐ LOCKS, LEADER ELECTION,
⭐     CONFIGURATION, INVENTORY THAT MUST NOT OVERSELL, ACCOUNT
⭐     BALANCES.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE COST, SAID PLAINLY: A CP SYSTEM CAN BE DOWN WHILE
⭐     EVERY MACHINE IS HEALTHY.

⭐⭐ AP — ANSWER, POSSIBLY STALE, RECONCILE LATER.
⭐   ⭐ Both sides keep serving; they converge afterwards.
⭐   ⭐ Examples: Cassandra, DynamoDB (default), Riak, ⭐ DNS,
⭐     ⭐ most caches, ⭐ CRDT-based collaborative editors.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Correct choice for: ⭐⭐ FEEDS, PROFILES, PRODUCT
⭐     CATALOGUES, LIKE COUNTS, ANALYTICS, SHOPPING CARTS.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE COST: YOU MUST DEFINE A MERGE RULE, AND "LAST
⭐     WRITE WINS" IS A MERGE RULE THAT ***DELETES DATA*** —
⭐     with unsynchronised clocks, the "last" write may be the
⭐     earlier one.

⭐⭐ AND THE INSIGHT THAT MAKES THIS USEFUL RATHER THAN
⭐   ACADEMIC:
⭐   ***THE CHOICE IS PER-OPERATION, NOT PER-SYSTEM.***
⭐   ⭐ Amazon's cart is AP: never refuse an "add to cart", and
⭐     merge divergent carts by union — ⭐⭐ A RESURRECTED
⭐     DELETED ITEM IS A BETTER OUTCOME THAN A LOST SALE.
⭐   ⭐ Amazon's checkout is CP: ⭐⭐ THE CHARGE MUST BE
⭐     LINEARIZABLE.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ ONE COMPANY, ONE CHECKOUT FLOW, BOTH ANSWERS. Saying
⭐     this is the single most useful thing you can say about
⭐     CAP in an interview.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ PACELC: the half that describes your actual Tuesdays

```
⭐⭐ ***IF (P)ARTITION: CHOOSE (A)VAILABILITY OR (C)ONSISTENCY.
⭐      (E)LSE: CHOOSE (L)ATENCY OR (C)ONSISTENCY.***

⭐⭐ WHY IT IS THE BETTER TOOL:
⭐   ⭐ Partitions are rare — hours per year.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE LATENCY/CONSISTENCY TRADE IS PAID ON ***EVERY
⭐     SINGLE REQUEST***, forever.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SO THE 'ELSE' CLAUSE DESCRIBES 99.9% OF YOUR SYSTEM'S
⭐     LIFE, AND CAP DESCRIBES THE OTHER 0.1%.

⭐⭐ WHERE THE ELSE-COST COMES FROM, CONCRETELY:
⭐   ⭐ Linearizable = the write must reach a quorum, or the
⭐     read must confirm leadership, ⭐⭐ WHICH IS A NETWORK
⭐     ROUND TRIP YOU CANNOT OPTIMISE AWAY.
⭐   ⭐ Same DC: +0.5–2 ms. ⭐ Cross-region: ⭐⭐ +100–200 ms,
⭐     PER OPERATION.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ That is why a multi-region strongly-consistent write
⭐     path is a product decision, not a config flag.

⭐⭐ THE CLASSIFICATIONS WORTH KNOWING:
⭐   ⭐ ***PA/EL***  — Cassandra, DynamoDB, Riak. ⭐ Fast always,
⭐     stale sometimes.
⭐   ⭐ ***PC/EC***  — ZooKeeper, etcd, ⭐ Spanner, ⭐ HBase.
⭐     ⭐ Correct always, slower always.
⭐   ⭐ ***PC/EL***  — MongoDB, ⭐ Postgres with async replicas.
⭐     ⭐⭐ THE MOST COMMON REAL SHAPE: consistent when it
⭐     matters, fast reads from replicas the rest of the time.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND SPANNER IS THE INTERESTING ONE: IT IS PC/EC AND
⭐     BUYS ITS CONSISTENCY WITH ***ATOMIC CLOCKS*** —
⭐     TrueTime's bounded uncertainty, with commits that
⭐     deliberately ***wait out*** the error interval. ⭐ It did
⭐     not beat CAP; ⭐⭐ IT PAID FOR C IN LATENCY AND HARDWARE.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐ Partition behaviour you can actually design

```
⭐⭐ THE FOUR QUESTIONS TO ASK OF ANY SYSTEM UNDER PARTITION:
⭐   ① ⭐ WHO IS THE MAJORITY? ⭐⭐ WITH TWO DATACENTRES THERE IS
⭐        NO MAJORITY — which is why quorum systems need three,
⭐        and why a two-DC "HA" setup usually cannot fail over
⭐        safely at all. ⭐ A cheap third witness node solves it.
⭐   ② ⭐ WHAT DOES THE MINORITY DO? Refuse writes but serve
⭐        stale reads is often the right, and rarely the
⭐        default, answer.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ HOW DO WE DETECT HEALING, AND WHAT MERGES?
⭐   ④ ⭐ WHAT DOES THE USER SEE? ⭐⭐ "SAVED" MUST NEVER BE
⭐        SHOWN FOR A WRITE THAT MAY BE DISCARDED.

⭐⭐ AND THE DESIGN MOVE THAT BEATS THE WHOLE ARGUMENT:
⭐   ***MAKE THE OPERATION COMMUTATIVE AND YOU STOP NEEDING TO
⭐      CHOOSE.***
⭐   ⭐ `balance = 100` conflicts. `balance += 10` does not.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ WHICH IS WHY DAY 315 SAID "A LEDGER, NOT A BALANCE" —
⭐     ⭐⭐ AN APPEND-ONLY LOG OF DELTAS MERGES BY UNION,
⭐     UNCONDITIONALLY. ⭐ That LLD decision turns out to have
⭐     been a distributed-systems decision.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ CRDTs are this idea taken seriously: G-counters,
⭐     OR-sets, and the LWW-register (⭐ which is a CRDT that
⭐     legitimises data loss, and should be named as such).
```

# Part 6 — ⭐⭐ The sentences that get CAP wrong in interviews

```
⭐⭐ ❌ "WE CHOSE AP." ⭐ For everything? Including auth and
⭐   payments? ⭐⭐ SAY WHICH OPERATIONS.

⭐⭐ ❌ "MONGODB IS CP, CASSANDRA IS AP." ⭐ Both are tunable.
⭐   Mongo with `w:1` behaves AP-ish; Cassandra with
⭐   `QUORUM`+`QUORUM` behaves CP-ish. ⭐⭐ THE LABEL IS A
⭐   DEFAULT, NOT AN IDENTITY.

⭐⭐ ❌ "WE'RE CA BECAUSE OUR NETWORK IS RELIABLE." ⭐⭐ THIS IS
⭐   THE ANSWER THAT ENDS ROUNDS. ⭐ Reliable networks partition
⭐   too — and a long GC pause, a full connection pool or a
⭐   saturated NIC is a partition as far as the algorithm can
⭐   tell.

⭐⭐ ❌ "EVENTUAL CONSISTENCY IS FINE." ⭐ Eventual by when?
⭐   ⭐⭐ "EVENTUALLY" IS NOT AN SLO. ⭐ Say "p99 convergence
⭐   under 2 seconds, and here is how I measure it" (Day 322's
⭐   freshness SLI).

⭐⭐ ❌ "CAP MEANS PICK TWO." ⭐⭐ IT MEANS PICK ONE, AND ONLY
⭐   DURING A PARTITION.

⭐⭐ ✅ THE ANSWER THAT LANDS:
⭐   ***"Partitions aren't optional, so the real question is
⭐      what each operation does during one. Cart-add stays
⭐      available and merges by union; checkout refuses rather
⭐      than double-charge. And on a normal day — PACELC's else
⭐      branch — I take the latency hit for the charge and read
⭐      the catalogue from a replica."***
```

# Part 7 — ⭐ Where this shows up in your own stack

```
⭐⭐ YOU HAVE ALREADY MADE FIVE CAP DECISIONS WITHOUT NAMING
⭐   THEM. NAME THEM NOW:
⭐   ① ⭐ Reading from a Postgres replica (Day 328) ⇒ ***EL***.
⭐        ⭐ You chose latency over consistency, every request.
⭐   ② ⭐ Redis cache in front of the database (Day 326) ⇒
⭐        ***EL***, with the TTL as your staleness bound.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ THE OUTBOX PATTERN (Day 263) ⇒ ***EC WITHIN THE
⭐        TRANSACTION, EL OUTSIDE IT.*** ⭐ It exists precisely
⭐        to avoid a distributed transaction while keeping the
⭐        local write linearizable.
⭐   ④ ⭐ `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on a seat (Day 316) ⇒ ***PC/EC***
⭐        for that operation, deliberately.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐ A CDN (Day 327) ⇒ ***the most aggressively AP/EL thing
⭐        you own***, and nobody objects, because the staleness
⭐        budget was chosen consciously.

⭐⭐ THE POINT: ⭐⭐ CAP IS NOT A CHOICE YOU MAKE ONCE ON A
⭐   WHITEBOARD. ⭐ It is a property of every read path you have
⭐   already built — and the mature version of this skill is
⭐   being able to walk your own system and label each path.
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ "Pick two" | P isn't a choice. You pick one, and only while a partition is happening |
| ⭐⭐ Claiming CA for a distributed system | Only a single node is CA. Claiming it signals you haven't thought about the network |
| ⭐⭐ "Our network is reliable" | A GC pause, a full pool or a saturated NIC is indistinguishable from a partition |
| ⭐ Using ACID's C for CAP's C | CAP's C is linearizability — one register behaving like one copy |
| ⭐ Thinking A means uptime | A means every non-failing node answers. "Try again later" already failed A |
| ⭐⭐ One CAP label for a whole system | The choice is per-operation. Cart AP, checkout CP, same flow |
| ⭐ Treating database labels as identities | Mongo and Cassandra are both tunable. The label is a default |
| ⭐⭐ "Eventually consistent" with no number | Eventually isn't an SLO. Give a p99 convergence target and measure it |
| ⭐ Last-write-wins as a merge strategy | It's a rule that deletes data, and with skewed clocks it can keep the older write |
| ⭐⭐ Two datacentres for quorum | Two sides, no majority. You need three, or a witness |
| ⭐ Ignoring the ELSE branch | You pay latency-vs-consistency on every request; you pay CAP a few hours a year |

## Interview questions

**1. "Explain CAP."**
> ⭐⭐ It's not pick-two. Partitions are weather, not a choice, so the theorem says one thing: **during
> a partition, a node either refuses to answer or answers possibly-stale.** ⭐ Then I'd give the
> two-node proof in three lines, because it shows it's an information limit rather than an engineering
> one — N2 cannot know something nobody told it. ⭐⭐ And I'd say the choice is per-operation: cart-add
> stays available and merges by union, checkout refuses rather than double-charge.

**2. "What's PACELC and why do you prefer it?"**
> ⭐ If partitioned, choose availability or consistency; **else, choose latency or consistency.**
> ⭐⭐ I prefer it because partitions are hours a year while the else-branch is paid on every request
> forever — linearizability costs a quorum round trip, which is a couple of milliseconds in one
> datacentre and a hundred and fifty across regions. ⭐ Most real systems are PC/EL: consistent when it
> matters, replica reads the rest of the time.

**3. "Is Spanner a counterexample?"**
> ⭐ No — it's PC/EC and it pays for consistency in latency and hardware. ⭐⭐ TrueTime gives bounded
> clock uncertainty using GPS and atomic clocks, and commits **deliberately wait out the uncertainty
> interval** before acknowledging. ⭐ It didn't defeat the theorem; it bought a tighter bound and made
> the cost predictable enough to live with.

**4. "Design a shopping cart under partition."**
> ⭐ AP, without hesitation: never refuse an add, because a lost sale is worse than a resurrected item.
> ⭐⭐ Merge by union, and store the cart as **a log of add/remove operations rather than a final list**
> — that makes the merge commutative and removes the conflict instead of resolving it. ⭐ Checkout
> flips to CP: at that point I'd rather show "try again" than charge twice.

**5. "What breaks with two datacentres?"**
> ⭐⭐ There's no majority — a partition gives you two equal halves, so a quorum system can't safely
> elect anyone and either both sides serve (split brain) or neither does. ⭐ The fix is a third site,
> even a tiny witness node that holds no data and only votes. ⭐ Two-DC "HA" setups usually can't fail
> over automatically without risking divergence, and that surprises people.

**6. "Where has your own system already made this choice?"**
> ⭐ Five places. Replica reads are EL. The Redis cache is EL with the TTL as the staleness bound.
> ⭐⭐ The outbox pattern is EC inside the transaction and EL outside it — **it exists precisely to
> avoid a distributed transaction while keeping the local write linearizable.** ⭐ `SELECT … FOR
> UPDATE` on a seat is PC/EC on purpose. And the CDN is the most aggressively AP thing I own, which
> nobody objects to because the staleness budget was chosen deliberately.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Correct the framing first: **CAP is not pick-two, because partitions are weather
rather than a choice** — the theorem says only that during a partition a node must either refuse or
answer possibly-stale. Give the proof in three lines, because it proves it's an information limit, not
an engineering one: **two nodes, the link cut, a write to one, a read from the other — it can return
the old value or refuse, and no cleverness creates a third option.** Then say the thing that makes it
usable: **the choice is per operation, not per system** — Amazon's cart stays available and merges by
union because a resurrected item beats a lost sale, and Amazon's checkout refuses rather than
double-charge. Then move to PACELC and explain why it's the better tool: **partitions cost you hours a
year, but the latency-versus-consistency trade is paid on every request forever** — a quorum round
trip is a couple of milliseconds in one datacentre and a hundred and fifty across regions, which is
why multi-region strong consistency is a product decision. Close on the move that dissolves the
argument: **make the operation commutative and there's nothing to choose** — `balance = 100` conflicts,
`balance += 10` doesn't, which is why "a ledger, not a balance" was a distributed-systems decision
disguised as a class-design one.
