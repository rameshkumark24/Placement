# Day 398 🕳️

**P-398** · 🕳️ **The eight fallacies** — ⭐⭐ and the ninth, which is the real one

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: the eight fallacies are all the same mistake wearing eight costumes —
> believing a remote call is a local call — and the one consequence that matters is that a remote call
> has *three* outcomes, not two: succeeded, failed, and **unknown**. Everything in this stage is an
> answer to the third one.**

# Part 1 — The eight, each with the Python that embodies it

```
⭐⭐ ① ***THE NETWORK IS RELIABLE.***
⭐    ⭐ The code: `requests.get(url)` — ⭐⭐ NO TIMEOUT, NO RETRY,
⭐      NO CIRCUIT BREAKER, NO IDEMPOTENCY KEY.
⭐    ⭐⭐ THE COST: ***THE UNKNOWN OUTCOME*** (PART 3).

⭐⭐ ② ***LATENCY IS ZERO.***
⭐    ⭐ In-process call: ~100 ns.
⭐    ⭐ Same-datacentre RPC: ~500 µs – 1 ms.
⭐    ⭐⭐ THAT IS A ***5,000–10,000×*** DIFFERENCE, ⭐ which is why
⭐      "just extract a microservice" is not a refactor —
⭐      ⭐⭐ IT CHANGES WHICH ALGORITHMS YOU ARE ALLOWED TO USE.
⭐      ⭐ A loop that calls a helper 500 times is free in-process
⭐      and half a second across the wire (Day 399).

⭐⭐ ③ ***BANDWIDTH IS INFINITE.***
⭐    ⭐ Returning the whole object graph because it was free when
⭐      it was a Python object. ⭐⭐ DAY 345's 50× METADATA PAYLOAD.

⭐⭐ ④ ***THE NETWORK IS SECURE.***
⭐    ⭐⭐ THE INTERNAL NETWORK IS NOT A TRUST BOUNDARY. ⭐ One
⭐      compromised pod is inside it (Day 382's SSRF). ⭐ mTLS,
⭐      and authenticate service-to-service calls.

⭐⭐ ⑤ ***TOPOLOGY DOES NOT CHANGE.***
⭐    ⭐⭐ ADDRESSES CHANGE CONSTANTLY, AND YOUR POOL HOLDS OPEN
⭐      SOCKETS TO THE OLD ONES. ⭐ Stage 11's second shape, and
⭐      it cost you forty minutes on Day 387.

⭐⭐ ⑥ ***THERE IS ONE ADMINISTRATOR.***
⭐    ⭐ The other team deploys at 4 p.m. on a Friday and does not
⭐      tell you. ⭐⭐ SO YOUR SERVICE MUST TOLERATE ITS
⭐      DEPENDENCIES RESTARTING, WITHOUT COORDINATION.

⭐⭐ ⑦ ***TRANSPORT COST IS ZERO.***
⭐    ⭐ Two real costs: ⭐⭐ SERIALISATION CPU (JSON-PARSING A 5 MB
⭐      RESPONSE IS REAL WORK AND IT IS ON YOUR CRITICAL PATH),
⭐      ⭐ and money (Day 380 — every cross-AZ byte, both ways).

⭐⭐ ⑧ ***THE NETWORK IS HOMOGENEOUS.***
⭐    ⭐ Different MTUs, proxies, TLS terminators, ⭐ and — the one
⭐      that bites — ⭐⭐ ***A DIFFERENT TIMEOUT AT EVERY LAYER.***
⭐      ⭐ ALB 60 s, nginx 60 s, gunicorn 30 s, requests ∞,
⭐      psycopg ∞. ⭐⭐ ⇒ THE RULE: ***THE TIMEOUT BUDGET MUST
⭐      DECREASE AS YOU GO DOWN THE STACK***, ⭐ or a slow leaf
⭐      holds every layer above it open until the top one gives
⭐      up (Day 336).
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The ninth fallacy

```
⭐⭐ ***"THE OTHER END IS THE VERSION YOU TESTED AGAINST."***

⭐ Why it deserves to be on the list — ⭐⭐ AND WHY IT IS ARGUABLY
⭐   THE ***MOST*** EXPENSIVE ONE IN A MODERN SYSTEM:
⭐   ⭐ In a continuously-deployed system, ⭐⭐ THERE ARE ***ALWAYS***
⭐     AT LEAST TWO VERSIONS OF EVERYTHING RUNNING. ⭐ Not during
⭐     the deploy — ⭐⭐ ***PERMANENTLY***, because someone is
⭐     always deploying something.
⭐   ⭐ A message published by v2 is consumed an hour later by v1
⭐     after a rollback (Day 371).
⭐   ⭐ A browser that loaded v2's JavaScript calls v1's API.
⭐   ⭐ An event written in 2024 is replayed into a projection
⭐     written in 2026 (Day 405).
⭐   ⭐⭐ A KAFKA TOPIC CONTAINS SEVEN YEARS OF SCHEMA HISTORY AND
⭐     YOUR CONSUMER MUST READ ALL OF IT.

⭐⭐ ⇒ SO THE DISCIPLINE IS NOT "TEST AGAINST THE CURRENT
⭐   VERSION". ⭐ It is: ⭐⭐ ***EVERY WIRE FORMAT IS FOREVER, AND
⭐   EVERY CHANGE TO ONE MUST BE COMPATIBLE IN BOTH DIRECTIONS AT
⭐   ONCE.*** ⭐ Day 400 gives you the rules for protobuf, ⭐ Day
⭐   405 for events, ⭐ and Day 371 already gave you the five
⭐   application-level requirements.

⭐ ⭐ (There is an implicit tenth — ⭐⭐ "WE ALL AGREE WHAT TIME IT
⭐   IS" — ⭐ and it is wrong enough to need its own day. Day 409.)
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ Three outcomes, and the one that is the whole subject

```
⭐ A LOCAL FUNCTION CALL HAS TWO OUTCOMES: ⭐ it returned, or it
⭐   raised.
⭐⭐ A REMOTE CALL HAS ***THREE***:

⭐   ⭐ ① ***SUCCEEDED*** — ⭐ you got a response. ⭐ Nothing to do.
⭐   ⭐ ② ***FAILED, DEFINITELY*** — ⭐ connection refused, DNS
⭐      failure, a 400. ⭐⭐ THE REQUEST NEVER RAN. ⭐ Safe to retry.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ③ ***UNKNOWN*** — ⭐ a timeout, a connection reset, a 502.
⭐      ⭐⭐ ***DID THE WORK HAPPEN OR NOT? YOU CANNOT TELL, AND
⭐      YOU NEVER WILL BE ABLE TO.***

⭐⭐ AND THE REASON IS NOT AN ENGINEERING LIMITATION — IT IS
⭐   INFORMATION-THEORETIC:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ***YOU CANNOT DISTINGUISH A LOST REQUEST FROM A LOST
⭐   RESPONSE.*** ⭐ Both look identical from your side. ⭐ Adding an
⭐   acknowledgement does not help, because the acknowledgement
⭐   can also be lost, ⭐⭐ AND SO CAN THE ACKNOWLEDGEMENT OF THE
⭐   ACKNOWLEDGEMENT. ⭐ That is the ***TWO GENERALS PROBLEM***,
⭐   and it has no solution.

⭐⭐ ⇒ SO EVERY TECHNIQUE IN THIS STAGE IS ONE OF EXACTLY THREE
⭐   RESPONSES TO OUTCOME ③:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ① ***MAKE IT NOT MATTER*** — idempotency, so a retry is
⭐      harmless (Days 335, 407).
⭐   ⭐⭐ ② ***ASK AGAIN LATER*** — read back the state, or
⭐      reconcile (Day 358's `unknown` payment).
⭐   ⭐⭐ ③ ***COMPENSATE*** — do a new, visible thing that undoes
⭐      the business effect (Day 408).
⭐   ⭐ There is no fourth. ⭐⭐ AND A SYSTEM THAT HAS NOT CHOSEN ONE
⭐     OF THE THREE HAS CHOSEN "SILENT INCONSISTENCY".
```

# Part 4 — Two Generals and FLP, read usefully

```
⭐ ***TWO GENERALS*** — ⭐ no protocol over a lossy channel can
⭐   give two parties ***common knowledge*** of a decision.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ THE PRACTICAL READING: ***YOU CAN NEVER BE CERTAIN THE
⭐     OTHER SIDE KNOWS WHAT YOU KNOW.*** ⭐ Which is why "the
⭐     client confirmed" is a fact about your logs, not about the
⭐     client.

⭐ ***FLP (1985)*** — ⭐ in an asynchronous system with even ***one***
⭐   crash failure, ⭐⭐ NO DETERMINISTIC CONSENSUS ALGORITHM CAN
⭐   GUARANTEE TERMINATION.

⭐⭐ AND THE READING THAT MAKES FLP USEFUL RATHER THAN TRIVIA —
⭐   ⭐ because people quote it as "consensus is impossible", which
⭐   is wrong and unhelpful:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ***FLP DOES NOT SAY YOU CANNOT ACHIEVE CONSENSUS. IT SAYS
⭐   YOU CANNOT GUARANTEE YOU WILL ACHIEVE IT ***IN BOUNDED
⭐   TIME*** WITHOUT ADDING AN ASSUMPTION.***
⭐   ⭐ So every real system adds one:
⭐     ⭐⭐ RAFT ADDS A ***TIMING*** ASSUMPTION — a randomised
⭐       election timeout (Day 410). ⭐ If the network is slower
⭐       than the timeout forever, Raft never elects a leader —
⭐       ⭐⭐ AND THAT IS EXACTLY FLP, VISIBLE IN PRODUCTION AS
⭐       "THE CLUSTER KEEPS RE-ELECTING".
⭐     ⭐ Others add randomisation and accept termination with
⭐       probability 1.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ SO THE SENTENCE TO CARRY: ***THE IMPOSSIBILITY RESULTS
⭐     DO NOT FORBID ANYTHING; THEY TELL YOU WHICH ASSUMPTION YOU
⭐     ARE MAKING. AND IF YOU CANNOT NAME YOUR ASSUMPTION, YOU
⭐     ARE STILL MAKING ONE.***
```

# Part 5 — The Python "wait forever" audit

```
⭐⭐ ***EVERY ONE OF THESE DEFAULTS TO INFINITY IN PYTHON.***
⭐   ⭐ Print this and go through your codebase once.

⭐ requests.get(url)                    ⭐⭐ → NO TIMEOUT
⭐   ✅ requests.get(url, timeout=(3, 10))   ⭐⭐ THE ***TUPLE***
⭐      (connect, read) — a bare `timeout=10` is read only.
⭐ httpx.AsyncClient()                  ⭐ → 5 s default ✅ (rare
⭐   sanity), ⭐ but set it explicitly anyway.
⭐ psycopg.connect(...)                 ⭐⭐ → NO connect_timeout
⭐ redis.Redis(...)                     ⭐⭐ → NO socket_timeout
⭐ boto3.client(...)                    ⭐ → 60 s connect/read
⭐   ✅ Config(connect_timeout=3, read_timeout=10,
⭐             retries={"max_attempts": 3, "mode": "standard"})
⭐ subprocess.run(cmd)                  ⭐⭐ → NO TIMEOUT
⭐ asyncio.gather(*tasks)               ⭐⭐ → NO TIMEOUT
⭐   ✅ async with asyncio.timeout(5): ...   ⭐ (3.11+)
⭐ socket.create_connection(...)        ⭐⭐ → OS DEFAULT (~130 s)
⭐ kafka consumer poll()                ⭐ → pass a timeout
⭐ sqlalchemy engine                    ⭐⭐ → NO statement timeout
⭐   ✅ ALTER ROLE app SET statement_timeout='10s' (Day 387)

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE RULE THAT MAKES THE AUDIT WORTH DOING: ***AN
⭐   UNBOUNDED WAIT IS NOT "PATIENT" — IT IS A WORKER YOU HAVE
⭐   DONATED TO A BROKEN DEPENDENCY***, ⭐ and with N workers you
⭐   have donated all of them within one p99 (Day 336).
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. ⭐⭐ "What's the fundamental difference between a local call and a remote one?"**
> ⭐ A local call has two outcomes; a remote call has three. ⭐⭐ **Succeeded, definitely failed, and
> unknown — and unknown is the whole subject.** ⭐ A timeout or a reset tells you nothing about whether
> the work happened, ⭐⭐ **and that's not an engineering limitation you can fix: you can never
> distinguish a lost request from a lost response, because the acknowledgement can be lost too, and so
> can the acknowledgement of the acknowledgement.** ⭐ That's Two Generals. ⭐ So there are exactly
> three responses: ⭐⭐ **make it not matter with idempotency, ask again later by reading back or
> reconciling, or compensate with a new visible fact.** ⭐ A system that hasn't chosen one has chosen
> silent inconsistency.

**2. "Which fallacy costs the most in practice?"**
> ⭐ Latency-is-zero costs the most *design* mistakes, because the difference is 5,000 to 10,000× —
> ⭐⭐ **so extracting a microservice isn't a refactor, it changes which algorithms you're allowed to
> use; a loop calling a helper 500 times is free in-process and half a second across the wire.**
> ⭐ But the one I'd add to the list is a ninth: ⭐⭐ **the other end is not the version you tested
> against.** ⭐ In a continuously-deployed system there are always at least two versions of everything
> running — permanently, not just during a deploy — ⭐⭐ **a message published by v2 is consumed by v1
> after a rollback, and a Kafka topic contains seven years of schema history that one consumer must
> read.** ⭐ So every wire format is forever and every change must be compatible in both directions at
> once.

**3. ⭐⭐ "What does FLP actually say, and does it matter?"**
> ⭐ It says that in an asynchronous system with even one crash failure, no deterministic consensus
> algorithm can guarantee termination. ⭐⭐ **People quote it as "consensus is impossible", which is
> wrong and unhelpful. What it actually says is that you can't guarantee reaching consensus in bounded
> time without adding an assumption** — ⭐ so every real system adds one. ⭐⭐ **Raft adds a timing
> assumption in the form of a randomised election timeout, and if the network is slower than that
> timeout indefinitely, Raft never elects a leader — which is FLP visible in production as "the cluster
> keeps re-electing".** ⭐ The useful sentence is that impossibility results don't forbid anything, they
> tell you which assumption you're making — ⭐⭐ **and if you can't name yours, you're still making one.**

**4. "How do you decide timeouts across a stack?"**
> ⭐⭐ **The budget must decrease as you go down.** ⭐ If the load balancer allows 60 seconds, the app
> allows 30, the downstream call allows 10 and the database statement allows 5, ⭐⭐ **then a slow leaf
> fails at the leaf instead of holding every layer above it open until the top one gives up.** ⭐ Get
> that backwards and one slow dependency consumes every worker in every service in the chain — which
> is the cascade. ⭐ And I'd audit for Python's defaults specifically, ⭐⭐ **because `requests`,
> `psycopg`, `redis-py`, `subprocess` and `asyncio.gather` all wait forever by default** — and an
> unbounded wait isn't patience, it's a worker donated to a broken dependency.

**5. "Your service calls a payment API and times out. Now what?"**
> ⭐ That's outcome three, so the answer is decided before the call, not after. ⭐⭐ **I send an
> idempotency key so a retry is safe; if I can't, I read back by querying the provider for that key;
> and if the provider can't tell me either, the charge sits in an explicit `unknown` state that a
> reconciliation job resolves against their settlement file.** ⭐ What I never do is guess — ⭐⭐ **a
> timeout treated as a failure double-charges, and a timeout treated as a success loses money, so the
> state machine needs a third state and the interface needs to show it.**

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

remote call is a local call** — and then go straight to the consequence that matters. **A local call

entire subject.** And it's not fixable: **you can never distinguish a lost request from a lost

later by reading back or reconciling, or compensate with a new visible fact — and a system that hasn't

isn't on the list: **the other end is not the version you tested against. In a continuously deployed
