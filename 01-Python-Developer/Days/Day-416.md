# Day 416 🏛️

**P-416** · 🏛️ **The transformer and attention** — ⭐⭐ enough architecture to reason about cost

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: attention is every token looking at every other token, which is O(n²) — and
> that single quadratic is why long contexts are expensive, why the window has a limit at all, why
> prefill and decode have completely different performance characteristics, and why KV caching exists.
> You do not need the maths; you need the exponent.**

# Part 1 — The stack, in the order data flows

```
⭐ ① ⭐ ***TOKENISE*** — text → integers (Day 415).
⭐ ② ⭐ ***EMBED*** — each integer → a vector (e.g. 4,096 floats).
⭐    ⭐⭐ AT THIS POINT THE MODEL IS WORKING WITH ***MEANING***,
⭐    NOT CHARACTERS.
⭐ ③ ⭐ ***POSITIONAL ENCODING*** — add position information,
⭐    ⭐⭐ BECAUSE ATTENTION IS ORDER-BLIND BY CONSTRUCTION AND
⭐    "DOG BITES MAN" WOULD OTHERWISE EQUAL "MAN BITES DOG".
⭐    ⭐ Modern models use RoPE (rotary), ⭐⭐ WHICH IS WHY CONTEXT
⭐    WINDOWS CAN BE EXTENDED SOMEWHAT AFTER TRAINING — ⭐ and why
⭐    a model stretched far past its trained length degrades.
⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***N TRANSFORMER BLOCKS*** (32–120 of them). ⭐ Each is:
⭐      ⭐ multi-head ***attention*** (Part 2)
⭐      ⭐ a ***feed-forward*** network (where most parameters
⭐        live, ⭐⭐ AND WHERE MOST "KNOWLEDGE" IS STORED)
⭐      ⭐ residual connections + layer norm (⭐⭐ THE PART THAT
⭐        MAKES 100 LAYERS TRAINABLE AT ALL)
⭐ ⑤ ⭐ ***UNEMBED*** → a score for every token in the
⭐    vocabulary → softmax → a probability distribution.
⭐ ⑥ ⭐ ***SAMPLE*** one token (Day 418), append, ⭐⭐ AND RUN THE
⭐    ENTIRE STACK AGAIN (DAY 414).
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ Attention, and the exponent that matters

```
⭐ THE INTUITION, WITHOUT THE MATHS: ⭐ for each token, the model
⭐   asks ***"which other tokens should I be looking at to
⭐   predict what comes next?"***

⭐   "The trophy didn't fit in the suitcase because ***it***
⭐    was too big."
⭐   ⭐⭐ TO RESOLVE "IT", THE MODEL MUST ATTEND TO "TROPHY"
⭐   RATHER THAN "SUITCASE". ⭐ Attention is the mechanism that
⭐   lets a token 8 positions back influence this one directly,
⭐   ⭐⭐ WITHOUT PASSING THROUGH EVERY TOKEN IN BETWEEN — ⭐ which
⭐   is exactly what RNNs could not do, and is the whole reason
⭐   transformers won.

⭐ MECHANICALLY: ⭐ every token produces a ***query***, a
⭐   ***key*** and a ***value***. ⭐ Query · key = "how relevant
⭐   is that token to me". ⭐ Softmax the scores, ⭐ take a
⭐   weighted sum of the values.
⭐ ⭐ ***MULTI-HEAD***: do it 32–128 times in parallel with
⭐   different learned projections, ⭐⭐ SO DIFFERENT HEADS LEARN
⭐   DIFFERENT RELATIONSHIPS — ⭐ one tracks syntax, one tracks
⭐   coreference, one tracks the topic.

⭐⭐ ***AND HERE IS THE ENGINEERING FACT: EVERY TOKEN COMPUTES A
⭐   SCORE AGAINST EVERY OTHER TOKEN. THAT IS n × n.***
⭐   ⭐ 1,000 tokens → 1,000,000 scores
⭐   ⭐ 10,000 tokens → ⭐⭐ 100,000,000 — ***100× THE WORK FOR 10×
⭐     THE TEXT***
⭐   ⭐ 100,000 tokens → 10,000,000,000
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ SO "MY PROMPT GOT 4× LONGER" IS NOT 4× THE ATTENTION
⭐     COMPUTE — IT IS ***16×***. ⭐ Providers bill input roughly
⭐     linearly, so you do not see the full curve in the price,
⭐     ⭐⭐ BUT YOU SEE IT IN LATENCY, IN RATE LIMITS, AND IN WHY
⭐     THE CONTEXT WINDOW HAS A CEILING AT ALL.
⭐ ⭐ (This is what FlashAttention, sliding-window attention,
⭐   GQA and MQA are all attacking. ⭐⭐ THE EXPONENT IS THE
⭐   INDUSTRY'S CENTRAL ENGINEERING PROBLEM.)
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ Prefill vs decode, and why they behave differently

```
⭐⭐ ***PREFILL*** — processing your prompt.
⭐   ⭐ Every token is known up front, so ⭐⭐ ALL OF THEM ARE
⭐     PROCESSED IN ***PARALLEL*** IN ONE PASS.
⭐   ⭐ ***COMPUTE-BOUND.*** ⭐ The GPU's arithmetic units are the
⭐     bottleneck. ⭐ Scales with n².
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ THIS IS YOUR ***TIME TO FIRST TOKEN***.

⭐⭐ ***DECODE*** — generating the answer.
⭐   ⭐ One token at a time, each needing a full forward pass
⭐     through every layer.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ***MEMORY-BANDWIDTH-BOUND.*** ⭐ The bottleneck is
⭐     ***reading the model's weights out of GPU memory***, once
⭐     per token. ⭐ A 70-billion-parameter model at 2 bytes each
⭐     is 140 GB read per token.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ THIS IS YOUR ***TOKENS PER SECOND***, AND IT IS
⭐     ROUGHLY CONSTANT REGARDLESS OF HOW MUCH COMPUTE IS FREE.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND NOW THE FOUR THINGS FROM PART 0 EXPLAIN THEMSELVES:
⭐   ⭐ ① A long prompt costs quadratic ***prefill*** — ⭐⭐ SLOW
⭐      FIRST TOKEN, THEN NORMAL STREAMING.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ② ***BATCHING HELPS DECODE ENORMOUSLY*** — ⭐ you read the
⭐      weights once and serve 64 requests with that read, ⭐⭐ SO
⭐      THROUGHPUT MULTIPLIES WHILE ***YOUR*** REQUEST GETS NO
⭐      FASTER. ⭐ That is the entire economics of a hosted API,
⭐      and of vLLM (Day 452).
⭐   ⭐ ③ Streaming exists because decode is inherently
⭐      incremental — ⭐⭐ SO SHOWING TOKENS AS THEY ARRIVE IS FREE
⭐      AND TRANSFORMS PERCEIVED LATENCY (DAY 453).
⭐   ⭐ ④ A bigger model is slower per token in direct
⭐      proportion to its weights, ⭐⭐ WHICH IS WHY A SMALL MODEL
⭐      IS NOT SLIGHTLY FASTER, IT IS SEVERAL TIMES FASTER
⭐      (DAY 457).
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ The KV cache, and the memory limit nobody expects

```
⭐ THE PROBLEM: ⭐ generating token 501 needs attention over
⭐   tokens 1–500. ⭐⭐ RECOMPUTING THEIR KEYS AND VALUES EVERY
⭐   TIME WOULD MAKE GENERATION CUBIC.
⭐ THE FIX: ⭐⭐ ***CACHE EACH TOKEN'S KEYS AND VALUES. THE KV
⭐   CACHE.*** ⭐ Then each new token attends over cached state
⭐   and generation is linear per token.

⭐⭐ AND THE COST IS ***GPU MEMORY, PROPORTIONAL TO CONTEXT
⭐   LENGTH × BATCH SIZE***:
⭐   ⭐ 2 × layers × heads × head_dim × 2 bytes, per token,
⭐     per sequence.
⭐   ⭐⭐ FOR A LARGE MODEL THIS IS ROUGHLY ***HUNDREDS OF KB TO A
⭐     MEGABYTE PER TOKEN***, ⭐ so a 100k-token conversation can
⭐     hold tens of gigabytes of KV cache ***for one user***.

⭐⭐ ⇒ THREE CONSEQUENCES THAT EXPLAIN REAL BEHAVIOUR:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ① ***CONTEXT LENGTH AND CONCURRENCY TRADE OFF DIRECTLY.***
⭐      ⭐ A server's GPU memory is fixed, ⭐⭐ SO LONG CONTEXTS MEAN
⭐      FEWER SIMULTANEOUS USERS. ⭐ This is why providers price
⭐      long context higher and why self-hosting (Day 452) makes
⭐      you feel it immediately.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ② ***THIS IS WHAT PROMPT CACHING SELLS YOU*** (Day 415):
⭐      ⭐ the provider keeps the KV cache for your stable prefix,
⭐      ⭐⭐ SO THEY SKIP THE PREFILL ENTIRELY — ⭐ which is why the
⭐      discount is large and why the prefix must be
⭐      byte-identical and at the ***front***.
⭐   ⭐ ③ GQA/MQA exist to shrink this cache, ⭐⭐ WHICH IS WHY
⭐      MODERN MODELS SUPPORT LONGER CONTEXTS AT ALL.
```

# Part 5 — Parameters, and what "70B" tells you

```
⭐ PARAMETERS ARE THE LEARNED WEIGHTS. ⭐ Most of them live in
⭐   the feed-forward layers, ⭐⭐ WHICH IS WHERE MOST FACTUAL
⭐   KNOWLEDGE IS STORED — ⭐ attention routes information,
⭐   feed-forward ***holds*** it.

⭐ WHAT THE NUMBER PREDICTS:
⭐   ⭐ MEMORY: ⭐⭐ ~2 BYTES/PARAMETER AT fp16 ⇒ ***70B ≈ 140 GB***,
⭐     ⭐ which does not fit on one 80 GB GPU — ⭐⭐ SO YOU NEED TWO
⭐     CARDS OR QUANTISATION (DAY 452). ⭐ Quantised to 4-bit it
⭐     is ~35 GB and fits, ⭐ with a measurable quality cost.
⭐   ⭐ SPEED: ⭐⭐ INVERSELY PROPORTIONAL. ⭐ A 7B model is roughly
⭐     ten times faster per token than a 70B.
⭐   ⭐ CAPABILITY: ⭐⭐ CORRELATED BUT ***NOT*** DETERMINED. ⭐ Data
⭐     quality and post-training matter enormously — ⭐⭐ A
⭐     WELL-TRAINED 8B MODEL BEATS A BADLY-TRAINED 70B ON REAL
⭐     TASKS, ⭐ which is why parameter count is a poor
⭐     shopping metric.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE ENGINEERING VERSION: ***PARAMETER COUNT TELLS YOU
⭐   WHAT IT COSTS TO RUN, NOT WHAT IT IS GOOD AT.*** ⭐ For "what
⭐   is it good at", you run your own evals (Day 425). ⭐⭐ THERE IS
⭐   NO SUBSTITUTE AND NO LEADERBOARD THAT ANSWERS IT FOR YOUR
⭐   TASK.
```

# Part 6 — Mixture of Experts, in one paragraph

```
⭐ ⭐⭐ MoE MODELS (Mixtral, DeepSeek, and several frontier
⭐   models) HAVE MANY FEED-FORWARD "EXPERTS" AND ***ACTIVATE
⭐   ONLY A FEW PER TOKEN.***
⭐   ⭐ So a "141B" model might activate 39B per token.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ ***COMPUTE COST TRACKS THE ACTIVE PARAMETERS; MEMORY
⭐     COST TRACKS THE TOTAL.***
⭐   ⭐ Which means: ⭐⭐ FAST AND CHEAP TO ***RUN***, EXPENSIVE TO
⭐     ***HOST***, ⭐ because all the experts must be resident even
⭐     though most are idle.
⭐ ⭐⭐ ⇒ THE PRACTICAL READING: WHEN A PROVIDER OFFERS A VERY
⭐   CAPABLE MODEL AT A SURPRISINGLY LOW PRICE, MoE IS USUALLY
⭐   WHY — ⭐ and it also means self-hosting that same model is
⭐   much less attractive than the API price suggests (Day 452).
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. ⭐⭐ "Explain attention, and tell me why you care."**
> ⭐ Every token asks which other tokens it should look at, computes a relevance score against each of
> them, and takes a weighted sum — ⭐⭐ **so a token eight positions back can influence this one
> directly, without passing through everything in between, which is exactly what RNNs couldn't do and
> is why transformers won.** ⭐ Why I care is the exponent: ⭐⭐ **every token scores against every other
> token, so it's n². Ten times the text is a hundred times the attention work — which is why the
> context window has a ceiling at all, why time-to-first-token grows fast with prompt length, and why
> FlashAttention, GQA and sliding-window attention all exist.**

**2. ⭐⭐ "Why is the first token slow and the rest fast?"**
> ⭐ Because prefill and decode are different problems. ⭐⭐ **Prefill processes the whole prompt in
> parallel and is compute-bound, scaling with n² — that's your time-to-first-token. Decode produces one
> token per full forward pass and is memory-bandwidth-bound, because the bottleneck is reading the
> model's weights out of GPU memory once per token — a 70B model at two bytes each is 140 GB read per
> token.** ⭐ So tokens-per-second is roughly constant no matter how much compute is free, ⭐⭐ **and a
> smaller model isn't slightly faster, it's proportionally faster — which is the whole argument for
> model routing.**

**3. "Why does batching help throughput but not my latency?"**
> ⭐⭐ **Because decode is bandwidth-bound: you read the weights once and can serve sixty-four requests
> with that single read.** ⭐ So throughput multiplies while any individual request generates at the same
> rate. ⭐⭐ **That's the entire economics of a hosted API — and it's why self-hosting for one user is
> terrible value and self-hosting at scale can be excellent.**

**4. ⭐⭐ "What's the KV cache and why does it matter to me?"**
> ⭐ Generating token 501 needs attention over the previous 500, ⭐⭐ **so their keys and values are
> cached instead of recomputed — otherwise generation would be cubic.** ⭐ The cost is GPU memory
> proportional to context length times batch size, ⭐⭐ **roughly hundreds of kilobytes to a megabyte per
> token, so a 100k-token conversation can hold tens of gigabytes for one user.** ⭐ Three consequences:
> ⭐⭐ **context length and concurrency trade off directly, because GPU memory is fixed — long contexts
> mean fewer simultaneous users, which is why providers price long context higher. It's also exactly
> what prompt caching sells you: the provider keeps the KV cache for your stable prefix and skips
> prefill entirely, which is why the discount is large and why the prefix must be byte-identical and at
> the front.**

**5. "Does parameter count tell you how good a model is?"**
> ⭐⭐ **It tells you what it costs to run, not what it's good at.** ⭐ Memory is about two bytes per
> parameter, so 70B is 140 GB and doesn't fit on one 80 GB card without quantisation; speed is roughly
> inversely proportional, so a 7B is about ten times faster per token. ⭐⭐ **Capability is correlated but
> not determined — data quality and post-training matter enormously, and a well-trained 8B beats a
> badly-trained 70B on real tasks.** ⭐ For "is it good at *my* task" there's no substitute for running
> your own evals; no leaderboard answers that.

**6. "What's Mixture of Experts, practically?"**
> ⭐ Many feed-forward experts, only a few activated per token — ⭐⭐ **so compute cost tracks the active
> parameters while memory cost tracks the total. Fast and cheap to run, expensive to host, because
> every expert must be resident even though most are idle.** ⭐ The practical reading is that ⭐⭐ **when a
> provider offers a very capable model at a surprisingly low price, MoE is usually why — and it also
> means self-hosting that same model is far less attractive than the API price suggests.**

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

Then attention in one line and the exponent immediately: **every token scores against every other
token, so it's n² — ten times the text is a hundred times the work, and that single quadratic explains
why the context window has a ceiling, why time-to-first-token grows fast, and why half the research in
