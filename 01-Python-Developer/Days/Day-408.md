# Day 408 🔀

**P-408** · 🔀 **Sagas** — orchestration, choreography and compensation

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: a saga is what you build when you cannot have a transaction, and the hard
> part is never the happy path — it is that compensation is a *new visible fact*, not an undo, and
> that every saga has a point after which it can no longer be reversed at all.**

---

# Part 1 — What you lose, precisely

```
⭐ A LOCAL TRANSACTION GIVES YOU ***ACID***.
⭐⭐ A SAGA GIVES YOU ***ACD***. ⭐ You lose the ***I***.

⭐⭐ AND LOSING ISOLATION IS NOT A DETAIL — IT MEANS
⭐   ***INTERMEDIATE STATES ARE VISIBLE TO EVERYONE***, ⭐ so you
⭐   inherit exactly the anomalies a database spends its life
⭐   preventing (Day 189):
⭐   ⭐ ***DIRTY READS*** — ⭐ another process sees the order as
⭐     `PLACED` while step 3 of 5 is still running, ⭐⭐ AND ACTS
⭐     ON IT.
⭐   ⭐ ***LOST UPDATES*** — ⭐ two sagas touch the same aggregate
⭐     and the second overwrites the first.
⭐   ⭐ ***FUZZY READS*** — ⭐ a report run mid-saga sees half of a
⭐     business operation and balances to nothing.

⭐⭐ THE FIVE COUNTERMEASURES, AND THE FIRST IS THE ONE YOU WILL
⭐   ACTUALLY USE:
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***SEMANTIC LOCK*** — ⭐ a status field that says "in
⭐    progress": `PENDING`, `RESERVED`, `AWAITING_PAYMENT`.
⭐    ⭐⭐ EVERY READER MUST UNDERSTAND IT, WHICH IS THE COST —
⭐    ⭐ the intermediate state leaks into every query, every
⭐    screen and every report in the system.
⭐ ② ⭐ ***COMMUTATIVE UPDATES*** — ⭐ `balance += x` rather than
⭐    `balance = y`, ⭐⭐ SO ORDER STOPS MATTERING.
⭐ ③ ⭐ ***PESSIMISTIC ORDERING*** — ⭐⭐ DO THE REVERSIBLE THINGS
⭐    FIRST AND THE IRREVERSIBLE THINGS LAST. ⭐ This is Part 3,
⭐    and it is the most important design rule here.
⭐ ④ ⭐ ***RE-READ VALUE*** — ⭐ check the value has not changed
⭐    before acting (optimistic concurrency, Day 405).
⭐ ⑤ ⭐ ***VERSION FILE*** — ⭐ record out-of-order operations and
⭐    reorder them. ⭐⭐ RARE, AND A SIGN THE DESIGN IS WRONG.
```

# Part 2 — Orchestration vs choreography, decided

```
⭐ ***CHOREOGRAPHY*** — ⭐ services react to each other's events.
⭐   ⭐ No coordinator. ⭐ Each service knows only its own step.
⭐   ⭐ ✅ Loose coupling, ⭐ easy to add a participant, ⭐ no single
⭐     point of failure.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ❌ AND THE KILLER: ***NOBODY CAN ANSWER "WHERE IS ORDER
⭐     12345?"*** ⭐ The flow exists only as an emergent property
⭐     of seven services' event handlers. ⭐⭐ TO UNDERSTAND IT YOU
⭐     READ SEVEN CODEBASES, AND TO DEBUG IT YOU CORRELATE SEVEN
⭐     LOG STREAMS.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ❌ AND CYCLES ARE INVISIBLE UNTIL PRODUCTION.

⭐ ***ORCHESTRATION*** — ⭐ one coordinator owns the sequence and
⭐   calls each step.
⭐   ⭐ ✅ ⭐⭐ THE FLOW IS ***IN ONE FILE***, READABLE, TESTABLE,
⭐     AND ITS STATE IS QUERYABLE. ⭐ "Where is order 12345?" is
⭐     a `SELECT`.
⭐   ⭐ ✅ Timeouts, retries and compensation live in one place.
⭐   ⭐ ❌ The coordinator is a new component to make reliable,
⭐     ⭐⭐ AND IT CAN BECOME A GOD OBJECT IF IT STARTS MAKING
⭐     BUSINESS DECISIONS INSTEAD OF SEQUENCING THEM.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND DAY 333's RULE IS STILL THE ANSWER, NOW WITH THE
⭐   MECHANISM BEHIND IT:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ***ORCHESTRATE THE TRANSACTION. CHOREOGRAPH THE SIDE
⭐   EFFECTS.***
⭐   ⭐ The five steps that must all happen or all be compensated
⭐     — payment, inventory, shipment — ⭐⭐ ARE ORCHESTRATED,
⭐     BECAUSE SOMEONE MUST OWN "DID ALL OF THIS COMPLETE?"
⭐   ⭐ The email, the analytics row, the recommendation refresh
⭐     — ⭐⭐ ARE CHOREOGRAPHED, BECAUSE NOBODY NEEDS TO KNOW AND
⭐     THE PRODUCER IS CORRECT WITHOUT THEM.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ THE TEST IS DAY 393's: DELETE EVERY SUBSCRIBER. IF THE
⭐     BUSINESS OPERATION IS STILL CORRECT, CHOREOGRAPH IT.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The pivot transaction

> The most useful saga concept, and the one most material never mentions.

```
⭐⭐ ***EVERY SAGA HAS A POINT AFTER WHICH IT CANNOT BE
⭐   COMPENSATED. THAT STEP IS THE PIVOT.***

⭐ ⭐⭐ BEFORE THE PIVOT: every step is ***compensatable***.
⭐   ⭐ You can still abort the whole thing and leave the world as
⭐     you found it. ⭐ A reservation can be released; ⭐ a draft
⭐     can be deleted.
⭐ ⭐⭐ AT THE PIVOT: the step that changes the world irreversibly
⭐   or is externally visible. ⭐ Usually ***taking the money***.
⭐   ⭐ Sometimes dispatching a physical shipment, ⭐ sending an
⭐     SMS, ⭐ filing a regulatory report.
⭐ ⭐⭐ AFTER THE PIVOT: every step must be ***retriable*** —
⭐   ⭐ because you can no longer go back, ⭐⭐ SO THE ONLY DIRECTION
⭐   LEFT IS FORWARD, AND THE SAGA MUST KEEP RETRYING UNTIL IT
⭐   SUCCEEDS OR A HUMAN INTERVENES.

⭐⭐ ⇒ TWO DESIGN RULES FALL OUT, AND THEY ARE WORTH MORE THAN
⭐   ANY DIAGRAM:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ① ***PUT THE PIVOT AS LATE AS POSSIBLE.*** ⭐ Every step
⭐      you move before it is a step you can still undo cleanly.
⭐      ⭐ Reserve inventory before charging, not after. ⭐ Validate
⭐      the address before charging. ⭐⭐ CHARGE LAST.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ② ***EVERYTHING AFTER THE PIVOT MUST BE IDEMPOTENT AND
⭐      MUST NOT BE ABLE TO PERMANENTLY FAIL.*** ⭐ If a
⭐      post-pivot step ***can*** fail permanently, you have a
⭐      business problem with no technical solution — ⭐⭐ AND YOU
⭐      MUST BUILD A HUMAN PATH FOR IT, ON PURPOSE, BEFORE
⭐      LAUNCH.

⭐ ⭐ Naming the pivot in a design review is a senior move.
⭐   ⭐⭐ "WHERE IS THE PIVOT IN THIS FLOW?" IS A QUESTION THAT
⭐   REORGANISES A WHOLE DISCUSSION, ⭐ because half the room has
⭐   not realised there is one.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Compensation is a new fact, not an undo

```
⭐⭐ ***A REFUND IS NOT AN UN-CHARGE.***
⭐   ⭐ The customer saw the charge on their banking app.
⭐   ⭐ They received an email.
⭐   ⭐ It appears on their statement, ⭐⭐ AND SO DOES THE REFUND,
⭐     AS A ***SECOND LINE***.
⭐   ⭐ Their card issuer counted it. ⭐ Your accounting counted
⭐     it. ⭐⭐ IT HAPPENED, AND NOTHING CAN MAKE IT NOT HAVE
⭐     HAPPENED.

⭐⭐ ⇒ THREE CONSEQUENCES, ALL OPERATIONAL:
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***COMPENSATIONS CAN THEMSELVES FAIL***, ⭐ and they fail
⭐    into a state that is worse than either endpoint: ⭐ money
⭐    taken, order not fulfilled, refund failing. ⭐⭐ SO EVERY
⭐    COMPENSATION NEEDS ITS OWN RETRY, ITS OWN ALARM AND ITS OWN
⭐    HUMAN PATH. ⭐ A compensation with no monitoring is the
⭐    worst code in the system.
⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***SOME THINGS CANNOT BE COMPENSATED AT ALL.*** ⭐ An
⭐    email sent. ⭐ An SMS. ⭐ A parcel on a van. ⭐ A message
⭐    posted to a customer's timeline. ⭐⭐ ⇒ SO THOSE STEPS GO
⭐    ***AFTER*** THE PIVOT, OR BEHIND A DELIBERATE DELAY — ⭐ the
⭐    "we'll send the confirmation email in 60 seconds" that
⭐    looks like laziness and is actually a design decision.
⭐ ③ ⭐ ***COMPENSATION MUST BE IDEMPOTENT*** — ⭐⭐ THE
⭐    COMPENSATOR WILL BE RETRIED, AND REFUNDING TWICE IS WORSE
⭐    THAN THE ORIGINAL BUG.

⭐⭐ AND THE PRODUCT CONSEQUENCE PEOPLE FORGET: ***A COMPENSATED
⭐   SAGA NEEDS A USER-FACING STORY.*** ⭐ "Your order could not
⭐   be completed and you have been refunded" is a message
⭐   someone must write, ⭐⭐ AND IF NOBODY WRITES IT, THE CUSTOMER
⭐   EXPERIENCES YOUR SAGA AS A SILENT CHARGE AND A SILENT REFUND
⭐   AND CALLS SUPPORT.
```

# Part 5 — The orchestrator in Python

```python
# ⭐⭐ THE SAGA STATE IS A ROW. It lives in the SAME DATABASE as
# ⭐   the first step, and every transition is written with the
# ⭐   outbox (Day 407). ⭐⭐ NO STATE IN MEMORY. EVER.
@dataclass
class SagaState:
    id: str
    step: str                    # ⭐ current step
    status: str                  # RUNNING | COMPENSATING | DONE | FAILED
    context: dict                # ⭐ ids returned by each step
    attempts: int
    updated_at: datetime         # ⭐⭐ THE STUCK-SAGA DETECTOR

STEPS = [
    #  name          action              compensation      pivot?
    ("reserve_stock", reserve_stock,     release_stock,    False),
    ("validate_addr", validate_address,  None,             False),
    ("charge_card",   charge_card,       refund_card,      True),   # ⭐⭐ PIVOT
    ("create_ship",   create_shipment,   None,             False),
    ("notify",        send_confirmation, None,             False),
]

def advance(saga: SagaState) -> None:
    i = index_of(saga.step)
    name, action, compensate, is_pivot = STEPS[i]
    try:
        # ⭐⭐ IDEMPOTENCY KEY DERIVED FROM SAGA + STEP, SO A RETRY
        # ⭐   OF THIS EXACT STEP IS FREE (Day 335).
        result = action(saga.context, key=f"{saga.id}:{name}")
        saga.context |= result
        saga.step = STEPS[i + 1][0] if i + 1 < len(STEPS) else "done"
    except PermanentError:
        if past_pivot(i):
            # ⭐⭐ CANNOT GO BACK. ONLY FORWARD OR TO A HUMAN.
            saga.status = "NEEDS_INTERVENTION"
            page_oncall(saga)
        else:
            saga.status = "COMPENSATING"
    except TransientError:
        saga.attempts += 1        # ⭐ backoff, retry later
    persist(saga)                 # ⭐⭐ SAME TXN AS THE OUTBOX ROW
```

```
⭐⭐ THE FIVE THINGS THAT MAKE IT PRODUCTION-GRADE:
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ EVERY STEP IS IDEMPOTENT, KEYED BY `saga_id:step`.
⭐    ⭐ The orchestrator ***will*** retry; ⭐⭐ THE STEP MUST NOT
⭐    CARE.
⭐ ② ⭐ Every step has a ***timeout***, and a timeout is not a
⭐    failure — ⭐⭐ IT IS DAY 398's UNKNOWN, SO THE ORCHESTRATOR
⭐    MUST ***QUERY*** THE STEP'S STATUS BEFORE DECIDING. ⭐ This
⭐    is the part people omit and it is where double-charges come
⭐    from.
⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***AN ALARM ON `updated_at` OLDER THAN N MINUTES WITH
⭐    STATUS RUNNING.*** ⭐ The stuck-saga detector. ⭐⭐ WITHOUT IT,
⭐    SAGAS SILENTLY WEDGE AND YOU FIND OUT FROM A CUSTOMER.
⭐ ④ ⭐ State transitions written with the outbox, ⭐⭐ SO THE SAGA
⭐    CANNOT ADVANCE WITHOUT ITS EVENT BEING PUBLISHED.
⭐ ⑤ ⭐⭐ A DASHBOARD: IN-FLIGHT SAGAS BY STEP AND BY AGE.
⭐    ⭐ A histogram of ages is the single most useful operational
⭐    view you can build for this, ⭐⭐ AND THE LONG TAIL IS ALWAYS
⭐    A BUG.
```

# Part 6 — Temporal, and when to stop hand-rolling

```
⭐⭐ AT SOME POINT THE ORCHESTRATOR YOU ARE WRITING IS A WORSE
⭐   VERSION OF A PRODUCT THAT EXISTS.

⭐ ***TEMPORAL*** (or Step Functions, or Cadence) gives you
⭐   ⭐⭐ ***DURABLE EXECUTION***: ⭐ your saga is written as an
⭐   ordinary Python function with `await` calls, ⭐⭐ AND THE
⭐   FRAMEWORK PERSISTS EVERY STEP'S RESULT SO THE FUNCTION CAN
⭐   BE ***REPLAYED*** AFTER A CRASH AND CONTINUE FROM WHERE IT
⭐   WAS.
```

```python
@workflow.defn
class PlaceOrder:
    @workflow.run
    async def run(self, order: Order) -> str:
        await workflow.execute_activity(
            reserve_stock, order, start_to_close_timeout=timedelta(seconds=30))
        try:
            await workflow.execute_activity(
                charge_card, order, start_to_close_timeout=timedelta(seconds=60))
        except ActivityError:
            await workflow.execute_activity(release_stock, order)  # ⭐ compensate
            raise
        await workflow.execute_activity(create_shipment, order)
        return "placed"
        # ⭐⭐ THE PROCESS CAN DIE ANYWHERE IN THIS FUNCTION —
        # ⭐   INCLUDING BETWEEN TWO AWAITS, FOR THREE DAYS —
        # ⭐   AND IT RESUMES EXACTLY HERE.
```

```
⭐⭐ WHAT YOU GET: ⭐ retries, timeouts, the state machine, the
⭐   history, and the dashboard — ⭐⭐ AS A `try/except` YOU CAN
⭐   READ.
⭐⭐ WHAT IT COSTS, HONESTLY: ⭐ a server cluster (or Temporal
⭐   Cloud), ⭐ a new mental model, ⭐⭐ AND A DETERMINISM
⭐   CONSTRAINT THAT SURPRISES PYTHON DEVELOPERS — ***WORKFLOW
⭐   CODE MUST BE DETERMINISTIC, SO NO `datetime.now()`, NO
⭐   `random`, NO I/O, NO `uuid4()` IN THE WORKFLOW BODY*** ⭐ (all
⭐   of that goes in activities). ⭐⭐ WHICH IS EXACTLY DAY 405's
⭐   PURE-`apply` RULE, FOR THE SAME REASON: REPLAY MUST PRODUCE
⭐   THE SAME ANSWER.

⭐⭐ ⇒ THE THRESHOLD: ***HAND-ROLL ONE OR TWO SAGAS. AT FOUR OR
⭐   FIVE, OR AT THE FIRST MULTI-DAY WORKFLOW WITH HUMAN
⭐   APPROVAL STEPS, TAKE THE FRAMEWORK.*** ⭐ The state machine,
⭐   the timers and the visibility are the whole product,
⭐   ⭐⭐ AND YOU WILL BUILD ALL THREE BADLY.
```

# Part 7 — The operational reality

```
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***YOU WILL NEED A HUMAN.*** ⭐ Some sagas will end in
⭐    `NEEDS_INTERVENTION`, ⭐⭐ SO BUILD THE TOOL: ⭐ show the state,
⭐    the context, every step's outcome, ⭐ and offer ***retry
⭐    step***, ***skip step*** and ***force compensate*** as
⭐    explicit, audited actions. ⭐⭐ IF YOU DO NOT BUILD IT,
⭐    SOMEONE WILL DO IT WITH `UPDATE` STATEMENTS IN A PSQL
⭐    SESSION AT MIDNIGHT.
⭐ ② ⭐ ***CORRELATION ID ON EVERYTHING*** — ⭐ the saga id flows
⭐    into every downstream call, log line and event
⭐    (Days 376, 405).
⭐ ③ ⭐ ***TEST THE COMPENSATIONS.*** ⭐⭐ THEY ARE THE LEAST-RUN
⭐    CODE IN YOUR SYSTEM AND THE MOST EXPENSIVE WHEN WRONG.
⭐    ⭐ A test per compensation, and a chaos test that fails step
⭐    N for every N (Day 413).
⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***WATCH THE COMPENSATION RATE AS A BUSINESS METRIC.***
⭐    ⭐ A rising rate is not an engineering problem — ⭐⭐ IT MEANS
⭐    A DOWNSTREAM PARTNER IS DEGRADING, OR YOUR INVENTORY DATA
⭐    IS WRONG, ⭐ and it is often the earliest signal either has
⭐    happened.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. ⭐⭐ "What's a saga and what does it cost?"**
> ⭐ A sequence of local transactions, each with a compensating action, used when a business operation
> spans systems that can't share a transaction. ⭐⭐ **You get ACD and lose the I — and losing isolation
> means intermediate states are visible, so you inherit dirty reads, lost updates and fuzzy reads: the
> exact anomalies a database spends its life preventing.** ⭐ The countermeasure you actually use is a
> semantic lock — a `PENDING` status — ⭐⭐ **and its cost is that the intermediate state leaks into
> every query, every screen and every report in the system, because every reader now has to understand
> it.**

**2. ⭐⭐ "Orchestration or choreography?"**
> ⭐ Orchestrate the transaction, choreograph the side effects. ⭐⭐ **The steps that must all happen or
> all be compensated get a coordinator, because somebody has to own the question "did all of this
> complete?" — and with choreography nobody can answer "where is order 12345?", since the flow only
> exists as an emergent property of seven services' event handlers.** ⭐ To understand it you read seven
> codebases; to debug it you correlate seven log streams. ⭐ The email, the analytics row and the
> recommendation refresh get choreographed, ⭐⭐ **and the test is the same one as for events: delete
> every subscriber and ask whether the business operation is still correct.**

**3. ⭐⭐ "What's the pivot transaction?"**
> ⭐ The point after which the saga can no longer be compensated. ⭐⭐ **Before it, every step is
> reversible and you can abort cleanly. At it — usually taking the money, sometimes dispatching a
> physical shipment — the world changes irreversibly. After it, the only direction is forward, so every
> remaining step must be retriable and must not be able to permanently fail.** ⭐ Two rules follow:
> ⭐⭐ **put the pivot as late as possible, so reserve inventory and validate the address before you
> charge; and if a post-pivot step *can* fail permanently, that's a business problem with no technical
> solution and you must build a human path for it before launch.** ⭐ Asking "where's the pivot in this
> flow?" in a design review usually reorganises the whole discussion, because half the room hasn't
> realised there is one.

**4. ⭐⭐ "Isn't compensation just rollback?"**
> ⭐ No, and the difference is the whole difficulty. ⭐⭐ **A refund isn't an un-charge: the customer saw
> the charge on their banking app, got an email, and it's on their statement — and so is the refund, as
> a second line. It happened, and nothing can make it not have happened.** ⭐ Three consequences:
> ⭐⭐ **compensations can themselves fail, into a state worse than either endpoint — money taken, order
> not fulfilled, refund failing — so each one needs its own retry, alarm and human path. Some things
> can't be compensated at all: an email, an SMS, a parcel on a van — so those go after the pivot or
> behind a deliberate delay. And compensation must be idempotent, because refunding twice is worse
> than the original bug.** ⭐ Plus the product half: someone has to write the message the customer sees,
> or they experience a silent charge and a silent refund and call support.

**5. "How do you keep saga state?"**
> ⭐⭐ **In a row, in the same database as the first step, with every transition written through the
> outbox — never in memory.** ⭐ Each step gets an idempotency key derived from saga id plus step name,
> so a retry is free. ⭐ Each step gets a timeout, ⭐⭐ **and a timeout is not a failure — it's the unknown
> outcome, so the orchestrator has to query the step's status before deciding, and skipping that is
> where double-charges come from.** ⭐ And the thing people omit: ⭐⭐ **an alarm on `updated_at` older
> than N minutes with status RUNNING — the stuck-saga detector. Without it, sagas wedge silently and
> you find out from a customer.**

**6. "When would you use Temporal?"**
> ⭐ When the orchestrator I'm writing has become a worse version of a product that exists. ⭐⭐ **It
> gives you durable execution: the saga is an ordinary Python function with awaits, and the framework
> persists every step's result so the function can be replayed after a crash and continue exactly where
> it was — the process can die between two awaits for three days and resume.** ⭐ You get retries,
> timers, the state machine, history and a dashboard as a `try/except` you can read. ⭐ The costs are a
> server to run, a new mental model, ⭐⭐ **and a determinism constraint that surprises Python
> developers: no `datetime.now()`, no `random`, no I/O in the workflow body — which is exactly the
> pure-`apply` rule from event sourcing, for exactly the same reason.** ⭐ My threshold is one or two
> sagas hand-rolled; at four or five, or the first multi-day workflow with human approval, take the
> framework.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Define it and price it in one breath: **a saga is a sequence of local transactions
with compensating actions, used when you can't have a transaction — you get ACD and lose the I, and
losing isolation means intermediate states are visible, so you inherit the exact anomalies a database
exists to prevent.** The countermeasure is a semantic lock, **and its real cost is that a `PENDING`
status leaks into every query, screen and report in the system.** Then the decision: **orchestrate the
transaction, choreograph the side effects — because with choreography nobody can answer "where is
order 12345?", since the flow only exists as an emergent property of seven services' handlers.** Then
the concept most people don't have: **every saga has a pivot — the point after which it can't be
compensated, usually taking the money. Before it everything is reversible; after it the only direction
is forward. So put the pivot as late as possible, and if a post-pivot step can permanently fail,
that's a business problem with no technical solution and you build a human path before launch.** Then
the thing that makes compensation hard: **a refund is not an un-charge. The customer saw the charge,
got an email, and it's on their statement — and so is the refund, as a second line. So compensations
can fail into a state worse than either endpoint, some things can't be compensated at all, and someone
has to write the message the customer sees.** Close on the operational half: **state in a row written
through the outbox, an idempotency key per saga-step, a timeout treated as unknown rather than failure
— that's where double-charges come from — an alarm on stuck sagas, and an intervention tool, because
if you don't build it someone will do it with UPDATE statements in psql at midnight.**
