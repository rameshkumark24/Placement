# Day 471 🎯

**P-471** · 🎯 **The 45-minute deep dive** — ⭐⭐ run it, score it, find the two rows you lose

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** MOCK + SCORED

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: the deep dive is not a conversation about your project — it is a structured
> search for the edge of what you understand, and the interviewer will keep going deeper until they find
> it, so the only question that matters is whether that edge is deep enough.**

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ The four phases, and where points go

```
⭐ ***PHASE 1 — THE OVERVIEW (5 min).***
⭐   ⭐ Your three-minute version (Day 467), then their first
⭐     probe. ⭐⭐ THEY ARE DECIDING WHERE TO DIG.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ LOSE POINTS BY: ⭐ taking eight minutes; leading with
⭐     the stack; no constraint; no number.

⭐ ***PHASE 2 — THE DECISION INTERROGATION (15–20 min).***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE LARGEST BLOCK AND THE ONE THAT DECIDES THE ROUND.
⭐   ⭐ "Why X and not Y?" ×4–6, with follow-ups (Day 468).
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ LOSE POINTS BY: ⭐ preference answers; dismissing the
⭐     alternative; ⭐⭐ NEVER NAMING A COST.

⭐ ***PHASE 3 — THE FAILURE INTERROGATION (10–15 min).***
⭐   ⭐ What breaks, at what number, how you would know
⭐     (Day 469).
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ LOSE POINTS BY: ⭐ "we'd add caching and scale
⭐     horizontally"; ⭐⭐ HAVING NO ANSWER TO "HOW WOULD YOU
⭐     KNOW?"

⭐ ***PHASE 4 — THE FORWARD LOOK (5 min).***
⭐   ⭐ Another month · what you would do differently · your
⭐     questions for them.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ LOSE POINTS BY: ⭐ a wish list with no ranking; ⭐⭐ "NO
⭐     QUESTIONS, YOU'VE COVERED EVERYTHING."

⭐ ⭐⭐ THE STRUCTURAL INSIGHT: ⭐ ***PHASE 2 IS HALF THE ROUND***,
⭐   and it is entirely predictable — ⭐⭐ IT IS YOUR DECISION LOG
⭐   READ BACK TO YOU AS QUESTIONS. ⭐ Candidates who prepare the
⭐   *overview* and not the *log* prepare the smallest phase.
```

# Part 4 — The four scripted moments

```
⭐ ***REHEARSE THESE UNTIL THEY ARE AUTOMATIC. EACH IS WORTH A
⭐   RUBRIC ROW, AND EACH FEELS UNNATURAL THE FIRST TIME.***

⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***THE PRICE, VOLUNTEERED*** (row 7).
⭐   ⭐ "…and what we pay for that is the HNSW index sharing RAM
⭐     with the OLTP working set, ⭐⭐ SO A BIG REINDEX SHOWS UP
⭐     AS ***THE APP GOT SLOW***, NOT AS A SEARCH PROBLEM."

⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***THE DELETION*** (row 8).
⭐   ⭐ "We tried HyDE. It measured flat, and I understand why —
⭐     it amplifies the model's priors, ⭐⭐ SO IT IS
⭐     SYSTEMATICALLY WORST WHERE OUR POLICY DIFFERS FROM THE
⭐     INDUSTRY NORM. ⭐ We deleted it."

⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***THE SILENT FAILURE*** (row 10).
⭐   ⭐ "The failure I worry about most returns 200. A
⭐     cross-tenant leak has no exception, no error rate and no
⭐     latency change — ⭐⭐ GENERATION LAUNDERS IT INTO FLUENT
⭐     PROSE — ⭐ so there is nothing to alert on. ⭐⭐ THE
⭐     DETECTOR HAD TO BE A TEST THAT ***CONSTRUCTS*** THE
⭐     FAILURE."

⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***THE GOOD "I DON'T KNOW"*** (row 13).
⭐   ⭐ "I don't know. ⭐ What I do know is the adjacent thing…
⭐     and specifically how I'd check the rest."
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ IF YOU GET THROUGH 45 MINUTES WITHOUT SAYING IT ONCE,
⭐     EITHER THE INTERVIEWER DID NOT DIG HARD ENOUGH OR YOU
⭐     BLUFFED. ⭐ Neither is good.

⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE ONE THAT IS NOT SCRIPTABLE: ⭐ ***the moment you
⭐   realise mid-sentence that you are wrong.*** ⭐ Say so,
⭐   correct it, continue. ⭐⭐ DO NOT APOLOGISE. SELF-CORRECTION
⭐   IS A POSITIVE SIGNAL (DAY 466).
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. "Tell me about your project." (Phase 1, timed)**

⭐ Three minutes, five beats, then stop. ⭐⭐ Problem in user terms — support agents spending twenty
minutes finding answers that already existed. ⭐ Constraint — twenty million tokens so no window holds
it, and per-team permissions which turned out to be the hard part. ⭐ One decision I want probed — the
permission filter inside the query rather than after the search, with what post-filtering actually costs.
⭐ The measurement — recall 0.62 to 0.91, two-thirds of it free, which changed how I sequenced everything
after. ⭐⭐ And what it can't do, unprompted. ⭐ Then I stop, because the next question is them telling me
where they want to dig, and that's more useful than another two minutes of me talking.

**2. "Why reciprocal rank fusion rather than weighting the scores?" (Phase 2)**

⭐ The force is that the two score distributions aren't comparable and can't be made comparable.
⭐⭐ Cosine similarity sits in a narrow band, roughly 0.3 to 0.9 on real corpora, while BM25 is unbounded
and its distribution shifts **per query** — so there's no fixed normalisation that works. ⭐ The
alternative I'd steelman is per-query min-max normalisation, which is what most people do and is
genuinely reasonable at first glance. ⭐⭐ It introduces a worse bug: a document's score starts depending
on what *else* was retrieved, so adding an irrelevant result can reorder your top hit. ⭐ The price of
RRF is that I throw away score magnitude entirely, so I can't use it as a relevance threshold — which is
exactly why the rerank floor exists as a separate mechanism.

**3. "Are you sure post-filtering is a security problem and not just a performance one?" (the planted challenge)**

⭐ Let me re-check the reasoning rather than just repeat it. ⭐⭐ Yes — and the distinction is specific.
Post-filtering means the other tenant's vectors were searched, scored, and returned into my process
memory, with one line of application code standing between them and the prompt. ⭐ That's a security
control living somewhere any refactor can move it, and every retry path or debugging shortcut is one typo
from disclosure. ⭐⭐ Pre-filtering means those rows were never candidates at all. ⭐ Same output on the
happy path, completely different blast radius when something goes wrong — and in this domain you design
for the failure, because the failure is invisible.

**4. "What breaks first at ten times the scale?" (Phase 3)**

⭐ Tokens per minute, and it's arithmetic rather than intuition: 6,500 tokens a request against a 200k
TPM tier is thirty requests a minute. ⭐⭐ I'd know from queue wait time, which moves before latency
does. ⭐ The fix is separate keys per workload class and moving batch work to the batch API — separate
pool, half the price. ⭐⭐ And the constraint I can't fix in the moment is that quota increases take days
to weeks, so it's a planning item. ⭐ At ten times the *tenants* something different breaks: filter
selectivity, so my smallest customers degrade first and silently.

**5. "Where does your understanding of this run out?"**

⭐ Two places I can name. ⭐⭐ The internals of HNSW graph construction — I understand the parameters and
their effect on recall and latency well enough to tune them and measure the result, but I couldn't
implement it or reason precisely about why a particular `m` produces a particular graph topology.
⭐ And the training side generally: I can fine-tune with LoRA and evaluate it honestly, and I know when
it's the wrong tool, but pretraining and architecture work are a different job. ⭐⭐ I'd rather mark
those boundaries than have them found, because knowing where my knowledge ends is what makes the
confident parts trustworthy.

**6. "Anything you'd like to add?"**

⭐ One thing. ⭐⭐ Earlier I said the eval set was thirty questions, and I want to add the caveat rather
than leave it sounding stronger than it is: thirty cases is small, the confidence interval is wide, and
I treat a two-point difference as noise rather than a result. ⭐ That's why the CI gate has a tolerance
band above measured run-to-run variance instead of a fixed threshold. ⭐⭐ If sampling noise blocks
builds, the team learns to ignore the gate, and then it protects nothing.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

⭐⭐ **Forty-five minutes: run the real thing. Then two minutes on this.**

After the mock, before you look at the recording, say the six-clause sentence out loud about your own
system and notice which clauses come easily and which you have to reach for:

**"I can tell you what I built"** — everyone has this. **"why each decision was necessary"** — most
people can approximate this. **"what it cost me"** — this is where it starts thinning out, and it's
rubric row 7. **"what breaks first"** — needs a named component and a number, not a technique. **"how
I'd know"** — needs a leading indicator, and for the interesting failures, needs you to have *built* the
detector. **"and which of my own choices I'd reverse"** — almost nobody says this unprompted, and it's
the single strongest thing in the list, because adopting something proves you read about it and deleting
it proves you measured it.

Then say the four scripted moments once each, out loud, cold. **The price volunteered. The deletion with
its reason. The silent failure and the detector you had to build for it. And a good "I don't know" that
marks the boundary instead of hiding it.** If any of them feels unnatural, that's the one to rehearse
before the next run — because it will feel far more unnatural at minute thirty of a real interview than
it does now.
