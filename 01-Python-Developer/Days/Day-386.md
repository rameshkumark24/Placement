# Part 1 — What it actually is

```
⭐⭐ ***A FLAT MAP FROM KEY (A STRING) TO BYTES, BEHIND AN HTTP
⭐   API.*** ⭐ That is the entire data model.

⭐ WHAT FOLLOWS, AND WHY EACH ONE SURPRISES SOMEONE:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ① ***THERE ARE NO DIRECTORIES.*** ⭐ `a/b/c.jpg` is a
⭐      key containing slashes. ⭐ The console draws folders;
⭐      ⭐⭐ THEY DO NOT EXIST. ⭐ "Delete the folder" is "list a
⭐      prefix and delete every key", ⭐ which is why it is slow
⭐      and why it costs one request per 1,000 objects.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ② ***THERE IS NO RENAME.*** ⭐ Rename = COPY + DELETE.
⭐      ⭐⭐ FOR A 5 GB OBJECT THAT IS A 5 GB SERVER-SIDE COPY,
⭐      NOT A METADATA EDIT.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ③ ***THERE IS NO APPEND AND NO PARTIAL WRITE.*** ⭐ You
⭐      replace the whole object. ⭐⭐ SO "APPEND A LINE TO THE LOG
⭐      IN S3" IS READ-ALL, CONCAT, WRITE-ALL — ⭐ which is fine
⭐      at 10 KB and catastrophic at 500 MB.
⭐   ⭐ ④ Reads *can* be partial: ⭐⭐ RANGE GETs ARE FIRST-CLASS,
⭐      ⭐ which is what makes Parquet and video streaming work.
⭐   ⭐ ⑤ Listing is lexicographic by key, ⭐ paginated at 1,000.
⭐      ⭐⭐ THERE IS NO "SORT BY DATE" AND NO "COUNT". ⭐ If you
⭐      need those, ⭐⭐ THE INDEX BELONGS IN A DATABASE (DAY 337).

⭐⭐ AND THE CONSISTENCY FACT WITH ITS DATE, BECAUSE HALF THE
⭐   INTERNET IS STILL WRONG ABOUT IT:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ***SINCE DECEMBER 2020, S3 IS STRONGLY READ-AFTER-WRITE
⭐   CONSISTENT FOR ALL OPERATIONS, INCLUDING OVERWRITES AND
⭐   LISTS.*** ⭐ No extra cost, no flag.
⭐   ⭐ Older books, older blog posts and older interviewers will
⭐     tell you to design around eventual consistency.
⭐   ⭐⭐ KNOWING THE DATE IS THE ANSWER — ⭐ it shows you learned
⭐     it rather than absorbed it. ⭐ (List-after-delete is still
⭐     strongly consistent too; ⭐ what remains eventual is
⭐     cross-region replication, which is a different feature.)
```

# Part 2 — Performance, and the prefix story

```
⭐ THE PUBLISHED LIMITS, ***PER PREFIX***:
⭐   ⭐ 3,500 PUT/COPY/POST/DELETE per second
⭐   ⭐ 5,500 GET/HEAD per second
⭐   ⭐⭐ AND THERE IS NO LIMIT ON THE NUMBER OF PREFIXES — ⭐ so
⭐     the ceiling is effectively "however much you parallelise".

⭐⭐ THE OUTDATED ADVICE: "PUT A RANDOM HASH AT THE FRONT OF YOUR
⭐   KEY." ⭐ That was necessary before 2018. ⭐⭐ S3 NOW SPLITS
⭐   PARTITIONS AUTOMATICALLY AS LOAD RISES, ⭐ so random prefixes
⭐   cost you readability for nothing.
⭐   ⭐⭐ BUT THE UNDERLYING PHYSICS DID NOT VANISH: ***SPLITTING
⭐   TAKES MINUTES TO HOURS.*** ⭐ So a workload that goes from
⭐   zero to 20,000 writes/second into `logs/2026-08-22/…` ⭐⭐ WILL
⭐   GET `503 SlowDown` UNTIL S3 CATCHES UP. ⭐ Retry with
⭐   backoff and it resolves itself — ⭐⭐ AND IF YOU KNOW A BURST
⭐   IS COMING, SPREAD ACROSS PREFIXES FOR THAT WORKLOAD ONLY.
⭐   ⭐ Date-first keys are fine for steady traffic and are much
⭐     better for lifecycle rules and Athena partitions.

⭐ MULTIPART UPLOAD: ⭐ required above 5 GB, ⭐ worth it above
⭐   ~100 MB. ⭐ Parallel parts, and a failed part retries alone.
⭐   ⭐⭐ AND THE TRAP IT CREATES IS COST TRAP #4 BELOW.

⭐ ⭐ For very high throughput on one object: S3 Transfer
⭐   Acceleration (edge ingress) or ⭐ byte-range parallel GETs.
⭐   ⭐⭐ `s3transfer` (WHAT `upload_file`/`download_file` USE)
⭐   DOES THE RANGE PARALLELISM FOR YOU — ⭐ which is why
⭐   `download_file` beats a hand-written `get_object().read()`
⭐   by several times on large objects.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ Presigned uploads, done properly

```
⭐ WHY THEY EXIST (Day 327): ⭐⭐ A 200 MB UPLOAD SHOULD NOT
⭐   OCCUPY A gunicorn WORKER FOR NINETY SECONDS. ⭐ The browser
⭐   sends bytes straight to S3; ⭐ your app only signs.

⭐⭐ THE FOUR THINGS YOU MUST PIN — ⭐ and an unpinned one is a
⭐   real vulnerability, not a style issue:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ① THE ***KEY***, SERVER-GENERATED. ⭐ Never take a
⭐      client-supplied path, ⭐⭐ OR A USER UPLOADS TO
⭐      `config/prod.json`.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ② A ***CONTENT-LENGTH RANGE***, ⭐ or someone uploads
⭐      500 GB and you pay for it.
⭐   ⭐ ③ THE CONTENT-TYPE.
⭐   ⭐ ④ A SHORT EXPIRY — minutes, not days.
```

```python
# ⭐⭐ POST, NOT PUT, FOR BROWSER UPLOADS — because only POST
# ⭐ supports Conditions, which is where the pinning lives.
post = s3.generate_presigned_post(
    Bucket="uploads-prod",
    Key=f"u/{user.id}/{uuid4()}.jpg",        # ⭐⭐ WE CHOOSE IT
    Fields={"Content-Type": "image/jpeg"},
    Conditions=[
        {"Content-Type": "image/jpeg"},
        ["content-length-range", 1, 5_242_880],   # ⭐⭐ 1B–5MB
    ],
    ExpiresIn=300,                            # ⭐ 5 minutes
)
# ⭐ Client POSTs post["fields"] + the file to post["url"].
```

```
⭐⭐ AND THE THREE THINGS PEOPLE GET WRONG AFTER THAT:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ① ***A PRESIGNED URL IS A BEARER CREDENTIAL.*** ⭐ Anyone
⭐      holding it has the access. ⭐⭐ IT LANDS IN BROWSER
⭐      HISTORY, IN `Referer` HEADERS, IN YOUR ACCESS LOGS AND IN
⭐      YOUR APM TRACES — ⭐ so keep expiry short ⭐⭐ AND SCRUB
⭐      QUERY STRINGS IN LOGGING (DAY 375).
⭐   ⭐⭐ ② ***CONTENT-TYPE IS A CLAIM, NOT A FACT.*** ⭐ Pinning
⭐      `image/jpeg` does not make it a JPEG. ⭐⭐ VALIDATE THE
⭐      MAGIC BYTES SERVER-SIDE AFTER UPLOAD, ⭐ and never serve
⭐      user content from a domain that holds your session
⭐      cookie.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ③ ***YOU DO NOT KNOW THE UPLOAD HAPPENED.*** ⭐ The
⭐      browser talks to S3, not to you. ⭐⭐ SO CONFIRM WITH AN
⭐      S3 EVENT NOTIFICATION → SQS/LAMBDA, ⭐ not by trusting a
⭐      client callback — ⭐⭐ WHICH IS RESERVE-THEN-CONFIRM
⭐      (DAY 359) WITH S3 AS THE CONFIRMING PARTY.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ The four cost traps, with arithmetic

```
⭐⭐ ① ***EGRESS.*** ⭐ Storage is $0.023/GB-month.
⭐    ⭐⭐ EGRESS TO THE INTERNET IS $0.09/GB.
⭐    ⭐ 10 TB stored ................. $230/month
⭐    ⭐ 10 TB served once ............ ⭐⭐ $900
⭐    ⭐⭐ ⇒ FOR ANYTHING PUBLICLY SERVED, ***EGRESS EXCEEDS
⭐      STORAGE, USUALLY BY A LOT.*** ⭐ CloudFront in front is
⭐      cheaper per GB ***and*** caches, ⭐⭐ AND ORIGIN-TO-EDGE
⭐      TRANSFER IS FREE — ⭐ so it is close to a pure win
⭐      (Day 392).

⭐⭐ ② ***REQUESTS.*** ⭐ $0.005 per 1,000 PUT. $0.0004 per 1,000
⭐    GET. ⭐ Sounds like nothing.
⭐    ⭐⭐ A PYTHON LOOP WRITING 50 MILLION 2 KB EVENTS: $250 IN
⭐      REQUESTS TO STORE 100 GB THAT COSTS $2.30 TO KEEP.
⭐    ⭐⭐ ⇒ ***SMALL OBJECTS ARE A REQUEST PROBLEM, NOT A STORAGE
⭐      PROBLEM.*** ⭐ Batch them. ⭐ One Parquet file per hour
⭐      beats 3,600 JSON files, ⭐⭐ AND IT IS ALSO 100× FASTER TO
⭐      QUERY LATER.

⭐⭐ ③ ***STORAGE CLASSES — THE MINIMUMS.*** ⭐ Standard-IA is
⭐    ~45% cheaper per GB. ⭐ But it carries: ⭐ a 30-day minimum
⭐    duration charge, ⭐ a per-GB retrieval fee, ⭐⭐ AND A 128 KB
⭐    MINIMUM BILLABLE OBJECT SIZE.
⭐    ⭐⭐ ⇒ SO LIFECYCLING A BUCKET OF 5 KB THUMBNAILS TO IA
⭐      CHARGES YOU FOR 128 KB EACH — ***A 25× INCREASE ON A
⭐      MOVE YOU MADE TO SAVE MONEY.*** ⭐ Glacier's minimums are
⭐      90 and 180 days.
⭐    ⭐ Intelligent-Tiering exists exactly because this is hard:
⭐      ⭐⭐ A SMALL MONITORING FEE PER OBJECT AND IT MOVES THINGS
⭐      FOR YOU — ⭐ correct default for unpredictable access,
⭐      ⭐ wrong for millions of tiny objects (that fee is
⭐      per-object too).

⭐⭐ ④ ***VERSIONING WITH NO LIFECYCLE RULE.*** ⭐ With versioning
⭐    on, ⭐⭐ `DELETE` DOES NOT DELETE — IT WRITES A MARKER, AND
⭐    EVERY OLD VERSION KEEPS BILLING FOREVER. ⭐ A bucket
⭐    "holding 200 GB" bills 4 TB. ⭐ Same for abandoned
⭐    multipart parts, ⭐⭐ WHICH ARE INVISIBLE IN THE CONSOLE
⭐    OBJECT LIST ENTIRELY.
⭐    ⭐⭐ ⇒ ***TWO LIFECYCLE RULES ON EVERY BUCKET, DAY ONE:***
⭐      ⭐ expire noncurrent versions after N days
⭐      ⭐⭐ ABORT INCOMPLETE MULTIPART UPLOADS AFTER 7 DAYS
⭐      ⭐ (This is thirty seconds of Terraform and it is the
⭐        commonest silent line on an S3 bill.)
```

# Part 5 — Security, in the order things go wrong

```
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***BLOCK PUBLIC ACCESS AT THE ACCOUNT LEVEL.*** ⭐ Then
⭐    no bucket can be made public by accident, ever, by anyone.
⭐    ⭐ If you need public content, ⭐⭐ SERVE IT VIA CLOUDFRONT
⭐    WITH AN ORIGIN ACCESS CONTROL — ⭐ the bucket stays private
⭐    and only the distribution can read it.
⭐ ② ⭐ ACLs are ***disabled by default*** on new buckets
⭐    (bucket-owner-enforced). ⭐⭐ LEAVE THEM DISABLED. ⭐ Bucket
⭐    policies are the mechanism.
⭐ ③ ⭐ ENCRYPTION: SSE-S3 is on by default now. ⭐ SSE-KMS gives
⭐    you a key policy, ⭐ an audit trail of decryptions, ⭐⭐ AND
⭐    THE ABILITY TO REVOKE ACCESS TO DATA BY REVOKING A KEY —
⭐    ⭐ which is a genuinely different control (Day 395).
⭐    ⭐⭐ ITS COSTS ARE REAL: A KMS CALL PER OBJECT OPERATION,
⭐    ⭐ ~$0.03/10k requests, ⭐⭐ AND A PER-KEY THROTTLE THAT A
⭐    PARALLEL PYTHON JOB WILL HIT. ⭐ Turn on ***S3 Bucket
⭐    Keys***: it cuts KMS calls by ~99%, ⭐ one setting.
⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ THE CROSS-ACCOUNT OWNERSHIP TRAP: ⭐ an object written
⭐    ***by another account*** used to be owned by the writer,
⭐    ⭐⭐ SO THE BUCKET OWNER COULD NOT READ THEIR OWN BUCKET.
⭐    ⭐ Bucket-owner-enforced ownership fixes it — ⭐ another
⭐    reason to leave ACLs off.
⭐ ⑤ ⭐ For the data that must survive you: ⭐⭐ VERSIONING +
⭐    MFA DELETE + REPLICATION TO A ***SEPARATE ACCOUNT*** —
⭐    ⭐ or Object Lock in compliance mode, ⭐⭐ WHICH EVEN THE
⭐    ROOT USER CANNOT OVERRIDE. ⭐ That is your ransomware
⭐    control (Day 380).
```

# Part 6 — Python that does not embarrass you

```python
# ⭐⭐ ① STREAM. DO NOT .read() A 2 GB OBJECT INTO A LIST.
obj = s3.get_object(Bucket=b, Key=k)
for chunk in obj["Body"].iter_chunks(1024 * 1024):
    sink.write(chunk)                    # ⭐ constant memory

# ⭐⭐ ② PAGINATE. list_objects_v2 CAPS AT 1,000 AND PEOPLE
# ⭐    SILENTLY PROCESS THE FIRST PAGE ONLY — a data bug, not
# ⭐    an error.
for page in s3.get_paginator("list_objects_v2").paginate(
        Bucket=b, Prefix="u/"):
    for o in page.get("Contents", []):
        ...

# ⭐⭐ ③ PARALLELISE. S3 IS I/O-BOUND, SO THREADS WORK DESPITE
# ⭐    THE GIL (DAY 065) — the socket wait releases it.
#      10,000 sequential head_object at 20 ms = 200 s
#      ⭐⭐ 32 THREADS = ~7 s
from concurrent.futures import ThreadPoolExecutor
cfg = Config(max_pool_connections=64,          # ⭐⭐ MUST MATCH
             retries={"max_attempts": 5, "mode": "adaptive"})
s3 = boto3.client("s3", config=cfg)
with ThreadPoolExecutor(32) as ex:
    list(ex.map(head, keys))
# ⭐⭐ THE DEFAULT POOL IS 10. THIRTY-TWO THREADS ON A TEN-
# ⭐   CONNECTION POOL SERIALISE, AND YOU GET NO SPEEDUP AND NO
# ⭐   ERROR — the single most common "threads didn't help".

# ⭐ ④ For big files, let s3transfer do it:
s3.upload_file(path, b, k)   # ⭐⭐ MULTIPART + PARALLEL, FREE

# ⭐ ⑤ `mode="adaptive"` retries back off on 503 SlowDown —
# ⭐   ⭐⭐ WHICH IS THE PREFIX-SPLIT BEHAVIOUR FROM PART 2.
```

# Part 7 — S3 as a data-lake surface (one paragraph, for later)

```
⭐ Athena queries S3 directly with SQL. ⭐ You pay ***per byte
⭐   scanned*** ($5/TB), ⭐⭐ SO LAYOUT IS THE ENTIRE COST MODEL:
⭐   ⭐ ***PARTITION*** by the column you filter on
⭐     (`dt=2026-08-22/`) ⭐⭐ SO A ONE-DAY QUERY SCANS ONE DAY;

⭐   ⭐ ***BIG FILES***: 128 MB–1 GB. ⭐⭐ THOUSANDS OF TINY FILES

⭐     cost trap #2 arriving a second time.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. ⭐⭐ "Is S3 a filesystem?"**
> ⭐ No, and the differences are the interesting part. ⭐⭐ **It's a flat key→bytes map over HTTP:
> there are no directories — slashes are just characters and the console draws folders that don't
> exist; there's no rename, so renaming a 5 GB object is a 5 GB copy plus a delete; and there's no
> append or partial write, so "add a line to the file" is read-all, concat, write-all.** ⭐ Reads can
> be partial — range GETs are first-class, which is what makes Parquet and video streaming work.
> ⭐ And listing is lexicographic and paginated, with no sort-by-date and no count, ⭐⭐ **so if you need
> to query your objects, the index belongs in a database.**

**2. "What's S3's consistency model?"**
> ⭐⭐ **Strong read-after-write for all operations including overwrites and listing, since December
> 2020, at no extra cost.** ⭐ It's worth knowing the date, because a lot of material and a lot of
> interviewers still describe designing around eventual consistency, and that advice is obsolete.
> ⭐ Cross-region replication is still asynchronous — that's a different feature.

**3. ⭐⭐ "Design an upload flow for 200 MB files."**
> ⭐ Presigned POST direct to S3, ⭐⭐ **because a 200 MB upload should never occupy a gunicorn worker
> for ninety seconds** — the app only signs. ⭐ Four things get pinned: ⭐⭐ **the key, server-generated,
> because a client-supplied path lets someone upload to `config/prod.json`**; a content-length range;
> the content type; and a short expiry. ⭐ POST rather than PUT because only POST supports Conditions.
> ⭐ Then three things people miss: ⭐⭐ **the URL is a bearer credential and lands in browser history,
> Referer headers and your logs, so expiry is short and query strings get scrubbed in logging;
> content-type is a claim not a fact, so validate magic bytes server-side; and you don't know the
> upload happened, because the browser talked to S3 — so confirm with an S3 event notification rather
> than a client callback.** ⭐ That last part is reserve-then-confirm with S3 as the confirming party.

**4. ⭐⭐ "Our S3 bill is much larger than our stored data. Where do you look?"**
> ⭐ Four places, in order. ⭐⭐ **Egress: $0.09 a gigabyte out versus $0.023 to store, so serving 10 TB
> costs $900 against $230 of storage — CloudFront is cheaper per GB, caches, and origin-to-edge is
> free.** ⭐⭐ **Requests: 50 million small PUTs is $250 to store 100 GB that costs $2.30 — small
> objects are a request problem, not a storage problem, so batch into Parquet.** ⭐⭐ **Storage-class
> minimums: IA has a 128 KB minimum billable size, so lifecycling 5 KB thumbnails to IA multiplies
> that bill by 25 on a change you made to save money.** ⭐⭐ **And versioning with no lifecycle rule —
> delete writes a marker, old versions bill forever, and abandoned multipart parts don't even appear
> in the object list.** ⭐ Every bucket gets two lifecycle rules on day one: expire noncurrent
> versions, and abort incomplete multiparts after seven days.

**5. "Do you still need random key prefixes?"**
> ⭐ No — S3 splits partitions automatically now, so random prefixes cost readability for nothing, and
> date-first keys are better for lifecycle rules and Athena partitions. ⭐⭐ **But the physics didn't
> vanish: splitting takes minutes to hours, so a workload that jumps to 20,000 writes a second into
> one prefix gets 503 SlowDown until S3 catches up.** ⭐ Adaptive retries handle it, ⭐ and if I know a
> burst is coming I'd spread that specific workload across prefixes.

**6. ⭐⭐ "You parallelised S3 calls with 32 threads and got no speedup. Why?"**
> ⭐ Almost certainly the botocore connection pool, which defaults to ten — ⭐⭐ **so 32 threads
> serialise on 10 connections and you get neither a speedup nor an error.** ⭐ Set
> `max_pool_connections` to match the pool size. ⭐ And to be clear about why threads are right here at
> all: ⭐⭐ **S3 is I/O-bound and the socket wait releases the GIL**, so threads genuinely parallelise —
> 10,000 sequential `head_object` calls at 20 ms is 200 seconds, and 32 threads makes it about seven.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Open flat: **S3 is not a filesystem — it's a flat key→bytes map over HTTP, and every
expensive mistake comes from treating it as one.** No directories, so deleting a folder is listing a
prefix; no rename, so renaming 5 GB is a 5 GB copy; no append, so adding a log line is read-all,
write-all. **Reads can be partial though — range GETs are why Parquet and video streaming work.** Give
the consistency fact with its date: **strongly consistent for everything since December 2020, so the
design-around-eventual-consistency advice is obsolete, and knowing the date shows you learned it
rather than absorbed it.** Then uploads: **presigned POST direct to S3, because a 200 MB upload
shouldn't hold a gunicorn worker for ninety seconds — with the key server-generated, a content-length
range, the type, and a short expiry.** And the three follow-ons: **the URL is a bearer credential that
lands in browser history and your logs; content-type is a claim, so check magic bytes; and you don't
know the upload happened, so confirm with an S3 event, not a client callback.** Then the money, with
numbers: **egress at $0.09 versus storage at $0.023 means serving 10 TB costs four times storing it;
50 million small PUTs is $250 to store $2.30 of data; IA's 128 KB minimum billable size means
lifecycling thumbnails to IA multiplies the bill by 25; and versioning without a lifecycle rule bills
every deleted version forever.** Close on the Python detail: **threads do parallelise S3 because the
socket wait releases the GIL — but the botocore pool defaults to ten, so 32 threads serialise with no
speedup and no error.**
