# Day 462 📄

**P-462** · 📄 **The resume** — ⭐⭐ every line is a question you invited

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: a resume is not a record of what you did — it is a list of questions you
> are inviting, and a line you cannot defend for five minutes is not an asset on the page, it is a
> landmine you placed yourself.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ Who actually reads it, and how

```
⭐ ***THREE READERS, IN ORDER, AND THEY WANT DIFFERENT THINGS:***

⭐ ① ***THE ATS*** — ⭐ a parser. ⭐⭐ IT DOES NOT UNDERSTAND
⭐   SYNONYMS. ⭐ "Python 3" does not match "Python3". "FastAPI"
⭐   does not match "Fast API". ⭐⭐ MATCHING IS LITERAL, SO THE
⭐   JOB DESCRIPTION'S OWN SPELLING IS THE SPELLING YOU USE.

⭐ ② ***THE RECRUITER*** — ⭐⭐ SIX TO TEN SECONDS. ⭐ They scan
⭐   for: current title, current company, years, ⭐ and three or
⭐   four keywords from the requisition. ⭐⭐ THEY ARE NOT READING
⭐   YOUR BULLETS. ⭐ They are deciding whether to read them.

⭐ ③ ***THE ENGINEER*** — ⭐ reads it ***five minutes before the
⭐   interview***, ⭐⭐ LOOKING FOR SOMETHING TO ASK YOU ABOUT.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE READER THAT MATTERS, AND THE ONE PEOPLE
⭐     WRITE FOR LEAST. ⭐ They will pick the most interesting or
⭐     most suspicious line on the page ⭐⭐ AND SPEND TWENTY
⭐     MINUTES ON IT.

⭐ ⭐⭐ ⇒ SO THE DESIGN GOAL IS NOT "LOOK IMPRESSIVE". ⭐ It is:
⭐   ***make the line they will pick be the line you most want
⭐   to talk about.*** ⭐⭐ YOU ARE ROUTING THE INTERVIEW.
```

# Part 2 — The bullet, and the formula that survives probing

```
⭐ ***THE SHAPE:***
⭐   ***[verb] [what you built] [with what] [→ measured outcome]***

⭐ ❌ "Worked on backend services using Python and FastAPI."
⭐   ⭐⭐ NO SCOPE, NO OUTCOME, NO QUESTION WORTH ASKING. ⭐ The
⭐     engineer skips it.

⭐ ✅ "Built the document-retrieval service behind our support
⭐   assistant (FastAPI, Postgres/pgvector) — ⭐⭐ RAISED
⭐   RECALL@5 FROM 0.62 TO 0.91 AND CUT COST PER QUESTION TO
⭐   1.2p."
⭐   ⭐⭐ NOW THE ENGINEER HAS FOUR QUESTIONS AND YOU HAVE
⭐     ANSWERS FOR ALL OF THEM (Day 463).

⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE HONEST CAVEAT NOBODY SAYS OUT LOUD: ⭐ ***NOT
⭐   EVERY BULLET HAS A NUMBER, AND INVENTING ONE IS FATAL.***
⭐   ⭐ Where you have no metric, use ***scope*** instead:
⭐     ⭐ "12 endpoints, 40k rows/day, 4-person team, 8 months"
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ SCOPE IS A FACT YOU CAN DEFEND. A FABRICATED
⭐     PERCENTAGE IS A TRAP YOU BUILT FOR YOURSELF.
```

```
⭐ ⭐⭐ THE VERB IS A LEVELLING SIGNAL, AND INTERVIEWERS READ IT
⭐   THAT WAY (DAY 487):
⭐   ⭐ ***"contributed to"*** → you were nearby
⭐   ⭐ ***"built"*** → you wrote it
⭐   ⭐ ***"owned"*** → you made the decisions and carried the
⭐     pager
⭐   ⭐ ***"led"*** → ⭐⭐ YOU DIRECTED OTHER PEOPLE, ⭐ and you
⭐     will be asked who and how many
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ USE THE STRONGEST VERB THAT IS ***TRUE***. ⭐ Using one
⭐     level up is the single most common way candidates lose a
⭐     round they would otherwise have passed — ⭐⭐ NOT BECAUSE
⭐     THE CLAIM IS BIG, BUT BECAUSE THE FOLLOW-UP EXPOSES IT.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ Positioning: the thing this track actually bought you

```
⭐ ***THE MARKET PROBLEM WITH "PYTHON DEVELOPER":*** ⭐ it is the
⭐   most crowded label there is, ⭐⭐ AND IT SAYS NOTHING ABOUT
⭐   WHAT YOU CAN BUILD.

⭐ ***WHAT 488 DAYS ACTUALLY MADE YOU*** — ⭐ name it precisely:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ A BACKEND ENGINEER WHO SHIPS AND OPERATES SERVICES
⭐     (Stages 3–12: FastAPI, Postgres, containers, AWS,
⭐     distributed systems) —
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ***WHO CAN ALSO BUILD AND MEASURE AN LLM SYSTEM***
⭐     (Stages 13–14).
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THAT COMBINATION IS GENUINELY SCARCE RIGHT NOW. ⭐ The
⭐     market has plenty of people who have called an LLM API and
⭐     very few who can tell you their recall, their abstention
⭐     rate and their cost per request.

⭐ ***SO THE HEADLINE IS THE ***MEASUREMENT***, NOT THE STACK:***
⭐   ⭐ ❌ "Experienced with LangChain, OpenAI API, vector
⭐     databases and prompt engineering."
⭐     ⭐⭐ THIS READS AS "I FOLLOWED A TUTORIAL", ⭐ because
⭐       everyone writes it.
⭐   ⭐ ✅ "Built a retrieval system over 8,000 internal
⭐     documents — ⭐⭐ RECALL@5 0.91, MEASURED HALLUCINATION
⭐     RATE 0.4%, 1.2p PER QUESTION, ⭐ with per-tenant isolation
⭐     gated in CI."
⭐     ⭐⭐ ALMOST NOBODY CAN WRITE THAT SENTENCE, WHICH IS
⭐       EXACTLY WHY IT WORKS.
```

# Part 4 — Structure, and what to delete

```
⭐ ***THE ORDER:***
⭐   ⭐ contact (⭐⭐ NOT IN A HEADER — ATS PARSERS DROP HEADERS)
⭐   ⭐ a two-line summary ***only if it says something specific***
⭐   ⭐ experience (reverse chronological)
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ PROJECTS — ***ABOVE*** EDUCATION ONCE YOU HAVE ANY
⭐     PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE, AND ABOVE EXPERIENCE IF YOU HAVE
⭐     NONE
⭐   ⭐ skills
⭐   ⭐ education

⭐ ***DELETE, WITHOUT REGRET:***
⭐   ⭐ "Objective: to leverage my skills in a challenging role"
⭐   ⭐ "References available on request"
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ EVERY SOFT-SKILL ADJECTIVE — "passionate",
⭐     "detail-oriented", "team player". ⭐ They are unfalsifiable,
⭐     so they carry zero information ⭐⭐ AND THE READER KNOWS IT.
⭐   ⭐ GPA, after ~2 years of experience
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE 40-ITEM SKILLS LIST. ⭐ Listing every library you
⭐     have ever imported reads as inexperience, not breadth —
⭐     ⭐⭐ AND IT INVITES A QUESTION ABOUT THE ONE YOU KNOW
⭐     LEAST.
⭐   ⭐ skill bars and percentage ratings (⭐⭐ "PYTHON: 85%" —
⭐     85% OF WHAT?)

⭐ ***THE SKILLS SECTION, DONE PROPERLY:*** ⭐ group by kind, and
⭐   ⭐⭐ ONLY LIST WHAT YOU WOULD BE HAPPY TO BE INTERVIEWED ON
⭐   FOR THIRTY MINUTES.
⭐   ⭐ Languages · Backend · Data · Infra · ⭐⭐ AI/LLM
⭐   ⭐ ⭐ If it is on the page, it is fair game. ⭐⭐ THAT IS THE
⭐     ONLY RULE THAT MATTERS HERE.
```

# Part 5 — ATS mechanics, cheaply

```
⭐ ⭐⭐ THESE ARE NOT SUPERSTITIONS — THEY ARE PARSER FAILURES
⭐   PEOPLE HIT REPEATEDLY:
⭐   ⭐ ***single column.*** ⭐ Two-column layouts interleave into
⭐     nonsense when linearised.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ NO TABLES, TEXT BOXES, GRAPHICS OR ICONS ⭐ around
⭐     content that must be read.
⭐   ⭐ ***standard section headers*** — "Experience", "Skills",
⭐     "Education". ⭐⭐ NOT "WHERE I'VE BEEN".
⭐   ⭐ ***contact details in the body***, not the header/footer.
⭐   ⭐ ***PDF***, unless they ask for .docx.
⭐     ⭐ Filename: `Firstname-Lastname-Python-Engineer.pdf`
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ DATES AS `MMM YYYY – MMM YYYY`, ⭐ consistently.
⭐   ⭐ ***the JD's own vocabulary***, verbatim, where it is true
⭐     of you.

⭐ ***TAILORING — 20 MINUTES, AND IT IS THE HIGHEST-RETURN 20
⭐   MINUTES IN THE PROCESS:***
⭐   ⭐ rewrite the summary for ***this*** role;
⭐   ⭐ reorder the top three bullets of your most recent role so
⭐     the ones matching the JD are first;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ REORDER THE SKILLS GROUPS SO THEIRS LEADS;
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ DO NOT REWRITE THE WHOLE DOCUMENT — ⭐ you will
⭐     introduce a line you have not rehearsed, and that is the
⭐     line they will ask about.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. "Walk me through your resume."**

⭐ I'd treat it as a routing question rather than a recitation, because reading the page back is a wasted
three minutes — they have it in front of them. ⭐⭐ So: thirty seconds of shape, then straight to the
thing I most want probed. ⭐ "Backend engineer, four years, mostly Python and Postgres. The last year I
built the retrieval system behind our support assistant, which is the piece I'd most like to talk about
because it's where I learned the most — particularly about measuring something you can't unit-test."
⭐⭐ That hands them the line I've rehearsed hardest, and it's an honest offer rather than a deflection.

**2. "Why is there no number on this bullet?"**

⭐ Because I didn't measure it and I'm not going to invent one. ⭐⭐ Where I have a number I use it; where
I don't, I use scope, which is a fact I can defend — twelve endpoints, forty thousand rows a day, four
people, eight months. ⭐ A fabricated percentage is a trap I'd be setting for myself, because the
follow-up is always "from what to what, measured how, over what period", and my interviewer has built
the same thing.

**3. "You've listed twenty technologies. Tell me about Kafka."**

⭐ That question is exactly why I don't list twenty. ⭐⭐ My rule is that anything on the page is
something I'd be happy to be interviewed on for thirty minutes — so the skills section is short and
grouped, and things I've touched once aren't on it. ⭐ Listing everything reads as inexperience rather
than breadth, and it hands the interviewer a free shot at whatever I know least.

**4. "What makes your resume different from the other Python candidates?"**

⭐ Most of them can say they've used an LLM API. ⭐⭐ Very few can tell you their recall, their
abstention rate on unanswerable questions, or their cost per request. ⭐ So my headline isn't the
stack, it's the measurement: recall@5 at 0.91 with the ladder that got it there, a measured
hallucination rate, per-tenant isolation gated in CI, and a cost per question. ⭐⭐ The scarce
combination right now isn't "knows Python" or "has used an LLM" — it's a backend engineer who can build
an LLM system *and* operate it, and that's what I'd want the page to say in the first six seconds.

**5. "How do you tailor for a specific role?"**

⭐ Twenty minutes, and only the top third. ⭐⭐ I rewrite the summary for that role, reorder the top three
bullets of my most recent job so the ones matching the JD come first, and reorder the skills groups so
theirs leads. ⭐ I use their vocabulary verbatim where it's true of me, because ATS matching is literal —
"FastAPI" doesn't match "Fast API". ⭐⭐ What I don't do is rewrite the whole document, because that
introduces a line I haven't rehearsed, and an unrehearsed line is exactly the one an engineer picks.

## Today's build

```
⭐ ① ***PRINT YOUR CURRENT RESUME AND CIRCLE EVERY CLAIM.***
⭐   ⭐⭐ FOR EACH, WRITE THE FIRST QUESTION IT INVITES. ⭐ Any
⭐   circle whose question you cannot answer in 60 seconds is
⭐   today's work.

⭐ ② ***REWRITE EVERY BULLET*** into the Part 2 shape. ⭐⭐ VERB
⭐   FIRST, AND THE STRONGEST VERB THAT IS ***TRUE***.

⭐ ③ ***REPLACE MISSING METRICS WITH SCOPE.*** ⭐⭐ NEVER WITH AN
⭐   INVENTED PERCENTAGE.

⭐ ④ ***WRITE THE AI ENGINEER BLOCK*** using your own Stage 13/14
⭐   numbers — ⭐ recall, abstention, cost, isolation.

⭐ ⑤ ***CUT THE SKILLS SECTION IN HALF.*** ⭐ Keep only what you

⭐   standard headers, PDF, named properly. ⭐⭐ COPY-PASTE THE
```

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

⭐⭐ **Two minutes, out loud: "walk me through your resume."**

Don't read it back. **Thirty seconds of shape, then hand them the line you want. "Backend engineer,
four years, mostly Python, FastAPI and Postgres, with the last year on our support assistant. The piece
I'd most like to talk about is the retrieval system behind it, because that's where I learned the
most — particularly about measuring something you can't unit-test."**

Then give them the hook with a number in it. **"The interesting part was that we started at recall@5 of
0.62 and got to 0.91, and two-thirds of that gain was free — structure-aware chunking and putting the
heading path into the text before embedding. I'd expected the reranker to be the win and it was the
smallest piece."**

Then say what you own, precisely. **"I owned the retrieval service end to end — chunking, the hybrid
search, the eval harness and the per-tenant isolation. The generation side and the frontend were other
people. On a four-person team over eight months."** That sentence does two jobs at once: it's honest
attribution, and it's a levelling signal.

Close by inviting the probe you're ready for. **"The thing I'd be happy to be pushed on is the security
side. A retrieval permission leak is silent — it returns a fluent, well-cited answer built from another
customer's data with no error anywhere — and getting that gated in CI took longer than the retrieval
quality work did."**
