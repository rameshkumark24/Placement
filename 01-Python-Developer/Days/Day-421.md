# Day 421 💸

**P-421** · 💸 ⭐⭐ **Cost, latency and the token budget** — the unit economics of a feature

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: an LLM feature has a cost per request that a *user* controls by typing more
> — which makes it the first thing you have ever shipped where a customer can raise your marginal cost
> without limit, so the unit economics must be computed before the feature is built rather than
> discovered on the invoice.**

---

# Part 1 — Why this is different from every other cost

```
⭐ ⭐⭐ ORDINARY INFRASTRUCTURE IS ***PROVISIONED*** (Day 396):
⭐   ⭐ you pay for a server whether it is busy or not, ⭐ and one
⭐   more user costs approximately nothing until you add a
⭐   server. ⭐⭐ MARGINAL COST ≈ 0, STEPPED.

⭐ ⭐⭐ AN LLM FEATURE IS ***CONSUMED***:
⭐   ⭐ Every request has a real, variable, per-request cost.
⭐   ⭐⭐ MARGINAL COST > 0, ***CONTINUOUS***, AND ***CONTROLLED BY
⭐     THE USER***: ⭐ a longer message, a longer document, a
⭐     longer conversation, a "make it more detailed" follow-up.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ SO A SINGLE USER CAN COST 500× ANOTHER ON THE SAME
⭐     PLAN, ⭐ and nothing in your pricing page noticed.

⭐⭐ ⇒ THREE CONSEQUENCES THAT ARE NEW TO YOU:
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***A FLAT-RATE SUBSCRIPTION OVER A CONSUMED RESOURCE IS
⭐    AN UNCAPPED LIABILITY*** UNLESS YOU METER IT. ⭐ Every
⭐    company doing this has eventually added limits; ⭐⭐ THE ONES
⭐    THAT ADDED THEM LATE DID IT IN AN EMERGENCY AND ANNOYED
⭐    THEIR BEST USERS.
⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***COST IS NOW A ***PRODUCT*** DECISION.*** ⭐ "Unlimited
⭐    chat history" (Day 414's quadratic), "summarise any
⭐    document", "regenerate as many times as you like" — ⭐ each
⭐    is a line on an invoice, ⭐⭐ AND ENGINEERING IS THE ONLY
⭐    PARTY IN THE ROOM WHO KNOWS THAT.
⭐ ③ ⭐ ***ABUSE IS ECONOMIC, NOT JUST OPERATIONAL.*** ⭐ Day 335's
⭐    rate limiting was about protecting capacity; ⭐⭐ HERE IT
⭐    PROTECTS ***MONEY***, AND THE ATTACK IS SIMPLY "USE THE
⭐    PRODUCT A LOT".
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ Computing the number, properly

```
⭐ ***COST PER REQUEST*** =
⭐   (prompt_tokens × input_price
⭐    + completion_tokens × output_price) / 1,000,000

⭐ WORK ONE REAL EXAMPLE ALL THE WAY THROUGH — ⭐ a RAG support
⭐   assistant on a mid-tier model at $2.50/M in, $10/M out:

⭐   ⭐ system prompt ................ 400 tok
⭐   ⭐ 5 retrieved chunks × 600 .... 3,000 tok
⭐   ⭐ conversation history ........ 1,200 tok
⭐   ⭐ user question ................. 80 tok
⭐   ⭐⭐ INPUT TOTAL ................ 4,680 tok → $0.0117
⭐   ⭐ answer ....................... 350 tok → $0.0035
⭐   ⭐⭐ ═══════════════════════════════════════════
⭐   ⭐⭐ ***PER REQUEST ≈ $0.0152***

⭐ ⭐ Now scale it, and this is the step people skip:
⭐   ⭐ 10 questions/user/day × 5,000 daily active users
⭐     = 50,000 requests/day
⭐   ⭐⭐ = $760/DAY = ***$22,800/MONTH***
⭐   ⭐ At a $10/month subscription with 5,000 users you bill
⭐     $50,000 — ⭐⭐ SO 46% OF REVENUE IS GOING TO ONE VENDOR
⭐     BEFORE INFRASTRUCTURE, SALARIES OR SUPPORT. ⭐ That is a
⭐     viable-but-tight business, ⭐⭐ AND IT IS A COMPLETELY
⭐     DIFFERENT CONVERSATION FROM "THE API IS CHEAP".

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND NOTICE WHERE THE MONEY IS: ***77% OF THE COST IS
⭐   RETRIEVED CONTEXT AND HISTORY, NOT THE ANSWER.*** ⭐ So the
⭐   optimisations that matter are ⭐⭐ FEWER/SMALLER CHUNKS,
⭐   TRUNCATED HISTORY AND PROMPT CACHING — ⭐ not shortening the
⭐   reply. ⭐⭐ MOST TEAMS OPTIMISE THE ANSWER BECAUSE IT IS THE
⭐   PART THEY CAN SEE.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The optimisation ladder, by return per hour

```
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***USE A SMALLER MODEL FOR THE TASK*** — ⭐ 10–30× cheaper.
⭐    ⭐⭐ THE SINGLE BIGGEST LEVER, AND IT IS FREE IF YOUR EVALS
⭐    SAY QUALITY HOLDS (DAY 425). ⭐ Most production tasks —
⭐    extraction, classification, routing, formatting — ⭐⭐ DO NOT
⭐    NEED A FRONTIER MODEL AND ARE ROUTINELY GIVEN ONE OUT OF
⭐    HABIT.
⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***PROMPT CACHING*** (Day 415) — ⭐ up to ~90% off the
⭐    stable prefix. ⭐ In the Part 2 example the system prompt
⭐    plus a stable chunk set is most of the input.
⭐    ⭐⭐ ORDER THE PROMPT STABLE-FIRST AND THIS IS ALMOST FREE
⭐    MONEY.
⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***SEND LESS CONTEXT.*** ⭐ 5 chunks instead of 10 is a
⭐    50% input cut — ⭐⭐ AND IF YOUR RERANKER IS GOOD (DAY 433),
⭐    QUALITY OFTEN ***IMPROVES***, BECAUSE CHUNKS 6–10 WERE
⭐    NOISE COMPETING FOR ATTENTION.
⭐ ④ ⭐ ***TRUNCATE OR SUMMARISE HISTORY*** (Day 443) — ⭐ kills
⭐    the quadratic.
⭐ ⑤ ⭐⭐ ***CACHE WHOLE RESPONSES*** (Day 454) — ⭐ in support and
⭐    documentation workloads, ⭐⭐ 20–40% OF QUESTIONS ARE THE
⭐    SAME QUESTION.
⭐ ⑥ ⭐ ***BATCH API*** for anything non-interactive — ~50% off.
⭐ ⑦ ⭐⭐ ***DON'T CALL THE MODEL AT ALL.*** ⭐ A regex, a lookup, a
⭐    nearest-centroid classifier (Day 419), a cached FAQ.
⭐    ⭐⭐ THE CHEAPEST TOKEN IS THE ONE YOU NEVER SEND, ⭐ and the
⭐    best AI features usually call the model less than people
⭐    assume.
⭐ ⑧ ⭐ ***FINE-TUNE A SMALL MODEL*** for one narrow high-volume
⭐    task (Days 417, 451) — ⭐ real, and the most effort.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND DAY 396's COUNTERWEIGHT STILL APPLIES: ***ENGINEER
⭐   TIME COSTS MORE THAN MOST INFERENCE.*** ⭐ A week saving
⭐   $50/month is a bad trade. ⭐⭐ AT $22,800/MONTH IT IS AN
⭐   EXCELLENT ONE. ⭐ Compute the number first, ⭐⭐ THEN DECIDE
⭐   WHETHER TO SPEND THE WEEK.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ The model cascade

```
⭐⭐ ***RUN THE CHEAP MODEL FIRST. ESCALATE ONLY WHEN YOU HAVE
⭐   EVIDENCE YOU NEED TO.***

⭐   ┌─ cheap model ──────────────────────────────┐
⭐   │ ⭐⭐ 80% OF TRAFFIC ENDS HERE               │
⭐   └────────────┬───────────────────────────────┘
⭐                │ escalate if:
⭐                │ ⭐⭐ • LOW MEAN LOGPROB (DAY 418)
⭐                │ ⭐ • schema validation failed
⭐                │ ⭐ • a self-reported "I'm not sure"
⭐                │ ⭐ • a classifier says the task is hard
⭐                │ ⭐ • the user clicked "regenerate"
⭐   ┌────────────▼───────────────────────────────┐
⭐   │ strong model — ⭐⭐ 20% OF TRAFFIC           │
⭐   └────────────────────────────────────────────┘

⭐ ⭐⭐ THE ARITHMETIC: ⭐ if the cheap model is 20× cheaper and
⭐   handles 80%, ⭐⭐ YOUR BLENDED COST IS 0.8/20 + 0.2 = ***24%
⭐   OF ALWAYS USING THE STRONG MODEL.*** ⭐ A 4× saving from a
⭐   routing rule.
⭐ ⭐ The costs, stated honestly:
⭐   ⭐ Escalated requests pay ***both*** models — ⭐⭐ SO IF YOUR
⭐     ESCALATION RATE IS 50% THE CASCADE IS WORSE THAN NOT
⭐     HAVING ONE.
⭐   ⭐ Latency on escalation is the sum.
⭐   ⭐⭐ AND YOU NOW HAVE TWO PROMPTS AND TWO EVAL SUITES
⭐     (DAY 425).
⭐ ⭐⭐ ⇒ MEASURE THE ESCALATION RATE AS A FIRST-CLASS METRIC.
⭐   ⭐ It is the number that decides whether the cascade earns
⭐   its complexity, ⭐⭐ AND IT DRIFTS AS YOUR TRAFFIC CHANGES.
```

# Part 5 — Latency, and what users actually feel

```
⭐ THE BUDGET, BROKEN DOWN (Day 416):
⭐   ⭐ retrieval ............... 50–200 ms
⭐   ⭐ reranking ............... 50–300 ms
⭐   ⭐⭐ TIME TO FIRST TOKEN .... 300 ms – 3 s ⭐ (grows with
⭐     prompt length, quadratically in attention)
⭐   ⭐⭐ GENERATION ............. ***OUTPUT TOKENS ÷ tok/s***
⭐     ⭐ 350 tokens at 60 tok/s = ⭐⭐ ~6 SECONDS

⭐⭐ ⇒ SO THE LEVERS, IN ORDER OF EFFECT:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ① ***STREAM*** (Day 418). ⭐ Perceived latency becomes
⭐      time-to-first-token, ⭐⭐ SO 6 SECONDS FEELS LIKE 0.5.
⭐      ⭐ Free, and it is the biggest single win.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ② ***ASK FOR LESS OUTPUT.*** ⭐ Halving the answer halves
⭐      the generation time. ⭐ Nothing else has that ratio.
⭐   ⭐ ③ A smaller model — proportionally faster per token.
⭐   ⭐ ④ Shorter prompt → faster first token.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⑤ PARALLELISE RETRIEVAL AND ANYTHING ELSE INDEPENDENT
⭐      (`asyncio.gather`, Day 420).
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⑥ AND THE STRUCTURAL ONE: ***IF IT TAKES 30 SECONDS, MAKE
⭐      IT ASYNCHRONOUS.*** ⭐ A job, a notification, a progress
⭐      indicator (Day 399). ⭐⭐ A 30-SECOND SYNCHRONOUS HTTP
⭐      REQUEST IS A DESIGN ERROR, NOT A SLOW FEATURE.

⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE ONE THAT IS NOT UNDER YOUR CONTROL: ***PROVIDER
⭐   p99 IS SEVERAL TIMES p50 AND VARIES BY HOUR.*** ⭐ You cannot
⭐   tune it. ⭐⭐ YOU CAN ONLY SET A TIMEOUT, HAVE A FALLBACK, AND
⭐   DEGRADE (DAYS 456, 458) — ⭐ which is why your SLO must be
⭐   written knowing that a dependency's tail is outside your
⭐   control.
```

# Part 6 — Metering, limits and the guard rails

```
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***METER PER USER AND PER TENANT, IN TOKENS***, ⭐ from
⭐    day one. ⭐ Not requests — ⭐⭐ TOKENS, BECAUSE THAT IS WHAT
⭐    YOU PAY.
⭐ ② ⭐ ***LIMITS AT THREE LEVELS***: ⭐ per request
⭐    (`max_tokens`, input size), ⭐ per user per day, ⭐ per
⭐    tenant per month.
⭐    ⭐⭐ THE PER-REQUEST ONE IS THE ONLY ONE THAT STOPS A SINGLE
⭐    PATHOLOGICAL INPUT, AND IT IS FREE.
⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***A SPEND ALARM ON YOUR PROVIDER ACCOUNT, TODAY***, ⭐ at
⭐    2× and 5× expected (Day 396). ⭐⭐ AND A HARD BUDGET CAP IF
⭐    THE PROVIDER OFFERS ONE — ⭐ a runaway retry loop can spend
⭐    a month's budget in an hour, ⭐⭐ AND THE ANOMALY DETECTION
⭐    THAT SAVES YOU IS THE ONE YOU TURNED ON BEFORE IT HAPPENED.
⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***PUBLISH COST PER 1,000 REQUESTS AND COST PER ACTIVE
⭐    USER*** AS METRICS NEXT TO YOUR LATENCY SLO (DAY 396).
⭐    ⭐ Total spend cannot distinguish growth from inefficiency;
⭐    ⭐⭐ UNIT COST CAN, AND IT IS THE ONLY NUMBER THAT SURVIVES
⭐    THE PRODUCT SUCCEEDING.
⭐ ⑤ ⭐ ***DEGRADE RATHER THAN FAIL ON BUDGET EXHAUSTION***: ⭐ fall
⭐    back to the cheap model, ⭐ then to a cached/templated
⭐    answer, ⭐ then to "this feature is temporarily
⭐    unavailable". ⭐⭐ THREE RUNGS, DECIDED IN ADVANCE (DAY 335).
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. ⭐⭐ "How is LLM cost different from normal infrastructure cost?"**
> ⭐⭐ **Normal infrastructure is provisioned — you pay for a server whether it's busy or not, so
> marginal cost is roughly zero and stepped. An LLM feature is consumed: every request has a real,
> variable cost, and the user controls it by typing more.** ⭐ So one user can cost 500× another on the
> same plan. ⭐ Three consequences: ⭐⭐ **a flat-rate subscription over a consumed resource is an uncapped
> liability unless you meter it; cost becomes a product decision, because "unlimited history" and
> "regenerate freely" are lines on an invoice and engineering is the only party in the room who knows
> that; and abuse becomes economic rather than operational — the attack is just using the product a
> lot.**

**2. ⭐⭐ "Walk me through costing a feature."**
> ⭐ I'd do it in tokens, per request, then scale. ⭐⭐ **A RAG assistant: 400-token system prompt, five
> 600-token chunks, 1,200 tokens of history, an 80-token question — that's 4,680 input plus a
> 350-token answer, about $0.015 a request on a mid-tier model. Ten questions a day across 5,000 users
> is 50,000 requests, which is $760 a day or $22,800 a month.** ⭐ At a $10 subscription that's 46% of
> revenue to one vendor before infrastructure or salaries. ⭐⭐ **And the important part is where the
> money is: 77% of it is retrieved context and history, not the answer — so the optimisations that
> matter are fewer chunks, truncated history and prompt caching, not shortening the reply. Most teams
> optimise the answer because it's the part they can see.**

**3. "How would you cut that bill?"**
> ⭐ In order of return: ⭐⭐ **a smaller model for the task, which is 10 to 30× cheaper and free if evals
> say quality holds — most production work is extraction, classification and formatting, which get
> given frontier models out of habit.** ⭐ Then prompt caching, up to 90% off the stable prefix. ⭐⭐ **Then
> sending less context — five chunks instead of ten is a 50% input cut, and with a good reranker
> quality often *improves*, because chunks six to ten were noise competing for attention.** ⭐ Then
> history truncation, response caching — ⭐⭐ **in support workloads 20 to 40% of questions are the same
> question** — the batch API, ⭐⭐ **and not calling the model at all: a regex, a lookup, a
> nearest-centroid classifier. The cheapest token is the one you never send.**

**4. ⭐⭐ "What's a model cascade and when is it wrong?"**
> ⭐ Run the cheap model first and escalate only on evidence — low mean logprob, a failed schema
> validation, a self-reported uncertainty, or the user clicking regenerate. ⭐⭐ **If the cheap model is
> 20× cheaper and handles 80%, your blended cost is 24% of always using the strong model — a 4× saving
> from a routing rule.** ⭐ It's wrong when the escalation rate is high: ⭐⭐ **escalated requests pay both
> models, so at a 50% escalation rate the cascade is worse than not having one.** ⭐ So escalation rate
> is a first-class metric, ⭐⭐ **and it drifts as traffic changes** — plus you now maintain two prompts
> and two eval suites.

**5. "How do you make it feel fast?"**
> ⭐⭐ **Stream — perceived latency becomes time-to-first-token, so six seconds feels like half a
> second. It's free and it's the biggest single win.** ⭐ Then ask for less output, ⭐⭐ **because halving
> the answer halves the generation time and nothing else has that ratio.** ⭐ Then a smaller model, a
> shorter prompt, and parallelising retrieval with anything independent. ⭐⭐ **And the structural one:
> if it takes 30 seconds, make it asynchronous — a 30-second synchronous HTTP request is a design
> error, not a slow feature.** ⭐ The part I can't tune is provider p99, which is several times p50 and
> varies by hour, ⭐⭐ **so my SLO has to be written knowing a dependency's tail is outside my control.**

**6. ⭐⭐ "What guard rails would you put in on day one?"**
> ⭐ A spend alarm at 2× and 5× expected, and a hard budget cap if the provider offers one — ⭐⭐ **a
> runaway retry loop can spend a month's budget in an hour, and the anomaly detection that saves you
> is the one you turned on beforehand.** ⭐ Metering per user and per tenant in **tokens**, not requests.
> ⭐ Limits at three levels, ⭐⭐ **where the per-request cap is the only one that stops a single
> pathological input and it's free.** ⭐ Unit cost published next to the latency SLO, ⭐⭐ **because total
> spend can't distinguish growth from inefficiency and unit cost can.** ⭐ And a three-rung degradation
> ladder decided in advance: cheap model, then cached answer, then unavailable.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Frame the difference first: **ordinary infrastructure is provisioned, so marginal
cost is roughly zero. An LLM feature is consumed — every request has a real variable cost and the user
controls it by typing more. That makes it the first thing you've shipped where a customer can raise
your marginal cost without limit, so a flat-rate subscription over it is an uncapped liability unless
you meter it.** Then do the arithmetic out loud, because that's what separates a real answer: **a RAG
assistant is about fifteen thousandths of a dollar per request; ten questions a day across five
thousand users is $22,800 a month, which against a $10 subscription is 46% of revenue to one vendor.**
Then the insight that redirects the work: **77% of that is retrieved context and history, not the
answer — so the optimisations that matter are fewer chunks, truncated history and prompt caching, and
most teams shorten the reply because it's the part they can see.** Give the ladder briefly, leading
with the biggest lever: **a smaller model is 10 to 30× cheaper and free if evals say quality holds —
and most production tasks get a frontier model out of habit.** Add the cascade with its arithmetic and
its failure: **20× cheaper at 80% coverage is a blended 24%, a 4× saving — but escalated requests pay
both models, so at a 50% escalation rate the cascade is worse than none, which is why escalation rate
is a first-class metric.** Close on latency and guard rails: **stream, because six seconds of
generation feels like half a second and it's free; ask for less output, because that's the only lever
with a linear ratio; and if it takes 30 seconds make it asynchronous, because a 30-second synchronous
request is a design error. Then a spend alarm and a hard cap on day one — a runaway retry loop can
spend a month's budget in an hour.**
