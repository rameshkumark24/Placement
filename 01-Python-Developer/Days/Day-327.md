# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ Why the CDN is the highest-leverage box on the diagram

```
⭐⭐ THREE THINGS IT DOES, AND ONLY THE FIRST IS OBVIOUS:
⭐   ① ⭐ PROXIMITY. 150 ms cross-continent (Day 323) becomes
⭐        10 ms to a nearby PoP. ⭐⭐ AND NOTHING ELSE CAN FIX
⭐        THAT NUMBER — it is the speed of light.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ OFFLOAD. 95% of requests never reach your origin.
⭐        ⭐ Your capacity problem shrinks by 20×, and it is the
⭐        cheapest 20× you will ever buy.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ ABSORPTION. It eats volumetric DDoS, TLS handshakes,
⭐        and slow clients (Day 325) before they cost you a
⭐        worker.

⭐⭐ AND THE CONNECTION BENEFIT PEOPLE MISS:
⭐   ⭐ TCP + TLS setup is 2–3 round trips. To a PoP that is
⭐     ~30 ms; to your origin, ~450 ms.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE CDN TERMINATES TLS NEARBY AND KEEPS A WARM,
⭐     LONG-LIVED CONNECTION TO YOUR ORIGIN — ⭐ so even a
⭐     ***cache miss*** is faster through the CDN than direct.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ WHICH IS WHY PUTTING DYNAMIC, UNCACHEABLE API
⭐     TRAFFIC BEHIND A CDN IS OFTEN STILL WORTH IT — and
⭐     saying that shows you understand what it is doing.
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The cache key at the edge, which is where breaches happen

```
⭐⭐ THE CACHE KEY IS, BY DEFAULT: host + path + query.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ NOTE WHAT IS ***NOT*** IN IT: THE COOKIE. THE
⭐     `Authorization` HEADER. THE USER.

⭐⭐ SO THE ONE-LINE BREACH (Day 267, at 100× the blast radius):
⭐   ⭐ `/api/me` returns your profile with `Cache-Control:
⭐     public, max-age=60`.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE FIRST USER'S PROFILE IS NOW SERVED TO EVERY
⭐     OTHER USER IN THAT REGION FOR SIXTY SECONDS.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ At the application cache this was bad. ⭐⭐ AT THE EDGE
⭐     IT IS A REGIONAL INCIDENT, AND YOU CANNOT UN-SERVE IT.

⭐⭐ THE FOUR RULES, AND THEY ARE NOT NEGOTIABLE:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ ANYTHING PERSONALISED: `Cache-Control: private,
⭐        no-store`. ⭐ `private` alone still allows the browser
⭐        to store it; `no-store` is the one that means nothing
⭐        writes it down.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ IF IT VARIES BY A HEADER, SAY SO: `Vary: Accept-
⭐        Encoding, Accept-Language`. ⭐ Missing `Vary` on
⭐        `Accept-Encoding` serves gzip bytes to a client that
⭐        asked for identity.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ NEVER `Vary: Cookie` AS A SAFETY BLANKET — every
⭐        distinct cookie value is a distinct cache entry, so
⭐        your hit ratio goes to zero and you have a very
⭐        expensive proxy.
⭐   ④ ⭐ DEFAULT DENY: configure the CDN to cache ***only***
⭐        what you explicitly mark, not everything it can.

⭐⭐ AND THE QUERY-STRING TRAP: `?utm_source=twitter` makes a
⭐   ***new*** cache entry. ⭐⭐ A MARKETING CAMPAIGN CAN DRIVE
⭐   YOUR HIT RATIO TO ZERO OVERNIGHT. ⭐ Strip or allow-list
⭐   query parameters in the cache key.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ Invalidation at the edge: don't

```
⭐⭐ PURGING A CDN IS SLOW (seconds to minutes), GLOBAL,
⭐   RATE-LIMITED, AND SOMETIMES BILLED.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SO THE ANSWER IS TO NEVER NEED IT:
⭐     ***CONTENT-ADDRESSED URLS.***

⭐⭐ THE PATTERN:
⭐   ⭐ `/static/app.a3f9c21.js` — the hash is in the name.
⭐   ⭐ `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable`
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ NEW BUILD ⇒ NEW URL ⇒ NOTHING TO INVALIDATE. ⭐ The
⭐     old file can sit in caches forever; nobody asks for it.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND THIS IS WHY THE HTML ITSELF MUST BE SHORT-TTL
⭐     OR `no-cache` — ⭐ it is the one document that names the
⭐     hashed URLs, so it is the only thing that must change.

⭐⭐ WHEN YOU GENUINELY CANNOT HASH THE URL:
⭐   ⭐ Short TTL + `stale-while-revalidate=60`. ⭐⭐ SERVE THE
⭐     STALE COPY INSTANTLY AND REFRESH IN THE BACKGROUND —
⭐     ⭐ this is Day 326's stampede fix, at the edge, given to
⭐     you as one header.
⭐   ⭐ `stale-if-error=86400` ⇒ ⭐⭐ IF YOUR ORIGIN IS DOWN, THE
⭐     CDN KEEPS SERVING. ⭐ A one-line availability upgrade
⭐     that most people never enable.
⭐   ⭐ Cache tags / surrogate keys, if your CDN supports them:
⭐     purge "everything tagged product:42" in one call.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Object storage: what it is and what it is not

```
⭐⭐ IT IS NOT A FILESYSTEM. ⭐ Say this early.
⭐   ⭐ ✘ No append. ⭐ ✘ No rename (it is copy + delete).
⭐   ⭐ ✘ No partial write. ⭐ ✘ No real directories — ⭐⭐ "/"
⭐     IS JUST A CHARACTER IN THE KEY, AND `LIST` WITH A PREFIX
⭐     IS A ***SCAN***, WHICH IS WHY LISTING A MILLION-OBJECT
⭐     "FOLDER" IS SLOW AND BILLED.
⭐   ⭐ ✔ Unlimited, cheap, 11 nines of durability, HTTP-native.

⭐⭐ WHAT BELONGS IN IT: user uploads, images, video, exports,
⭐   backups, logs, ML artefacts, static builds.
⭐⭐ WHAT DOES NOT: anything you need to query, and ⭐⭐ ANYTHING
⭐   WHOSE ***METADATA*** YOU NEED TO QUERY.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE RULE: ***THE BYTES GO IN THE BUCKET, THE ROW GOES
⭐     IN THE DATABASE.*** ⭐ `files(id, key, size, mime, owner,
⭐     sha256, status, created_at)`.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Without that row you cannot answer "show me this
⭐     user's files" without a scan, and you cannot enforce
⭐     ownership at all.

⭐⭐ CONSISTENCY: S3 has been read-after-write consistent for
⭐   new objects since Dec 2020. ⭐⭐ SAY THIS PRECISELY — many
⭐   candidates repeat the pre-2020 "eventually consistent"
⭐   folklore and get corrected. ⭐ Overwrites and deletes are
⭐   strongly consistent too; ***LIST*** is the operation to
⭐   still be careful with in some stores.

⭐⭐ STORAGE CLASSES AND THE TRAP:
⭐   ⭐ Standard → Infrequent Access → Glacier. Lifecycle rules
⭐     move objects automatically.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ IA CHARGES A 30-DAY MINIMUM AND A PER-GB RETRIEVAL
⭐     FEE. ⭐ Moving hot thumbnails to IA to "save money" can
⭐     cost more than it saves — ⭐⭐ RETRIEVAL COST IS THE PART
⭐     PEOPLE FORGET, AND IT IS WHERE THE BILL SURPRISE COMES
⭐     FROM.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐⭐ The upload that must not touch your API

```
⭐⭐ THE NAIVE FLOW, AND WHY IT IS FATAL:
⭐   ⭐ Browser POSTs a 200 MB video to FastAPI, which streams
⭐     it to S3.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Your worker is occupied for the whole upload (Day 325's
⭐     slow client, ×1000).
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ You pay ingress + egress and double the transfer time.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Body-size limits, proxy timeouts, memory spikes.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND IT DOES NOT SCALE HORIZONTALLY IN ANY USEFUL
⭐     WAY: TEN PODS = TEN CONCURRENT UPLOADS.

⭐⭐ THE FLOW THAT WORKS — ***FIVE STEPS***, and Day 267 built
⭐   it; here is the scaled version:
⭐   ① ⭐ Client asks your API for permission to upload.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ API AUTHORISES, INSERTS `files` ROW AS `pending`,
⭐        AND RETURNS A ***PRESIGNED URL*** WITH ⭐⭐ THE KEY,
⭐        MAX SIZE, CONTENT-TYPE AND EXPIRY ALL PINNED.
⭐        ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE CLIENT MUST NOT CHOOSE THE KEY. Ever. Let
⭐          it, and you get path traversal, overwrite of
⭐          someone else's object, and a bucket you cannot
⭐          reason about.
⭐   ③ ⭐ Client PUTs bytes ***directly to S3***. Your API is
⭐        not involved and is serving other requests.
⭐   ④ ⭐⭐ S3 EMITS AN EVENT ⇒ your worker verifies size, real
⭐        MIME (by sniffing, not by trusting), dimensions, virus
⭐        scan ⇒ flips the row to `ready`.
⭐        ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE CLIENT'S "I'M DONE" CALL IS AN OPTIMISATION,
⭐          NOT THE SOURCE OF TRUTH — clients close laptops.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐ A sweeper deletes `pending` rows older than 24 h and
⭐        orphaned objects with no row. ⭐⭐ BIDIRECTIONAL:
⭐        rows without objects ***and*** objects without rows.

⭐⭐ MULTIPART, FOR ANYTHING OVER ~100 MB:
⭐   ⭐ Parallel parts, resumable, per-part retry.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND THE LIFECYCLE RULE NOBODY SETS:
⭐     ***ABORT INCOMPLETE MULTIPART UPLOADS AFTER 7 DAYS.***
⭐     ⭐ Without it you are billed forever for parts of files
⭐     that never existed, and they are invisible in `LIST`.
```

```python
# ⭐ The API's entire involvement in a 200 MB upload
@router.post("/uploads")
async def create_upload(req: UploadRequest, user: User = Depends(current_user)):
    if req.size > MAX_BYTES:                     # ⭐ check BEFORE
        raise HTTPException(413)
    key = f"u/{user.tenant_id}/{user.id}/{uuid7()}"   # ⭐⭐ WE choose
    file = await files.create(key=key, owner=user.id, status="pending")
    url = s3.generate_presigned_url(
        "put_object",
        Params={"Bucket": BUCKET, "Key": key,
                "ContentType": req.mime,          # ⭐⭐ PINNED
                "ContentLength": req.size},       # ⭐⭐ PINNED
        ExpiresIn=900,                            # ⭐ 15 min
    )
    return {"file_id": file.id, "url": url}
# ⭐⭐ THREE HUNDRED MICROSECONDS OF YOUR API, FOR A 200 MB UPLOAD.
```

# Part 6 — ⭐⭐ Serving private files, and egress as the bill

```
⭐⭐ FOUR OPTIONS, AND ONE RIGHT ANSWER PER SITUATION:
⭐   ① ⭐ PUBLIC BUCKET — only for genuinely public assets.
⭐        ⭐⭐ AND NEVER FOR "PUBLIC BUT UNGUESSABLE" — that is
⭐        not access control, that is a URL nobody has leaked
⭐        yet.
⭐   ② ⭐ PROXY THROUGH THE API — full control, ⭐⭐ AND EVERY
⭐        BYTE COSTS YOU A WORKER. Only for small, rare files.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ PRESIGNED GET — short expiry (60–300 s), one object.
⭐        ⭐ The default answer for private downloads.
⭐        ⇒ ⭐ Its weakness: the URL is bearer-authorised, so a
⭐          leaked link works until it expires. ⭐⭐ HENCE THE
⭐          SHORT EXPIRY, AND HENCE NOT PUTTING IT IN AN EMAIL.
⭐   ④ ⭐⭐ SIGNED CDN URLS / COOKIES — you get edge caching
⭐        ***and*** authorisation. ⭐ The right answer for
⭐        private video and large private downloads.

⭐⭐ THE COST MODEL, WHICH DECIDES DESIGNS (Day 353 lives here):
⭐   ⭐ Storage:  ~$0.023/GB/month
⭐   ⭐ PUT:      ~$0.005 per 1,000
⭐   ⭐ GET:      ~$0.0004 per 1,000
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ EGRESS TO INTERNET: ~$0.09/GB — ⭐⭐ ROUGHLY 4× THE
⭐        MONTHLY COST OF ***STORING*** THAT GIGABYTE.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SO: 100 TB/month of video egress ≈ $9,000/month,
⭐     while storing 100 TB ≈ $2,300/month. ⭐ Serving costs
⭐     more than keeping.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ WHICH IS THE ENTIRE ARGUMENT FOR A CDN IN FRONT OF
⭐     THE BUCKET: CDN EGRESS IS CHEAPER, AND ORIGIN-TO-CDN
⭐     TRANSFER IS OFTEN FREE.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ And it is why "many small objects" hurts: 10 million
⭐     PUTs is $50 on its own, before a byte is stored.
```

# Part 7 — ⭐ Deletion, versioning, and the four places a file lives

```
⭐⭐ "DELETE THIS FILE" MEANS DELETING FROM FOUR PLACES
⭐   (Day 267, and it is worth repeating because people still
⭐   ship three):
⭐   ① ⭐ The database row.
⭐   ② ⭐ The object.
⭐   ③ ⭐ Derived artefacts — thumbnails, transcodes, previews,
⭐        extracted text, ⭐⭐ AND THE SEARCH INDEX (Day 337).
⭐   ④ ⭐⭐ THE CDN CACHE — ⭐ or it is served from the edge for
⭐        the rest of its TTL, ⭐⭐ WHICH IS A GDPR PROBLEM WITH
⭐        A CLOCK ON IT.

⭐⭐ AND THE VERSIONING TRAP:
⭐   ***WITH BUCKET VERSIONING ON, `DELETE` DOES NOT DELETE.***
⭐   ⭐ It writes a delete marker. The bytes remain, billed, and
⭐     recoverable — ⭐⭐ WHICH IS EXCELLENT FOR RANSOMWARE AND
⭐     TERRIBLE FOR AN ERASURE REQUEST.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Erasure needs delete-by-version-id, plus a lifecycle
⭐     rule for noncurrent versions. ⭐⭐ KNOWING THIS DISTINCTION
⭐     IS A STRONG SIGNAL — it is where most "we deleted it"
⭐     claims turn out to be false.
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ Personalised response marked `public` | The first user's data is served to a whole region. You cannot un-serve it |
| ⭐⭐ Uploads streamed through the API | A worker per upload, double transfer cost, and ten pods means ten uploads |
| ⭐⭐ Client chooses the object key | Path traversal and overwriting other tenants' objects. The server always chooses |
| ⭐ Trusting the client's "upload complete" call | Clients close laptops. The storage event is the truth |
| ⭐⭐ No abort rule for multipart | You are billed forever for parts of files that never existed and don't appear in LIST |
| ⭐ `Vary: Cookie` to be safe | Every cookie value becomes a cache entry. Hit ratio zero |
| ⭐ Not stripping tracking query params | A marketing campaign destroys your cache hit ratio overnight |
| ⭐⭐ Relying on CDN purge for correctness | Slow, global, rate-limited. Hash the URL so purge is never needed |
| ⭐ "Unguessable URL" as access control | That is not authorisation, that is a link nobody has leaked yet |
| ⭐⭐ Forgetting egress in the cost model | Egress is ~4× the monthly cost of storing the same gigabyte |
| ⭐⭐ Assuming DELETE deletes, with versioning on | It writes a marker. The bytes stay, billed and recoverable |
| ⭐ Repeating "S3 is eventually consistent" | Read-after-write since Dec 2020. Stale folklore gets corrected in interviews |

## Interview questions

**1. "How do you handle a 2 GB video upload?"**
> ⭐⭐ It never touches my API. ⭐ The client asks for permission, I authorise, insert a `pending` row,
> and return a presigned multipart URL **with the key, content-type, max size and expiry all pinned by
> the server**. ⭐ The client uploads directly to S3; a storage event triggers verification — real MIME
> by sniffing, size, virus scan — and flips the row to `ready`. ⭐⭐ The client's completion callback
> is an optimisation, not the source of truth, and there's a bidirectional sweeper for rows without
> objects and objects without rows, plus a lifecycle rule aborting incomplete multipart uploads after
> seven days.

**2. "What is the CDN cache key, and why does it matter?"**
> ⭐ Host, path and query by default — ⭐⭐ **not the cookie and not the Authorization header.** So a
> personalised endpoint marked `public` gets one user's data served to an entire region for the TTL,
> and it can't be un-served. ⭐ Personalised means `private, no-store`; anything that varies by header
> declares `Vary`; never `Vary: Cookie` because it zeroes the hit ratio; and the CDN should be
> default-deny, caching only what I explicitly mark.

**3. "How do you invalidate the CDN?"**
> ⭐⭐ I design so I never have to. ⭐ Content-addressed URLs with the build hash in the filename, and
> a one-year immutable TTL — a new build is a new URL, so there's nothing to purge; only the HTML that
> names them is short-TTL. ⭐ Where I can't hash, `stale-while-revalidate` gives me the stampede fix as
> a header, and ⭐⭐ `stale-if-error` keeps the site up when the origin is down, which is a one-line
> availability upgrade almost nobody enables.

**4. "How do you serve a private file?"**
> ⭐ Presigned GET with a short expiry for most cases — with the caveat said out loud that it's a
> bearer URL, so a leaked link works until it expires, which is why the expiry is minutes and why it
> doesn't go in an email. ⭐⭐ For private video or large downloads, signed CDN URLs, because that's
> the only option that gives me edge caching *and* authorisation. ⭐ Proxying through the API costs a
> worker per byte and is for small, rare files only.

**5. "What does deleting a file actually involve?"**
> ⭐ Four places: the row, the object, every derived artefact including thumbnails and the search
> index, and the CDN cache — ⭐⭐ and the trap, which is that with versioning enabled `DELETE` writes a
> marker rather than deleting. The bytes remain, billed and recoverable. ⭐ Real erasure means
> delete-by-version-id plus a lifecycle rule for noncurrent versions.

**6. "Why is egress the number that decides video architecture?"**
> ⭐⭐ Because at roughly nine cents a gigabyte, serving a gigabyte costs about four times what storing
> it for a month costs. ⭐ A hundred terabytes of monthly egress is about nine thousand dollars against
> twenty-three hundred to store it. ⭐⭐ That's the whole argument for a CDN in front of the bucket —
> cheaper egress, and origin-to-CDN transfer that's often free.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Start with the principle: **bytes identical for every user should never be produced by
your application** — that single rule gives you the CDN and it gives you presigned uploads. On the CDN,
lead with the security half, because it's where the incidents are: **the default cache key is host,
path and query, which means it does not include the cookie** — so a personalised endpoint marked
`public` serves one user's profile to an entire region, and you cannot un-serve it. Then invalidation:
**hash the URL and you never purge**, because a new build is a new URL, and `stale-if-error` keeps you
serving when the origin is down for the price of one header. On object storage, say plainly that it
isn't a filesystem — no append, no rename, no directories — and give the rule: **the bytes go in the
bucket, the row goes in the database**, or you cannot enforce ownership or answer a listing query.
Then the upload: **a two-gigabyte file must never touch your API** — authorise, insert a pending row,
return a presigned URL with key, type, size and expiry pinned by the server, and let the storage event
be the truth, because clients close laptops. Close on money: **egress is about four times the monthly
cost of storing the same gigabyte**, which is why the CDN sits in front of the bucket — and on the
deletion trap, that with versioning on, delete doesn't delete.
