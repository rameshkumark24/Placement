# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ The pattern card: nine problems, five shapes

```
⭐⭐ BEFORE THE GATE, CONSOLIDATE. ⭐ Nine problems, and almost
⭐   all of them were the same five decisions:

⭐⭐ ① ***HOLD → ACT → COMMIT, WITH AN EXPIRY.***
⭐     ⭐ seat booking (316) · ATM authorisation (317) · driver
⭐       offer (320) · parking spot (311)
⭐     ⇒ ⭐⭐ WHENEVER SOMETHING MUST BE RESERVED ACROSS AN
⭐       OPERATION YOU DO NOT CONTROL. ⭐ Data with an expiry,
⭐       never a lock.

⭐⭐ ② ***CHECK-AND-ACT AS ONE OPERATION.***
⭐     ⭐ `try_occupy` (311) · conditional `UPDATE` (316, 320) ·
⭐       token bucket under a lock (314)
⭐     ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE OVERSELL, THE DOUBLE BOOKING AND THE DOUBLE
⭐       ASSIGNMENT ARE ONE BUG IN THREE COSTUMES (Day 310).

⭐⭐ ③ ***A LEDGER, NOT A BALANCE.***
⭐     ⭐ Splitwise (315) · the vending audit log (317) · the
⭐       chess undo stack (319)
⭐     ⇒ ⭐⭐ IMMUTABLE FACTS, DERIVED STATE — WHICH GIVES YOU
⭐       AUDIT, UNDO, REPLAY AND RECOVERY FROM ONE STRUCTURE.

⭐⭐ ④ ***THE THIRD OUTCOME.***
⭐     ⭐ payment timeout (316) · jammed motor (317) · push
⭐       notification timeout (318)
⭐     ⇒ ⭐⭐ SUCCESS, FAILURE, AND ***I DO NOT KNOW*** — with
⭐       reconciliation, and your own reference sent with the
⭐       request so reconciliation is possible at all.

⭐⭐ ⑤ ***A STRATEGY ON THE AXIS THAT ACTUALLY VARIES.***
⭐     ⭐ eviction policy (313) · allocation (311) · rate-limit
⭐       algorithm (314) · channels (318)
⭐     ⇒ ⭐ And the discipline of ***declining*** one out loud
⭐       (318), because that scores higher than building it.

⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ IF YOU CAN NAME THE SHAPE IN THE FIRST FIVE MINUTES OF
⭐   AN UNSEEN PROBLEM, YOU HAVE ALREADY DESIGNED HALF OF IT.
```

# Part 2 — ⭐ The gate format

```
⭐   ⭐ TWO PROBLEMS YOU HAVE NOT SEEN, 45 minutes each,
⭐     ⭐⭐ SEPARATED BY A REAL BREAK — because fatigue on the
⭐     second one is part of what is being simulated.
⭐   ⭐ RECORD BOTH: screen and audio.
⭐   ⭐ Narrate continuously (Day 308). ⭐⭐ IF THE RECORDING
⭐     HAS A NINETY-SECOND SILENCE, THAT IS A FINDING REGARDLESS
⭐     OF HOW GOOD THE CODE IS.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ END WITH CODE THAT ***RUNS***. ⭐ A `main()` or a
⭐     test that exercises the flow.

⭐⭐ CANDIDATE PROBLEMS (pick two you have not done):
⭐   ⭐ a car rental system with overlapping bookings
⭐   ⭐ a library with holds and due dates
⭐   ⭐ an online auction with a hard closing time
⭐   ⭐ a hotel booking with room types and overbooking
⭐   ⭐ a job scheduler with dependencies and retries
⭐   ⭐ a file storage system with versions and quotas
⭐   ⭐ a coupon engine with stacking rules and limits
⭐   ⭐ a delivery locker with pickup codes and expiry
⭐ ⇒ ⭐ Notice that six of these are shape ①. ⭐⭐ THAT IS NOT AN
⭐   ACCIDENT — IT IS WHAT LLD INTERVIEWS ARE ABOUT.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The scoring rubric

> ⭐ Score each interview honestly. ⭐⭐ Below 3 on any row is a study target, not a verdict.

```
⭐⭐ | # | CRITERION | 1 | 3 | 5 |
⭐   | 1 | SCOPING | started coding | asked some | ⭐⭐ 5 QUESTIONS
⭐        THAT EACH CHANGED THE DESIGN + AN OUT-OF-SCOPE LIST |
⭐   | 2 | MODELLING | every noun a class | mostly right |
⭐        ⭐ 4–6 classes, enums and value objects correct |
⭐   | 3 | ⭐⭐ THE INVARIANT | never stated | mentioned |
⭐        ⭐⭐ STATED EARLY AND ENFORCED STRUCTURALLY |
⭐   | 4 | ⭐⭐ CONCURRENCY | "add a lock" | identified |
⭐        ⭐⭐ SHARED STATE + INVARIANT + MECHANISM + MULTI-PROCESS |
⭐   | 5 | CODE | pseudocode | compiles | ⭐ runs, typed, raises
⭐        domain exceptions |
⭐   | 6 | ⭐⭐ NARRATION | long silences | some | ⭐⭐ DECISIONS
⭐        NARRATED, TRADE-OFFS STATED AS MADE |
⭐   | 7 | EXTENSION | design broke | absorbed it |
⭐        ⭐ absorbed with one named change |
⭐   | 8 | CLOSE | ran out of time | mentioned tests |
⭐        ⭐ tests + one change + what breaks at 100× |
⭐   | 9 | ⭐⭐ TIME | still adding classes at 38 | finished |
⭐        ⭐⭐ STOPPED ADDING AT 30, SPENT 30–40 ON THE HARD PART |

⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ ROWS 3, 4 AND 9 ARE THE ONES THAT DECIDE REAL
⭐   INTERVIEWS. ⭐ A 5 on code with a 2 on concurrency is a
⭐   fail, and a 3 on code with a 5 on concurrency usually is not.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Watch the recordings

```
⭐⭐ THIS IS THE PART PEOPLE SKIP, AND IT IS WHERE THE VALUE IS.
⭐   ⭐ Count silences over 20 seconds. ⭐⭐ EACH ONE IS A
⭐     MOMENT THE INTERVIEWER LEARNED NOTHING AND COULD NOT
⭐     CORRECT YOU.
⭐   ⭐ Mark the timestamp where you stopped adding classes.
⭐     ⭐ Is it before 30 minutes?
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ FIND EVERY PLACE YOU SAID "AND THEN WE'D JUST…"
⭐     ⭐ That phrase almost always precedes the hard part being
⭐     skipped, and interviewers hear it as avoidance.
⭐   ⭐ Note where you were ***told*** something rather than
⭐     asking — ⭐ a hint you needed is a gap.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE HARSHEST, MOST USEFUL CHECK: ***WOULD
⭐     SOMEONE WATCHING WITHOUT THE SCREEN UNDERSTAND YOUR
⭐     DESIGN FROM THE AUDIO ALONE?*** ⭐ In a real interview,
⭐     half the panel is only half-watching.
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Reading this day instead of doing it | The gate is two live attempts; reading proves nothing. |
| Choosing problems you've seen | The point is unseen; six of the eight listed are shape ①. |
| No recording | You lose the only objective evidence about narration and timing. |
| Not watching the recordings | It's where the value is, and it's the step people skip. |
| Scoring generously | Below 3 is a study target; inflating it removes the target. |
| Treating code quality as the top row | A 5 on code with a 2 on concurrency is a fail. |
| "And then we'd just…" | It almost always precedes the hard part being skipped. |
| Needing hints | A hint you needed is a gap; write it down. |
| Doing both problems back to back | Fatigue on the second is part of the simulation, so take the break properly. |
| Stopping after two attempts | Fix your top finding and do a third to confirm it took. |

## Interview questions

**Q: What have you learned to look for in any design problem?**

> Five shapes, and I can usually name one in the first five minutes. Hold-act-commit with an expiry,
> whenever something must be reserved across an operation I don't control — a seat, an ATM
> authorisation, a driver offer. Check-and-act as one operation, because the oversell, the double
> booking and the double assignment are one bug in three costumes. A ledger rather than a stored
> balance, which gives audit, undo, replay and crash recovery from one structure. The third outcome,
> where a timeout means "I don't know" rather than failure. And a strategy only on the axis that
> actually varies — including declining one out loud.

**Q: How do you know you'd do well in a design interview?**

> Because I've recorded myself doing them and watched the recordings, which is uncomfortable and the
> only honest evidence available. I score nine rows, and I know which three decide real interviews:
> whether I state the invariant early and enforce it structurally, whether my concurrency answer names
> the shared state and the multi-process consequence rather than saying "add a lock", and whether I
> stopped adding classes at minute thirty. The check I found most useful is whether someone listening
> to the audio alone would understand the design — because in a real interview half the panel is only
> half-watching.

**Q: What was your biggest weakness, and what did you do?**

> Silence. My first recording had four gaps over twenty seconds, which are four moments where the
> interviewer learned nothing and couldn't correct a wrong assumption I was building on. The fix was
> narrating decisions rather than typing — "I'm making the spot own its occupancy rather than the lot
> owning a map, because the invariant is per-spot" — and then doing a third problem to confirm it
> stuck rather than assuming it had.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes: "How do you approach a design problem you've never seen?"**

Start with pattern recognition rather than process: **five shapes cover almost every LLD problem**, and
naming one in the first five minutes designs half of it for you. Hold-act-commit with an expiry
whenever something is reserved across an operation you don't control — **and the key line is that it's
data with an expiry, not a lock, so it survives a restart and works across replicas.** Check-and-act as
one operation, because **the oversell, the double booking and the double assignment are one bug in
three costumes.** A ledger rather than a stored balance, which gives audit, undo, replay and recovery
from one structure. The third outcome, where a timeout is the absence of information. And a strategy
only on the axis that varies — **including declining one out loud, which scores higher than building
it.** Then the process in one breath: five scoping questions and an out-of-scope sentence, prune to
four to six classes, state the invariant early, **stop adding classes at minute thirty**, spend the
next ten on concurrency and the extension, and close on tests, one change, and what breaks at 100×.
Close honestly: **I know this works because I recorded myself doing it, watched it back, and counted
the silences** — which is uncomfortable and the only real evidence there is.
