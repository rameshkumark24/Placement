# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ Two-phase commit, and why it blocks

```
⭐⭐ THE PROTOCOL:
⭐   ⭐ PHASE 1 — PREPARE: the coordinator asks every participant
⭐     "can you commit?" ⭐⭐ EACH ONE LOCKS ITS RESOURCES,
⭐     WRITES ITS INTENT DURABLY, AND ANSWERS YES/NO — AND
⭐     HAVING SAID YES, ***IT CAN NO LONGER DECIDE FOR
⭐     ITSELF.***
⭐   ⭐ PHASE 2 — COMMIT/ABORT: if all said yes, commit; else
⭐     abort.

⭐⭐ IT IS GENUINELY ATOMIC. ⭐ And the price is severe:

⭐⭐ ① ***IT IS A BLOCKING PROTOCOL.*** ⭐ If the coordinator
⭐      dies after phase 1, ⭐⭐ EVERY PARTICIPANT HOLDS ITS
⭐      LOCKS ***INDEFINITELY***. ⭐ They cannot commit (maybe
⭐      someone said no) and cannot abort (maybe everyone said
⭐      yes). ⭐⭐ THEY MUST WAIT, AND THE ROWS THEY LOCKED ARE
⭐      UNAVAILABLE TO EVERYONE ELSE MEANWHILE.
⭐⭐ ② ***AVAILABILITY IS THE PRODUCT OF ALL PARTICIPANTS.***
⭐      ⭐ Five services at 99.9% ⇒ ⭐⭐ 99.5% FOR THE
⭐      TRANSACTION. ⭐ You made every service a dependency of
⭐      every other.
⭐⭐ ③ ***LOCKS ARE HELD FOR A NETWORK ROUND TRIP***, so
⭐      throughput collapses on contended rows.
⭐⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ IT DOES NOT WORK WITH THINGS THAT DO NOT SUPPORT
⭐      PREPARE — ⭐ Stripe has no prepare phase. Neither does
⭐      an email. Neither does most of the internet.

⭐⭐ WHERE IT IS STILL RIGHT: ⭐ within one organisation, few
⭐   participants, all of them XA-capable databases, low volume,
⭐   ⭐ and correctness worth more than availability. ⭐ Bank
⭐   core systems. ⭐⭐ NOT MICROSERVICES OVER HTTP.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ (3PC adds a phase to reduce blocking and ⭐⭐ STILL
⭐     FAILS UNDER NETWORK PARTITION — worth one sentence, no
⭐     more.)
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The saga: a sequence with an undo per step

```
⭐⭐ ***A SAGA IS A SEQUENCE OF LOCAL TRANSACTIONS. EACH ONE
⭐   COMMITS IMMEDIATELY. IF STEP N FAILS, YOU RUN
⭐   COMPENSATIONS FOR STEPS N−1 … 1, IN REVERSE.***

⭐⭐ THE THREE PROPERTIES THAT FOLLOW, AND YOU MUST STATE THEM:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ NO ISOLATION. ⭐ Intermediate states are ***visible
⭐     to everyone***. Someone will read a partially-completed
⭐     order. ⭐ This is not a bug to fix; ⭐⭐ IT IS THE PRICE,
⭐     AND THE UI MUST TELL THE TRUTH ABOUT IT ("processing").
⭐   ② ⭐ Eventually consistent, with a window you should be
⭐     able to state in seconds.
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ COMPENSATIONS MUST BE SEMANTICALLY SOUND, BECAUSE
⭐     THEY CANNOT BE PHYSICAL UNDO.

⭐⭐ THE PIVOT — ⭐ a concept that makes sagas designable rather
⭐   than scary:
⭐   ⭐ ***COMPENSATABLE*** steps: can be undone (reserve
⭐     inventory, authorise payment).
⭐   ⭐ ***PIVOT***: the point of no return (capture the payment,
⭐     hand to the courier).
⭐   ⭐ ***RETRIABLE***: after the pivot, everything must
⭐     eventually succeed — ⭐⭐ THEY MAY ONLY BE RETRIED, NEVER
⭐     COMPENSATED.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SO DESIGN THE ORDER SO THAT EVERYTHING THAT CAN FAIL
⭐     HAPPENS ***BEFORE*** THE PIVOT. ⭐ Validate the address
⭐     before charging, not after.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ Compensation is not rollback

```
⭐⭐ THE DISTINCTION THAT SEPARATES CANDIDATES:
⭐   ⭐ ***ROLLBACK*** erases history. Nobody ever saw it.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ***COMPENSATION*** ADDS A NEW FACT THAT COUNTERACTS
⭐     AN OLD ONE. ***BOTH REMAIN TRUE, AND BOTH ARE VISIBLE.***

⭐⭐ FOUR EXAMPLES, AND EACH ONE IS A PRODUCT DECISION:
⭐   ⭐ Charge ⇒ ⭐⭐ REFUND — not "un-charge". ⭐ The customer
⭐     sees two lines on the statement. ⭐ The fee may not come
⭐     back. ⭐ It may take five days.
⭐   ⭐ Email sent ⇒ ⭐⭐ THERE IS NO COMPENSATION. ⭐ You can
⭐     send an apology. ⭐⭐ THE ORIGINAL EMAIL EXISTS FOREVER,
⭐     WHICH IS WHY SENDING EMAIL SHOULD BE ***AFTER THE
⭐     PIVOT***, ALWAYS.
⭐   ⭐ Inventory reserved ⇒ release. ⭐ Clean — ⭐⭐ ***IF*** IT
⭐     WAS A RESERVATION WITH AN EXPIRY (Day 316's hold, and
⭐     that Stage 8 shape is now load-bearing here).
⭐   ⭐ Seat assigned on a flight ⇒ ⭐⭐ SOMEONE ELSE TOOK IT
⭐     MEANWHILE. ⭐ The compensation is "assign a different
⭐     seat and apologise", ⭐⭐ WHICH IS A BUSINESS RULE
⭐     SOMEBODY MUST DECIDE, NOT A LINE OF CODE.

⭐⭐ THE THREE RULES FOR WRITING ONE:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ IT MUST BE IDEMPOTENT — it will be retried.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ IT MUST ***ALWAYS EVENTUALLY SUCCEED.*** ⭐ There is
⭐      no compensating the compensation. ⭐ If it can fail
⭐      permanently, it needs a manual queue with a human.
⭐   ③ ⭐ It must be commutative-safe: ⭐⭐ WRITE IT SO IT WORKS
⭐      EVEN IF THE FORWARD STEP NEVER ACTUALLY HAPPENED
⭐      (a timeout with an unknown outcome — Day 317's third
⭐      outcome). ⭐ "Release the hold if held by me" rather
⭐      than "release the hold".
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Implementing a saga in Python

```
⭐⭐ THE STATE MUST BE DURABLE. ⭐⭐ A SAGA HELD IN A PYTHON
⭐   VARIABLE IS AN OUTAGE WITH A PLAN.

⭐⭐ THE TABLE IS THE DESIGN:
⭐   `saga_id · type · current_step · state · payload ·
⭐    compensating(bool) · attempts · next_attempt_at ·
⭐    created_at · updated_at`
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Every step transition is a database write.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND THE STEP RESULT IS RECORDED ***BEFORE*** THE NEXT
⭐     STEP STARTS, SO RECOVERY IS UNAMBIGUOUS.

⭐⭐ THE RECOVERY LOOP THAT MAKES IT REAL:
⭐   ⭐ A worker scans for sagas stuck in a non-terminal state
⭐     past a timeout and resumes them.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ WITHOUT THIS, A CRASHED ORCHESTRATOR LEAVES ORDERS
⭐     HALF-DONE FOREVER AND NOBODY FINDS OUT UNTIL A CUSTOMER
⭐     COMPLAINS.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ It is the same shape as Day 264's watermark job: ⭐⭐ THE
⭐     RECOVERY LOOP IS NOT AN EXTRA — IT ***IS*** THE DURABILITY
⭐     GUARANTEE.
```

```python
# ⭐ Orchestrated saga — the shape, and the two lines that matter
STEPS = [
    Step("reserve_inventory", do=reserve,  undo=release),     # ⭐ compensatable
    Step("authorise_payment", do=authorise, undo=void),       # ⭐ compensatable
    Step("capture_payment",   do=capture,  undo=None),        # ⭐⭐ PIVOT
    Step("create_shipment",   do=ship,     undo=None),        # ⭐ retriable
]

async def advance(saga: Saga) -> None:
    step = STEPS[saga.current_step]
    try:
        result = await step.do(saga.payload, key=saga.id)     # ⭐⭐ IDEMPOTENCY KEY
        await save(saga, step=saga.current_step + 1, result=result)  # ⭐⭐ DURABLE
    except PermanentError:
        if step.undo is None:                                 # ⭐⭐ PAST THE PIVOT
            await escalate_to_human(saga)                     # ⭐ the only option
        else:
            await save(saga, compensating=True)
    except TransientError:
        await save(saga, next_attempt_at=backoff(saga.attempts))
```

```
⭐⭐ AND THE PART NOBODY BUILDS UNTIL IT IS TOO LATE:
⭐   ***A HUMAN-VISIBLE VIEW OF STUCK SAGAS, WITH A BUTTON TO
⭐      RETRY OR FORCE-COMPENSATE.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Some sagas will always end up needing a person. ⭐⭐ AN
⭐     ADMIN PAGE IS PART OF THE DESIGN, NOT AN ADMISSION OF
⭐     DEFEAT — and saying so out loud is a strong signal.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐⭐ Avoiding the distributed transaction entirely

```
⭐⭐ THE BEST DISTRIBUTED TRANSACTION IS THE ONE YOU DO NOT
⭐   HAVE. ⭐ Four ways out, in order of preference:

⭐⭐ ① ***PUT THE INVARIANT INSIDE ONE AGGREGATE / ONE SERVICE.***
⭐   ⭐ If order and inventory must be consistent atomically,
⭐     ⭐⭐ ASK WHETHER THEY SHOULD BE ONE SERVICE. ⭐ Day 305's
⭐     "the aggregate is the transaction boundary" and Day 340's
⭐     boundary test are the same question — ⭐⭐ AND A SAGA IS
⭐     OFTEN THE SMELL THAT YOU DREW THE BOUNDARY WRONG.

⭐⭐ ② ***MAKE THE OPERATION COMMUTATIVE / MONOTONIC*** (Day
⭐   330). ⭐ Append to a ledger rather than setting a balance.
⭐   ⭐ Order stops mattering, ⭐ so there is nothing to
⭐   coordinate.

⭐⭐ ③ ***USE THE OUTBOX AND ACCEPT EVENTUAL CONSISTENCY.***
⭐   ⭐ One local transaction + reliable publication (Day 263,
⭐     Day 335) covers a very large fraction of what people
⭐     reach for 2PC to do.

⭐⭐ ④ ***RESERVE-THEN-CONFIRM*** — ⭐⭐ THE PATTERN THAT KEEPS
⭐   APPEARING. ⭐ Reserve with an expiry, do the risky work,
⭐   confirm or let it lapse.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SEATS (316), THE ATM (317), THE DRIVER OFFER (320),
⭐     PAYMENT AUTHORISATION, INVENTORY HOLDS. ⭐ Five
⭐     appearances across two stages — ⭐⭐ IT IS THE MOST
⭐     REUSABLE IDEA IN THIS ENTIRE CURRICULUM, AND IT IS A
⭐     SAGA WITH ONE COMPENSATABLE STEP AND A TIMER THAT
⭐     COMPENSATES FOR YOU.

⭐⭐ AND THE QUESTION THAT SHOULD PRECEDE ALL OF THIS:
⭐   ***DOES THE BUSINESS ACTUALLY REQUIRE ATOMICITY, OR DOES
⭐      IT REQUIRE ***EVENTUAL CORRECTNESS WITH AN AUDIT
⭐      TRAIL***?*** ⭐ Banks do not use 2PC between institutions.
⭐   ⭐⭐ THEY USE MESSAGES, RECONCILIATION AND REVERSALS —
⭐   WHICH IS TO SAY, SAGAS WITH BETTER PAPERWORK.
```

# Part 6 — ⭐ Orchestration vs choreography, for sagas specifically

```
⭐⭐ CHOREOGRAPHED SAGA — each service listens and emits.
⭐   ⭐ ✔ No central component.
⭐   ⭐ ✘ ⭐⭐ THE COMPENSATION LOGIC IS SCATTERED ACROSS EVERY
⭐     SERVICE, AND NO SINGLE PLACE KNOWS HOW FAR THE SAGA GOT.
⭐   ⭐ ✘ ⭐⭐ ADDING A STEP IN THE MIDDLE MEANS EDITING TWO
⭐     SERVICES THAT MUST DEPLOY IN ORDER.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Acceptable for 2–3 steps with trivial compensation.

⭐⭐ ORCHESTRATED SAGA — one coordinator with a durable state
⭐   machine.
⭐   ⭐ ✔ ⭐⭐ THE PROCESS IS ONE READABLE FILE; "WHERE IS ORDER
⭐     123?" IS ONE QUERY; compensation order is explicit;
⭐     ⭐ adding a step is one deploy.
⭐   ⭐ ✘ A component to build and run.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ FOR ANYTHING WITH MONEY OR MORE THAN THREE STEPS,
⭐     ORCHESTRATE. ⭐ Day 333's rule, and this is the day it
⭐     matters most.

⭐⭐ AND THE TOOLS, NAMED SO YOU KNOW THEY EXIST: ⭐ Temporal,
⭐   AWS Step Functions, Camunda. ⭐⭐ THEY ARE DURABLE STATE
⭐   MACHINES WITH RETRIES AND TIMERS BUILT IN — ⭐ and the
⭐   honest note is that ⭐⭐ TEMPORAL'S PYTHON SDK MAKES SAGAS
⭐   LOOK LIKE ORDINARY `async def` CODE WHOSE EXECUTION SURVIVES
⭐   PROCESS DEATH, ⭐ which removes most of Part 4's plumbing.
⭐   ⭐ Knowing when a hand-rolled table is enough (⭐ under ~5
⭐     steps, one team) is the judgement half.
```

# Part 7 — ⭐ Answering the question in an interview

```
⭐⭐ THE STRUCTURE:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ "CAN THIS BE ONE TRANSACTION IN ONE DATABASE?" ⭐ If
⭐      yes, do that and say why it is worth keeping.
⭐   ② ⭐ "What is the actual business requirement — atomicity,
⭐      or eventual correctness with an audit trail?"
⭐   ③ ⭐ Identify the ***pivot***.
⭐   ④ ⭐⭐ ORDER THE STEPS SO EVERYTHING FALLIBLE PRECEDES IT.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐ Define each compensation ***as a business outcome***,
⭐      and name who decided it.
⭐   ⑥ ⭐ State the visible intermediate state and how the UI
⭐      handles it.
⭐   ⑦ ⭐⭐ NAME THE MANUAL PATH FOR SAGAS THAT GET STUCK.

⭐⭐ THE LINE THAT LANDS:
⭐   ***"I'd avoid the distributed transaction if I can — a
⭐      saga is often a sign the service boundary is wrong.
⭐      Where I can't, it's an orchestrated saga with a durable
⭐      state machine, a clearly identified pivot, everything
⭐      fallible before it, compensations defined as business
⭐      outcomes rather than rollbacks, and an admin screen,
⭐      because some of these will always need a human."***
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ 2PC across microservices over HTTP | Blocking protocol; a dead coordinator holds everyone's locks indefinitely |
| ⭐ Forgetting availability multiplies | Five participants at 99.9% gives 99.5% for the transaction |
| ⭐⭐ Treating compensation as rollback | It's a new visible fact. A refund is two statement lines, not an erased charge |
| ⭐⭐ No pivot identified | Fallible steps end up after the point of no return, and you can't undo an email |
| ⭐ Compensation that can fail permanently | There's no compensating the compensation. It needs a human queue |
| ⭐⭐ Compensation that assumes the forward step happened | Timeouts have unknown outcomes. "Release if held by me", not "release" |
| ⭐⭐ Saga state in memory | An outage with a plan. It must be a durable row per transition |
| ⭐ No recovery loop | A crashed orchestrator leaves orders half-done until a customer complains |
| ⭐ Choreographing a money saga | Compensation logic scattered, and nothing knows how far it got |
| ⭐⭐ No admin screen for stuck sagas | Some always need a person. It's part of the design, not a defeat |
| ⭐ Not asking whether it should be one service | A saga is frequently the smell that you drew the boundary wrong |
| ⭐ Pretending intermediate states are invisible | They aren't. The UI must say "processing" and mean it |

## Interview questions

**1. "Why not just use 2PC?"**
> ⭐⭐ Because it's a **blocking** protocol: once a participant votes yes it can't decide for itself, so
> a coordinator that dies after phase one leaves everyone holding locks indefinitely — they can't commit
> and can't abort. ⭐ Availability also multiplies: five services at three nines gives 99.5% for the
> transaction. ⭐ Locks are held across a network round trip, so throughput collapses on contended rows.
> ⭐⭐ And most participants can't prepare at all — **Stripe has no prepare phase, and neither does an
> email.**

**2. "Explain a saga."**
> ⭐ A sequence of local transactions, each committing immediately, with a compensation per step run in
> reverse if a later step fails. ⭐⭐ The three consequences I'd state upfront: **no isolation, so
> intermediate states are visible and the UI must say "processing"**; eventual consistency with a window
> I should be able to quote in seconds; and compensations that must be semantically sound because they
> can't be physical undo. ⭐ Then the pivot: compensatable steps before it, retriable after, and design
> the order so everything fallible happens first.

**3. "What's the difference between compensation and rollback?"**
> ⭐⭐ A rollback erases history — nobody ever saw it. **A compensation adds a new fact that counteracts
> an old one, and both remain true and visible.** ⭐ A charge compensates to a refund: two lines on the
> statement, possibly a lost fee, possibly five days. ⭐⭐ An email has no compensation at all, which is
> exactly why sending email belongs after the pivot. ⭐ And a seat someone else has taken meanwhile
> compensates to "assign a different seat and apologise" — which is a business rule someone must decide,
> not a line of code.

**4. "How do you implement one?"**
> ⭐⭐ Durable state, always — **a saga in a Python variable is an outage with a plan.** ⭐ A row per
> saga with current step, state, payload and a compensating flag; every transition is a write, and the
> step result is recorded before the next step starts so recovery is unambiguous. ⭐ Every step takes
> the saga id as an idempotency key. ⭐⭐ Plus a recovery loop scanning for sagas stuck past a timeout —
> **that loop isn't an extra, it *is* the durability guarantee** — and an admin screen, because some
> will always need a human.

**5. "How would you avoid needing one?"**
> ⭐ Four ways. ⭐⭐ Put the invariant inside one service — **a saga is often the smell that the
> boundary is wrong.** ⭐ Make the operation commutative, appending to a ledger rather than setting a
> balance, so ordering stops mattering. ⭐ Use the outbox and accept eventual consistency, which covers
> most of what people reach for 2PC to do. ⭐⭐ Or reserve-then-confirm — **the seat hold, the ATM
> authorisation, the driver offer, the payment auth — which is a saga with one compensatable step and a
> timer that compensates for you.**

**6. "Where's the pivot in an e-commerce order?"**
> ⭐ Capturing the payment, usually — before it I can void an authorisation and release an inventory
> hold cleanly; after it, refunds are visible, fees may be lost and the courier may already have the
> parcel. ⭐⭐ So the design rule is to **move everything fallible before the pivot**: validate the
> address, check fraud, confirm inventory and verify the shipping option first, then capture. ⭐ And
> anything with no compensation — the confirmation email — goes strictly after it.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** State the constraint: **you cannot have ACID across service boundaries**, so the real
choice is between blocking and being visibly, temporarily wrong. Dispatch 2PC on its actual weakness —
**it's a blocking protocol, so a coordinator that dies after the prepare phase leaves every participant
holding locks it can neither commit nor release** — plus availability multiplying across participants,
and the fact that most real participants have no prepare phase at all, including Stripe and email. Then
sagas, with the three consequences stated up front: **no isolation, so intermediate states are visible
and the UI must say "processing"**; eventual consistency with a window you can quote; and compensations
that are semantic rather than physical. Give the pivot, because it's what makes sagas designable:
compensatable before, retriable after, **so order the steps to put everything fallible before the point
of no return.** Then the distinction that separates candidates: **a compensation is not a rollback — a
charge compensates to a refund, which is two lines on a statement, a possibly-lost fee and five days,
and an email compensates to nothing at all.** Close on implementation and honesty: durable state with
every transition written, a recovery loop that *is* the durability guarantee, compensations written to
work even when the forward step's outcome is unknown — **and an admin screen for stuck sagas, because
some will always need a person, and saying so is design, not defeat.** And the framing to end on: **a
saga is often the smell that you drew the service boundary in the wrong place.**
