# Day 458 🚦

**P-458** · 🚦 **Rate limits and multi-provider** — ⭐⭐ your eval job can starve your users

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: you are rate-limited on tokens, not requests, so a RAG application hits the
> ceiling at a request rate that looks trivially low — and because one API key is one shared bucket,
> the first thing that ceiling takes down is usually your own background job eating your users' quota.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ Three limits, and the one that actually bites

```
⭐ ① ***RPM — REQUESTS PER MINUTE.*** ⭐ The one people plan
⭐   for. ⭐ Rarely the binding constraint.

⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***TPM — TOKENS PER MINUTE. THE REAL CEILING.***
⭐   ⭐ It counts ***input + output***, ⭐⭐ AND YOUR INPUT IS
⭐     ENORMOUS BECAUSE YOU ARE DOING RAG.
⭐   ⭐ Worked: ⭐ 6,000 tokens of context + 500 out = 6,500
⭐     per request.
⭐     ⭐ At a 200,000 TPM tier: ⭐⭐ 30 REQUESTS PER MINUTE.
⭐       ***HALF A REQUEST PER SECOND.***
⭐     ⭐ Your RPM limit might be 5,000. ⭐⭐ IT IS IRRELEVANT.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ⇒ THIS IS WHY "WE'LL JUST ADD MORE CHUNKS" IS A
⭐     CAPACITY DECISION AS WELL AS A COST ONE (D433/457).
⭐     ⭐ k=12 instead of k=6 ***halves your throughput ceiling***.

⭐ ③ ***CONCURRENT REQUESTS*** — ⭐ sometimes explicit, often
⭐   implicit. ⭐ Day 453's semaphore.

⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE PLANNING FACT PEOPLE LEARN TOO LATE: ⭐ quota
⭐   increases have ***lead time***. ⭐⭐ DAYS TO WEEKS, WITH A
⭐   HUMAN IN THE LOOP AND USAGE HISTORY REQUIRED. ⭐ You cannot
⭐   get quota on launch day. ⭐⭐ ASK FOR IT WHEN YOU WRITE THE
⭐   DESIGN DOC, NOT WHEN THE 429s START.
```

# Part 2 — Count before you send

```
⭐ ***REACTIVE RATE LIMITING IS BACKOFF AFTER A 429. ⭐⭐ THAT IS
⭐   A WASTED ROUND TRIP AND, UNDER LOAD, A RETRY STORM.***
⭐ ***PROACTIVE IS A TOKEN BUCKET YOU KEEP YOURSELF.***

⭐ ***THE SHAPE:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ***ESTIMATE*** the request's tokens ***before*** sending
⭐     — `tiktoken` on the prompt, plus `max_tokens` for the
⭐     completion ⭐⭐ (RESERVE THE WORST CASE; RELEASE THE
⭐     DIFFERENCE AFTERWARDS);
⭐   ⭐ ② ***ACQUIRE*** that many tokens from a bucket refilling
⭐     at your TPM;
⭐   ⭐ ③ if unavailable, ⭐⭐ WAIT OR SHED — DECIDED BY PRIORITY
⭐     (PART 3), NOT BY ARRIVAL ORDER;
⭐   ⭐ ④ ***RECONCILE*** with the real usage from the response.

⭐ ⭐⭐ AND IT MUST BE ***DISTRIBUTED***: ⭐ ten pods each holding
⭐   a local bucket is ten times your quota.
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ Redis with a Lua script for atomic
⭐     check-and-decrement, ⭐ or divide the quota by replica
⭐     count and accept the waste. ⭐⭐ THE SECOND IS FINE UNTIL
⭐     REPLICAS ARE UNEVEN — WHICH THEY ARE DURING A DEPLOY.

⭐ ***THEN THE 429 HANDLING YOU STILL NEED:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ HONOUR `Retry-After` — IT BEATS YOUR FORMULA;
⭐   ⭐ full jitter (Day 456);
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AND A RETRY BUDGET, BECAUSE RETRYING INTO A RATE
⭐     LIMIT IS THE PUREST FORM OF MAKING IT WORSE.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ One quota, many workloads

```
⭐ ***THE INCIDENT THAT WILL HAPPEN TO YOU:***
⭐   ⭐ 02:00 — the nightly eval suite starts (Day 427).
⭐   ⭐ 02:01 — it consumes the entire TPM bucket.
⭐   ⭐ 02:02 — ⭐⭐ EVERY REAL USER GETS 429s, ⭐ and your
⭐     alerting says the provider is fine, ⭐⭐ BECAUSE IT IS.

⭐ ***YOUR WORKLOADS HAVE DIFFERENT URGENCY AND ONE BUCKET DOES
⭐   NOT KNOW THAT:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ INTERACTIVE — a human is waiting. Highest.
⭐   ⭐ ***AGENT RUNS*** — already started; ⭐⭐ STARVING A
⭐     HALF-COMPLETED RUN WASTES EVERYTHING ALREADY SPENT
⭐     (D439), ⭐ so in-flight runs outrank ***new*** interactive
⭐     requests.
⭐   ⭐ ***BACKGROUND*** — ingestion, summarisation, reindexing.
⭐   ⭐ ***EVALS AND CANARIES*** — important, ⭐ infinitely
⭐     deferrable.

⭐ ***THE FIXES, IN ORDER OF HOW CLEAN THEY ARE:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***SEPARATE API KEYS / PROJECTS PER WORKLOAD
⭐     CLASS.*** ⭐ The provider enforces the isolation for you.
⭐     ⭐⭐ CLEANEST, FREE, AND ALMOST NOBODY DOES IT UNTIL AFTER
⭐     THE INCIDENT.
⭐   ⭐ ② ***PRIORITY QUEUE IN FRONT OF THE BUCKET*** — ⭐ high
⭐     priority takes tokens first; low priority yields.
⭐   ⭐ ③ ***RESERVED SHARE*** — ⭐ background work may never
⭐     exceed 30% of the bucket, ⭐⭐ SO INTERACTIVE TRAFFIC
⭐     ALWAYS HAS HEADROOM.
⭐   ⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***THE BATCH API FOR ANYTHING DEFERRABLE*** — ⭐ it
⭐     is a ***separate quota pool*** ***and*** 50% cheaper
⭐     (Day 457). ⭐⭐ IT SOLVES THE COST PROBLEM AND THE
⭐     CONTENTION PROBLEM WITH ONE CHANGE, ⭐ which makes it the
⭐     highest-leverage line on this page.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. "Which rate limit hits you first?"**

⭐ Tokens per minute, almost always, and it surprises people because they plan for requests per minute.
⭐⭐ TPM counts input plus output, and in a RAG application the input is enormous. ⭐ Six thousand tokens
of context plus five hundred out is 6,500 per request, so a 200,000 TPM tier gives me **thirty requests
a minute** — half a request per second — while my RPM limit might be five thousand and completely
irrelevant. ⭐⭐ Which is why adding chunks is a capacity decision as well as a cost one: k=12 instead of
k=6 halves my throughput ceiling. ⭐ And quota increases have lead time — days to weeks with a human in
the loop and usage history required — so I ask for it when I write the design doc, not when the 429s
start.

**2. "How do you avoid hitting it?"**

⭐ Count before sending rather than backing off after a 429, because a 429 is a wasted round trip and,
under load, the start of a retry storm. ⭐⭐ I estimate the request's tokens with tiktoken plus
`max_tokens` for the completion, reserve the worst case from a bucket refilling at my TPM, and reconcile
against actual usage from the response. ⭐ The critical detail is that the bucket has to be
**distributed** — ten pods each with a local bucket is ten times my quota — so it's Redis with a Lua
script for atomic check-and-decrement, or divide by replica count and accept the waste. ⭐⭐ The second
is fine until replicas are uneven, which they are during every deploy.

**3. "What's the incident you'd predict?"**

⭐ The nightly eval suite eating the entire TPM bucket at two in the morning and 429-ing every real user,
while alerting says the provider is healthy — because it is. ⭐⭐ One API key is one shared bucket, and
the bucket doesn't know that interactive traffic matters more than a background job. ⭐ The cleanest fix
is separate keys or projects per workload class, so the provider enforces the isolation for me — it's
free and almost nobody does it until after the incident. ⭐⭐ Then a priority queue in front of the
bucket, and a reserved share so background work can never exceed thirty percent. ⭐ And the ordering
that people get wrong: an **in-flight agent run** outranks a new interactive request, because starving a
half-completed run wastes everything already spent on it.

**4. "Anything that solves two problems at once?"**

⭐ The batch API. ⭐⭐ It's a separate quota pool *and* fifty percent cheaper, so moving deferrable work
onto it fixes the contention problem and the cost problem with one change. ⭐ Ingestion, nightly
summarisation, reindexing, bulk classification — all of it has a twenty-four-hour tolerance and none of
it should be competing with a human who's waiting. ⭐⭐ That's the highest-leverage line in this area and
it's usually a day's work.

**5. "How portable is your code across providers?"**

⭐ The transport is portable behind my client protocol; the behaviour isn't. ⭐⭐ System prompt handling
and how strongly it's followed, tool-calling schemas and reliability, whether structured output is
constrained decoding or a polite request — which is a capability difference, not a syntax one —
tokenisation, so every budget and cost estimate is wrong after a switch, streaming event shapes, stop
reason names, prompt caching mechanisms, and refusal thresholds, which changes my guardrail
false-positive rate. ⭐ So a second provider isn't a config value. ⭐⭐ It's a second prompt version with
its own eval scores, its own cost model and its own guardrail calibration.

**6. "How would you route across providers?"**

⭐ Failover first, because it's simple and covers the real risk. ⭐ Then capability-based routing, which
is deterministic and explainable — this task needs constrained decoding, that one needs a huge window.
⭐⭐ Cost-based routing is only safe with per-model eval scores; without them you've automated a silent
quality regression and called it optimisation. ⭐ Load-based routing across providers to multiply
effective quota is legitimate, and it makes quality non-deterministic across users, ⭐⭐ so I log which
provider served each request or my evals stop meaning anything.

**7. "Would you use a gateway?"**

⭐ As a library, usually; as a hosted service, more carefully. ⭐⭐ LiteLLM or similar buys one interface
across many models, key management, per-key spend limits and built-in fallback — real value, and I'd
rather not write the normalisation myself. ⭐ The cost is another network hop and another dependency in
the critical path, and the bytes test: can I still see exactly what was sent? ⭐⭐ Self-hosted LiteLLM as
a library is often the sweet spot — the normalisation without the extra hop or the third party. ⭐ And
on provisioned throughput or committed spend: those reintroduce the provisioned cost model, so they're
worth it only under steady, predictable, measured load — the same condition as self-hosting, for the
same reason.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

⭐⭐ **Two minutes, out loud: "what's your rate limit strategy?"**

Start with the number that surprises people. **We're limited on tokens per minute, not requests. TPM
counts input plus output, and RAG input is enormous — six thousand tokens of context plus five hundred
out is 6,500 a request, so a two-hundred-thousand TPM tier is thirty requests a minute. Half a request
per second. The RPM limit of five thousand is irrelevant. Which also means adding chunks is a capacity
decision, not just a cost one: doubling k halves the throughput ceiling.**

Then how you stay under it. **We count before sending rather than backing off after a 429, because a 429
is a wasted round trip and the start of a retry storm. Estimate with tiktoken plus max_tokens, reserve
the worst case, reconcile against real usage. And the bucket is in Redis, because ten pods with local
buckets is ten times the quota — dividing by replica count works until replicas are uneven, which is
every deploy.**

Then the incident, because it's the part that shows operational experience. **The failure I'd predict is
our own nightly eval suite eating the whole bucket at two in the morning and 429-ing every real user,
while alerting says the provider is healthy — because it is. One key is one bucket, and the bucket
doesn't know interactive traffic matters more than a background job. So: separate keys per workload
class, which makes the provider enforce isolation for free; a reserved share so background work can't
exceed thirty percent; and priority ordering where an in-flight agent run outranks a *new* interactive
request, because starving a half-finished run wastes everything already spent. And the single best move
is putting deferrable work on the batch API — a separate quota pool and half the price, so it fixes
contention and cost at once.**

Close on the portability caveat. **On multi-provider: the transport is portable behind our client
protocol, the behaviour isn't. Tool-calling reliability, whether structured output is constrained
decoding or a polite request, tokenisation so every budget is wrong, refusal thresholds that shift
guardrail false positives. So a second provider is a second prompt version with its own eval scores, not
a config value. I'd start with plain failover, use capability-based routing where it's deterministic,
and only route on cost once I have per-model eval scores — otherwise I've automated a silent quality
regression and called it optimisation.**
