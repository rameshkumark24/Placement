# Day 332 🗄️

**P-332** · 🗄️ **SQL vs NoSQL at scale** — ⭐⭐ when you must commit to your queries

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: the real difference is not scale or schema, it is *when you must know your
> queries* — a relational database lets you decide tomorrow at the cost of a join, and a key-value or
> document store makes you decide today in exchange for a guaranteed constant-time answer.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ "NoSQL" names five unrelated things

```
⭐⭐ THE WORD IS USELESS. THE FIVE FAMILIES ARE NOT:
⭐   ① ⭐ KEY-VALUE — Redis, DynamoDB, Memcached.
⭐        ⭐ Get/put by key. ⭐⭐ O(1), AND NOTHING ELSE EXISTS.
⭐   ② ⭐ DOCUMENT — MongoDB, Couchbase, ⭐ Postgres JSONB.
⭐        ⭐ Nested values, queryable, per-document atomicity.
⭐   ③ ⭐ WIDE-COLUMN — Cassandra, HBase, Bigtable.
⭐        ⭐⭐ NOT "COLUMNAR". It is a partitioned, sorted map:
⭐        `(partition key) → (clustering key → value)`. ⭐ This
⭐        misunderstanding is extremely common; ⭐⭐ GETTING IT
⭐        RIGHT IS A SIGNAL.
⭐   ④ ⭐ GRAPH — Neo4j, Neptune. ⭐ Edges as first-class, so
⭐        multi-hop traversal is cheap.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐ SEARCH / ANALYTICS — Elasticsearch, ClickHouse,
⭐        Druid. ⭐ Inverted or columnar, ⭐⭐ AND ALMOST ALWAYS
⭐        A ***DERIVED*** STORE (Day 337).

⭐⭐ THEY SHARE ALMOST NOTHING. ⭐ Comparing "SQL vs NoSQL" is
⭐   comparing one thing to five, ⭐⭐ AND THE FIRST MOVE IN AN
⭐   INTERVIEW IS TO REFUSE THE FRAMING AND NAME THE FAMILY
⭐   YOU MEAN.
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The one real distinction

```
⭐⭐ ***RELATIONAL: DEFINE THE DATA, DISCOVER THE QUERIES LATER.
⭐      NORMALISED, WITH THE JOIN AS THE PRICE OF FLEXIBILITY.***
⭐   ⭐ New requirement Tuesday? Write a new query. Maybe an
⭐     index. ⭐ Ship it.

⭐⭐ ***KEY-VALUE / WIDE-COLUMN: DEFINE THE QUERIES, THEN SHAPE
⭐      THE DATA TO SERVE THEM. DENORMALISED, WITH THE WRITE
⭐      AMPLIFICATION AS THE PRICE OF SPEED.***
⭐   ⭐ New access pattern Tuesday? ⭐⭐ YOU MAY NEED A NEW TABLE,
⭐     A BACKFILL OF EVERY EXISTING ROW, AND A DUAL-WRITE
⭐     MIGRATION.

⭐⭐ SO THE QUESTION IS NEVER "WHICH IS FASTER". IT IS:
⭐   ***DO I KNOW MY ACCESS PATTERNS, AND WILL THEY HOLD?***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ MATURE, HIGH-VOLUME, WELL-UNDERSTOOD ⇒ commit to
⭐     them and buy the constant-time answer.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ A PRODUCT STILL BEING DISCOVERED ⇒ ***YOU DO NOT
⭐     KNOW YOUR QUERIES, AND ANYONE WHO SAYS OTHERWISE IS
⭐     GUESSING.*** ⭐ Use Postgres.

⭐⭐ AND THE ASYMMETRY THAT DECIDES IT FOR MOST TEAMS:
⭐   ⭐ Being wrong with Postgres costs you a slow query and an
⭐     index.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ BEING WRONG WITH DYNAMO COSTS YOU A DATA MIGRATION.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ Single-table design, and why it looks insane

```
⭐⭐ DYNAMODB'S MODEL, MINIMALLY:
⭐   ⭐ ***PARTITION KEY (PK)*** ⇒ which physical partition.
⭐   ⭐ ***SORT KEY (SK)***      ⇒ order within it; range queries
⭐     live here and ***only*** here.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ QUERY = ONE PK + AN OPTIONAL SK CONDITION. ⭐ That is
⭐     the entire query language. ⭐ `Scan` exists and is a
⭐     confession.

⭐⭐ SO TO FETCH "AN ORDER AND ITS ITEMS" IN ONE CALL, YOU PUT
⭐   THEM IN THE SAME PARTITION:
⭐     PK              SK                  attrs
⭐     ORDER#123       META                status, total
⭐     ORDER#123       ITEM#1              sku, qty
⭐     ORDER#123       ITEM#2              sku, qty
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ `Query(PK="ORDER#123")` RETURNS THE ORDER AND ALL ITS
⭐     ITEMS IN ONE ROUND TRIP, ALWAYS, AT ANY SCALE. ⭐ That is
⭐     the thing you bought.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ AND IT IS DAY 305'S AGGREGATE, ENFORCED BY THE
⭐     STORAGE ENGINE INSTEAD OF BY DISCIPLINE. ⭐ The partition
⭐     ***is*** the aggregate, and the transaction boundary and
⭐     the aggregate boundary become the same thing by
⭐     construction.

⭐⭐ TO ALSO QUERY "ALL ORDERS FOR A CUSTOMER", YOU ADD A ***GSI***
⭐   (a second copy of the data, keyed differently, ⭐ maintained
⭐   asynchronously — ⭐⭐ SO IT IS EVENTUALLY CONSISTENT, AND
⭐   THAT IS A DAY-331 DECISION YOU JUST MADE BY ADDING AN
⭐   INDEX).

⭐⭐ THE HONEST SUMMARY:
⭐   ⭐ ✔ Predictable single-digit-ms at any scale.
⭐   ⭐ ✔ No connection pool, no vacuum, no failover to run.
⭐   ⭐ ✘ ⭐⭐ EVERY NEW ACCESS PATTERN IS A DESIGN EXERCISE,
⭐     SOMETIMES A MIGRATION.
⭐   ⭐ ✘ ⭐⭐ AD HOC ANALYTICS IS IMPOSSIBLE. You export to S3
⭐     and query elsewhere. ⭐ Budget for that on day one, or
⭐     your first "can you pull the numbers for Q3" is a
⭐     two-week project.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ What Postgres already does that people leave

```
⭐⭐ THE MOST COMMON ARCHITECTURAL MISTAKE OF THE LAST DECADE:
⭐   ***LEAVING POSTGRES FOR SOMETHING IT ALREADY DID.***

⭐   ⭐ Need documents? ⇒ ⭐ `JSONB` + GIN. ⭐⭐ AND YOU KEEP
⭐     JOINS, TRANSACTIONS AND CONSTRAINTS ON THE SAME ROW.
⭐   ⭐ Need a queue? ⇒ ⭐ `SKIP LOCKED` (Day 261). ⭐ Good to
⭐     thousands/second.
⭐   ⭐ Need key-value? ⇒ ⭐ `UNLOGGED` table, or just a table.
⭐   ⭐ Need search? ⇒ ⭐ `tsvector` + GIN (Day 337). ⭐ Good to
⭐     millions of documents.
⭐   ⭐ Need geo? ⇒ ⭐ PostGIS, ⭐⭐ WHICH IS BETTER THAN MOST
⭐     PURPOSE-BUILT GEO STORES.
⭐   ⭐ Need time-series? ⇒ ⭐ partitioning + BRIN, or TimescaleDB.
⭐   ⭐ Need pub/sub? ⇒ ⭐ `LISTEN/NOTIFY` for modest volumes.
⭐   ⭐ Need vectors? ⇒ ⭐ `pgvector` (Day 292 used it).

⭐⭐ THE RULE THAT FOLLOWS:
⭐   ***ONE DATABASE UNTIL A ***MEASURED*** LIMIT FORCES A
⭐      SECOND, AND THEN ONE SPECIALISED STORE FOR THAT ONE
⭐      LIMIT.***
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ EVERY ADDITIONAL DATASTORE COSTS: a sync path that
⭐     can lag or break, a backup and restore story, an
⭐     on-call runbook, a consistency model, ⭐ and a new class
⭐     of bug where the two disagree.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Day 286's smallest-infrastructure rule, applied to
⭐     data.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐ Where Postgres genuinely stops

```
⭐⭐ SAY THESE UNPROMPTED — KNOWING THE LIMITS IS WHAT MAKES
⭐   "JUST USE POSTGRES" CREDIBLE RATHER THAN DOGMATIC:

⭐   ① ⭐⭐ WRITE THROUGHPUT BEYOND ONE NODE. ⭐ Tens of
⭐        thousands of writes/second is achievable; ⭐ hundreds
⭐        of thousands is not, on one primary. ⭐ Day 329 or
⭐        Citus.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ MULTI-REGION ACTIVE-ACTIVE WRITES. ⭐ Single-leader
⭐        by design. ⭐ Cassandra/Dynamo/Spanner territory.
⭐   ③ ⭐ PETABYTE ANALYTICAL SCANS. ⭐ Row storage loses badly
⭐        to ClickHouse/BigQuery — ⭐⭐ OFTEN BY 50–100×, because
⭐        a columnar engine reads three columns rather than
⭐        three hundred bytes per row.
⭐   ④ ⭐ MASSIVE FAN-OUT READS with strict per-key latency at
⭐        any scale. ⭐ Dynamo's constant-time promise is real.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐⭐ CONNECTION COUNT. ⭐ Every connection is a process
⭐        (~5–10 MB). ⭐⭐ 10,000 CONNECTIONS IS NOT A
⭐        CONFIGURATION, IT IS A PGBOUNCER — ⭐ and in a Python
⭐        shop with many gunicorn workers you hit this
⭐        embarrassingly early: ⭐⭐ 30 PODS × 8 WORKERS × A POOL
⭐        OF 10 IS 2,400 CONNECTIONS BEFORE ANY LOAD AT ALL.
⭐        ⇒ ⭐ Transaction-mode pooling, ⭐⭐ AND KNOW THAT IT
⭐          BREAKS SESSION-SCOPED FEATURES: prepared statements,
⭐          `SET LOCAL`, advisory locks held across statements,
⭐          `LISTEN/NOTIFY`. ⭐ SQLAlchemy needs
⭐          `prepared_statement_cache_size=0` with asyncpg
⭐          behind pgbouncer.
```

# Part 6 — ⭐⭐ Polyglot persistence, and the price nobody quotes

```
⭐⭐ THE REAL ARCHITECTURE OF A GROWN SYSTEM:
⭐   ⭐ Postgres — ⭐⭐ THE SYSTEM OF RECORD. Money, identity,
⭐     anything with an invariant.
⭐   ⭐ Redis — sessions, cache, rate limits, ephemeral state.
⭐   ⭐ S3 — blobs.
⭐   ⭐ Elasticsearch — search, derived.
⭐   ⭐ ClickHouse — analytics, derived.
⭐   ⭐ Kafka — the transport between them (Day 334).

⭐⭐ AND THE TWO RULES THAT KEEP IT SANE:
⭐   ① ⭐⭐ ***EXACTLY ONE SYSTEM OF RECORD PER FACT.*** ⭐ Every
⭐      other copy is derived, ⭐⭐ AND MUST BE REBUILDABLE FROM
⭐      THE SOURCE BY A COMMAND YOU HAVE ACTUALLY RUN.
⭐      ⇒ ⭐ If you cannot rebuild the search index, it is not
⭐        derived — it is a second system of record you did not
⭐        mean to create, ⭐⭐ AND IT WILL DISAGREE WITH THE
⭐        FIRST ONE ON A TUESDAY.
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ ***THE SYNC PATH IS A FIRST-CLASS COMPONENT.*** It
⭐      needs the outbox (Day 263), monitoring, a lag SLO
⭐      (Day 322), and a reconciliation job that ***counts and
⭐      compares*** rather than trusting.

⭐⭐ THE PRICE, ITEMISED — QUOTE IT WHEN SOMEONE PROPOSES A NEW
⭐   STORE: ⭐ a sync path, a lag SLO, a backup/restore drill,
⭐   an on-call runbook, a client library and its failure modes,
⭐   a consistency model to explain, ⭐⭐ AND ONE NEW WAY FOR
⭐   TWO SOURCES OF TRUTH TO DISAGREE IN FRONT OF A CUSTOMER.
```

# Part 7 — ⭐ Answering the question well

```
⭐⭐ THE STRUCTURE THAT SCORES:
⭐   ① ⭐ Refuse the framing: ⭐⭐ "NOSQL ISN'T ONE THING — DO
⭐      YOU MEAN KEY-VALUE, DOCUMENT, WIDE-COLUMN, GRAPH OR
⭐      SEARCH?"
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ ASK THE DECIDING QUESTION: "DO WE KNOW THE ACCESS
⭐      PATTERNS, AND WILL THEY STILL BE TRUE IN A YEAR?"
⭐   ③ ⭐ State the invariants. ⭐⭐ ANYTHING THAT MUST NEVER BE
⭐      WRONG WANTS TRANSACTIONS AND CONSTRAINTS.
⭐   ④ ⭐ Give the numbers (Day 323): writes/s, data size,
⭐      connections. ⭐ Compare against Part 5's limits.
⭐   ⑤ ⭐⭐ DECIDE, AND NAME THE COST YOU ACCEPTED.

⭐⭐ THE ANSWER THAT LANDS, IN ONE BREATH:
⭐   ***"Postgres as the system of record, because the
⭐      invariants are real and the access patterns will change.
⭐      Redis for sessions and rate limits. When write
⭐      throughput exceeds one node — and I'd want the measured
⭐      number first — I'd shard by tenant before I'd change
⭐      engine, because that keeps transactions inside a tenant.
⭐      The one place I'd reach for Dynamo on day one is a
⭐      genuinely key-value, enormous-volume path with a
⭐      pattern that cannot change: session storage, feed
⭐      entries, event dedup keys."***
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ Treating "NoSQL" as one thing | Five unrelated families. Naming which one is the first signal you know the area |
| ⭐⭐ Choosing on scale rather than access patterns | Almost nobody's write volume needs it; almost everybody's query set changes |
| ⭐ "Wide-column means columnar" | It's a partitioned sorted map. Cassandra is not ClickHouse |
| ⭐⭐ Denormalising before knowing the queries | You've committed to guesses and priced in a migration |
| ⭐ Leaving Postgres for documents, queues, search or geo | JSONB, `SKIP LOCKED`, `tsvector` and PostGIS already do these |
| ⭐⭐ Adding a datastore without quoting the price | Sync path, lag SLO, backups, runbook, and a new way for two truths to disagree |
| ⭐⭐ A derived store you cannot rebuild | It's a second system of record you created by accident |
| ⭐ Ignoring Postgres's connection limit | 30 pods × 8 workers × pool 10 is 2,400 connections before any traffic |
| ⭐ pgbouncer transaction mode without checking features | Breaks prepared statements, `SET LOCAL`, cross-statement advisory locks and `LISTEN/NOTIFY` |
| ⭐⭐ Forgetting a GSI is eventually consistent | Adding an index quietly made a consistency decision |
| ⭐ No analytics plan with DynamoDB | Ad hoc queries are impossible. The first "pull Q3's numbers" becomes a project |

## Interview questions

**1. "SQL or NoSQL for this system?"**
> ⭐⭐ First I'd ask which NoSQL — key-value, document, wide-column, graph and search share almost
> nothing. ⭐ Then the deciding question: **do we know the access patterns, and will they hold for a
> year?** ⭐⭐ Because the real trade isn't scale, it's *when you must commit to your queries* —
> relational lets me decide tomorrow at the cost of a join; Dynamo makes me decide today and gives me a
> constant-time answer. ⭐ Being wrong with Postgres costs an index. Being wrong with Dynamo costs a
> migration.

**2. "Explain single-table design."**
> ⭐ A Dynamo query is one partition key plus an optional sort-key condition — that's the whole
> language. ⭐ So you co-locate everything you need to fetch together: the order metadata and its items
> share a partition key with different sort keys, and one query returns all of it in one round trip at
> any scale. ⭐⭐ It's the aggregate boundary from DDD, **enforced by the storage engine instead of by
> discipline** — the partition is the aggregate and the transaction boundary is the same line. ⭐ New
> access patterns need a GSI, which is a second eventually-consistent copy.

**3. "When would you actually leave Postgres?"**
> ⭐ Five measured limits: write throughput past one node, active-active multi-region writes, petabyte
> analytical scans where columnar wins by fifty to a hundred times, extreme per-key latency at massive
> fan-out, and connection count. ⭐⭐ The connection one hits Python shops first — **thirty pods times
> eight workers times a pool of ten is twenty-four hundred connections before any traffic**, and each
> one is a process. ⭐ pgbouncer in transaction mode, knowing it breaks prepared statements and
> session-scoped features.

**4. "What does adding Elasticsearch cost?"**
> ⭐ A sync path that can lag or break, a lag SLO, a backup and restore drill, an on-call runbook, a
> client library with its own failure modes, ⭐⭐ and one new way for two sources of truth to disagree
> in front of a customer. ⭐ I'd accept all of it only against a measured limit, and I'd require that
> **the index be rebuildable from Postgres by a command I've actually run** — otherwise it's a second
> system of record I created by accident.

**5. "What does Postgres already do that people leave for?"**
> ⭐ Documents via JSONB with GIN, and you keep joins and constraints on the same row. Queues via `SKIP
> LOCKED`, good to thousands a second. Search via `tsvector` to millions of documents. Geo via PostGIS,
> which beats most purpose-built stores. Time-series via partitioning and BRIN. Vectors via pgvector.
> ⭐⭐ **One database until a measured limit forces a second** — and then one specialised store for that
> one limit, not a zoo.

**6. "You're building a session store for 50 million users. What do you pick?"**
> ⭐⭐ That's the case where I'd take Dynamo or Redis on day one without arguing: it's genuinely
> key-value, the access pattern is get-by-key and cannot change, the volume is large, and there are no
> cross-entity invariants. ⭐ TTL handles expiry natively. ⭐ It's exactly the shape where committing
> to the query costs nothing, because there was only ever one query.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Refuse the framing first: **NoSQL names five unrelated families**, and key-value,
document, wide-column, graph and search share almost nothing — so the first question is which one we
mean. Then give the real distinction, which is not scale: **it's when you must commit to your
queries.** Relational lets you decide tomorrow and charges you a join; Dynamo makes you decide today
and pays you a constant-time answer. State the asymmetry that decides it for most teams: **being wrong
with Postgres costs an index, being wrong with Dynamo costs a migration.** Explain single-table design
in one breath — one partition key plus a sort condition is the entire query language, so you co-locate
what you fetch together — **and point out it's the DDD aggregate enforced by the storage engine instead
of by discipline.** Then be honest in both directions: list what Postgres already does that people
leave for — JSONB, `SKIP LOCKED`, `tsvector`, PostGIS, pgvector — and then, unprompted, where it
genuinely stops: **write throughput past one node, active-active multi-region, petabyte columnar scans,
and connection count, which bites Python shops first because thirty pods times eight workers times a
pool of ten is twenty-four hundred connections before any traffic.** Close on the two polyglot rules:
**exactly one system of record per fact, and every derived store must be rebuildable by a command
you've actually run** — otherwise it isn't derived, it's a second source of truth you created by
accident.
