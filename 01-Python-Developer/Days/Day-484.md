# Day 484 💳

**P-484** · 💳 **System design round 4 — payments** — ⭐⭐ where refusing service is the correct answer

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** MOCK + SCORED

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: every instinct you have built over four stages says choose availability and
> degrade gracefully — and in payments that instinct is inverted, because it is always better to decline
> a valid payment than to accept an invalid one, and a system that stays up while getting money wrong is
> worse than one that stops.**

---

# Part 1 — ⭐⭐ The inversion

```
⭐ ***EVERYWHERE ELSE:*** ⭐ a stale feed is fine. ⭐ A degraded
⭐   search result is fine. ⭐⭐ `stale-if-error` IS A FEATURE.
⭐   ⭐ Availability wins, ⭐ and Stage 9 through Stage 14 trained
⭐     that instinct deliberately.

⭐ ***HERE:*** ⭐⭐ THERE IS NO ACCEPTABLE STALE BALANCE AND NO
⭐   ACCEPTABLE "PROBABLY CHARGED".
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ A DECLINED VALID PAYMENT COSTS YOU ONE TRANSACTION AND
⭐     AN ANNOYED CUSTOMER.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AN ACCEPTED INVALID ONE COSTS YOU MONEY, A CHARGEBACK,
⭐     A REGULATOR AND POSSIBLY A LICENCE.
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ THE ASYMMETRY IS ENORMOUS, AND IT IS WHY THE CAP
⭐     ANSWER FLIPS: ***CHOOSE CONSISTENCY, AND MAKE UNAVAILABLE
⭐     MEAN "DECLINE", NOT "GUESS".***

⭐ ***SAY THIS OUT LOUD EARLY. ⭐⭐ IT REFRAMES THE WHOLE ROUND
⭐   AND IT IS THE SINGLE HIGHEST-VALUE SENTENCE AVAILABLE:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ "MOST SYSTEMS I'VE DESIGNED CHOOSE AVAILABILITY. THIS
⭐     ONE DOESN'T. ⭐ If I can't establish the truth, the correct
⭐     behaviour is to decline and let the user retry — ⭐⭐ NOT TO
⭐     ACCEPT OPTIMISTICALLY AND RECONCILE LATER."

⭐ ***AND THE ONE PLACE THAT ***DOES*** INVERT BACK:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ ***READING*** A PAYMENT'S STATUS CAN BE EVENTUALLY
⭐     CONSISTENT. ⭐ A dashboard showing a five-second-old view
⭐     is fine.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ IT IS THE ***WRITE*** — TAKING MONEY — THAT MUST NOT
⭐     GUESS. ⭐ Being precise about which half needs which
⭐     guarantee is what stops this sounding dogmatic.
```

# Part 2 — The state machine, and why nothing is deleted

```
⭐ ***A PAYMENT IS A ***STATE MACHINE***, AND THE TRANSITIONS ARE
⭐   THE DESIGN:***

⭐   `created` ──► `authorised` ──► `captured` ──► `settled`
⭐        │             │                │
⭐        ▼             ▼                ▼
⭐    `failed`      `expired`        `refunded`
⭐                  `voided`         `disputed`

⭐ ***FOUR RULES, AND EACH IS A SEPARATE INSIGHT:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***TRANSITIONS ARE APPEND-ONLY. NOTHING IS EVER
⭐     UPDATED IN PLACE.*** ⭐ A payment row's history is the
⭐     sequence of events, ⭐⭐ AND "WHY IS THIS PAYMENT IN THIS
⭐     STATE?" MUST BE ANSWERABLE MONTHS LATER.
⭐   ⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***A REFUND IS NOT A REVERSAL.*** ⭐ It is a ***new
⭐     transaction*** in the opposite direction, linked to the
⭐     original. ⭐⭐ THE ORIGINAL CHARGE STILL HAPPENED AND MUST
⭐     REMAIN VISIBLE — ⭐ that is Day 411's "compensation is a
⭐     new fact, not an undo", and here it is a legal requirement
⭐     rather than a design preference.
⭐   ⭐ ③ ***illegal transitions must be impossible, not
⭐     unlikely*** — ⭐⭐ ENFORCE IN THE DATABASE (A CHECK ON
⭐     `(from_state, to_state)`), ⭐ not in a service method that
⭐     four call sites can bypass.
⭐   ⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***AUTHORISE AND CAPTURE ARE SEPARATE ON PURPOSE.***
⭐     ⭐ Authorisation reserves funds; capture takes them.
⭐     ⭐⭐ SHIP-THEN-CHARGE NEEDS THAT GAP, ⭐ and an
⭐     authorisation ***expires*** — typically seven days — ⭐⭐ SO
⭐     "EXPIRED" IS A REAL STATE YOUR CODE MUST HANDLE, NOT AN
⭐     EDGE CASE.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ The third outcome, with money attached

```
⭐ ***YOU CALL THE PAYMENT PROVIDER. THE REQUEST TIMES OUT.***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ DID THE CUSTOMER GET CHARGED?
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ YOU DO NOT KNOW, AND BOTH GUESSES ARE EXPENSIVE:
⭐     ⭐ assume no and retry ⇒ ***double charge***;
⭐     ⭐ assume yes and move on ⇒ ***you shipped for free***.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THIS IS DAY 398'S THIRD OUTCOME, AND IT IS THE ENTIRE
⭐     PROBLEM OF THIS DOMAIN.

⭐ ***THE FOUR MECHANISMS, IN ORDER:***
⭐   ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***AN IDEMPOTENCY KEY GENERATED BY THE CLIENT, AT
⭐     THE POINT OF INTENT.*** ⭐ Sent to the provider on every
⭐     attempt. ⭐⭐ A RETRY WITH THE SAME KEY RETURNS THE
⭐     ***ORIGINAL*** RESULT RATHER THAN CHARGING AGAIN.
⭐     ⭐ ⭐⭐ IT MUST BE THE CLIENT'S, NOT GENERATED INSIDE YOUR
⭐       SERVICE — ⭐ a service-generated key differs on each
⭐       retry and never fires (D444).
⭐   ⭐ ② ***record `PENDING` before the call, never after.***
⭐     ⭐⭐ WRITING AFTER LOSES EXACTLY THE RECORD YOU CANNOT
⭐     RECONSTRUCT.
⭐   ⭐ ③ ⭐⭐ ***ON RECOVERY, ***ASK*** RATHER THAN RETRY.*** ⭐ Query
⭐     the provider by your key. ⭐⭐ THE PROVIDER IS THE ONLY
⭐     COMPONENT THAT KNOWS.
⭐   ⭐ ④ ⭐⭐ ***AND IF THE ANSWER IS STILL UNKNOWN, IT STAYS
⭐     `PENDING` AND A HUMAN SEES IT.*** ⭐ A payment stuck in
⭐     pending for an hour is an alert, not a silent row.
⭐     ⭐⭐ REFUSING TO GUESS IS THE CORRECT ENGINEERING ANSWER
⭐       HERE, AND SAYING SO IS THE SIGNAL.

⭐ ***WEBHOOKS — THE OTHER DIRECTION, AND THEY ARE HOSTILE:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THEY ARRIVE ***OUT OF ORDER*** ⭐ — `captured` before
⭐     `authorised` is normal.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THEY ARRIVE ***TWICE***, ⭐ or ⭐⭐ THREE DAYS LATE
⭐     AFTER A PROVIDER OUTAGE.
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ VERIFY THE SIGNATURE ⭐ (an unauthenticated webhook
⭐     endpoint is a way to mark your own payments as captured);
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ MAKE HANDLERS IDEMPOTENT AND ***ORDER-INDEPENDENT***
⭐     — ⭐ apply by event id with a uniqueness constraint, and
⭐     ⭐⭐ IGNORE AN EVENT THAT WOULD MOVE THE STATE MACHINE
⭐     BACKWARDS;
⭐   ⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ ACK FAST, PROCESS ASYNCHRONOUSLY. ⭐ Providers retry
⭐     on a slow response, so slow processing manufactures
⭐     duplicates.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Reconciliation is the product

```
⭐ ***THE UNCOMFORTABLE TRUTH:*** ⭐ your view of what happened
⭐   and the provider's view ***will*** diverge. ⭐⭐ NOT MIGHT.
⭐   WILL. ⭐ Timeouts, dropped webhooks, manual refunds someone
⭐   issued in the provider's dashboard, partial captures.

⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ ***A NIGHTLY RECONCILIATION JOB THAT COMPARES YOUR
⭐   LEDGER AGAINST THEIR SETTLEMENT FILE, LINE BY LINE, AND
⭐   PRODUCES A DISCREPANCY REPORT.***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THAT REPORT IS NOT A NICE-TO-HAVE. IT IS THE MECHANISM
⭐     BY WHICH YOU KNOW YOUR SYSTEM IS CORRECT AT ALL, ⭐ and it
⭐     is the same argument as the double-entry sum from Day 480:
⭐     ⭐⭐ A SYSTEM THAT CANNOT DETECT ITS OWN ERRORS DOES NOT
⭐     HAVE FEWER ERRORS, IT HAS UNDETECTED ONES.

⭐ ***THE FOUR DISCREPANCY CLASSES, AND WHAT EACH MEANS:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ IN YOURS, NOT THEIRS ⭐ → you recorded a charge that
⭐     never happened. ⭐ You shipped for free.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ IN THEIRS, NOT YOURS ⭐ → they charged a customer and
⭐     you have no record. ⭐⭐ THE WORST ONE: YOU TOOK MONEY AND
⭐     DO NOT KNOW WHY.
⭐   ⭐ ***amount mismatch*** → currency, rounding, or a partial
⭐     capture you did not model.
⭐   ⭐ ***status mismatch*** → a dropped webhook, ⭐ usually
⭐     resolved by replaying it.

⭐ ***AND THE THING THAT MAKES RECONCILIATION POSSIBLE AT ALL:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE DOUBLE-ENTRY LEDGER (DAY 480). ⭐ Every movement is
⭐     an entry; the internal sum must be zero; ⭐⭐ AND THE
⭐     PROVIDER'S SETTLEMENT IS AN ***EXTERNAL*** CHECK ON THE
⭐     SAME NUMBERS. ⭐ Two independent verifications of one truth
⭐     is what "auditable" actually means.
```

# Part 5 — Scope: the best security decision is not having the data

```
⭐ ***PCI-DSS SCOPE IS THE DOMINANT ARCHITECTURAL CONSTRAINT AND
⭐   CANDIDATES ALMOST NEVER MENTION IT.***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ IF A CARD NUMBER TOUCHES YOUR SERVER, ***THAT SERVER
⭐     IS IN SCOPE***, ⭐ and so is everything that can reach it,
⭐     and so are your logs, your backups and your traces.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THAT IS AN ANNUAL AUDIT AND A PERMANENT TAX ON EVERY
⭐     ENGINEERING DECISION.

⭐ ⇒ ⭐⭐ ***NEVER RECEIVE THE CARD NUMBER.***
⭐   ⭐ The card goes ***browser → provider*** directly (hosted
⭐     fields or an iframe); ⭐ you receive a ***token***.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ YOUR SERVERS NEVER SEE A PAN, SO THEY ARE LARGELY OUT
⭐     OF SCOPE, AND THE HARDEST COMPLIANCE PROBLEM IN THE
⭐     PROJECT DISAPPEARS BY CONSTRUCTION.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ THE GENERALISABLE SENTENCE — SAY IT: ⭐ ***"THE BEST
⭐     SECURITY DECISION AVAILABLE IS USUALLY NOT TO HAVE THE
⭐     DATA."*** ⭐ It applies far beyond payments.

⭐ ***AND THE TWO OTHER THINGS THAT COME UP:***
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ MULTI-STEP FLOWS ARE ***SAGAS*** (D411): ⭐ reserve
⭐     inventory → authorise → ship → capture. ⭐⭐ THE PIVOT IS
⭐     CAPTURE; ⭐ before it, everything is cancellable, ⭐⭐ AFTER
⭐     IT, EVERYTHING IS A REFUND — ⭐ and knowing where your
⭐     pivot is, is the design.
⭐   ⭐ ***fraud and 3DS add latency and declines.*** ⭐⭐ A FRAUD
⭐     CHECK IS A ***MODEL***, SO IT HAS FALSE POSITIVES, ⭐ and
⭐     each one is a real customer refused — ⭐⭐ WHICH IS DAY
⭐     448'S FALSE-POSITIVE ARGUMENT WITH REVENUE ATTACHED.
```

```
⭐  ┌────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────┬─────┐
⭐  │  1 │ ⭐⭐ STATED THE AVAILABILITY INVERSION EARLY    │     │
⭐  │  2 │ ⭐⭐ SEPARATED READ (EVENTUAL) FROM WRITE       │     │
⭐  │    │ (STRICT)                                      │     │
⭐  │  3 │ Modelled it as an explicit state machine      │     │
⭐  │  4 │ ⭐⭐ APPEND-ONLY; NOTHING UPDATED IN PLACE      │     │

⭐  │  6 │ Illegal transitions enforced in the database  │     │
⭐  │  7 │ ⭐⭐ AUTHORISE/CAPTURE SPLIT, AND EXPIRY        │     │
⭐  │  8 │ ⭐⭐ IDEMPOTENCY KEY FROM THE ***CLIENT***       │     │
⭐  │  9 │ PENDING written before the provider call     │     │
⭐  │ 10 │ ⭐⭐ ON RECOVERY, QUERIES RATHER THAN RETRIES   │     │

⭐  │ 12 │ Webhooks: signed, idempotent, order-independent│    │
⭐  │ 13 │ ⭐⭐ RECONCILIATION AS THE CORRECTNESS MECHANISM│     │
⭐  │ 14 │ ⭐⭐ RAISED PCI SCOPE AND TOKENISATION          │     │
⭐  └────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────┴─────┘

⭐ ⭐⭐ ROWS 1, 11, 13 AND 14 ARE THE SEPARATORS. ⭐ Row 11
⭐   especially: ⭐⭐ MOST CANDIDATES TRY TO ENGINEER AWAY THE
⭐   UNKNOWN OUTCOME, ⭐ and the correct answer is that some
⭐   uncertainty is irreducible and the right response is to stop
⭐   and surface it.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. "Design a payment system."**

⭐ I'd start by inverting the instinct I've built everywhere else. ⭐⭐ Most systems choose availability and
degrade gracefully — a stale feed is fine, a degraded search result is fine. ⭐ This one doesn't. ⭐⭐ It
is always better to decline a valid payment than to accept an invalid one: a decline costs one
transaction and an annoyed customer, an incorrect acceptance costs money, a chargeback, a regulator and
possibly a licence. ⭐ So I choose consistency, and "unavailable" has to mean *decline*, not *guess*.
⭐⭐ I'd be precise though: reading a payment's status can be eventually consistent — a dashboard five
seconds behind is fine. It's the write, taking money, that must not guess.

**2. "The provider call times out. Did the charge go through?"**

⭐ I don't know, and both guesses are expensive: retry and I double-charge, assume success and I ship for
free. ⭐⭐ So four mechanisms. An idempotency key generated by the *client* at the point of intent, sent on
every attempt — it has to be the client's, because a service-generated key differs on each retry and
never fires. ⭐ A PENDING row written before the call, never after, because writing after loses exactly
the record I can't reconstruct. ⭐⭐ On recovery I *ask* rather than retry — query the provider by my key,
since they're the only component that knows. ⭐ And if the answer is still unknown, it stays pending and a
human sees it. ⭐⭐ Refusing to guess is the correct engineering answer, not a cop-out.

**3. "Why is a refund not just reversing the charge?"**

⭐ Because the charge happened, and pretending otherwise destroys the audit trail. ⭐⭐ A refund is a new
transaction in the opposite direction, linked to the original, and both remain visible forever. ⭐ That's
compensation as a new fact rather than an undo — and here it's a legal requirement rather than a design
preference, because a regulator asking "what happened to this customer's money" needs the whole sequence.
⭐⭐ The same principle makes the whole state machine append-only: I never update a payment row in place,
because "why is this payment in this state?" has to be answerable months later.

**4. "Webhooks arrive out of order."**

⭐ They do, and they also arrive twice and sometimes three days late after a provider outage — so I design
for hostile input rather than treating those as edge cases. ⭐⭐ Signature verification first, because an
unauthenticated webhook endpoint is a way for anyone to mark their own payments as captured. ⭐ Then
handlers that are idempotent and order-independent: apply by event id with a uniqueness constraint, and
ignore any event that would move the state machine backwards. ⭐⭐ And ack fast, process asynchronously —
providers retry on a slow response, so slow processing manufactures the very duplicates I'm defending
against.

**5. "How do you know the system is correct?"**

⭐ Reconciliation, and I'd call it the product rather than a background job. ⭐⭐ My view and the
provider's *will* diverge — timeouts, dropped webhooks, manual refunds someone issued in their dashboard,
partial captures. ⭐ So a nightly job compares my ledger against their settlement file line by line and
produces a discrepancy report. ⭐⭐ Four classes: in mine not theirs means I recorded a charge that never
happened; in theirs not mine is the worst, because I took money and don't know why; amount mismatches are
usually currency or partial captures; status mismatches are usually a dropped webhook. ⭐ It's the same
argument as the double-entry sum: ⭐⭐ a system that can't detect its own errors doesn't have fewer
errors, it has undetected ones.

**6. "Where do you store card numbers?"**

⭐ Nowhere, and that's the most important architectural decision in the design. ⭐⭐ If a card number
touches my server, that server is in PCI scope — and so is everything that can reach it, plus my logs,
backups and traces. That's an annual audit and a permanent tax on every engineering decision. ⭐ So the
card goes from the browser to the provider directly via hosted fields, and I receive a token. ⭐⭐ My
servers never see a PAN, so they're largely out of scope and the hardest compliance problem in the
project disappears by construction. ⭐ The generalisable version is worth stating: **the best security
decision available is usually not to have the data.**

**7. "Checkout involves inventory, payment and shipping. How do you coordinate?"**

⭐ It's a saga, because there's no transaction spanning three services. ⭐⭐ Reserve inventory, authorise
payment, ship, capture — and the thing I'd identify explicitly is the pivot. ⭐ Before capture everything
is cancellable: I can void the authorisation and release the reservation with no visible consequence.
⭐⭐ After capture, everything is a refund, which is customer-visible and creates a support interaction.
⭐ Knowing where the pivot sits is the design, and it's why authorise and capture being separate isn't an
implementation detail — it's what gives me a window where mistakes are still free.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

⭐⭐ **Three minutes, out loud: "design a payment system."**

Open by inverting, and say that you're inverting. **"Everything I've designed so far chooses
availability — stale feeds are fine, degraded search is fine, serve-stale-on-error is a feature. This one
doesn't, and I want to say that up front because it changes every subsequent decision. It's always better
to decline a valid payment than to accept an invalid one: a decline costs one transaction and an annoyed
customer, an incorrect acceptance costs money, a chargeback and a regulator. So I choose consistency, and
unavailable has to mean decline, not guess. Though I'd be precise — reading a payment's status can be
eventually consistent. It's the write that must not guess."**

Then go to the hardest thing immediately. **"The core problem is the third outcome. I call the provider,
the request times out, and I don't know whether the customer was charged. Retrying double-charges;
assuming success ships for free. So: an idempotency key generated by the client at the point of intent —
client, not service, because a service-generated key differs on every retry and never fires. A PENDING row
written before the call, never after, because writing after loses the one record I can't reconstruct. On
recovery I query the provider by my key rather than retrying, because they're the only component that
knows. And if it's still unknown, it stays pending and a human sees it. Refusing to guess is the correct
answer — some uncertainty here is irreducible."**

Then the structure, with the rule that makes it auditable. **"A payment is an append-only state machine —
created, authorised, captured, settled, with failure and refund branches. Nothing is updated in place,
and a refund is a new transaction in the opposite direction rather than a reversal, because the original
charge happened and must remain visible. Illegal transitions are enforced by a database constraint, not a
service method four call sites can bypass."**

Close on the two things candidates skip. **"Two more. Reconciliation isn't a background job, it's how I
know the system is correct at all — my view and the provider's will diverge through timeouts, dropped
webhooks and manual refunds, so a nightly line-by-line comparison produces a discrepancy report, and the
worst class is a charge in theirs and not mine, because I took money and don't know why. And PCI scope:
if a card number touches my server, that server and everything reachable from it is in scope, including
my logs. So the card goes browser-to-provider directly and I only ever hold a token. The general version
of that is worth stating — the best security decision available is usually not to have the data."**
