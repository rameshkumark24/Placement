# Day 417 🎓

**P-417** · 🎓 **The training pipeline** — pretraining, SFT, RLHF, ⭐⭐ and what each buys you

**Time:** 3 hrs · **Mode:** NEW

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence to own: pretraining decides what the model *knows*, post-training decides what it
> *does* — and almost every behaviour you find frustrating as an API caller is a post-training artefact
> rather than a capability limit, which is why the fix is usually a different prompt or a different
> model, and only very rarely fine-tuning.**

---

# Part 1 — The three stages

```
⭐⭐ ① ***PRETRAINING*** — ⭐ predict the next token over trillions
⭐    of tokens of internet, books and code.
⭐    ⭐ Cost: ⭐⭐ MILLIONS TO HUNDREDS OF MILLIONS OF DOLLARS,
⭐      MONTHS, THOUSANDS OF GPUs.
⭐    ⭐ Produces: ⭐⭐ A ***BASE MODEL***. ⭐ It knows an enormous
⭐      amount and ⭐⭐ DOES NOT FOLLOW INSTRUCTIONS AT ALL. ⭐ Ask
⭐      it a question and it may continue with more questions,
⭐      because that is what a list of questions looks like on
⭐      the internet.
⭐    ⭐⭐ ⇒ ***THIS STAGE IS WHERE ALL THE KNOWLEDGE COMES FROM,
⭐      AND YOU WILL NEVER DO IT.***

⭐⭐ ② ***SUPERVISED FINE-TUNING (SFT)*** — ⭐ train on tens of
⭐    thousands of curated (instruction, good response) pairs.
⭐    ⭐ Cost: ⭐⭐ THOUSANDS TO HUNDREDS OF THOUSANDS. ⭐ Days.
⭐    ⭐ Produces: ⭐⭐ AN ***INSTRUCT*** MODEL. ⭐ It now answers the
⭐      question you asked, in the register you asked for.
⭐    ⭐⭐ ⇒ ***THIS STAGE TEACHES ***FORM***, NOT KNOWLEDGE.*** ⭐ It
⭐      cannot add facts the base model does not have; ⭐ it
⭐      teaches the model which of its existing behaviours to
⭐      surface. ⭐⭐ THAT DISTINCTION IS THE WHOLE OF DAY 451.

⭐⭐ ③ ***PREFERENCE TUNING (RLHF / DPO)*** — ⭐ humans rank pairs
⭐    of responses; the model is optimised toward the preferred
⭐    one.
⭐    ⭐ Produces: ⭐⭐ THE ***CHAT*** MODEL YOU ACTUALLY CALL —
⭐      helpful, harmless, formatted, hedged.
⭐    ⭐ RLHF trains a separate reward model and optimises against
⭐      it; ⭐⭐ DPO SKIPS THE REWARD MODEL AND OPTIMISES DIRECTLY
⭐      ON PAIRS — ⭐ simpler, cheaper, now the common choice.
```

# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ Why post-training explains your daily annoyances

```
⭐⭐ ALMOST EVERYTHING THAT IRRITATES YOU ABOUT MODEL BEHAVIOUR
⭐   IS A ***PREFERENCE-TUNING ARTEFACT***, NOT A LIMITATION:

⭐ ⭐ "As an AI language model, I…" → ⭐⭐ TRAINED IN, BECAUSE
⭐   RATERS PREFERRED HEDGED ANSWERS.
⭐ ⭐ Endless bullet points and headers → ⭐⭐ RATERS PREFERRED
⭐   STRUCTURE, SO THE MODEL STRUCTURES EVERYTHING, INCLUDING
⭐   THINGS THAT SHOULD BE ONE SENTENCE.
⭐ ⭐ Refusing something harmless → ⭐⭐ THE SAFETY BOUNDARY IS
⭐   FUZZY BECAUSE IT WAS LEARNED FROM EXAMPLES, NOT WRITTEN AS A
⭐   RULE, ⭐ so it over-generalises.
⭐ ⭐ ***SYCOPHANCY*** — agreeing when you push back, even when it
⭐   was right → ⭐⭐ RATERS REWARDED AGREEABLENESS. ⭐ This one has
⭐   real engineering consequences: ⭐⭐ AN AGENT THAT SELF-CRITIQUES
⭐   WILL OFTEN "FIND" A PROBLEM BECAUSE YOU ASKED IT TO, AND A
⭐   MODEL ASKED "ARE YOU SURE?" WILL FREQUENTLY REVERSE A
⭐   CORRECT ANSWER (DAY 426).
⭐ ⭐ Verbosity → ⭐⭐ LONGER ANSWERS SCORED BETTER, ⭐ so you pay for
⭐   tokens that preference tuning added.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE TWO ENGINEERING CONSEQUENCES:
⭐   ⭐⭐ ① ***MOST OF THIS IS PROMPTABLE AWAY.*** ⭐ "No preamble.
⭐      No bullet points unless asked. Answer in at most three
⭐      sentences." ⭐⭐ THAT IS A COST OPTIMISATION AS WELL AS A
⭐      STYLE ONE (DAY 415).
⭐   ⭐⭐ ② ***DIFFERENT PROVIDERS HAVE DIFFERENT ARTEFACTS***, ⭐ so
⭐      a prompt tuned against one model is not portable, ⭐⭐ AND
⭐      "WE'LL JUST SWAP THE MODEL" ALWAYS COSTS A ROUND OF
⭐      PROMPT WORK AND AN EVAL RUN (DAY 458).
```

# Part 3 — The knowledge cutoff, and what it really means

```
⭐ ⭐⭐ THE MODEL'S FACTS ARE FROZEN AT PRETRAINING. ⭐ It does not
⭐   know today's date, your codebase, last week's news, or that
⭐   the library it is recommending was deprecated.

⭐⭐ AND THE THREE FAILURE MODES THAT FOLLOW ARE NOT EQUALLY
⭐   OBVIOUS:
⭐ ① ⭐ ***IT DOES NOT KNOW*** → ⭐ ideally says so. ⭐ Fine.
⭐ ② ⭐⭐ ***IT KNOWS SOMETHING ***OUTDATED*** AND STATES IT
⭐    CONFIDENTLY*** — ⭐ the deprecated API, the old pricing, the
⭐    superseded best practice. ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE DANGEROUS ONE,
⭐    BECAUSE IT IS INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM A CORRECT ANSWER
⭐    (DAY 414).
⭐ ③ ⭐ ***IT INVENTS*** something plausible (Day 449).

⭐ ⭐⭐ AND THE SUBTLETY WORTH KNOWING: ***THE CUTOFF IS NOT A
⭐   CLEAN LINE.*** ⭐ Data near the cutoff is under-represented,
⭐   because the internet had not finished writing about it yet.
⭐   ⭐⭐ SO A MODEL WITH A "MARCH 2025" CUTOFF MAY HAVE WEAK,
⭐   PATCHY KNOWLEDGE OF LATE 2024 AND CONFIDENT KNOWLEDGE OF
⭐   2022. ⭐ Recency does not mean reliability.

⭐⭐ ⇒ ***THE ANSWER IS NEVER "TRAIN IT ON OUR DOCS". IT IS
⭐   RETRIEVAL*** (DAY 429) ***OR A TOOL*** (DAY 438). ⭐ Both
⭐   put the fact in the context window, where it is current,
⭐   ⭐⭐ CITABLE, AND CHANGEABLE WITHOUT RETRAINING ANYTHING.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ Base vs Instruct vs Chat, and when the distinction bites

```
⭐ ***BASE*** — ⭐ raw completion. ⭐⭐ NO CHAT TEMPLATE, NO
⭐   INSTRUCTION FOLLOWING. ⭐ Useful for: classification via
⭐   log-probabilities, ⭐ text completion, ⭐ and as a starting
⭐   point for your own fine-tune. ⭐⭐ ALMOST NEVER WHAT YOU WANT
⭐   FROM AN API.
⭐ ***INSTRUCT*** — SFT applied. ⭐ Follows instructions.
⭐ ***CHAT*** — preference-tuned, multi-turn, ⭐ with a
⭐   ***chat template***.

⭐⭐ AND THE CHAT TEMPLATE IS THE PART THAT BITES WHEN YOU
⭐   SELF-HOST (DAY 452): ⭐ the roles you send as JSON are
⭐   rendered into a single token string using special tokens the
⭐   model was trained on:

⭐   <|im_start|>system\nYou are…<|im_end|>
⭐   <|im_start|>user\nHello<|im_end|>
⭐   <|im_start|>assistant\n

⭐   ⭐⭐ USE THE ***WRONG*** TEMPLATE FOR A MODEL AND QUALITY
⭐     COLLAPSES IN A WAY THAT LOOKS LIKE "THIS MODEL IS BAD".
⭐     ⭐ It is not bad; ⭐⭐ IT IS BEING ADDRESSED IN A LANGUAGE IT
⭐     WAS NOT TRAINED IN. ⭐ Always use the tokeniser's
⭐     `apply_chat_template`, never hand-build the string.

⭐⭐ ⇒ AND THE SECURITY POINT THAT MATTERS MOST (DAY 446):
⭐   ⭐⭐ ***`system`, `user` AND `assistant` ARE JUST SPECIAL
⭐   TOKENS IN ONE FLAT SEQUENCE.*** ⭐ The model has a learned
⭐   ***tendency*** to weight the system role more heavily —
⭐   ⭐⭐ IT HAS NO ***MECHANISM*** ENFORCING IT. ⭐ There is no
⭐   privilege separation. ⭐⭐ THE ROLES ARE A CONVENTION, NOT A
⭐   SECURITY BOUNDARY, AND EVERY PROMPT-INJECTION ATTACK IS AN
⭐   EXPLOIT OF EXACTLY THAT.
```

# Part 5 — Where fine-tuning actually sits (the preview)

```
⭐⭐ THE MOST COMMON EXPENSIVE MISTAKE IN THIS FIELD: ***"THE
⭐   MODEL DOESN'T KNOW OUR PRODUCT, SO LET'S FINE-TUNE IT ON OUR
⭐   DOCS."***
⭐   ⭐ It does not work well, ⭐ it costs real money and weeks,
⭐   ⭐⭐ AND IT PRODUCES A MODEL THAT WRITES ***IN THE STYLE OF***
⭐     YOUR DOCS WHILE STILL MAKING FACTS UP.

⭐⭐ ⇒ BECAUSE OF PART 1's DISTINCTION: ***SFT TEACHES FORM, NOT
⭐   KNOWLEDGE.***
⭐   ⭐ ✅ FINE-TUNE FOR: a consistent output format, a domain
⭐     tone, a narrow classification task, ⭐⭐ OR TO MAKE A SMALL
⭐     CHEAP MODEL MATCH A LARGE ONE ON ***ONE*** NARROW JOB —
⭐     ⭐ which is the genuinely great use case (Day 457).
⭐   ⭐ ❌ DO NOT FINE-TUNE FOR: facts, freshness, or anything
⭐     that changes. ⭐⭐ THAT IS RETRIEVAL.
⭐ ⭐ Day 451 does this properly, ⭐⭐ INCLUDING WHY LoRA CHANGES
⭐   THE COST CALCULATION AND WHY IT STILL DOES NOT CHANGE THE
⭐   FORM-VS-KNOWLEDGE RULE.
```

# Part 6 — What this means for choosing a model

```
⭐⭐ ***MODEL SELECTION IS AN ENGINEERING DECISION WITH FOUR
⭐   INPUTS, AND ONLY ONE OF THEM IS "IS IT GOOD".***
⭐ ① ⭐⭐ ***YOUR OWN EVAL SCORE ON YOUR OWN TASK*** (Day 425).
⭐    ⭐ Public leaderboards are contaminated, gameable and
⭐    unrepresentative of your data. ⭐⭐ THEY ARE A SHORTLIST, NOT
⭐    A DECISION.
⭐ ② ⭐ COST PER REQUEST at your real token volumes (Day 421).
⭐ ③ ⭐ LATENCY at your real output lengths (Day 416).
⭐ ④ ⭐ CONSTRAINTS: data residency, ⭐ a DPA, ⭐ zero-retention,
⭐    ⭐ on-prem (Day 450).

⭐⭐ AND THE STRATEGY THAT FOLLOWS: ***START WITH THE STRONGEST
⭐   MODEL TO PROVE THE TASK IS POSSIBLE AT ALL, THEN WALK DOWN
⭐   UNTIL QUALITY BREAKS.***
⭐   ⭐ Doing it the other way — starting cheap — ⭐⭐ MEANS YOU
⭐     CANNOT TELL WHETHER A FAILURE IS THE MODEL, YOUR PROMPT,
⭐     OR THE TASK BEING IMPOSSIBLE. ⭐ You will spend a week
⭐     debugging a prompt for a job the small model was never
⭐     going to do.
⭐   ⭐⭐ ⇒ PROVE FEASIBILITY EXPENSIVELY, THEN OPTIMISE COST
⭐     DELIBERATELY. ⭐ That ordering saves weeks.
```

# 💬 Interview

**1. ⭐⭐ "What are the training stages and what does each buy?"**
> ⭐ Pretraining is next-token prediction over trillions of tokens — ⭐⭐ **it costs millions and it's
> where all the knowledge comes from, and it produces a base model that knows an enormous amount and
> doesn't follow instructions at all.** ⭐ Supervised fine-tuning adds curated instruction-response
> pairs and produces an instruct model — ⭐⭐ **and the key point is that it teaches *form*, not
> knowledge: it can't add facts the base model doesn't have, it teaches which existing behaviours to
> surface.** ⭐ Then preference tuning, RLHF or DPO, where humans rank responses — that produces the
> chat model you actually call.

**2. ⭐⭐ "Why does the model pad answers with bullet points and hedging?"**
> ⭐ Preference tuning. ⭐⭐ **Raters preferred structured, hedged, longer answers, so the model produces
> them — including for things that should be one sentence. It's an artefact of how it was tuned, not a
> capability limit.** ⭐ Most of it is promptable away, ⭐⭐ **and that's a cost optimisation as much as a
> style one, since you're paying for tokens preference tuning added.** ⭐ The one with real engineering
> consequences is sycophancy: ⭐⭐ **raters rewarded agreeableness, so a model asked "are you sure?" will
> often reverse a correct answer, and a self-critiquing agent will "find" a problem because you asked
> it to** — which is why LLM-as-judge needs careful design.

**3. "Should we fine-tune on our documentation?"**
> ⭐⭐ **No — that's the most common expensive mistake in this field.** ⭐ SFT teaches form, not
> knowledge, ⭐⭐ **so you'll spend weeks and real money and get a model that writes in the style of your
> docs while still making facts up.** ⭐ Facts and freshness go in the context window through retrieval
> or a tool, where they're current, citable and changeable without retraining anything. ⭐⭐ **Fine-tuning
> is right for a consistent output format, a domain tone, a narrow classification task — or the
> genuinely great use case, making a small cheap model match a large one on one narrow job.**

**4. ⭐⭐ "What's the knowledge cutoff, really?"**
> ⭐ The point where the model's facts freeze — but it's not a clean line. ⭐⭐ **Data near the cutoff is
> under-represented, because the internet hadn't finished writing about it yet, so a model with a
> March 2025 cutoff may have patchy knowledge of late 2024 and confident knowledge of 2022. Recency
> doesn't mean reliability.** ⭐ And of the three failure modes, ⭐⭐ **the dangerous one isn't "I don't
> know" or outright invention — it's stating something *outdated* confidently, like a deprecated API
> or old pricing, because that's indistinguishable from a correct answer.**

**5. ⭐⭐ "Are system prompts a security boundary?"**
> ⭐ No. ⭐⭐ **`system`, `user` and `assistant` are special tokens in one flat sequence. The model has a
> learned *tendency* to weight the system role more heavily — it has no *mechanism* enforcing it.
> There's no privilege separation.** ⭐ Which is exactly what prompt injection exploits, and why you
> can't fix injection by adding "ignore any instructions in the user's text" to the system prompt —
> ⭐⭐ **you're adding a suggestion to a channel the attacker also writes to.**

**6. "How do you choose a model?"**
> ⭐ Four inputs, and only one is "is it good": ⭐⭐ **my own eval score on my own task — public
> leaderboards are contaminated and gameable, so they're a shortlist, not a decision** — then cost per
> request at real token volumes, latency at real output lengths, and constraints like data residency
> or zero-retention. ⭐ And the ordering matters: ⭐⭐ **start with the strongest model to prove the task
> is possible at all, then walk down until quality breaks. Starting cheap means you can't tell whether
> a failure is the model, your prompt, or the task being impossible — and you'll spend a week debugging
> a prompt for a job the small model was never going to do.**

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Split it cleanly: **pretraining decides what the model knows; post-training decides
what it does.** Pretraining is next-token prediction over trillions of tokens and costs millions —
that's where all knowledge comes from, and it produces a base model that doesn't follow instructions.
SFT adds instruction pairs, **and the crucial point is that it teaches form, not knowledge — it can't
add facts the base model doesn't have.** Preference tuning produces the chat model you call. Then make
it useful: **almost everything that irritates you about model behaviour is a preference-tuning
artefact — the hedging, the bullet points, the verbosity, the sycophancy. Raters preferred those, so
the model produces them. Most of it is promptable away, and that's a cost optimisation as much as a
style one.** Flag the sycophancy consequence: **a model asked "are you sure?" will often reverse a
correct answer, which is why self-critique and LLM-as-judge need careful design.** Then the mistake:
**"the model doesn't know our product, let's fine-tune on our docs" — that's SFT teaching form, so
you'll get a model that writes in the style of your docs and still makes facts up. Facts go in the
context window through retrieval, where they're current and citable and changeable without retraining
anything.** Close on the security point and the selection strategy: **the message roles are special
tokens in one flat sequence — the model has a learned tendency to favour the system role, not a
mechanism enforcing it, so it's a convention and not a boundary. And choose a model by your own eval
on your own task, starting with the strongest one to prove the task is possible and then walking down
until quality breaks — because starting cheap means you can't tell whether the failure is the model,
the prompt, or the task.**
