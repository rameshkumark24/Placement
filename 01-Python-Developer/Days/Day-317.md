# Part 2 — ⭐⭐ The state pattern, and when it earns its keep

```
⭐⭐ THE STATES: IDLE → COLLECTING_MONEY → DISPENSING →
⭐   (IDLE | REFUNDING), plus OUT_OF_SERVICE.

⭐⭐ THE `if` VERSION AND WHY IT ROTS:
⭐   ⭐ `insert_coin()` needs `if self.state == …` five times,
⭐     and so does `select()`, `cancel()`, `dispense()`.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ FIVE METHODS × FIVE STATES = TWENTY-FIVE BRANCHES
⭐     SCATTERED ACROSS FIVE FUNCTIONS, and adding a state means
⭐     finding all five. ⭐ You will find four.

⭐⭐ THE STATE PATTERN: one class per state, each implementing
⭐   the same operations.
⭐   ⭐ + ⭐⭐ ADDING A STATE IS ***ONE NEW FILE***, and the
⭐        compiler/type-checker tells you what you must implement.
⭐   ⭐ + Illegal operations are handled once, in a base class
⭐        that raises `InvalidOperation`.
⭐   ⭐ − more classes, and the transitions are distributed
⭐        across them.

⭐⭐ AND THE HONEST ALTERNATIVE, WHICH YOU SHOULD OFFER:
⭐   ⭐ ***A TRANSITION TABLE AS DATA*** (Day 254):
⭐        {(IDLE, "insert"): COLLECTING, …}
⭐   ⭐ + ⭐⭐ THE WHOLE MACHINE IS VISIBLE IN ONE PLACE, it is
⭐        testable by enumeration, and it generates the diagram
⭐        (Day 309).
⭐   ⭐ − behaviour still needs a home.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ SAYING "THE STATE PATTERN FOR BEHAVIOUR, A TABLE FOR
⭐     THE TRANSITIONS, AND THE TABLE IS THE DOCUMENTATION" IS A
⭐     BETTER ANSWER THAN RECITING EITHER ONE.
```

# Part 3 — ⭐⭐ 10–25: change-making, which is the algorithm

```python
def make_change(amount: int, inventory: dict[int, int]) -> dict[int, int]:
    """⭐⭐ GREEDY IS ***WRONG*** IN GENERAL, AND WRONG HERE
       WHENEVER A DENOMINATION IS EXHAUSTED."""
    coins = sorted(inventory, reverse=True)
    result: dict[int, int] = {}
    for c in coins:
        take = min(amount // c, inventory[c])
        if take:
            result[c] = take
            amount -= c * take
    if amount:
        raise InsufficientChange(amount)     # ⭐⭐ A REAL OUTCOME
    return result
```

```
⭐⭐ THREE THINGS TO SAY OUT LOUD:
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ GREEDY IS OPTIMAL FOR CANONICAL SYSTEMS (INR, USD)
⭐     AND ***NOT IN GENERAL***: with {1, 3, 4}, making 6 greedily
⭐     gives 4+1+1 = three coins when 3+3 = two. ⭐ Knowing that
⭐     your greedy choice depends on the currency being canonical
⭐     is exactly the kind of thing interviewers probe.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ AND EVEN IN A CANONICAL SYSTEM, GREEDY BREAKS WHEN A
⭐     DENOMINATION IS ***EXHAUSTED*** — needing ₹6 with no ₹5
⭐     coins left. ⭐ The exact answer is a bounded-coin DP over
⭐     amount × denominations, which for vending-machine sizes is
⭐     trivial to run.
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ `InsufficientChange` MUST BE CHECKED ***BEFORE***
⭐     DISPENSING, NOT AFTER — ⭐ otherwise you have delivered the
⭐     product and cannot pay the change, which is the exact
⭐     failure this whole day is about.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Which yields the ordering rule: ⭐⭐ RESERVE THE CHANGE,
⭐     THEN DISPENSE, THEN RELEASE THE CHANGE.
```

# Part 4 — ⭐⭐ 25–35: the transaction, and the two-phase problem

```
⭐⭐ WHAT MUST NEVER HAPPEN, STATED AS INVARIANTS:
⭐   ① money accepted ⇒ ⭐ product dispensed ***OR*** refunded
⭐   ② product dispensed ⇒ ⭐ money accepted
⭐   ③ ⭐⭐ CASH IN THE MACHINE = SUM OF THE AUDIT LOG. ALWAYS.

⭐⭐ THE ORDER THAT MAKES IT SAFE — AND ORDER IS THE ENTIRE
⭐   ANSWER, BECAUSE THERE IS NO TRANSACTION ACROSS A MOTOR:
⭐   ① validate: item in stock ***and*** change available
⭐   ② ⭐⭐ WRITE THE INTENT TO THE AUDIT LOG FIRST (persisted,
⭐        fsynced) — ⭐ "about to dispense A4 for ₹35, change ₹15"
⭐   ③ commit the money (it is now the machine's)
⭐   ④ dispense
⭐   ⑤ release change
⭐   ⑥ mark the log entry complete

⭐⭐ AND THE RECOVERY, WHICH IS WHY STEP ② EXISTS:
⭐   ⭐ ON BOOT, READ THE LOG. ⭐⭐ ANY INCOMPLETE ENTRY IS AN
⭐     INTERRUPTED TRANSACTION, AND THE MACHINE KNOWS EXACTLY
⭐     WHAT IT OWES.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Without the write-ahead entry, a power cut between ③
⭐     and ④ is ⭐⭐ INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM A NORMAL BOOT — the
⭐     money is in the box and nobody knows a customer is owed
⭐     anything.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ THIS IS WRITE-AHEAD LOGGING (Day 186), IN A VENDING
⭐     MACHINE. ⭐ Naming that connection is the strongest single
⭐     thing you can say in this problem.
```

# Part 5 — ⭐⭐ 35–42: the motor jams

```
⭐⭐ THE QUESTION: "THE MOTOR TURNS BUT NOTHING FALLS."

⭐   ⭐ You cannot know whether the product was delivered. ⭐⭐
⭐     THIS IS `payment_unknown` (Day 263) WITH A MOTOR INSTEAD
⭐     OF A PAYMENT PROVIDER — ⭐ the same three-outcome
⭐     problem, and saying so scores.
⭐   ⭐ THREE RESPONSES, AND A REAL MACHINE USES ALL THREE:
⭐     ⭐ ① ⭐⭐ A SENSOR AT THE CHUTE turns "unknown" into
⭐        "known" — ⭐ the correct engineering answer is to
⭐        ***remove the ambiguity*** rather than handle it.
⭐     ⭐ ② no sensor ⇒ ⭐ refund and mark the slot suspect;
⭐        favour the customer, because a wrongly-refunded snack
⭐        costs ₹35 and a wrongly-kept payment costs a customer.
⭐     ⭐ ③ ⭐ log it as `DISPENSE_UNCERTAIN` for the operator.

⭐⭐ AND THE ATM VERSION, WHERE THE STAKES INVERT:
⭐   ⭐ The account is debited, then the notes jam.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ YOU ***CANNOT*** JUST FAVOUR THE CUSTOMER: the money
⭐     is real and reconciliation is legally required.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ So: ⭐⭐ AUTHORISE (HOLD) FIRST, DISPENSE, ***THEN***
⭐     CAPTURE. ⭐ A hold that is never captured expires — which
⭐     is Day 316's seat hold, with money. ⭐ Plus a note counter
⭐     and an end-of-day cash reconciliation against the ledger.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐⭐ NOTE THE STRUCTURAL PATTERN: ***HOLD → ACT →
⭐     COMMIT***, with an expiry on the hold. ⭐ It is the same
⭐     shape as the seat booking and the outbox, and pointing
⭐     that out is what makes these look like one skill rather
⭐     than five puzzles.
```

# Part 6 — ⭐ Concurrency, and the extensions

```
⭐   ⭐ ⭐⭐ A VENDING MACHINE IS SINGLE-USER BY CONSTRUCTION —
⭐     one person at the front — so ⭐ the concurrency is
⭐     between the ***user thread and the maintenance thread***
⭐     (restocking, coin collection), not between customers.
⭐   ⇒ ⭐ Restocking mid-transaction must not change the
⭐     inventory a reservation depends on: ⭐⭐ RESERVE THE ITEM
⭐     AND THE CHANGE AT VALIDATION TIME, so the maintenance
⭐     thread sees them as unavailable.
⭐   ⭐ An ATM is genuinely concurrent — ⭐⭐ THE ACCOUNT IS
⭐     SHARED WITH EVERY OTHER CHANNEL, so the balance check is
⭐     a conditional UPDATE, never a read-then-write (Day 310).
⭐   ⭐ EXTENSIONS: ⭐ card payments ⇒ the same hold/capture
⭐     shape; ⭐ multiple items per transaction ⇒ all-or-nothing
⭐     reservation (Day 316); ⭐ a fleet ⇒ each machine is
⭐     authoritative for its own cash and syncs an audit log
⭐     upward, ⭐⭐ WHICH IS THE OUTBOX PATTERN OVER A FLAKY
⭐     NETWORK.
⭐ ⇒ ⭐ CLOSE: three tests (change exhaustion, power cut between
⭐   commit and dispense, restock during a transaction); one
⭐   change (chute sensor); ⭐⭐ AT 100×: nothing changes for one
⭐   machine — the scaling problem is the fleet's audit sync, and
⭐   it is at-least-once.
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| `if` chains over states | Twenty-five branches across five methods; adding a state means finding all five. |
| Reciting the state pattern only | Pattern for behaviour, table for transitions, table as documentation. |
| Assuming greedy change is optimal | It's optimal for canonical currencies only — {1,3,4} making 6 proves it. |
| Greedy with an exhausted denomination | It fails even in a canonical system; use bounded DP. |
| Checking change after dispensing | You've delivered the product and can't pay the change. |
| No write-ahead audit entry | A power cut is indistinguishable from a normal boot. |
| Not naming it as write-ahead logging | It's the same mechanism as a database WAL, and the connection scores. |
| Treating a jam as a definite failure | It's `payment_unknown` with a motor; you don't know. |
| Handling ambiguity instead of removing it | A chute sensor turns unknown into known — that's the real fix. |
| Favouring the customer at an ATM | The money is real and reconciliation is legally required. |
| Debit-then-dispense at an ATM | Authorise, dispense, capture — a hold with an expiry. |
| Ignoring the maintenance thread | Restocking mid-transaction is the actual concurrency here. |
| Read-then-write on an ATM balance | The account is shared with every other channel. |

## Interview questions

**Q: State pattern or a transition table?**

> Both, doing different jobs. The state pattern for behaviour, so each state is one class and adding a
> state is one new file with the type checker telling me what to implement — versus `if` chains, where
> five methods times five states is twenty-five branches scattered around, and adding a state means
> finding all five places and finding four. But the transitions themselves go in a table as data,
> because that makes the whole machine visible in one place, testable by enumeration, and able to
> generate its own diagram. Reciting either pattern alone is a weaker answer than saying which part
> each is good at.

**Q: How do you make change?**

> Greedy from the largest denomination, with two caveats I'd volunteer. Greedy is optimal only for
> canonical currency systems — with denominations of one, three and four, making six greedily gives
> three coins where two would do — and even in a canonical system it breaks the moment a denomination
> is exhausted, which is exactly what happens in a vending machine. For these sizes the exact answer
> is a bounded-coin dynamic program and it costs nothing to run. The critical part is ordering:
> insufficient change is checked and the change is *reserved* before dispensing, because otherwise
> you've delivered the product and can't pay.

**Q: The power fails after the coin is taken but before the product drops.**

> That's why the audit entry is written first. The order is: validate stock and change, write the
> intent to a persisted log, commit the money, dispense, release change, mark complete. On boot the
> machine reads the log, and any incomplete entry is an interrupted transaction it knows the details
> of. Without that write-ahead entry, a power cut in the middle is indistinguishable from a normal
> boot — the money's in the box and nobody knows a customer is owed anything. It's write-ahead logging,
> the same mechanism a database uses, in a vending machine.

**Q: The motor turns and nothing falls out.**

> You don't know whether the product was delivered, which is the same three-outcome problem as a
> payment timeout — it's `payment_unknown` with a motor. The best answer is to remove the ambiguity
> rather than handle it: a sensor at the chute turns unknown into known. Failing that, refund and mark
> the slot suspect, because a wrongly refunded snack costs thirty-five rupees and a wrongly kept
> payment costs a customer. The ATM version inverts the stakes — you can't just favour the customer
> with real money and a legal reconciliation duty — so it authorises a hold, dispenses, then captures,
> and an uncaptured hold expires. That's the seat hold from the booking problem, with money.

## 🎙️ Articulation drill

**Two minutes.** Say what the problem actually is: **not the state pattern, but what happens when the
motor jams after the coin is committed.** Cover structure briefly — state classes for behaviour, a
transition table for the transitions, and the table doubling as documentation. Then change-making with
the two caveats: **greedy is optimal only for canonical currencies, and it breaks anyway when a
denomination runs out** — and the ordering rule, that insufficient change is detected and reserved
*before* dispensing, because otherwise you've delivered the product and can't pay. Then the core:

machine. Close on the jam as **`payment_unknown` with a motor**, where the right engineering move is to
